The observer's journal

12 FEB

The night the sky turned red

+
Oxygen at three hundred kilometres glows crimson only when the storm is strong enough to reach it. We saw it for eleven minutes — a pulsing rose curtain above the green — and then it was gone, as if someone had closed a door upstairs.
28 JAN

How to read a corona

+
When the aurora is directly overhead, perspective folds the curtains into a crown of rays converging on a single point. Lie on your back in the snow. Do not blink. It will last less than a minute.
09 JAN

Camera settings for beginners

+
Wide lens, widest aperture, ISO 1600–3200, four to eight seconds. Focus manually on a bright star. Keep a spare battery inside your jacket — the cold drains them in twenty minutes.
22 DEC

Solstice, and no sun at all

+
Polar night: the sun stays below the horizon for six weeks. At noon the sky is a deep, luminous blue, and the aurora sometimes appears in daytime, faint as a thought.
