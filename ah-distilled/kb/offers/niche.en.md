## The gist {#gist}

A narrower market looks like fewer customers. In practice it is the opposite of a loss. "Accounting services" is a commodity that competes with every bookkeeper in town. "Accounting for dental practices" speaks to one owner's exact situation, so it sounds more relevant, feels more likely to work and supports a higher price. The product inside can be almost the same. The positioning changes what it is worth.

<div class="formula" markdown="1">
**Narrower market** → more relevant offer → higher perceived likelihood → **higher price**
</div>

The second half of the idea is **commitment**. Picking a niche is easy. Staying with it long enough to learn its language, collect proof and become known is what makes it pay. When results are slow, Alex's default is to keep the market and fix the offer, not to jump to a new market and start from zero.

## How it works {#how}

### Why narrowing raises value

A specific offer moves several levers of the [Value Equation](value-equation.html) at once:

- **Perceived likelihood rises.** A buyer believes something built for people like them will work for them. Case studies from the same industry are far more convincing than general ones.
- **Effort drops.** You already know their systems, their regulations and their typical problems, so they explain less and you deliver faster.
- **Comparison disappears.** A generalist is compared with every other generalist. A specialist in one niche has few direct competitors, which is the start of a [Grand Slam Offer](grand-slam-offer.html).

### How to narrow

Start from a broad [starving crowd](starving-crowd.html) and add one qualifier at a time. Typical qualifiers:

| Qualifier | Example |
|---|---|
| Industry | Manufacturers, dental practices, gyms |
| Size | 50 to 200 employees, single location, franchise owners |
| Role | CFO, production manager, practice owner |
| Situation | Moving to a new ERP, opening a second location, preparing for an audit |
| Problem | Month-end close takes too long, scrap is too high |

Each step makes the message sharper and the list of prospects shorter but easier to find. Rule of thumb: you have gone far enough when you can name the buyer, describe their problem in their own words, and list a hundred real prospects.

### Pick one and commit

Many people treat a niche like an experiment they abandon after a few weeks of weak results. Each switch resets the proof, the reputation and the market knowledge you built. Alex's rule of thumb is to stay with the market and treat poor results as a signal about the offer: change the promise, the price, the guarantee, the bonuses or the delivery before you change who you sell to. A market is worth leaving only if it fails the basic tests of pain, money, reach or growth.

### What commitment buys you

- **Language.** You learn the words buyers use for their problems, which improves every ad and sales call.
- **Proof.** Every client becomes a case study that is directly relevant to the next one.
- **Reusable assets.** Templates, checklists and ready-made solutions built for one client fit the next client in the same niche, so delivery gets cheaper as value goes up.
- **Reputation.** Niches are small worlds. People talk, and referrals come faster.

### You can widen later

A niche is a starting point, not a prison. Once you dominate one segment, you can expand to an adjacent one with similar problems, carrying your proof and assets with you. Narrowing first and widening later is far easier than starting broad and trying to stand out.

## Examples {#examples}

*Examples are invented for illustration.*

### Qlik consulting (B2B analytics)

<div class="ba" markdown="1">
<div class="before" markdown="1">
**Before.** "Qlik development and consulting for all industries." Rate €70 per hour. Every project starts from scratch, and each prospect compares three quotes.
</div>
<div class="after" markdown="1">
**After.** "Production analytics for mid-sized plastics and metal manufacturers": a fixed package with ready-made OEE, scrap and delivery apps that connect to the ERP systems common in that industry. Fixed price €18,000.
</div>
</div>

The same skills are now aimed at one buyer. Because most of the apps are reused, a project that used to take 200 hours takes 90. At €70 per hour, 200 hours would have earned €14,000. The niche package earns €18,000 for 90 hours, which is €200 per hour.

### Bookkeeping for dental practices (local service)

A bookkeeper switches from "accounting for small businesses" to "accounting for dental practices". She learns the practice software, the typical cost structure and the questions owners ask at year-end. She builds a monthly report showing revenue per chair and per dentist. Owners refer her to each other at professional events. Her fee rises from €250 to €400 per month, and she spends less time per client because every month looks familiar.

### Hopping versus iterating

| Month | Market hopper | Committed |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Gyms, no sales | Gyms, no sales |
| 2 | Switches to dentists | Adds a guarantee |
| 3 | Switches to restaurants | Changes the price and the name |
| 4 | Starting over again | First three clients and a case study |

## Common mistakes {#mistakes}

- **Fear of missing out on other customers.** Narrowing your message does not mean refusing other work. It means your marketing speaks to one buyer.
- **Hopping markets after a few weeks.** Each switch resets learning and proof. Change the offer first.
- **A niche defined by you, not by the buyer.** "Companies that value data" is not a group anyone identifies with. Use industry, role or situation.
- **Narrowing into a market with no money.** A niche still has to pass the [starving crowd](starving-crowd.html) tests.
- **Keeping generic delivery.** If the niche is only in the headline and the delivery is unchanged, the buyer notices.

## How Alex would apply it {#alex}

- **He narrows until the offer feels personal.** The test is whether the buyer reads it and thinks it was made for them.
- **He raises the price after narrowing.** A specific offer is worth more, so he expects to charge more for the same core work.
- **He keeps the market and changes the offer.** Weak results are a reason to rework the promise, price, guarantee or bonuses, not to start over in a new market.
- **He uses the niche to build reusable assets** so that delivery cost falls with every new client.
- **He widens only after winning.** Expansion goes to adjacent markets once the first one is clearly working.
- **Questions he would ask:** Who is the one buyer this is for? What do you know about them that a generalist doesn't? How long have you actually stayed with this market? What have you changed in the offer before blaming the market?

## Worksheet {#worksheet}

Answer in writing for your current business.

1. **Current market:** How do you describe your target customer today? Could a competitor use the same sentence?
2. **Narrowing:** Add industry, size, role and situation. Write the narrowest version you could still build a list of 100 prospects for.
3. **Advantage:** What do you already know about this niche (systems, regulations, problems) that a generalist doesn't?
4. **Commitment:** For how long will you stay with this market? Write the date.
5. **Iteration plan:** List three offer changes you will test before you consider changing the market.
6. **Next market:** Which adjacent niche could you widen into later with the same proof and assets?
