## The playbook in one page {#overview}

The four books describe Alex's frameworks: how to build an offer, get leads, structure a money model and close. This layer captures something different: how he thinks when a real owner tells him about a real problem. It is distilled from 103 of his videos (hotseats, live Q&As, lectures and sales trainings). The transcripts stay private. What you read here is paraphrased, organised by theme, and every entry lists the videos it comes from, with timestamps.

The books answer "what is the framework?". The playbook answers "what would he do first, what would he ignore, and why?". It favours patterns that recur across many videos, marks single-video points as such, and keeps his numbers as rules of thumb with their context. Where the notes say nothing, the playbook says nothing.

Across all of the videos, the same operating principles keep coming back:

1. **Find the one constraint.** First triage is supply versus demand: if customers doubled, what would break? Then attack the funnel stage furthest below benchmark and ignore the rest.
2. **The stated problem is rarely the real one.** "Leads" often turns out to be the offer or the sales process, "ads" turns out to be LTV, "people" turns out to be a broken model.
3. **Numbers before advice.** He asks in a fixed order: what you sell, to whom, at what price, how you acquire, close rate, LTV, CAC, churn, gross margin, capacity. Lifetime gross profit to CAC is the core lens.
4. **More, then better, then new.** Squeeze what already works before trying anything new. Never switch off a working channel to test another.
5. **Focus and subtract.** One business, one avatar, one channel, one priority, roughly until $1M a year. Kill side ventures and losing units.
6. **Price is the first and cheapest lever.** Most owners are underpriced; very high close rates and thin margins are warning signs. Add bonuses instead of discounting.
7. **Pull cash forward.** Solve cash problems through offer design (prepay, up-front fees, quick upsells) before debt or capital, so CAC comes back within about 30 days.
8. **Fix the leaky bucket before buying traffic.** Retention and lifetime value are where the advantage lives, and churn is usually an onboarding or communication problem.
9. **Talent is the long-term constraint.** Recruit like you acquire customers, pay more for people who have done it before, and judge people by the metrics they track.
10. **Move the founder from doer to teacher to leader.** Reducing founder dependence is the route from income to enterprise value.
11. **Nail it before you scale it.** No second location, product or business until the first is profitable and runs without the owner. Better before bigger.
12. **Change is costly, so choose few moves.** Two or three highest-impact moves, sequenced by impact, confidence and ease, one change at a time.

## How Alex would use these ideas {#alex}

- **Start with the goal and the numbers.** Ask what the owner actually wants (cash, freedom or an exit), then collect the key metrics from the diagnose section before forming any opinion.
- **Name the constraint as one question.** Decide whether the business is supply- or demand-constrained, find the weakest stage against benchmark, and phrase it as "how do I…?".
- **Place the business on its stage.** Revenue and headcount decide which rules apply; the scale section shows what the founder should give up next.
- **Pick two or three moves from the levers in his order:** price and cash first, then retention and the sales process, then more of the working channel, and only then something new.
- **Check who will execute.** If the owner or a key person is the bottleneck, the people section comes before any growth tactic.
- **Calibrate against a similar case.** Find the closest business in the casebook and compare its diagnosis with yours.
- **Say what not to do.** End with a short sequenced plan for the next 90 days and the distractions to drop.
