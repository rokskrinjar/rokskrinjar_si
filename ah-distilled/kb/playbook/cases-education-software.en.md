## The gist {#gist}

Coaching, course and creator businesses tend to bring Alex the same three complaints: ads stopped scaling, customers churn, and the founder has run out of hours. Underneath, he usually finds that the owner wants to switch something big (platform, avatar, B2B, a new product) when the cheaper fix is inside the current funnel. Creative volume is too low, the weakest funnel step has never been tested, value per customer is too low to absorb rising acquisition costs, and the back end is sold too late or not at all. Many founders also over-deliver: Slack access, long unstructured calls and group sessions nobody attends, which caps capacity without raising retention.

His prescriptions are consistent. Keep the channel that works and feed it far more creative. Raise lifetime value first, through upsells, renewal pitches timed to milestones and events, annual prepay, performance components or a diagnostic sale of the outcome. Fix churn at the start: qualify for the customers who activate, onboard one-on-one, and deliver an early win. On price he moves both ways, but he warns hard against cutting price to fix churn, citing his own costly mistakes. When a founder is the bottleneck, he adds structure (a scripted webinar, a self-serve checkout, clipped content) before adding people.

Software and sellability form a separate thread. For software he treats retention as the whole game: find the cohort that stays, then grow. For education businesses that want an exit, he notes that founder-led education is hard to sell, and points owners to the sellable assets their students or audiences create.

## Cases {#cases}

### Couples-coaching program · ~$480k/yr, ~$206k profit · founders at capacity, challenge attendance falling

- **Situation.** Two 12-month offers ($5k group, $25k high-touch with chat access), a five-day challenge funnel on paid social, slow payback.
- **Diagnosis.** A supply constraint caused by over-delivery (chat access, custom plans, group calls nobody valued). The cheap tier was built instead of fixing capacity, and event renewals failed because nobody pitched.
- **Advice.** One ~$25k offer: deep one-on-one onboarding, quarterly calls in groups of four, two or three in-person getaways, a curated community. Kill chat access and weekly calls; sunset the $5k tier. Collect ~$9k up front with layaway as a downsell. Test a short date-night event in place of the challenge, run ~100 image ads, and pitch renewals in person with a fast-action incentive.
- **Why.** Capacity rises from ~60 to 120–150 clients without hiring; the first payment drives conversion more than total price.
- **Source.** [If I Wanted To Scale A Service Business In 2026, Here's What I'd Do](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N5MExtki_VI&t=281s) (04:41)

### Online program for parents of neurodivergent children · ~$770k/yr, ~$555k profit · founder spends ~30 hours a week in DMs

- **Situation.** $2,500/year offer, 9–10% conversion of live attendees, 3–4 hour unstructured live sessions, selling only to a warm audience.
- **Diagnosis.** The founder is the bottleneck, held back by beliefs that selling and higher prices are bad; the one-on-one tier is mispriced.
- **Advice.** $2,500 per six months, recurring. A structured 45-minute webinar with slides and FAQ. Checkout at $2,500 plus bonus versus $3,000 to talk to someone first. A three-day guarantee and next-day onboarding. Reprice coaching ~5x or drop it. Clip Q&As into 30–50 posts a week, all pointing to DMs; winners become ads.
- **Why.** Structure lifts conversion and frees founder time. Follow-up: DM time fell about 90%.
- **Source.** [If You Hate Selling, Please Watch This | Scale or Fail](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8VqSFBMMS4M&t=98s) (01:38)

### Online tech courses for teens · from ~$700k to ~$150k a month · ROAS fell from 2–3x to ~1x

- **Situation.** Sold to mothers through paid social: $297 front end plus a $189 upsell taken by 30–40%. The owner was considering a high-ticket coaching offer.
- **Diagnosis.** Most likely a creative and pixel problem; the model is fine. The upsell is underpriced.
- **Advice.** In order: more and better creative aimed at the highest-LTV avatar; fire the pixel on the post-upsell purchase; test a ~$1,500 one-time offer (all courses or a light group bootcamp). Only if those fail, add a sales team for a $5–10k back end.
- **Why.** Even a 10% take on $1,500 adds ~$150 per order, more than the current upsell, without building a heavy back end.
- **Source.** ["Should He Walk Away From His $1.4M Business To Start Over?" | Alex Hormozi Answers Live](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0RjIw9M1j28&t=3581s) (59:41)

### Online craft-skills membership · ~$800k/yr, 30–35% profit · owner self-limits ad spend

- **Situation.** $27/month or ~$217/year membership sold through a $10 five-day bootcamp three times a year; ~$37 cost per ticket, 22% of buyers join, LTV ~$300. Only four ads running.
- **Diagnosis.** Pure demand constraint: too little creative, hooks aimed at already-aware buyers, low front-end cash.
- **Advice.** Follow the launch spend cadence; produce 80+ ads (re-shoots, member content unlocked by a secret bonus, clipped lives, AI statics); add pain-aware hooks and call-outs; accept up to ~$100 cost per buyer; sell annual-only during launch with a physical starter kit, monthly after cart close; then test the bootcamp as a $10 seven-day trial with tool upsells.
- **Why.** If clicks cost less than they earn, it scales; ~55% trial conversion beats 22%.
- **Source.** [Building a $3,000,000 Business for a Stranger in 31 Minutes | Scale or Fail](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j2TZMFkj71Q&t=335s) (05:35)

### Executive career coaching · ~$7.2M/yr, ~$2M profit · wants to add enterprise B2B

- **Situation.** B2C on paid social at $3–5k a day.
- **Diagnosis.** The new avatar is an escape from a sales-efficiency problem that would follow him into B2B anyway.
- **Advice.** Stay B2C and aim to scale spend 10–20x. Fix ROAS levers one at a time. If he needs something new, add a channel and keep the avatar.
- **Why.** The B2C market is vastly larger, and doing more of what works carries the least risk.
- **Source.** [Answering Your Top Business Questions for 1 Hour](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QwEb78e5a8Y&t=434s) (07:14)

### Women's weight-loss program · ~$4M/yr toward a $12M goal · nervous about a big lead-magnet launch

- **Situation.** Webinar funnel plus sales team; show rate about 30%.
- **Diagnosis.** The weakest step is the opt-in at ~5%, where 20%+ is possible; show rate is already above benchmark.
- **Advice.** Test many names for the lead magnet, break it into content, and focus all testing on the opt-in page and session availability. The booking rate could double too.
- **Why.** The step with the biggest gap to benchmark gives the biggest multiple on the whole funnel.
- **Source.** ["He Makes $1.5M a Year... and Keeps 1.3% of It" | Alex Hormozi Answers Live](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lidFtb1Ga0o&t=1576s) (26:16)

### Online weight-loss coaching · $3.2M/yr, wants $10M · churn after ~8 months, founder takes all sales calls

- **Situation.** About 15 sales calls a week by the founder; new ads at ~$135 CAC.
- **Diagnosis.** Churn is structural in weight loss, so the game is distribution and value per customer.
- **Advice.** Pitch renewals at milestones, at the halfway point and as a last chance; offer a buy-six-get-six deal on the back end; consider adjacent sticky products; then spend hard on ads, since CAC is tiny.
- **Why.** Higher LTV and gross profit fund more aggressive acquisition.
- **Source.** ["He Makes $1.5M a Year... and Keeps 1.3% of It" | Alex Hormozi Answers Live](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lidFtb1Ga0o&t=3279s) (54:39)

### Coaching back end with group calls · size not stated · ~30% churn, ~7% attend calls

- **Situation.** Buyers were placed straight into group calls after the sale.
- **Diagnosis.** Low consumption caused by weak onboarding.
- **Advice.** Have the closer book onboarding. Add four to six one-on-one sessions (start with two) before the group. Book the next session at the end of each call. Use automated and manual reminders. Offer a one-on-one start as a priced option.
- **Why.** Comfort and buy-in raise attendance, and attendance drives renewal.
- **Source.** [Answering Your Top Business Questions for 1 Hour](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QwEb78e5a8Y&t=145s) (02:25)

### Alex's gym-owner licensing business · 100+ new gyms a month · poor-fit clients swamping the team

- **Situation.** Non-owners, part-time owners, owners without a signed lease or staff consumed team time and got poor results.
- **Diagnosis.** A fit problem at the front door.
- **Advice.** Require at least 25 clients, a signed lease, a full-time owner and ideally one employee. Return to a program with a defined end. Market average results.
- **Why.** Better-fit clients pay more, earn more, need less work and stay longer. Sales fell from 100+ to 40–50 a month while escalations dropped from 22 to zero in four weeks.
- **Source.** [Pricing Lessons From a Private Equity God](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hnfh2jqNVEQ&t=336s) (05:36)

### Sales coaching and lead generation for financial advisers · ~$6.6M/yr, wants $20M+ · churn is the main problem

- **Situation.** Paid ads to a VSL to a phone team.
- **Diagnosis.** Activated clients stick, so this is an activation and avatar problem.
- **Advice.** Identify the segments that activate (demographics, quantifiables, behaviours) and sell only to them, adjusting price if needed. Run daily group role-play for clients' teams, reverse-engineer activation and deliver an early win.
- **Why.** Bad-fit customers cannot be helped regardless of effort.
- **Source.** [Helping 4 Educational Business Owners Build a $1M Business in 25 Minutes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BYpTRiRqS1Y&t=1106s) (18:26)

### Coaching for affiliates who want brand deals · ~$1.5M/yr · a price jump crushed close rate

- **Situation.** Price moved from $4k/month to $20k for three months plus a back-end share. Close rate fell from ~96% to 20–30%, margin from 80% to ~37%.
- **Diagnosis.** The jump hurt sales velocity and the owner's morale; the avatar is fine and unsaturated.
- **Advice.** Lower the upfront fee (for example to $15k or $10k) and raise the back-end percentage. Keep the avatar and make small tweaks.
- **Why.** Two levers allow experiments and restore velocity, while changing avatar is a huge move.
- **Source.** ["He Makes $1.5M a Year... and Keeps 1.3% of It" | Alex Hormozi Answers Live](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lidFtb1Ga0o&t=1239s) (20:39)

### Alex's high-ticket program · ~$42k/year · team pushed to cut price to reduce churn

- **Situation.** Price was about $800 a week.
- **Diagnosis.** In hindsight, price was never driving churn.
- **Advice.** What happened: price was cut to ~$600 a week and services costing ~$100k a month were added. The lesson he draws is to leave price alone when churn has another cause.
- **Why.** Churn stayed flat, topline fell ~25% while costs rose, and about $5M a year of profit was lost.
- **Source.** [NEVER lower your prices...](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BZQtuK-ucDM&t=92s) (01:32)

### Coaching business with paid coaches · 40 clients at $400/month · 75% gross margin

- **Situation.** The coach was paid $4k a month to serve 40 clients.
- **Diagnosis.** Gross margin of 75% sits below his 80% rule of thumb for this kind of business.
- **Advice.** Raise price by about $20 per client per month, or cut coach pay to about $3,200.
- **Why.** A few points of gross margin produce a large relative gain in net profit.
- **Source.** [If You Don't Understand Margin, You Don't Understand Business](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9xAMe0QBFhU&t=632s) (10:32)

### Men's self-development brand · ~$500k/yr · strong acquisition, no renewals, losing money on retreats

- **Situation.** $2k/year community plus four retreats; webinars close 41%.
- **Diagnosis.** Nothing is sold at the events, retreats are not full, and renewals are offered too late.
- **Advice.** Package the most valued items (in-person plus online) at ~$5–6k and let members pick two or three of the four events. Fill the retreats, and sell renewals and premium upgrades at the second event.
- **Why.** Events become profitable only when you sell at them; people buy while they are in the moment.
- **Source.** ["He Makes $1.5M a Year... and Keeps 1.3% of It" | Alex Hormozi Answers Live](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lidFtb1Ga0o&t=1003s) (16:43)

### Real-estate education with large events · ~$1.3M/yr · nothing to buy after the mentorship

- **Situation.** Two 600-person events a year; 20% buy a $12k one-year mentorship. The owner wanted more income and had no plan to sell.
- **Diagnosis.** Customers have nothing else to buy.
- **Advice.** Double the events by adding another region. Optionally downsell graduates into a ~$3–5k annual network and access membership.
- **Why.** Running the same playbook more often has almost no operational risk; a recurring back end would let him break even on the front and outspend competitors.
- **Source.** [$100M Investor Answers The Most Important Business Questions for 1 Hour](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=--9kOXNCUdE&t=676s) (11:16)

### Fitness coaching sold on professional-network ads · LTV ~$3k · ads stop working above ~$1,500/day

- **Situation.** $2k/$3k/$5k packages for three, six and twelve months.
- **Diagnosis.** Selling time where the goal should be sold; LTV too low to absorb diminishing returns at scale.
- **Advice.** Switch to a diagnostic sale priced weekly over the weeks needed to reach the goal, with a conditional "until you get there" guarantee. Add affiliate supplements sold at a nutrition consult about 48 hours after start. Then make more creative.
- **Why.** Selling the goal converts better and extends LTV; supplements can add about 50% revenue with no delivery work.
- **Source.** [10 Millionaires Asked Me How to Get Richer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6m6DCQMASEM&t=95s) (01:35)

### Executive-coaching style offer · ~$10k/month ad spend at ~3:1 · wants to switch platforms over low-salary leads

- **Situation.** Paid social brings leads with lower salaries than desired.
- **Diagnosis.** The issue is LTV; the platform is fine.
- **Advice.** First add the planned performance-based back end to lift value per customer from about $3k to $10–12k. Keep the current platform running, then test others.
- **Why.** Starting from roughly 12:1, diminishing returns at higher spend still leave a very profitable channel.
- **Source.** [10 Millionaires Asked Me How to Get Richer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6m6DCQMASEM&t=386s) (06:26)

### Weight-loss coaching for mothers · ~$288k/yr, needs ~$600k · tempted to change channel

- **Situation.** Tried local organic and local ads (CAC over $1k); now national ads to Instagram DMs at ~$750 CAC and ~4x ROAS.
- **Diagnosis.** He has a working channel; the risk is switching away from it.
- **Advice.** Alex first suggested a local in-person hybrid, then corrected himself: double spend on the 4x funnel and add DM setters. Do not run both.
- **Why.** When something works, put more into it. Local is only a fallback for speed to cash, and a winner stays.
- **Source.** ["He Makes $1.5M a Year... and Keeps 1.3% of It" | Alex Hormozi Answers Live](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lidFtb1Ga0o&t=4393s) (1:13:13)

### Travel-rewards education · ~$6.4M/yr, ~30% margin · LTV:CAC 1.4:1, more spend adds little

- **Situation.** ROAS 4.5:1, ~6 months to break even per customer, 85% of buyers through a book funnel; raising spend 50% added 10% sales; prospects find it too good to be true.
- **Diagnosis.** Demand-constrained by a creative ceiling; the outbound team is under-resourced and not prioritising leads.
- **Advice.** Ads: a member trip-video loop, variations on winners, a young short-form operator, diverse avatars, AI avatar videos. Conversion: score leads on travel and card spend, grow to ~6 callers on a parallel dialer, admit limitations early on calls, ask for a phone number in exchange for a free assessment.
- **Why.** Better creative lowers CAC; better conversion of ~300 daily prospects lifts LTV:CAC.
- **Source.** [Building a $12,000,000 Business for a Stranger in 25 Minutes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MH-IMJxbUY4&t=715s) (11:55)

### Coaching for real-estate agents · ~$2.5M/yr · five-day challenge straight to checkout, wants a brand manager

- **Situation.** Delivery could handle four times the volume.
- **Diagnosis.** A pure advertising and demand play.
- **Advice.** Poach an experienced in-house brand manager. Add paid ads together with a book-a-call sales motion, because cold traffic will not convert straight to checkout.
- **Why.** Brand is a core function, and warm and cold audiences need different selling.
- **Source.** [Helping 4 Educational Business Owners Build a $1M Business in 25 Minutes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BYpTRiRqS1Y&t=821s) (13:41)

### Done-with-you YouTube growth program · ~€700k/yr pace at €10k per three months · volatile months

- **Situation.** Nearly all sales from cold email; their own channel produces little.
- **Diagnosis.** Volatility comes from organic. The bigger warning sign is not succeeding with the very channel they sell.
- **Advice.** Scale cold email to about $1M, rework content to target the buying avatar (and get good at it), then add ads.
- **Why.** Education is transactional, so growth equals acquisition; views from the wrong avatar do not matter.
- **Source.** ["He Makes $1.5M a Year... and Keeps 1.3% of It" | Alex Hormozi Answers Live](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lidFtb1Ga0o&t=2743s) (45:43)

### Front-end sales for new real-estate wholesalers · CAC ~$5k, LTV ~$35k · prospects cannot afford the first payment

- **Situation.** Close rate very low; an unqualified lead flow drains the sales team.
- **Diagnosis.** Missing qualification tied to a defined avatar.
- **Advice.** Derive the avatar from the highest-spending, longest-staying customers (demographics, income and other quantifiables, geography, behaviour in the sales process). Put that friction into ads, landing pages, form drop-downs and the VSL. Send the best leads to sales and the rest to an automated self-liquidating offer.
- **Why.** Qualification raises cost per call but lowers CAC and raises LTV; he cannot recall it ever losing him money. Being willing to invest in yourself is no qualifier.
- **Source.** [How to Build a Business That Runs Without You](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TLpLmhWqKEU&t=3357s) (55:57)

### Content-template membership for real-estate agents · ~$20k/month at $49 · 12% monthly churn, thinking of a new niche

- **Situation.** About 300 members, ~50 sales a month; a similar product in another niche grew 5x.
- **Diagnosis.** Sales velocity is too low for the market, and churn likely clusters in part-time agents. Too early to judge the niche.
- **Advice.** Stay and learn. Find the two or three traits that separate churners from stayers; target agents with a recent sale. Push annual prepay (with ~8-month LTV, four months free is equivalent and pulls cash forward). Add a $197–397 offer on the thank-you page, test ads to learn CAC, and consider a $500–1,000/month AI-assisted managed upsell.
- **Why.** The market leader has a tiny share of the market; blaming the avatar is the easy answer.
- **Source.** [How to Build a Business That Runs Without You](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TLpLmhWqKEU&t=5187s) (1:26:27)

### Motocross training company · a few million a year · doubled one-day tour dates, fears a copycat

- **Situation.** One-day tours grew from 70 to 140 dates with heavy operational drag; each five-day camp netted $100k+.
- **Diagnosis.** The bottom ~40 tour dates were negative or negligible, and the business was underpriced.
- **Advice.** Shift to five-day camps, raise prices, and let the competitor copy the low-value format.
- **Why.** He tied it to his own costliest mistake: matching an undercutting competitor by cutting price for an existing recurring base, which cost a large share of monthly revenue, several million in annual profit and tens of millions in exit value, with no change in churn.
- **Source.** [Helping 4 Educational Business Owners Build a $1M Business in 25 Minutes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BYpTRiRqS1Y&t=435s) (07:15)

### Local house-flipping ecosystem with a coaching program · ~$4M/yr · wants national scale for impact

- **Situation.** Contracting, trades companies and local coaching; the owner is stuck in operations with a new COO.
- **Diagnosis.** The local market is not yet dominated, so national ambition is premature, and prices are far too low.
- **Advice.** Ignore national for now. Use spare capacity to sell many more people locally through paid ads and bigger workshops. Move to standard tiers (around $15–25k done-with-you, ~$100k turnkey). Use content for impact.
- **Why.** The local market could support a very large business; expanding too early collapses, and content scales impact for free.
- **Source.** [Helping 4 Educational Business Owners Build a $1M Business in 25 Minutes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BYpTRiRqS1Y&t=145s) (02:25)

### Weight-loss info-product creator · ~$4M/yr · planning apparel, supplements and creams

- **Situation.** A large following and a plateaued core offer.
- **Diagnosis.** The same "I don't know how to grow the core" problem, disguised as diversification.
- **Advice.** Put all effort into growing the existing offer toward $10M instead of launching new lines.
- **Why.** A new line does not fix the skill gap that caused the plateau.
- **Source.** [Entrepreneurs, stop starting new businesses. Fix the one you've got.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s59jbVBprn0&t=287s) (04:47)

### Solo founder of book-writing retreats and media · ~$1M last year · keeps starting new things

- **Situation.** Runs luxury retreats, a publishing house, a TV show and a business school, and gets bored easily.
- **Diagnosis.** Energy split across too many businesses; demand and margin clearly sit in the book business.
- **Advice.** Pick the book business and stop pushing the school. Use the TV show as media to sell book-writing and make more seasons. Put creative energy into marketing and keep a private list for new ideas.
- **Why.** Concentrated effort compounds, the market is already voting with money, and every change has a guaranteed cost.
- **Source.** [Why Doing More Is Actually Making You Less Money](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hfwZwPGsbIo&t=242s) (04:02)

### Alex's gym-owner licensing business · ~$1M a week · founder-led brand on one ad channel

- **Situation.** 100% of sales came from paid social and the founder appeared in every ad; the founders wanted an exit. Ads underperformed during the pandemic.
- **Diagnosis.** A guru brand more than a business: founder dependency and single-channel risk would cap the price.
- **Advice.** Hire someone from an outbound-led competitor to build an outbound department from scratch and stick with it through slow early months (keep going as long as each funnel stage improves). Take the founder's face off the door with specialists, ex-client leads and new faces in ads.
- **Why.** Within about 12 months outbound was roughly half of revenue, which reassured the acquirer.
- **Source.** [How I Scaled My Sales Team \[Compensation, Recruiting & Training\]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=okA9Yt2KZuk&t=139s) (02:19); [I Reverse Engineered the Perfect Business](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HKbFUWJwEG0&t=1297s) (21:37)

### Education business teaching people to run event venues · size not stated · founder wants to sell it

- **Situation.** The founder sold his own three venues and now teaches others to do the same.
- **Diagnosis.** He is trying to sell the goose (founder-led education); the sellable assets are the venues his students run.
- **Advice.** Standardise students' systems, branding and prices, and offer the top 10% a path to be rolled up in groups of 20–50 and sold periodically.
- **Why.** Venues are faceless, turnkey and proven sellable. He does the roll-up work while keeping the education business.

### Education business teaching marketplace sellers · size not stated · founder wants to sell it

- **Situation.** A former online-store owner now teaches others to build stores.
- **Diagnosis.** The education business is not recurring, carries key-person risk and volatile acquisition: few buyers, low price.
- **Advice.** Bundle the students' stores into a sellable asset.
- **Why.** Scaling something already sellable is much easier than fixing something inherently unsellable.

- **Diagnosis.** Stars generate impressions and cannot be sold.
- **Advice.** Route impressions into owned products (an incubator of products) or sell ad inventory; raise a small stake to fund launches.

### Enterprise building-efficiency software · same revenue for ~8 years · plans to double the sales team

- **Situation.** About 10 demos a week, about two closes a year, ~98% revenue retention.
- **Diagnosis.** Leads are fine. The pitch goes to owners while building managers hold the real decision power and have no incentive to take on more work.
- **Advice.** Do not hire more reps. Rescript the demo to raise the manager's status and make it about them.
- **Why.** Turning hundreds of demos into many more closes is a far bigger lever than doubling reps.

### Early-stage SaaS that reduces sales-call no-shows · pre-scale · founder from high-ticket education

- **Situation.** The founder knows marketing and sales from education.
- **Diagnosis.** A different game: a product and retention business.
- **Advice.** Use early marketing to learn, find the cohort that stays, retarget ideal customers, and validate the co-founder's track record.
- **Why.** If users do not stick, nothing else matters.

### Creator tools, agency and label · ~$4.3M/yr · 40,000 creators, ~1,000 earning, considering SaaS

- **Situation.** Several business lines serving creators plus brand-side revenue.
- **Diagnosis.** Too many businesses at once; marketplaces usually fail unless they first work as SaaS.
- **Advice.** Either charge a subscription with a free tier, or go high-ticket with white-glove onboarding for selected creators. Keep the brand-side revenue, then go all in on one path.

### Website-as-a-service for small businesses · ~$20M/yr at ~$450/month · fears AI will kill the product

- **Situation.** All growth from outbound cold calling, ~29-month average stay, ~$3.6M EBITDA, people-heavy delivery.
- **Diagnosis.** The fear does not show in the metrics. The real issues are single-channel risk and low margin.
- **Advice.** Do not rebuild the product. Add paid inbound. Spend ~6 months rebuilding workflows with AI and cut headcount ~50% to roughly double margin, then accept a negative first quarter on CAC, knowing the 29-month back end.
- **Why.** Margin funds acquisition without touching price; customers who would do it themselves with AI never bought anyway.
- **Source.** [Watch This If You Have a Service Business](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqo0lVveh98&t=907s) (15:07)

### Software selling an ad product to newspapers · size not stated · stagnant for years

- **Situation.** A friend's company with a good revenue-share offer and a capable sales team.
- **Diagnosis.** The market was shrinking by about a quarter each year.
- **Advice.** The lesson he draws: pick a growing market.
- **Why.** A shrinking market compounds against you no matter how good the offer and team are.
- **Source.** [GROWTH LEVERS: Big Business Owner Explains Real World Tactics That Work](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F3NyhOtRpOE&t=1348s) (22:28)

## Patterns {#patterns}

- **Keep the working channel and feed it (about 10 cases).** Owners want a new platform, avatar or B2B line; he says more creative, more spend, more volume on what already returns, and adds a channel before he adds an avatar.
- **The new thing is an escape (about 6 cases).** B2B, apparel and supplements, a business school, a new niche, SaaS: the plateau comes from a skill gap in the core, and a new line will not close it.
- **Raise value per customer before scaling ads (about 9 cases).** Upsells, ~$1,500 one-time offers, performance back ends, diagnostic sales of the goal, renewal pitches, annual prepay and memberships for graduates.
- **Churn is fixed at the start (about 7 cases).** Qualify for the avatar that activates, onboard one-on-one, book the next session, deliver an early win; he would rather lose sales than keep poor-fit buyers.
- **Never cut price to fix churn (about 3 cases).** His own price cuts lost millions with churn unchanged; he raises or restructures price instead, splitting upfront and back-end when velocity suffers.
- **Sell at events and milestones (about 4 cases).** Events and challenges pay off only when renewals and upgrades are pitched in the room.
- **Over-delivery caps capacity (about 3 cases).** Chat access, long unstructured calls and unused group sessions; he cuts them and adds structure and self-serve checkout.
- **Fix the weakest funnel step (about 3 cases).** Opt-in rate, pixel placement, cold traffic sent straight to checkout.
- **Education is hard to sell (about 4 cases).** Founder dependency and single channels cap exit value; he points to the sellable assets students or audiences create.
- **Software lives on retention and fit (about 5 cases).** Find the cohort that stays, sell to the real decision maker, protect margin, and choose a growing market.

## How Alex would apply it {#alex}

**First questions.**
1. What does a customer pay you over their lifetime, what does one cost to acquire, and how long until you are paid back?
2. Which funnel step sits furthest below benchmark?
3. How many ad creatives are live, and when did you last add new ones?
4. Which customers activate and stay, and what do they have in common?
5. What do customers buy after the first offer, and when do you pitch it?
6. How many hours does the founder spend on delivery and DMs, and which of those do customers actually value?
7. What new avatar, product or platform are you considering, and is the current one exhausted?

**Default moves by situation.**
- **Ads stopped scaling:** check creative volume and pixel setup first, then raise LTV with an upsell or back end; leave platform and model alone until those fail.
- **LTV:CAC thin:** add a performance or renewal component, sell the goal over its timeframe, push annual prepay; only then spend more.
- **High churn:** segment churners from stayers, qualify the front door to the stayers' profile, add one-on-one onboarding and an early win, and pitch renewals at milestones and events.
- **Price questions:** treat very high close rates as a sign of underpricing; when a price jump stalls sales, shift part of the price to the back end; never cut price to fix churn.
- **Founder at capacity:** strip over-delivery, structure the sale (scripted webinar, self-serve checkout), clip content for reach, then hire.
- **Itch for something new:** add a channel inside the current business; refuse a new avatar or product until the core is clearly exhausted.
- **Wanting an exit:** remove founder dependency and single-channel risk, or build around the sellable assets your students or audience create.
- **Software:** prove retention in one cohort first, sell to whoever really decides, and protect margin before spending on acquisition.
