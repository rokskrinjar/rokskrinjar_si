## The gist {#gist}

Local owners almost always arrive saying they need more leads. When Alex looks at the numbers, the leads are rarely the first problem. Close rates of 70 to 90 percent, show rates near 100 percent and ads returning 10:1 or better tell him the business is underpriced and spending far too little on a channel that already works. His first moves are usually a price increase of 10 to 40 percent, a dedicated landing page and more ad spend on the channel that is already profitable. Small changes per ticket (menu order, an attached drink, a rebooking step) go straight to profit in a high-volume business.

The second pattern is capacity. Many local businesses are capped by one doctor, one founder in the van or a shortage of technicians. Alex treats recruiting as a core business function with its own funnel and budget, and he works out what one practitioner or rep is worth in gross profit to show the owner how cheap a large hiring bonus really is. He moves the expert to the hardest work and gives the simpler half to associates.

The third pattern is cash timing. Quarterly billing, pay-per-visit models and fronted equipment starve growing local businesses. He restructures the offer so more cash comes in on day one: a front-end assessment with the card on file, a prepaid year, an enrolment fee, a first quarter paid up front. Underneath all of it sits focus. He cuts extra avatars, side ventures and partner deals so the owner can push one model in one market before expanding.

## Cases {#cases}

### Dental practice, multi-location · ~$2M/yr · one expert does every high-ticket case
- **Situation.** About 47% profit. The founder-doctor handles every high-ticket case (cap of ~25–30 patients a month), bills per visit and closes 80–90%; patients wait two to four weeks.
- **Diagnosis.** Supply constraint created by a single expert doing every step. The very high close rate signals underpricing.
- **Advice.** Associates take the simpler half of visits; the founder becomes architect and teacher. Payment up front, card on file at check-in. Raise price about 20%. A VSL before the treatment presentation and faster booking.
- **Why.** Halving the expert's load doubles capacity. In the follow-up the average ticket rose from about $28K to $44K and booking dropped to within a week, with no new locations.
- **Source.** [This Video Should Be Required Viewing for Business Owners](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uaLNfijnp-8&t=329s) (05:29)

### Residential trash collection · ~$640K/yr, losing ~$150K · growth starved of cash
- **Situation.** Under two years old, 2,500 customers, two avatars (homes and HOAs), five channels. Bins and commissions are paid out before quarterly billing brings cash in.
- **Diagnosis.** Too many avatars and channels, and an offer that collects too little cash up front to fund growth.
- **Advice.** Pick single homes as the avatar. Go all in on door-to-door with commission-only reps, trained by shadowing and role play. Restructure the offer: bin plus first quarter up front, free months spread later, and an anchor of a prepaid year with the bin free.
- **Why.** Each sale needs about $208 cash to cover labour, bin and commission. If about one in five takes the prepaid year, average cash per sale clears that bar and growth funds itself.
- **Source.** [Building a $1,000,000 Business for a Stranger in 69 Minutes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Wr6n_zNKvMk&t=0s) (00:00)

### Chiropractic chain, 6 locations · ~$5.2M/yr · inconsistent lead flow
- **Situation.** About 23% profit, CAC about $700 against LTV about $3,400, 80% show rate, 71% close, yet only $1.5–2K of ads per location per month. Insurance checks push the sale to day two, and each site sells differently.
- **Diagnosis.** Underspending on very profitable search ads, a weak social front-end offer, and selling after treatment instead of at peak pain.
- **Advice.** In order: raise search spend sharply until returns fall to about 5:1; a $29 front-end offer with card on file; collect insurance card on the booking call and print plans each morning; sell the same day after assessment; pilot a checklist sales process at one site; add local SEO.
- **Why.** Doubling revenue per location hits the profit goal without opening new sites.
- **Source.** [Building a $5,000,000 Business for a Stranger in 42 Mins](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sBJppqCeFGI&t=2251s) (37:31)

### HVAC and duct cleaning · ~$1.25M/yr · wants more and better leads
- **Situation.** About 38% profit, $7.2K a month on marketing, 99% show rate, 82% close, $60K of debt, a new van and new staff.
- **Diagnosis.** Demand-constrained with nothing badly broken: underpriced, weak landing page, under-spent ads, no reactivation, one productive referral partner.
- **Advice.** In order: raise price 10% and hold it while close stays above about 65%; keep paying down debt; a dedicated ad landing page; more creative variety, then more spend; retargeting and branded search; monthly reactivation campaigns; an affiliate offer where partners sell the dryer-vent service and keep the money while he upsells the core job; outbound to HOA events last.
- **Why.** A 10% price rise adds roughly 25% to net profit, ads already return about 13:1, and the order protects capacity.
- **Source.** [Building a $2,500,000 Business for a Stranger in 36 Minutes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OQf2Ba-Lp_4&t=234s) (03:54)

### Roofing and gutter contractor · ~$20K/month, two months old · thinks salespeople are the constraint
- **Situation.** 59% margin, sales from door-knocking (about one per 20 doors) and referrals. Replacements bring about $7K gross profit, repairs about $1.5K at 64%.
- **Diagnosis.** Repairs are an overlooked, uncontested, fast-cycle product with no cash drag. The owner's door-to-door skill is the engine; average ticket and recurring revenue are the missed levers.
- **Advice.** Systematise repairs and lift the ticket toward $2.5–5K with a menu upsell at follow-up; a VSL for each step; scale doors with commission framed per door; a $180–240/year maintenance plan credited toward repairs; a paid priority fee of about 20%.
- **Why.** Three repairs a day can match a replacement in gross profit. Ninety days later monthly jobs rose from about 15–20 to 60–85.
- **Source.** [How I Would Build a $10M Service Business (If I Had to Start Over)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o64cI6tebnU&t=194s) (03:14)

### Thai restaurant, one location · ~$3.5M/yr · opening a second, larger site
- **Situation.** 72 seats, 19% net, weekend queues, alcohol only about 1% of sales.
- **Diagnosis.** Great product, weak monetisation (pricing, menu layout, drinks, return of first-timers, review asks) and no staff training system.
- **Advice.** Add .99 to entrées; reorder the menu by profit with highlight boxes; drink pairings and a weekly drink special on a table tent; a first-timer napkin with free dessert and a manager card; a drink in exchange for a review at the check; a short daily staff skit for training; an influencer night to open the new site.
- **Why.** In high-volume, thin-margin businesses tiny per-ticket gains drop straight to profit and need almost no operational change.
- **Source.** [Building a $3,500,000 Business for Two Strangers in 52 Minutes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h9UyQiLjSHg&t=922s) (15:22)

### Lash salon chain, 7 locations · ~$2.3M/yr · leads, staff turnover, partners not pulling weight
- **Situation.** About 28% net after ten years. Managers are trained inconsistently, three partners are misaligned, the owner is unpaid and staff leave.
- **Diagnosis.** The partnership drains all bandwidth; upsell and rebooking are untrained; marketing is badly underfunded.
- **Advice.** Fix partners first: an equity swap for the misaligned one, a buyout for the one leaving in exchange for helping replace her. Then 10–20% weekend pricing, rebooking before the service, membership as the default, prepay at checkout, a two-year career path with skill unlocks and a profit share for top staff, an attribution checklist at check-in, a dedicated lead caller, and higher ad spend once tracking works.
- **Why.** Nothing executes while the partnership drain continues; after that, quick high-impact items come first.
- **Source.** [Building a $2,300,000/yr Business for a Stranger in 57 Mins](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-Koq14DPXC4&t=961s) (16:01)

### Physical therapy and pain clinic, 3 locations · ~$4.2M/yr, ~zero profit · reimbursements falling
- **Situation.** Out-of-network, too many services and avatars, no ad-driven acquisition, can't afford senior talent. Wants $20M on a cash-pay model.
- **Diagnosis.** Margin squeeze plus no acquisition system; choosing cash pay means choosing to get good at marketing and selling.
- **Advice.** Drop the lowest-paying insurers to restore cash flow. Build a cash front end: local Meta ads in a 10-mile radius, a cheap discounted assessment that puts a card on file, call leads within 60 seconds, sell after the assessment. Settle on one to three front-end offers and cross-sell the rest. Upsell existing patients first.
- **Why.** Cash businesses win on an efficient front door plus ascension to the highest-LTV service.
- **Source.** [$100M Investor Answers The Most Important Business Questions for 1 Hour](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=--9kOXNCUdE&t=97s) (01:37)

### Indoor family entertainment centre · ~$4.8M/yr, losing money · weeks from zero cash
- **Situation.** About 150 staff, $400K a month in revenue against $550K needed to break even, $180K a month of debt service, peak days at half capacity.
- **Diagnosis.** Cash is the immediate threat; the business needs about $600K extra before the slow season, and it tracks outputs instead of inputs.
- **Advice.** Bet on summer camp: hit the email list, run a 7–10 day giveaway (a whole summer as first prize, a big discount for everyone else), a 72-hour claim window, phone follow-up of every lead, then evergreen multi-week bundles. Later, more callers and the same giveaway for birthdays.
- **Why.** Summer is when parents spend big and book now. Reported result: about 3,700 leads and summer camp revenue up 325%, limited by too few callers.
- **Source.** [Can I Save This Failing Theme Park in 90 Days?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xRxVpjeHmeA&t=284s) (04:44)

### Moving company · ~$300K/yr · loses ~40% of quotes on price
- **Situation.** 62% profit, hourly pricing near the top of the market. Leads from maps/SEO and realtors; realtor jobs average about $3K versus about $1K from search. Wants more locations.
- **Diagnosis.** A commodity offer and a weak sales process; leads are sufficient.
- **Advice.** A VIP tier (senior movers, materials, packing, insurance, price cap) beside standard; a same-day surcharge starting at 10%; name the differentiators on every call; free VIP upgrades for realtors who refer; personalised SMS and DM outreach to realtors; meet brokerage heads in person; a review bonus for foremen; before/after content.
- **Why.** One brokerage can bring dozens of moves a year, and differentiation wins the jobs currently lost on price.
- **Source.** [Give Me 42 Minutes, I'll Show You How To Sell Like The Top 1% | Scale or Fail Episode 1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zzleYxkf39k&t=2001s) (33:21)

### Concierge longevity medical practice · ~$3M/yr · owner believes marketing is the bottleneck
- **Situation.** About $600K profit plus owner pay, ~475 patients, 16 staff. Growth from word of mouth and some SEO; one past event went badly.
- **Diagnosis.** The demand side is under-used; the sales process works for warm traffic; referrals happen but aren't systematised.
- **Advice.** Replace the small cash referral credit with a high-value treatment members can give a friend, and ask who they're bringing at each visit. A quarterly giveaway in the newsletter. Weekly events at local high-net-worth gatherings with a discounted scan and a same-day close. Leave the sales process alone.
- **Why.** Cash discounts mean nothing to wealthy clients, a guest treatment costs almost nothing, and high-trust sales close best in person.
- **Source.** [$100M Worth of Marketing Knowledge in 32 Minutes | Scale or Fail](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B-ogfFiQpXg&t=238s) (03:58)

### Online psychiatry practice, multi-state · ~$1.5M/yr · keeps 1.3% of it
- **Situation.** Seven providers, 89% insurance, practitioners paid half of collections, 85% of demand from one channel. Wants $5M.
- **Diagnosis.** One model problem instead of three constraints: insurers set the price, so margin can only come from lower cost or from cash pay.
- **Advice.** Ask a sorting question at intake (best possible outcome versus what insurance covers) and sell one simple one-time plan of roughly $300–600 on top of the insured visit. Push costs down too; a 1% margin is not a law of nature.
- **Why.** At 1.3% margin, every 10% of add-on revenue multiplies profit many times over, with little extra operational drag.
- **Source.** ["He Makes $1.5M a Year... and Keeps 1.3% of It" | Alex Hormozi Answers Live](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lidFtb1Ga0o&t=141s) (02:21)

### Short-let cleaning company · ~$30–40K/month · treated as a marketing business
- **Situation.** A former gym owner cleaning holiday rentals. LTV is high and CAC is about $6.
- **Diagnosis.** Customers are easy to get; the constraint is finding and keeping reliable cleaners. It is a recruiting and training business.
- **Advice.** Mirror the customer funnel for talent: generate applicants, interview, onboard, train and give people a path to advance.
- **Why.** Once the talent funnel worked, the business grew past $150K a month within about a year.
- **Source.** [Your Business Is NOT What You Think It Is](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rj7rzOdj84A&t=334s) (05:34)

### Family fence company · ~$20M/yr · hesitant to double the sales team
- **Situation.** Goal $50M, enough leads, five salespeople.
- **Diagnosis.** Sales capacity is the constraint, and the fear isn't backed by any cash-flow problem.
- **Advice.** Double the sales team and build a sales recruiting and training academy that runs parallel to the customer funnel.
- **Why.** Each rep brings about $1M in gross profit for about $200K in cost, roughly 5:1, and adds enterprise value.
- **Source.** [Watch This If You Have a Service Business](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqo0lVveh98&t=2443s) (40:43)

### Physical therapy for adult athletes, 2 locations · ~$3M/yr · more demand than practitioners
- **Situation.** Considering concierge pricing or an e-commerce side line.
- **Diagnosis.** Supply-constrained, and the owner hasn't worked out what a practitioner is worth.
- **Advice.** Treat recruiting as the business: a career path, outreach, large referral bonuses (around $20K instead of $500) and headhunters.
- **Why.** Each practitioner brings about $350K of gross profit a year, so paying $50–100K to land one is an easy return.
- **Source.** [Answering Your Top Business Questions for 1 Hour](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QwEb78e5a8Y&t=1533s) (25:33)

### High-end field service · ~$1.5M/yr · owner in everything, recruiting stopped
- **Situation.** About $700K net; each technician produces $180–300K a year.
- **Diagnosis.** The owner is the bottleneck by choice, and nobody is recruiting.
- **Advice.** Do a time study and delegate the bottom half of the owner's tasks. Spend about $50K per technician on national recruiting ads with a relocation bonus, and keep them as employees.
- **Why.** Getting half the owner's time back could double the business, and a technician is worth far more than the cost of recruiting one.
- **Source.** [Helping 6 Business Owners Scale in 33 Minutes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0EqJD2o-Mnk&t=755s) (12:35)

### Electrical contractor · ~$1.6M/yr · owner back in the van when crew are out
- **Situation.** About $650K kept, wants $5M. Work comes from six or seven HVAC companies.
- **Diagnosis.** Capacity constraint plus dependence on a handful of referral partners.
- **Advice.** Raise price 20–40%, backed by an on-time, on-budget guarantee. Use the extra cash to hire redundancy in the crew. Reach out to more HVAC firms for quotes.
- **Why.** Price funds the staff that removes the owner from the van, and more partners give negotiating leverage.
- **Source.** [Helping 6 Business Owners Scale in 33 Minutes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0EqJD2o-Mnk&t=421s) (07:01)

### Roofing, door-to-door · ~$3M/yr · bought leads and CAC overtook revenue
- **Situation.** Historically 100% door-knocking. After buying leads, reps stopped knocking; CAC reached about $7,500 against roughly $6,500 revenue per customer. Wants $10M.
- **Diagnosis.** Inbound and outbound mixed in one team.
- **Advice.** Send everyone back to the doors. One top closer works inbound leads while the company iterates on source, offer and process. Hire a strong in-house marketer.
- **Why.** Fewer new variables means faster learning, and inbound leads become a reward for the best sellers.
- **Source.** [Helping 6 Business Owners Scale in 33 Minutes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0EqJD2o-Mnk&t=1083s) (18:03)

### Nail salon in a mall · ~€600K/yr · half the seats empty in winter
- **Situation.** Average ticket €65, five of ten seats filled. One giveaway was run earlier without any back end to follow it.
- **Diagnosis.** A demand-generation problem rather than seasonality. The long-term constraint is keeping technicians.
- **Advice.** Rerun the giveaway now that the back end is fixed; alternate front-end offers and judge them by LTV:CAC. Build a career path for techs with skill levels and profit-share management roles.
- **Why.** Technicians leave once their book is full unless staying is the easiest path.
- **Source.** ["Should He Walk Away From His $1.4M Business To Start Over?" | Alex Hormozi Answers Live](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0RjIw9M1j28&t=6403s) (106:43)

### Chiropractic practice · ~$2.4M/yr, flat for five years · no attribution
- **Situation.** About 30% margin. A new doctor shifted it from supply- to demand-constrained. Half the patients come by referral, about 20% from Meta ads, with no tracking.
- **Diagnosis.** No input-output equation, demand-constrained and short of cash.
- **Advice.** Fix pricing and packaging first to free cash; install attribution; scale ads, which work well for a practice with local reputation; produce more thought-leadership content to widen the radius people will travel.
- **Why.** Cash flow is the first need; a trusted local brand makes ads convert and supports premium price.
- **Source.** [Watch This If You Have a Service Business](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqo0lVveh98&t=141s) (02:21)

### Teeth-whitening chain · ~$500K per location · franchise or own the units
- **Situation.** About $50K to open a unit, about $250K profit per location per year.
- **Diagnosis.** Very strong owned-unit economics, roughly 5x return on capital each year.
- **Advice.** Run the numbers both ways. At the multiples he assumed, each owned location adds about $2M of value, each franchise about $300K; $100M of value needs about 50 owned units or about 333 franchises. Choose by achievable opening rate and founder type, and hire for the gap (for example a leader who has already scaled a chain).
- **Why.** With that return on capital, owning compounds wealth far faster.
- **Source.** [How to Grow ANY Local Business (my framework)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BHMeYaHEMpc&t=96s) (01:36)

### Assisted living and memory care · private pay · profit arrives too slowly
- **Situation.** Sales are fine, residents stay a year or more, but profit only appears after two to three months.
- **Diagnosis.** A cash-timing problem; demand is fine.
- **Advice.** Three options: an up-front enrolment fee, the first three months paid up front, or a move-in bundle of services residents need anyway (transport, hair and beauty, furniture or bed upgrades) sold up front.
- **Why.** People resist monthly fees far more than one-time fees, and a life transition is a moment when they want everything handled.
- **Source.** [How to Build a Business That Runs Without You](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TLpLmhWqKEU&t=2831s) (47:11)

### Alex's own gym · boot camp at $99/month · a product he didn't believe in
- **Situation.** He thought semi-private training got far better results than the cheap boot camp.
- **Diagnosis.** A low price on the weaker product.
- **Advice.** He moved everyone to semi-private at $299 a month after a two-week free trial, with a hardship note for those who couldn't pay.
- **Why.** A third left and two-thirds stayed at three times the price, so revenue doubled with fewer clients and profit more than doubled.
- **Source.** [NEVER lower your prices...](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BZQtuK-ucDM&t=46s) (00:46)

### Alex's own gym at capacity · changed the product to fit more people
- **Situation.** Packed classes and members who loved them.
- **Diagnosis.** He merged and shortened sessions to squeeze in more people and lost about a third of members.
- **Advice.** When people love the product, replicate it in a new location or raise the price. Leave the product alone.
- **Why.** Product love is the hardest thing to earn; price is the easiest thing to change.
- **Source.** [How to Start a Business From Nothing (Thank Me Later)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=unshZobTt6Q&t=3958s) (65:58)

### In-home IV therapy · ~$1M/yr, ~50% EBITDA · thinking about consumer ads in new cities
- **Situation.** Growth comes from physician referrals.
- **Diagnosis.** Each referring affiliate costs about $500 to acquire but sends only about one $6K patient a year; nobody activates them or closes the loop.
- **Advice.** Assign an affiliate manager, systematise report-back calls to the referring doctor, choreograph the first referral. Then copy the same B2B playbook into new markets instead of switching to consumer ads.
- **Why.** The existing channel is far from squeezed; a couple of hundred activated affiliates referring regularly could reach the goal.
- **Source.** [$100M Investor Answers The Most Important Business Questions for 1 Hour](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=--9kOXNCUdE&t=3187s) (53:07)

### Restaurant operator, 3 sites via partner deals · wants a large fast-casual chain
- **Situation.** Runs three restaurants through profit shares and partial equity; opening a fourth he fully owns.
- **Diagnosis.** Attention split across partner deals before the model is proven.
- **Advice.** Exit the other deals quickly, live in the fourth location, design it for radical simplicity, optimise return on capital and payback per site, nail grand openings and a manager career path. Decide franchise versus owned later.
- **Why.** Payback per location sets how fast the next one can open.
- **Source.** ["He Makes $1.5M a Year... and Keeps 1.3% of It" | Alex Hormozi Answers Live](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lidFtb1Ga0o&t=6074s) (101:14)

### Commercial construction plus elevator service · ~$11M + ~$3M/yr · owner splits time
- **Situation.** Construction at about 18% margin; a new elevator service business did about $3M at 30% in year one. Wants to sell within 5–10 years.
- **Diagnosis.** Elevator service is the far better business (recurring, niche); splitting attention slows it.
- **Advice.** The safe route: spend roughly $800K of profit on leaders to run construction. Alex's own route: sell construction quickly, even at a discount, and go all in on elevators.
- **Why.** A focused quarter of growth in recurring revenue can equal the whole value of the old business. He did the same with his gyms.
- **Source.** [Helping 6 Business Owners Scale in 33 Minutes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0EqJD2o-Mnk&t=1700s) (28:20)

### Permanent holiday lighting · ~$1M/yr, ~51% net · no repeat revenue
- **Situation.** One location, about $5K ticket, CAC about $300.
- **Diagnosis.** A one-time sale with nothing to buy afterwards.
- **Advice.** Add seasonal temporary decoration campaigns and maintenance checks, use every visit to offer upgrades, and track the share of customers who buy again each year.
- **Why.** Reliable repeat purchases make the business valuable even without a subscription.
- **Source.** [7 Millionaires Asked Me How to Get Rich(er)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bgBIO6nZawg&t=342s) (05:42)

### Junk removal for property managers · ~$1M/yr · dabbling in every channel
- **Situation.** Mostly referrals, plus small tests of cold email, LinkedIn, social and networking. Wants $10M.
- **Diagnosis.** Effort spread across channels with no commitment.
- **Advice.** Any channel can work, so pick by skill. The owner is best in person: go from a few networking events a week to several a day, then use the extra cash to learn a second channel.
- **Why.** Doing more of what already works is the lowest-risk move and frees cash.
- **Source.** [Helping 6 Business Owners Scale in 33 Minutes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0EqJD2o-Mnk&t=1415s) (23:35)

### Roofing roll-up, 8 brands · ~$140M/yr · wants an irresistible offer
- **Situation.** Recently acquired by private equity; hard costs are high.
- **Diagnosis.** The offer is not the immediate constraint; centralising sales already gives a big gain and is needed for the exit.
- **Advice.** Centralise virtual sales first over six months or more and cut the bottom third of reps. Later add a profit guarantee (on time and on budget, or the company refunds its profit) instead of a money-back promise.
- **Why.** A business of that value shouldn't stack risky changes; centralisation alone might add about 25% to EBITDA.
- **Source.** [$100M Investor Answers The Most Important Business Questions for 1 Hour](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=--9kOXNCUdE&t=1593s) (26:33)

### Used-car dealership · ~$500–600K profit · wants five initiatives at once
- **Situation.** Branding, social ads, a commercial-van line ($6–12K margin per unit against about $2K per car) and a service shop, all at the same time.
- **Diagnosis.** Too many priorities; underneath, a gap of about three senior leaders.
- **Advice.** Hire a finance or controller type first, partly paid by finding their salary in cost cuts within 90 days. Then operators for service and the used lot. Then the owner pursues the van line with the freed time.
- **Why.** Finance discipline alone could find about $250K a year, and paying for senior talent is the next level the owner has to break into.
- **Source.** [10 Millionaires Asked Me How to Get Richer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6m6DCQMASEM&t=1217s) (20:17)

### Garage-door services · ~$7M/yr, ~$1.5M cash flow · building marketing from scratch
- **Situation.** Most leads come from eight home-warranty companies.
- **Diagnosis.** A working partner channel plus no owned marketing.
- **Advice.** Keep adding warranty partners. Outsource local search ads and SEO to vendors first. Hire a young in-house buyer for local social ads with performance upside. Later, roll up competitors.
- **Why.** Search ads are stable and suit agencies; local social creative fatigues every two to three weeks, so it needs in-house output.
- **Source.** [10 Millionaires Asked Me How to Get Richer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6m6DCQMASEM&t=2710s) (45:10)

### Implant-focused dental practice · priced below market · half referrals, half search ads
- **Situation.** The owner believes they give more value than competitors for a slightly lower price.
- **Diagnosis.** Underpriced.
- **Advice.** Raise prices. Document the existing sales process from call recordings. Long term, buy other practices.
- **Why.** Price increases go straight to profit, and in mispriced businesses close rates often rise after the increase.
- **Source.** [10 Millionaires Asked Me How to Get Richer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6m6DCQMASEM&t=3342s) (55:42)

### Telehealth clinic · ~$550K/month · wants to start a consultancy for physicians
- **Situation.** Grew fast for two years, then stalled. The treatment can't be advertised on the major social platforms.
- **Diagnosis.** The owner doesn't know how to scale acquisition and wants to escape into a new venture.
- **Advice.** Drop the consultancy. Restate the problem as finding a permitted alternative channel to acquire more patients, and focus entirely on that.
- **Why.** The new venture would enter a saturated market with no track record and nothing unique, while the existing engine already works.

### Roofing and exterior remodelling · ~$6M/yr · owner works 2–3 hours a week, wants $100M
- **Situation.** Held back by comfort, protecting family time and side investments in real estate.
- **Diagnosis.** A trade-off problem: he wants both outcomes, and growth requires giving something up.
- **Advice.** Give up short-term profit to hire senior leaders who drive growth. Hold firm on family time. Keep real estate passive.
- **Why.** Changing nothing changes nothing; profit can be earned back, family time cannot, and a career allows only a few big runs.
- **Source.** [Helping 6 Business Owners Scale in 33 Minutes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0EqJD2o-Mnk&t=47s) (00:47); [Watch This If You Have a Service Business](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqo0lVveh98&t=1723s) (28:43)

### Health restaurant, first year · ~$1.5M pace · constant fires and a tired partner
- **Situation.** 100–200 regulars, operational chaos, and a partner who keeps saying they have suffered enough.
- **Diagnosis.** Demand is proven, so this is the normal kind of hard. The real issue is a partner whose attitude undermines the culture.
- **Advice.** Keep going. Have a direct conversation that sets expected behaviour, or buy the partner out on a payment plan and take all the risk and upside. Hire aggressively and state standards openly.
- **Why.** Push when demand exists; staff copy the least hard-working owner.
- **Source.** ["Should He Walk Away From His $1.4M Business To Start Over?" | Alex Hormozi Answers Live](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0RjIw9M1j28&t=5639s) (93:59)

### Local business with 2–3 leads a day · slow follow-up
- **Situation.** A small daily flow of inbound leads, called back whenever someone had time (from Alex's gym years).
- **Diagnosis.** Lead response speed was the leak.
- **Advice.** Pay one full-time person whose only job is to call every lead the moment it arrives.
- **Why.** He closed about 55% of leads this way, against the roughly 10% he saw as typical.
- **Source.** [How to Start a Business From Nothing (Thank Me Later)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=unshZobTt6Q&t=4103s) (68:23)

### Alex's gym versus a bigger competitor · optimising the ad ratio
- **Situation.** He ran ads at about 10:1 while a competitor spent ten times as much at about 4:1.
- **Diagnosis.** He optimised the return ratio instead of total profit.
- **Advice.** Keep scaling spend for as long as absolute profit keeps growing.
- **Why.** The competitor netted about $45K a month against his $13.5K.
- **Source.** [How to Start a Business From Nothing (Thank Me Later)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=unshZobTt6Q&t=3860s) (64:20)

### Brick-and-mortar chain, two founders · plateaued
- **Situation.** A portfolio company whose growth had stalled under the founders.
- **Diagnosis.** It needed a seasoned operator.
- **Advice.** Bring in someone who had already taken a chain from about 100 to 2,000 locations.
- **Why.** Growth continued and profit expanded much faster; the operator even paused openings for a quarter to fix per-location profit, which also lowered risk for a buyer.
- **Source.** [I Reverse Engineered the Perfect Business](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HKbFUWJwEG0&t=288s) (04:48)

### Multi-location portfolio company · from ~$2M a year to ~$2M a month · plateaued
- **Situation.** The CEO didn't know where the profit went or how many new locations the business could afford.
- **Diagnosis.** No finance function.
- **Advice.** Build finance leadership, and treat that feeling of uncertainty as the signal that a function is missing.
- **Why.** Finance tells you what share of cash flow can fund new openings.
- **Source.** [I Reverse Engineered the Perfect Business](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HKbFUWJwEG0&t=3497s) (58:17)

### Alex's first gym · ~$6K left after build-out · rent ~$5K a month
- **Situation.** Almost no cash to pay for services the gym needed.
- **Diagnosis.** Cash constraint.
- **Advice.** Barter memberships for cleaning, IT, laundry, food and video production, and run cheap Facebook ads.
- **Why.** An extra member cost him almost nothing, so trading memberships was far cheaper than paying cash.
- **Source.** [How to Start a Business From Nothing (Thank Me Later)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=unshZobTt6Q&t=95s) (01:35)

### Alex's second gym · ~8x the investment of the first · same earnings
- **Situation.** About $250K went into a premium fit-out.
- **Diagnosis.** He spent on what he liked instead of what members valued: community, accountability and trainer quality.
- **Advice.** Learn the job the customer hires you for before reinvesting.
- **Why.** Spend only goes further where customers see value.
- **Source.** [How to Start a Business From Nothing (Thank Me Later)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=unshZobTt6Q&t=1761s) (29:21)

## Patterns {#patterns}

- **Underpriced, then a price rise first (about 10 cases).** Close rates of 70–90% and full calendars read to him as underpricing. Typical moves: 10–40% increases, VIP tiers, weekend or same-day surcharges, priority fees. He watches the close rate and holds the price while it stays healthy.
- **Underspending on a channel that already works (about 8 cases).** Search or social returning 5:1 to 13:1 on a tiny budget, an untapped door-to-door team or networking. He scales the winner before testing anything new, and judges success by total profit.
- **Capacity capped by one expert or too few staff (about 9 cases).** Founder-doctors, owners in the van, missing technicians, cleaners and practitioners. He prices out what one hire is worth in gross profit and makes recruiting a funnel with a real budget.
- **Cash collected too late (about 7 cases).** Quarterly billing, pay-per-visit, slow resident profit. He moves cash forward with card-on-file assessments, prepaid years, enrolment fees and up-front bundles.
- **Too many avatars, channels or ventures (about 8 cases).** Trash with two avatars and five channels, clinics with many services, owners with side ventures. He cuts to one avatar, one channel and one model before expanding.
- **Weak front door and slow selling (about 6 cases).** No dedicated landing page, sales delayed to day two, leads called back late. He adds a cheap front-end offer, same-day selling and an instant call-back.
- **Missing back end and rebooking (about 6 cases).** No membership, no rebook, no maintenance plan, no second visit for first-timers. He adds a default membership or plan and an ask at every visit.
- **Owner, partner or leadership gap (about 8 cases).** Misaligned partners, missing finance function, no senior operators. He fixes partnerships first and pays up for experienced leaders, accepting lower short-term profit.
- **Leave a loved product alone (2–3 cases, from his own gyms).** When customers love it, replicate or raise price; spend where customers see value.

## How Alex would apply it {#alex}

**First questions.**
1. What are your show rate, close rate and return on ad spend? Very high numbers mean you are underpriced and underspending.
2. Are you limited by demand (empty chairs, open slots) or by supply (waiting list, owner or expert fully booked)?
3. How much cash do you collect on day one per customer, and does it cover the cost of getting and serving them?
4. How many avatars, services, locations and side ventures are you running, and which one makes the money?
5. What does one more technician, practitioner or rep bring in gross profit per year?

**Default moves by situation.**
- **High close rate, open capacity:** raise price 10–20%, build a dedicated landing page, then increase spend on the channel that already works until returns drop toward about 5:1.
- **Waiting list or expert at capacity:** move simple work to associates, make recruiting a funnel with a budget sized to a hire's gross profit, then raise price.
- **Cash squeeze:** restructure the offer so more cash comes up front (assessment with card on file, prepaid year, enrolment fee, first quarter up front), cut the lowest-margin payers or services, and stop new initiatives.
- **Lots of leads, poor conversion:** call leads within a minute, sell the same day after the assessment, add a VIP tier and name the differentiators.
- **One-time sale:** add a membership or maintenance plan, rebook before the customer leaves, and ask for a referral at each visit.
- **Owner wants a new venture or national expansion:** first dominate the current market and fix the current constraint; a new business rarely fixes the skill gap behind the plateau.
- **Multiple locations:** fix profit and payback per location before opening the next, and bring in a finance lead and a seasoned operator.
