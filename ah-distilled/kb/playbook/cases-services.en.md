## The gist {#gist}

Service owners usually come to Alex with a growth goal and a vague sense that they need more leads. In most of these cases the business is underpriced, priced by the hour or by cost-plus, and sold to anyone who asks. His first move is to repackage the work as an outcome with a clear price, often a fixed fee with a performance component, an anchor tier far above it and a cheaper downsell below. He reads close rates near 100% as proof the price is too low, and he expects some fall in close rate when the price rises.

The second pattern is acquisition that depends on something the owner doesn't control: vendor referrals, a handful of partners, the founder's network, one ad platform. He adds an owned channel, usually outbound to a defined list (top agents by volume, high-spending advertisers, partner types), and keeps doing more of whatever already works before trying something new. Partnerships and affiliates get real attention in his advice; he wants them activated, measured per partner and given an offer that changes their behaviour.

The third pattern is churn. Agencies and recurring services lose clients in months three to six, and he traces it to the wrong clients and a weak start more than to the work itself. He tightens the avatar to those who activate, adds qualification friction before the sale, makes the first 30 days deliver a visible win and keeps communicating what was done. For exits he looks at what a buyer would pay for: recurring revenue, multiple channels and no dependence on the founder, sometimes reached by rolling up the clients instead of selling the service firm.

## Cases {#cases}

### B2B hospitality consultancy · ~$1.1M/yr · $30K programme sold cold, LTV:CAC 1.7
- **Situation.** About 11% net after 4.5 years. A $30K 90-day programme is sold to strangers from paid ads at about $11.8K CAC. Retainer clients churn. Goal: triple.
- **Diagnosis.** A high-ticket offer pushed at cold traffic, weak ads, a high-friction funnel, and churn because value isn't shown.
- **Advice.** Call-out ads with pain hooks and a lead magnet; a two-page funnel with a VSL above the scheduler; a $5.8K audit as the front end, closed in one call by the founder; roll that credit into a quarterly retainer, with the big fee waived for annual commitment; show clients their numbers monthly.
- **Why.** A lower first step turns strangers into customers, who are far more likely to buy again; a fast win funds the bigger purchase.
- **Source.** [Building a $3,000,000 Business for a Stranger in 57 Mins](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n6SHLmKcY0E&t=287s) (04:47)

### Event planning and AV company · ~$44K average deal, ~1 deal a week · thin margins
- **Situation.** The owner closes about 40%; two reps underperform; line-item pricing is invented case by case.
- **Diagnosis.** Mispriced, ad hoc offer (a margin problem) plus a demand problem because the reps produce little.
- **Advice.** Build a cost-based quote generator at 5–10x cost with a price lock and a prepay discount. Turn the reps into setters for the owner, with daily check-ins and role play. VSLs at three touchpoints. Ask for multi-event commitments. Go after B2B conferences via booths and speaking. Max out LinkedIn outreach plus a dialer.
- **Why.** Don't pour demand into thin margins; doubling appointments for an owner who closes 40% changes the business fast. Follow-up: pipeline up about 200%, one-year clients converted to three years, one booth produced a roughly $500K deal.
- **Source.** [How to Get Rich for Service-Based Businesses (Thank Me Later)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mRlSb0O5QNU&t=908s) (15:08)

### Music-industry law firm with side lines · ~$300K/yr, losing ~$100K · six months of cash
- **Situation.** Legal work at $250 an hour (about $1,500 per client), plus a $500 course and contract software burning about $7K a month. Large social audiences, but no clear call to action.
- **Diagnosis.** Demand is sprayed across three offers, hourly billing underprices the work, and the software loses most of the money.
- **Advice.** Kill or sell the software. Make education free to feed services. Route every call to action to DMs and qualify new followers. Sell an outcome package at about $5,800 plus a small share of deals, with a $25K anchor and a $2–3K DIY downsell, sold on a scripted call.
- **Why.** Services already earn; about 800 new followers a month at 5% close and $6K could mean roughly $240K a month.
- **Source.** [I Confronted a Stranger With a Failing Business...](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6Fg4VXjRphQ&t=0s) (00:00); [I Blew Up a Business Through 3 Small Changes (Just Copy Me)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3yAiVjcImQ4&t=0s) (00:00)

### Luxury personal styling · ~$309K/yr, 42% margin · strong economics, low volume
- **Situation.** About 40 recurring clients, about one churn a year; $8.5K onboarding, then $2K a month. About 1,800 paid clicks to a form with no headline gave 8 sales. Six partners, found with about 40 cold emails, bring a third of revenue.
- **Diagnosis.** Conversion is the bottleneck, and the partner channel is barely tapped. No price anchor, continuity isn't the default.
- **Advice.** A style-assessment lead magnet, a short VSL and BANT questions; qualified leads get a call, the rest a freebie. Send 20 partner emails a day and hold partner events. Add a 10x top-tier anchor and default continuity after onboarding.
- **Why.** The product already converts on calls, so the cheap gains are upstream; partners earned about $2,500 per email sent, better than ads.
- **Source.** [I Blew Up A Secret Business To Prove It's Not Luck](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SmiOK8Yun4s&t=47s) (00:47); [Building a $1,000,000 Business for a Stranger in 56 Mins](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nrounb8NlFQ&t=479s) (07:59)

### Agencies serving local businesses · $1,500/month retainer · ~0.5:1 return in the first 30 days
- **Situation.** The offer looks the same as every competitor's retainer.
- **Diagnosis.** A commoditised offer.
- **Advice.** Restructure it: one payment up front, the agency works the leads, then the client pays per person who shows up.
- **Why.** Response rate rose 2.5x, appointments went from 40 to 100, close rate from 16% to 37% at four times the price, and cash collected rose about 22x on the same ad spend.
- **Source.** [GROWTH LEVERS: Big Business Owner Explains Real World Tactics That Work](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F3NyhOtRpOE&t=576s) (09:36)

### New marketing agency for home-service SMBs · $0 to ~$500K in four months · goal eight figures
- **Situation.** About $2K a month per client, about ten sales a month.
- **Diagnosis.** The price sits in the small-business churn zone (clients stay four to six months on average). Growth will stall near $100K a month as churn catches new sales, CAC rises and margins shrink.
- **Advice.** Choose one: go much cheaper and automated ($300–500 a month with visible deliverables such as map rankings and review management), or move up-market to sophisticated clients.
- **Why.** Cheap, visible services can stick 30–40 months with low CAC; high-touch work only sticks with clients who already have a working model.
- **Source.** [Watch This If You Have a Service Business](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqo0lVveh98&t=523s) (08:43)

### Subscription service · ~$400K/month · 100 new sales a month, didn't know churn
- **Situation.** 380 clients at $1,000 a month, growing fast; churn and LTV untracked.
- **Diagnosis.** About 13% monthly churn caps the business near $770K a month; growth will flatten.
- **Advice.** Put the effort into cutting churn toward about 3% before adding acquisition.
- **Why.** At 3% churn LTV rises to about $33K and the ceiling to about $3.3M a month, which is far easier than 10x lead flow. At 13% a month, under 20% of clients remain after a year, and buyers won't value that.
- **Source.** ["My business has stopped growing..what should I do?"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f_ceDZqhKbw&t=96s) (01:36)

### Website-as-a-service for SMBs · ~$20M/yr, ~$3.6M EBITDA · afraid AI will kill the product
- **Situation.** About $450 a month per client, 100% of growth from outbound cold calling, clients stay about 29 months.
- **Diagnosis.** The fear isn't in the metrics. The real risks are a single channel and a thin margin from being people-heavy.
- **Advice.** Don't rebuild the product. Add paid inbound. Spend about six months rebuilding workflows with AI and cut headcount by about half to roughly double margin, then accept a negative first quarter on CAC, knowing the 29-month back end.
- **Why.** Margin funds acquisition without touching price, and customers who would build their own site with AI were never buyers.
- **Source.** [Watch This If You Have a Service Business](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqo0lVveh98&t=907s) (15:07)

### CFO and tax advisory · ~$2.9M/yr · paused new sales, wants to sell courses
- **Situation.** Growing 30–35% a year on organic demand alone, but sales are paused because delivery is hard. The owner has made courses and books and wants to sell them.
- **Diagnosis.** Supply-constrained. A course business would be a new and equally hard business.
- **Advice.** Resume selling. Add offshore staff for two to three times leverage per person. Build a data layer, then AI. Keep courses and books as future marketing assets.
- **Why.** Zero new sales guarantees zero growth; fix the constraint you have before creating demand you can't serve.
- **Source.** [Watch This If You Have a Service Business](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqo0lVveh98&t=1284s) (21:24)

### Virtual CPA firm · ~$3M/yr, ~$1.1M net · ~70% annual retention
- **Situation.** Quarterly fees from about $1,950. Departing clients say they have learned the strategies.
- **Diagnosis.** Bad onboarding and too little communication; value isn't really being lost.
- **Advice.** Audit the handoff from sales to onboarding, keep notes on each client, communicate often about the work done and train accountants on client touchpoints. Then hire a top tax specialist.
- **Why.** He targets retention above 80%; visible care signals competent work.
- **Source.** [7 Millionaires Asked Me How to Get Rich(er)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bgBIO6nZawg&t=1014s) (16:54)

### Enterprise software-implementation consultancy · ~$1.4M/yr · thinking of starting over
- **Situation.** About 12 clients, almost all from vendor referrals as a subcontractor: capped hourly rate, about 17% margin against about 83% when selling direct. Considering a new healthcare business with a spouse.
- **Diagnosis.** Fuzzy avatar, no owned acquisition channel, underpriced for very large clients. The real risk is split focus, and the owner mostly wants permission.
- **Advice.** Commit fully to the new venture (the spouse could run the old one) or stay. Plan which work to remove from the old business before deciding. If staying: add a direct acquisition channel and raise enterprise prices.
- **Why.** Straddling two businesses makes the new one grow far slower.
- **Source.** ["Should He Walk Away From His $1.4M Business To Start Over?" | Alex Hormozi Answers Live](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0RjIw9M1j28&t=47s) (00:47)

### Remote real-estate transaction coordination · ~$1M/yr · 70% of new business from the owner's networking
- **Situation.** Thin cash after a seasonal reserve; 73 of 87 appointments closed.
- **Diagnosis.** No leverage in acquisition, and an 84% close rate that points to underpricing.
- **Advice.** Pull top agents by sales volume from the listings database and run outbound (call, email, text) into small-group presentations. Later, local ads by city radius with high production thresholds. Raise prices.
- **Why.** Outbound is cheaper and safer with thin cash; ads will lose money while they learn.
- **Source.** ["He Makes $1.5M a Year... and Keeps 1.3% of It" | Alex Hormozi Answers Live](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lidFtb1Ga0o&t=5507s) (91:47)

### Offshore content-editor placement · ~$800K/yr · all clients from referrals
- **Situation.** $6.5K placement fee plus $2.5K a month; about 3% annual churn.
- **Diagnosis.** A pure demand problem; retention is excellent.
- **Advice.** Use the in-house team to make content about content (the process up to the sale) and turn the best pieces into ads. Partner with communities where the avatar gathers. Possibly raise prices and grandfather referrers; set a three-month minimum.
- **Why.** The content doubles as ad creative, and a couple of community partners could triple the business.
- **Source.** ["He Makes $1.5M a Year... and Keeps 1.3% of It" | Alex Hormozi Answers Live](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lidFtb1Ga0o&t=3856s) (64:16)

### Lead and sales service for new real-estate wholesalers · CAC ~$5K, LTV ~$35K · very low close rate
- **Situation.** Many prospects can't afford the first payment.
- **Diagnosis.** Unqualified lead flow drains the sales team and the close rate.
- **Advice.** Define the avatar from the highest-spending, longest-staying customers (demographics, quantifiables such as income, geography, behaviour in the sales process). Put that friction into ads, landing pages, form drop-downs and the VSL. Send the best leads to sales and the rest to an automated self-liquidating offer.
- **Why.** Qualification raises cost per call but lowers CAC and raises LTV. He couldn't recall a case where it lost him money, and selling to people spending their last dollar hurts ethics, morale and reputation.
- **Source.** [How to Build a Business That Runs Without You](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TLpLmhWqKEU&t=3357s) (55:57)

### B2B licensing for gym owners (Alex's own) · 100+ new gyms a month · poor-fit clients
- **Situation.** Non-owners, part-time owners, owners without a signed lease and owners with no staff took the most team time and got the worst results.
- **Diagnosis.** No minimum client standard.
- **Advice.** Require at least 25 members, a signed lease, a full-time owner and ideally one employee. Return to a programme with a defined end and market average results.
- **Why.** Better-fit clients pay more, earn more, need less work and stay longer. Sales fell from 100+ to 40–50 a month, and escalations went from 22 to zero in four weeks.
- **Source.** [Pricing Lessons From a Private Equity God](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hnfh2jqNVEQ&t=336s) (05:36)

### Lead-gen and sales coaching agency for financial advisers · ~$6.6M/yr · churn is the main problem
- **Situation.** Paid ads to a VSL to a phone team. Goal: $20M+.
- **Diagnosis.** Activated clients stick, so this is an activation and avatar problem.
- **Advice.** Find the segments (demographics, quantifiables, behaviours) that activate and sell only to them, possibly adjusting price. Run daily group role play for clients' teams. Reverse-engineer activation and deliver an early win.
- **Why.** Bad-fit clients can't be helped however hard you try; he saw the same with gym owners too small to pay.
- **Source.** [Helping 4 Educational Business Owners Build a $1M Business in 25 Minutes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BYpTRiRqS1Y&t=1106s) (18:26)

### DM-funnel agency for creators · clients churn in 3–5 months
- **Situation.** Clients who hit a record month stay; the rest leave.
- **Diagnosis.** The first 30 days decide retention, and client selection is too loose.
- **Advice.** Require prior selling experience, make the offer more turnkey, set expectations up front, then scale outbound volume.
- **Why.** Fewer variables to get wrong; churn is mostly about getting clients to month six.
- **Source.** ["He Makes $1.5M a Year... and Keeps 1.3% of It" | Alex Hormozi Answers Live](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lidFtb1Ga0o&t=5016s) (83:36)

### Wholesale deal business · deals fell from ~70 to ~40 a month · top people left
- **Situation.** The sales manager and second-best closer left; the top closer was promoted to manager and coaches poorly. The owner wants to hire a sales director.
- **Diagnosis.** The founder doesn't know sales, nothing is enforced, and the wrong personality was promoted.
- **Advice.** The owner gets into sales personally: takes calls, simplifies the script, enforces it for everyone, and trains through call review and drills.
- **Why.** A sales-led company whose CEO can't run sales is held hostage by whoever does.
- **Source.** [10 Millionaires Asked Me How to Get Richer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6m6DCQMASEM&t=781s) (13:01)

### Solo paid-ads agency, regulated niche · ~$90K/yr profit · anxious about the first hire
- **Situation.** One person serving a hard-to-advertise niche.
- **Diagnosis.** A broken model; people aren't the issue.
- **Advice.** Either charge ten times more per client or become ten times more efficient in delivery before thinking about hiring.
- **Why.** There is no margin to hire from, and scaling the current model would scale the problem.
- **Source.** [10 Millionaires Asked Me How to Get Richer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6m6DCQMASEM&t=2422s) (40:22)

### B2B licensing for gym owners (Alex's own) · ~$1M a week · founders want out
- **Situation.** 100% of sales from paid social, and the founder was the face of every ad.
- **Diagnosis.** A personal brand more than a business: founder dependency, one channel and churn would cap any exit price.
- **Advice.** Hire someone from a competitor that did outbound and have him build an outbound department from scratch; persist through slow early months as long as each funnel stage improves. Take the founder off the door: specialists, former clients as product leads, new faces in ads, ads handed to the general manager.
- **Why.** Within about a year outbound was roughly half of revenue, which reassured the acquirer; the business also became worth keeping.
- **Source.** [How I Scaled My Sales Team \[Compensation, Recruiting & Training\]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=okA9Yt2KZuk&t=139s) (02:19); [I Reverse Engineered the Perfect Business](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HKbFUWJwEG0&t=1297s) (21:37)

### Consultant to accounting firms · wants a sellable business
- **Situation.** Nobody buys a consulting practice like this one.
- **Diagnosis.** Buyers do buy accounting firms, especially in aggregate.
- **Advice.** Assemble roll-ups of 10–20 client firms every year or two and take a slice for arranging the deal.
- **Why.** Volume earns a premium: firms that sell at about 4x alone may sell at about 12x together, so even after giving up 30% they roughly double.
- **Source.** [How to Build a Valuable Company You Can Sell Someday](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VxKwz6hBVZU&t=812s) (13:32)

### Done-for-you marketing for medical clinics · ~$5M/yr · serves ~160 of ~2,000 clinics
- **Situation.** A healthy agency with a small share of a defined niche.
- **Diagnosis.** It captures a small fee from clients who together generate far more revenue.
- **Advice.** Consider rolling up 10–20 clients into a national group for a large exit, taking 20–30%.
- **Why.** Consolidation lifts the multiple, and being the one who did it changes pricing and positioning.
- **Source.** [7 Millionaires Asked Me How to Get Rich(er)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bgBIO6nZawg&t=0s) (00:00)

### Architecture firm · asking how to measure revenue retention
- **Situation.** Projects are one-off, so client retention looks naturally low.
- **Diagnosis.** Thinking at the project level.
- **Advice.** Measure retention one level up, at the referral partner (contractors and others who send repeat work), and build acquisition around winning partners, each with a known value and cost.
- **Why.** The same happened in his software company: agencies stayed even though their end clients churned.
- **Source.** [How to Build a Business That Runs Without You](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TLpLmhWqKEU&t=3839s) (63:59)

### Insurance agency, 29 agents · owner as the face or more recruits
- **Situation.** Most leads come from referrals and networking, not from the owner's content.
- **Diagnosis.** Demand-constrained.
- **Advice.** Recruit agents and build training so they generate their own leads. Alternate content calls to action between buyers and recruits.
- **Why.** Lead getters compound on the same owner effort.
- **Source.** [$100M Investor Answers The Most Important Business Questions for 1 Hour](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=--9kOXNCUdE&t=2753s) (45:53)

### Paid-ads agency · wants clients spending $100K+ a month · all business from referrals
- **Situation.** No owned acquisition; growth depends on who happens to refer.
- **Diagnosis.** Waiting for the right clients instead of going after them.
- **Advice.** Outbound to high-value targets, niche content, and their own ads with blunt call-outs that exclude small spenders, plus LinkedIn. Build a consulting phase into contracts for when clients take ads in-house.
- **Why.** You have to go and get the customer you want.
- **Source.** [10 Millionaires Asked Me How to Get Richer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6m6DCQMASEM&t=3099s) (51:39)

### Enterprise web development agency · two clients are ~90% of revenue · outbound failing
- **Situation.** Two 50/50 co-founders both doing client work.
- **Diagnosis.** A partner alignment problem: the partner who owns sales isn't doing the activity. Skill isn't the issue.
- **Advice.** Have a direct, unsoftened conversation that separates criticism (the gap between what was said and what was done) from insult. Agree whether both want growth; if not, split responsibilities or work on separate things.
- **Why.** When they lost a big client earlier they replaced it quickly, so the capability exists.
- **Source.** [10 Millionaires Asked Me How to Get Richer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6m6DCQMASEM&t=1702s) (28:22)

### Niche lead-gen agency plus software · ~$2M + ~$0.55M/yr · founder unmotivated
- **Situation.** About 15–20% share of a small market, goal $5M in two years. Operations are delegated, but the founder can't make himself do maker work.
- **Diagnosis.** Not real burnout: the work no longer rewards him and the stakes are too low. A hunter stuck farming.
- **Advice.** Raise the goal and shorten the timeline, hire a leader to run day-to-day, and go back to selling and finding bigger deals. Don't worry about the main ad channel changing; move budget gradually when needed.
- **Why.** Burnout comes when reward is delayed or no longer satisfies; 20–30% growth changes nothing in his life, 300% would.
- **Source.** ["Should He Walk Away From His $1.4M Business To Start Over?" | Alex Hormozi Answers Live](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0RjIw9M1j28&t=1198s) (19:58)

### Music law firm, later stage · ~$100K/month and growing · choosing between units
- **Situation.** Law firm growing, education flat at about $200K, contract software alongside.
- **Diagnosis.** Two avatars, large labels and semi-professional musicians. About 40% of software users still paying after a year signals fit with the smaller buyers.
- **Advice.** Go downmarket, use education to offset CAC, and focus everything on lifting month-12 retention from 40% to 60–70%.
- **Why.** Getting these buyers is easy for them, keeping them is the hard part, and retention is what makes the model compound.
- **Source.** [$100M Investor Answers The Most Important Business Questions for 1 Hour](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=--9kOXNCUdE&t=2467s) (41:07)

### House-flipping operation · ~$15M sales, ~$2.5M after team · 95% of deals from foreclosures
- **Situation.** Dominates a four-hour driving radius; no advertising at all.
- **Diagnosis.** No control over deal flow.
- **Advice.** Start advertising (search, mail, social) in the current market, which might double it. Study the largest national buyer and copy and improve their model before expanding.
- **Why.** The model is proven with eight years of learning; changing the business would reset the curve.

- **Diagnosis.** A financing problem: depreciating machines bought with cash.
- **Advice.** Test leasing from the manufacturer instead of buying; otherwise sell a small stake for capital.

### Bleacher rental · ~$4.5M/yr, ~$1M profit · leaders don't delegate
- **Situation.** The leadership layer holds on to work.
- **Diagnosis.** Vague expectations and weak training.

## Patterns {#patterns}

- **Underpriced, hourly or cost-plus (about 9 cases).** Hourly legal work, invented line items, capped subcontractor rates, close rates of 84–100%. He moves to an outcome price, often a fixed fee plus a performance share, with a high anchor and a cheaper downsell.
- **Acquisition depends on something the owner doesn't control (about 9 cases).** Vendor referrals, a few partners, the founder's network, one ad platform. He adds an owned channel, usually outbound to a defined list, and keeps scaling what already works.
- **Partners and affiliates under-used (about 6 cases).** Referral partners bringing a third of revenue from a few emails, community partners, partner-level retention. He scales partner outreach, gives partners an offer that changes behaviour, and measures value per partner.
- **Churn from the wrong clients and a weak start (about 7 cases).** Agencies losing clients in months three to six, CPA clients leaving at 70% retention, a subscription at 13% monthly churn. He tightens the avatar, adds qualification, engineers an early win and communicates the work done.
- **Offer mismatch with cold traffic (about 4 cases).** High-ticket offers pushed at strangers, commodity retainers. He lowers the first step (audit, front-end offer, pay per show) and ascends later.
- **Price in the churn zone (2–3 cases).** Mid-priced SMB services churn fast; he says go cheap and automated with visible deliverables, or go up-market.
- **Founder or partner as the sales problem (about 4 cases).** A founder who doesn't know sales, a partner who doesn't sell, an unmotivated hunter stuck farming. He puts the owner back into selling or forces the partner conversation.
- **Sellability (about 5 cases).** Consulting and talent businesses are hard to sell; he points to recurring revenue, multiple channels, no founder dependency, or rolling up the clients.
- **Supply before demand (2–3 cases).** Paused sales or a solo operator without margin; he fixes delivery leverage or price before adding leads.

## How Alex would apply it {#alex}

**First questions.**
1. How do you price: hourly, cost-plus, or by outcome? What is your close rate?
2. Where does each new client come from, and how much of it do you control?
3. How many clients reach month six, and what do the ones who stay have in common?
4. What happens in the first 30 days, and when does the client see a visible win?
5. If you sold tomorrow, what would a buyer actually be paying for?

**Default moves by situation.**
- **Close rate very high, hourly billing:** repackage as an outcome with a fixed fee, add a performance component, an anchor tier above and a downsell below; accept a lower close rate.
- **All work from referrals or one partner:** build outbound to a defined list (top performers in the target market, big spenders), and run a partner programme with an offer partners care about.
- **Churn in months three to six:** define the avatar from your best long-stay clients, qualify on the form and the call, set expectations up front, make onboarding personal and show the results regularly.
- **Selling high-ticket to cold traffic:** add a cheaper front-end step (audit, assessment, pay per show) and roll its value into the main offer.
- **Too many offers or units:** keep the one with demand and margin, turn the others into marketing assets or close them.
- **Delivery capacity full:** raise price first, add offshore staff and AI to the workflow, and keep selling.
- **Planning an exit:** add a second channel, remove the founder from the front, raise retention, or roll up the clients you serve.
