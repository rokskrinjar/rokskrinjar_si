The Core FourCore Four

$100M Leads · Core Four

There are only four ways to let people know about you, set by two questions. Do they already know you, and are you reaching one person or many? Pick one, master it, then add the next.Obstajajo samo štirje načini, kako ljudem sporočiš, da obstajaš, določata pa jih dve vprašanji. Te že poznajo in ali nagovarjaš eno osebo ali mnoge? Izberi enega, ga obvladaj, nato dodaj naslednjega.

The gist

Every way of getting engaged leads is a version of four activities. You can only reach people who know you or people who don't, and you can reach them one at a time or many at once. Those two axes give four combinations, and Alex calls them the Core Four.

One-to-one One-to-many
Known audience (warm) Warm outreach Posting free content
Unknown audience (cold) Cold outreach Paid ads

Seeing it this way stops the hunt for a secret channel. LinkedIn, email, events, podcasts and billboards are all one of these four. The question becomes which one you do, how much of it, and how well. The rule is simple: start with one, get good at it, then add the next.

How it works

The two axes

Known vs unknown audience. People who know you (friends, past clients, followers) trust you more and reply more often, but there are only so many of them. People who don't know you are an almost unlimited pool, but they trust you less and reply less.

One-to-one vs one-to-many. Talking to one person is personal and converts well, but your hours cap it. Talking to many at once scales, but each message is less personal and the average response is lower.

Combination Strength Limit
Known, one-to-one Free, fast, highest reply rate You run out of people you know
Known, one-to-many One piece reaches many and keeps working Slow to build, needs an audience
Unknown, one-to-one No limit on who you can reach, precise targeting Low reply rate, needs volume
Unknown, one-to-many Fastest to scale once it works Costs money, a bad ad burns budget faster

The four channels in one line each

  • Warm outreach: personal messages to people who already know you. The best place to start for almost everyone.
  • Posting free content: publishing useful things for an audience that follows you, so warm leads come to you over time.
  • Cold outreach: personal messages to people who don't know you, from a list you build. A volume game.
  • Paid ads: paying a platform to show your message to people who don't know you. The fastest way to scale a message that already works.

Start with one, master it, then add

Most people do a little of everything and none of it enough to learn anything. Alex's approach is the opposite:

  1. Pick one channel that fits your resources. More time than money points to warm outreach, content or cold outreach. More money than time points to paid ads.
  2. Do it at volume, every day. See the Rule of 100 for the daily-volume habit.
  3. Get it working predictably. You know roughly how many engaged leads a given amount of effort produces.
  4. Only then add a second channel. Larger businesses eventually run all four, but they added them one at a time.

A natural order for many small businesses

As a rule of thumb, many start with warm outreach (free and quick), add content (builds trust over time), then cold outreach (removes the limit of who you know), and finally paid ads (once profit pays for them). The right order depends on your time, money and market, so treat this as a default, not a law.

What every channel needs

Whichever you choose, the pieces are the same. An offer worth responding to (ideally a lead magnet), a message the right people notice, a clear next step, and enough volume to measure cost per engaged lead.

Examples

Examples are invented for illustration.

Freelance BI consultant with no new clients (B2B analytics)

Before. An occasional LinkedIn post saying "available for new projects", a website with no traffic, and two months without an enquiry. A bit of everything, nothing at volume.

After. He picks warm outreach only. He exports 400 contacts and sends 20 personal messages a day to former colleagues and clients, offering a free reporting review and asking whether they know anyone it would help. In three weeks: 9 conversations, 2 projects.

He moved from a passive presence to one active channel he controls. Once it runs, he can add content on the same themes that worked in conversations.

Hair salon with empty Tuesdays (local business)

Channel What it could look like
Warm outreach A personal text to clients who haven't visited in three months, offering a Tuesday slot
Free content Short before/after videos with an at-home care tip and a booking link
Cold outreach Visiting nearby offices with a staff offer
Paid ads A local ad within 5 km aimed at parents free on Tuesday mornings

The owner doesn't start with all four. She texts existing clients first, which is free and fills slots the same week. Once she knows which message works, she reuses it in content and later in an ad.

Production planning SaaS (manufacturing software)

A small team selling planning software to metalworking shops has few contacts in the industry, so warm outreach runs out fast. They choose cold outreach: 600 manufacturers from public registries, a personalised first line, and a free calculation of hours lost to rescheduled orders. At 50 messages a day with follow-ups and a 3% reply rate, that is about 1.5 replies a day, enough for several demos a week.

Common mistakes

  • All channels at once. No channel gets enough volume to show whether it works. Pick one and drive it to results.
  • Skipping warm outreach out of embarrassment. It is often the fastest path to the first clients, and asking for a referral isn't pushy.
  • Selling in the first message. Offer value first and get an engaged lead. The sale happens in the conversation.
  • Content and ads without a call to action. Followers and clicks are not engaged leads. Always say what to do next.
  • Quitting too early. Ten posts or one small campaign is not a test of a channel. Without volume you can't tell whether the channel or the message is the problem.

How Alex would apply it

  • He maps what you're doing onto the four. Whatever tactics you list, he sorts them into the grid. Usually it shows you're barely doing any of them.
  • He picks one channel based on resources. Little money means you buy leads with time: warm outreach, content or cold outreach. Money and no time means ads.
  • He asks about volume before tactics. If the channel "doesn't work", his first suspicion is that you didn't do enough of it.
  • He adds channels only when the current one is predictable, and treats each new one as its own skill to learn.
  • He keeps the same lead magnet across channels, so what wins in one channel can be reused in the next.
  • He cuts shiny-channel hopping. A new platform is still one of the four. He would rather you get better at the one you have.
  • Questions he would ask: Which of the four are you doing now, and how much each day? Do you have more time or more money? How many engaged leads did your main channel bring last month? Is that channel predictable yet? What is your offer in every message?

Worksheet

Answer in writing for your business. The goal is to pick one channel for the next 30 days.

  1. Inventory: How many people already know you (contacts, followers, past clients)? How many might know someone with your problem?
  2. Resources: Do you have more time or more money right now? Which channel fits that best?
  3. Choice: Which one channel will you run for 30 days, and how much volume per day?
  4. Engaged lead: What exactly must someone do to count as an engaged lead in this channel?
  5. Message: Write the first message, post or ad. What does it offer for free, and what is the call to action?
  6. Next channel: What has to be true (leads per week, cost) before you add a second one?

Bistvo

Vsak način pridobivanja zavzetih leadov je različica štirih dejavnosti. Dosežeš lahko samo ljudi, ki te poznajo, ali ljudi, ki te ne poznajo, in jih lahko nagovarjaš posamično ali mnoge naenkrat. Iz teh dveh osi nastanejo štiri kombinacije, ki jim Alex pravi Core Four.

Ena na ena (one-to-one) Ena na mnogo (one-to-many)
Znano občinstvo (warm) Topli outreach Objavljanje brezplačne vsebine
Neznano občinstvo (cold) Hladni outreach Plačani oglasi

S tem pogledom nehaš iskati skrivni kanal. LinkedIn, e-pošta, dogodki, podkasti in jumbo plakati so vsi eden od teh štirih. Vprašanje postane, katerega delaš, koliko in kako dobro. Pravilo je preprosto: začni z enim, postani dober v njem, nato dodaj naslednjega.

Kako deluje

Dve osi

Znano ali neznano občinstvo. Ljudje, ki te poznajo (prijatelji, nekdanje stranke, sledilci), ti bolj zaupajo in pogosteje odgovorijo, a jih je omejeno število. Ljudi, ki te ne poznajo, je skoraj neomejeno, a ti manj zaupajo in redkeje odgovorijo.

Ena na ena ali ena na mnogo. Pogovor z eno osebo je oseben in dobro konvertira, a ga omejujejo tvoje ure. Nagovarjanje mnogih naenkrat se skalira, a je vsako sporočilo manj osebno in povprečen odziv nižji.

Kombinacija Prednost Omejitev
Znani, ena na ena Brezplačno, hitro, najvišji odziv Ljudi, ki jih poznaš, zmanjka
Znani, ena na mnogo En kos doseže mnoge in deluje dolgo Počasna gradnja, potrebuješ občinstvo
Neznani, ena na ena Brez omejitve, koga dosežeš, natančno ciljanje Nizek odziv, potrebuješ volumen
Neznani, ena na mnogo Najhitrejše skaliranje, ko deluje Stane denar, slab oglas hitreje zažge proračun

Štirje kanali, vsak v eni vrstici

  • Topli outreach (warm outreach): osebna sporočila ljudem, ki te že poznajo. Najboljši začetek za skoraj vsakogar.
  • Objavljanje brezplačne vsebine (posting free content): objavljaš uporabne stvari za občinstvo, ki ti sledi, da topli leadi sčasoma pridejo sami.
  • Hladni outreach (cold outreach): osebna sporočila ljudem, ki te ne poznajo, s seznama, ki ga zgradiš. Igra volumna.
  • Plačani oglasi (paid ads): platformi plačaš, da tvoje sporočilo pokaže ljudem, ki te ne poznajo. Najhitrejši način, da skaliraš sporočilo, ki že deluje.

Začni z enim, ga obvladaj, nato dodaj

Večina ljudi dela malo vsega in ničesar dovolj, da bi se kaj naučila. Alexov pristop je nasproten:

  1. Izberi en kanal, ki ustreza tvojim virom. Več časa kot denarja kaže na topli outreach, vsebino ali hladni outreach. Več denarja kot časa kaže na plačane oglase.
  2. Delaj ga v velikem obsegu, vsak dan. Navado dnevnega volumna opisuje pravilo 100.
  3. Naj deluje predvidljivo. Približno veš, koliko zavzetih leadov prinese določena količina truda.
  4. Šele nato dodaj drugi kanal. Večja podjetja sčasoma uporabljajo vse štiri, a so jih dodajala enega za drugim.

Naraven vrstni red za mnoga mala podjetja

Kot pravilo palca mnogi začnejo s toplim outreachom (brezplačen in hiter), dodajo vsebino (sčasoma gradi zaupanje), nato hladni outreach (odpravi omejitev, koga poznaš) in na koncu plačane oglase (ko jih plača dobiček). Pravi vrstni red je odvisen od tvojega časa, denarja in trga, zato je to privzeta izbira, ne zakon.

Kaj potrebuje vsak kanal

Ne glede na izbiro so sestavni deli enaki. Ponudba, na katero se splača odzvati (najbolje lead magnet), sporočilo, ki ga opazijo pravi ljudje, jasen naslednji korak in dovolj volumna, da izmeriš strošek na zavzetega leada.

Primeri

Primeri so izmišljeni in služijo ponazoritvi.

Samostojni BI svetovalec brez novih strank (B2B analitika)

Pred. Občasna objava na LinkedInu »na voljo za nove projekte«, spletna stran brez obiska in dva meseca brez povpraševanja. Malo vsega, nič v velikem obsegu.

Po. Izbere samo topli outreach. Izvozi 400 kontaktov in vsak dan pošlje 20 osebnih sporočil nekdanjim sodelavcem in strankam, ponudi brezplačen pregled poročanja in vpraša, ali poznajo koga, ki bi mu koristil. V treh tednih: 9 pogovorov, 2 projekta.

S pasivne prisotnosti je prešel na en aktiven kanal, ki ga nadzira. Ko ta teče, lahko doda vsebino na teme, ki so delovale v pogovorih.

Frizerski salon s praznimi torki (lokalno podjetje)

Kanal Kako bi lahko izgledal
Topli outreach Oseben SMS strankam, ki jih ni bilo tri mesece, s ponudbo termina v torek
Brezplačna vsebina Kratki videi »pred in po« z nasvetom za nego doma in povezavo za rezervacijo
Hladni outreach Obisk bližnjih pisarn s ponudbo za zaposlene
Plačani oglasi Lokalni oglas v polmeru 5 km za starše, ki imajo čas v torek dopoldne

Lastnica ne začne z vsemi štirimi. Najprej pošlje SMS obstoječim strankam, kar je brezplačno in zapolni termine že isti teden. Ko ve, katero sporočilo deluje, ga uporabi v vsebini in pozneje v oglasu.

SaaS za planiranje proizvodnje (programska oprema za proizvodnjo)

Majhna ekipa, ki prodaja programsko opremo za planiranje kovinskim obratom, ima v panogi malo kontaktov, zato topli outreach hitro zmanjka. Izberejo hladni outreach: 600 proizvodnih podjetij iz javnih registrov, prilagojena prva vrstica in brezplačen izračun ur, izgubljenih zaradi prestavljanja naročil. Pri 50 sporočilih na dan z opomniki in 3-odstotnem odzivu je to približno 1,5 odgovora na dan, kar je dovolj za več demo predstavitev na teden.

Pogoste napake

  • Vsi kanali naenkrat. Noben kanal ne dobi dovolj volumna, da bi pokazal, ali deluje. Izberi enega in ga pelji do rezultatov.
  • Preskakovanje toplega outreacha iz sramu. Pogosto je najhitrejša pot do prvih strank, prošnja za priporočilo pa ni vsiljiva.
  • Prodaja v prvem sporočilu. Najprej ponudi vrednost in pridobi zavzetega leada. Prodaja se zgodi v pogovoru.
  • Vsebina in oglasi brez poziva k dejanju. Sledilci in kliki niso zavzeti leadi. Vedno povej, kaj naj naredijo naslednje.
  • Prehitro obupati. Deset objav ali ena majhna kampanja ni test kanala. Brez volumna ne veš, ali je problem kanal ali sporočilo.

Kako bi to uporabil Alex

  • Kar delaš, razvrsti v štiri polja. Katerekoli taktike našteješ, jih postavi v mrežo. Navadno pokaže, da skoraj nobene ne delaš zares.
  • En kanal izbere glede na vire. Malo denarja pomeni, da leade kupuješ s časom: topli outreach, vsebina ali hladni outreach. Denar in brez časa pomeni oglase.
  • Pred taktiko vpraša po volumnu. Če kanal »ne deluje«, najprej posumi, da ga nisi delal dovolj.
  • Kanale dodaja šele, ko je trenutni predvidljiv, in vsakega novega obravnava kot svojo veščino, ki se jo je treba naučiti.
  • Isti lead magnet uporabi v vseh kanalih, da lahko to, kar zmaga v enem, ponovno uporabi v naslednjem.
  • Odreže skakanje na nove kanale. Nova platforma je še vedno eden od štirih. Raje vidi, da postaneš boljši v tistem, ki ga imaš.
  • Vprašanja, ki bi jih postavil: Katere od štirih zdaj delaš in koliko vsak dan? Imaš več časa ali več denarja? Koliko zavzetih leadov je prejšnji mesec prinesel tvoj glavni kanal? Je ta kanal že predvidljiv? Kakšna je tvoja ponudba v vsakem sporočilu?

Delovni list

Odgovori pisno za svoje podjetje. Cilj je izbrati en kanal za naslednjih 30 dni.

  1. Inventar: Koliko ljudi te že pozna (kontakti, sledilci, nekdanje stranke)? Koliko jih morda pozna nekoga s tvojim problemom?
  2. Viri: Imaš trenutno več časa ali več denarja? Kateri kanal temu najbolj ustreza?
  3. Izbira: Kateri en kanal boš izvajal 30 dni in s kolikšnim dnevnim volumnom?
  4. Zavzeti lead: Kaj natančno mora nekdo narediti, da v tem kanalu šteje kot zavzeti lead?
  5. Sporočilo: Napiši prvo sporočilo, objavo ali oglas. Kaj ponuja brezplačno in kakšen je poziv k dejanju?
  6. Naslednji kanal: Kaj mora veljati (leadi na teden, strošek), preden dodaš drugega?
