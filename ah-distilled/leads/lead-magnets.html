$100M Leads · Lead Magnet

A free offer that completely solves one narrow problem and, in doing so, reveals the next problem your paid offer solves. It turns strangers into engaged leads who already trust you.Brezplačna ponudba, ki do konca reši en ozek problem in s tem razkrije naslednji problem, ki ga rešuje tvoja plačljiva ponudba. Neznance spremeni v zavzete leade, ki ti že zaupajo.

The gist

A lead magnet is a free offer that gives a complete solution to a narrow problem. Solving it reveals a next problem, and that next problem is the one your core offer solves. It is a small, real win, not a brochure or "an ebook with 50 tips". The person gets value, and you get an engaged lead who already trusts you and knows what they need next.

Narrow problem solved for free → reveals the next problem → solved by your core offer

The lead magnet is how you make every channel work better. A message, post or ad that offers something valuable and free gets far more responses than one that asks for a purchase. The quality of what you give away also sets what people expect from what you sell.

How it works

Three types of lead magnet

Type What it is Best fit
Reveal their problem A diagnosis, audit, test, assessment or calculator that shows where the problem is and what it costs Problems the buyer doesn't see or underestimates, hidden costs
Samples and trials A piece of the core offer: a free first session, one module, a limited trial, a tasting Offers where experience is the best proof
One step of a multi-step process You give away one step in full, often the first, of a solution that has several Processes with clear stages where the rest is work they'd rather hand off

Reveal their problem. Many people don't buy because they don't know they have a problem, or think it's small. A diagnosis turns "we'll see" into "we have to fix this", especially when it puts a number on it.

Samples and trials. There is no stronger proof than using the thing. The sample should impress, and stop before it solves everything. A free software trial is a sample.

One step of a process. The person makes real progress and at the same moment sees how many steps remain. The natural next question is whether you can do the rest.

How to build one: seven steps

  1. Pick the narrow problem and the audience. One concrete, painful problem your ideal buyer has, linked directly to your paid offer. "Better business" is too broad. "Which of our customers lose us money" is narrow.
  2. Choose the type. Reveal, sample or one step. Ask which one leads most naturally to the next problem you solve.
  3. Choose the delivery. Software (a tool, calculator, template that computes), information (a guide, short video, checklist), a service (an audit, a call, a workshop) or physical (a sample, a box). Pick what is easiest for the buyer and affordable for you to give at volume.
  4. Test the name. The name decides whether people take it. Write several, show them to real prospects or run them in small tests, and keep the one that gets the most responses. A name that states the outcome and the audience usually beats a clever one.
  5. Make it easy to consume. Few steps, little reading, no waiting. A short video or a filled-in template beats a 40-page PDF nobody opens. If they don't consume it, they never reach the next problem.
  6. Make it really good. The test is whether people would pay for it. If not, improve it until they would. People judge your paid offer by your free one.
  7. Add a clear call to action. Tell them exactly what to do next and why now, for example book a call to fix what the audit showed. A great lead magnet with no next step creates grateful people, not customers.

Why "complete" and "narrow" go together

If the problem is narrow, you can solve it completely for free without giving away your business. If it is broad, you can only solve it halfway, and a half-solution feels like a teaser. Narrow and complete builds trust. Broad and partial feels like a trick.

Give away the secrets, sell the implementation

A common fear is that a great free thing removes the need to buy. In practice, the more people learn, the more they see how much work the rest is, and the more they want someone to do it for them. You give away what to do. You sell doing it, doing it faster, or doing it with less risk.

Examples

Examples are invented for illustration.

ERP data-quality audit (B2B analytics)

Before. "Download our ebook: 10 business intelligence trends for 2026."

After. "Send us an export of customers and items from your ERP. Within 48 hours you get a report showing how many duplicates, empty fields and wrong codes you have, and how many invoices are misclassified because of it."

Type: reveal the problem, delivered as a service. The ebook solved nothing and gave no reason to hire anyone. The audit fully answers a narrow question ("how bad is our data?") and reveals the next problem: cleaning the data and building reliable reporting, which is the paid offer. Most of the checks are scripted, so each audit is cheap to deliver.

Bakery that wants office orders (local business)

Before. A flyer with catering prices left at reception desks of nearby companies.

After. On Monday mornings they bring a free tasting box for ten people to a team meeting, with a card: "Order a regular Friday delivery and get the first month at 15% off."

Type: sample, physical delivery. The flyer asked people to imagine the product. The box gives the experience and raises the next problem: who sorts out snacks every week? The call to action is concrete and time-limited.

Automation process map (AI consulting)

An AI consultancy offers a free 45-minute workshop. With a department head they list the five most common manual tasks and estimate hours per month and how suitable each is for automation. The client leaves with a priority table they can use without the consultant. It is step one of a four-step process (map, prototype, production, maintenance). The close: "We'll get the first process on your list live in three weeks."

Common mistakes

  • Too broad a problem. "Everything about digital transformation" solves nothing. A narrow problem solved completely beats a broad one solved halfway.
  • No link to the paid offer. If the next problem isn't the one you sell, you collect grateful people, not clients.
  • Low quality because it's free. An average lead magnet signals an average service.
  • Hard to consume. Long documents, complex forms and waiting mean most people never get the value, and never reach the next step.
  • No call to action. The person is impressed and then doesn't know what to do. Always give one next step and make it easy.

How Alex would apply it

  • He works backwards from the core offer. First he asks what problem the paid offer solves, then which problem comes right before it. That earlier problem is the lead magnet.
  • He raises the bar on quality. His rule of thumb: if people wouldn't pay for it, it isn't good enough to give away.
  • He prefers the cheapest delivery that still impresses. Software and recorded information scale for free; a service converts better but costs time. He picks based on volume and margin.
  • He tests names and hooks before polishing. The same magnet with a better name can get many more takers, so he tests several and keeps the winner.
  • He puts the lead magnet in every channel. Warm messages, posts, cold emails and ads all offer the same free thing, so every channel produces engaged leads instead of asking for a sale.
  • He cuts anything that slows consumption: long intros, extra form fields, downloads that need setup.
  • Questions he would ask: What problem does your paid offer solve, and what problem comes just before it? Would anyone pay for your free thing? How long does it take to consume? What do they find out at the end that makes them want more? What exactly do you ask them to do next?

Worksheet

Design one lead magnet for your main offer. Answer in writing.

  1. Narrow problem: What one painful problem does your ideal buyer have just before they need your paid offer?
  2. Type: Is it best solved by revealing the problem, a sample, or one step of your process? Why?
  3. Delivery: Software, information, service or physical? What is easiest for them and affordable for you at volume?
  4. Name: Write three names that state the outcome and the audience. Which would you test first?
  5. Quality test: Would someone pay for it? How much? What would you add so the answer is yes?
  6. Next step: What will they discover after using it, and what exactly is your call to action?

Bistvo

Lead magnet je brezplačna ponudba, ki da celovito rešitev za ozek problem. Ko ga rešiš, se pokaže naslednji problem, in ta naslednji problem rešuje tvoja glavna ponudba (core offer). To je majhna, a resnična zmaga, ne brošura ali »e-knjiga s 50 nasveti«. Človek dobi vrednost, ti pa zavzetega leada (engaged lead), ki ti že zaupa in ve, kaj potrebuje naprej.

Ozek problem, rešen brezplačno → razkrije naslednji problem → reši ga tvoja glavna ponudba

Z lead magnetom vsak kanal deluje bolje. Sporočilo, objava ali oglas, ki ponuja nekaj vrednega in brezplačnega, dobi veliko več odzivov kot tisto, ki zahteva nakup. Kakovost tega, kar podariš, hkrati določa, kaj ljudje pričakujejo od tega, kar prodajaš.

Kako deluje

Trije tipi lead magnetov

Tip Kaj je Najbolj ustreza
Razkrij jim problem (reveal their problem) Diagnoza, pregled (audit), test, ocena ali kalkulator, ki pokaže, kje je problem in koliko stane Problemom, ki jih kupec ne vidi ali podcenjuje, skritim stroškom
Vzorci in preizkusi (samples and trials) Košček glavne ponudbe: brezplačno prvo srečanje, en modul, omejen preizkus, degustacija Ponudbam, pri katerih je izkušnja najboljši dokaz
En korak večstopenjskega procesa (one step of a multi-step process) En korak, pogosto prvi, rešitve z več koraki, podarjen v celoti Procesom z jasnimi fazami, kjer bi ostalo delo kupec raje predal

Razkrij jim problem. Veliko ljudi ne kupi, ker ne ve, da ima problem, ali pa misli, da je majhen. Diagnoza spremeni »bomo videli« v »to moramo rešiti«, še posebej, če problemu pripiše številko.

Vzorci in preizkusi. Ni močnejšega dokaza kot uporaba same stvari. Vzorec mora navdušiti in se ustaviti, preden reši vse. Brezplačen preizkus programske opreme je vzorec.

En korak procesa. Človek naredi pravi napredek in v istem trenutku vidi, koliko korakov je še pred njim. Naravno naslednje vprašanje je, ali lahko ostalo narediš ti.

Kako ga narediš: sedem korakov

  1. Izberi ozek problem in občinstvo. En konkreten, boleč problem idealnega kupca, neposredno povezan s tvojo plačljivo ponudbo. »Boljše poslovanje« je preširoko. »Kateri kupci nam prinašajo izgubo« je ozko.
  2. Izberi tip. Razkritje, vzorec ali en korak. Vprašaj se, kateri najbolj naravno vodi do naslednjega problema, ki ga rešuješ.
  3. Izberi način dostave. Programska oprema (orodje, kalkulator, predloga, ki računa), informacija (vodnik, kratek video, kontrolni seznam), storitev (pregled, klic, delavnica) ali fizično (vzorec, škatla). Izberi tisto, kar je kupcu najlažje in kar si lahko privoščiš dajati v velikem številu.
  4. Preizkusi ime. Ime odloči, ali ga bodo ljudje vzeli. Napiši več imen, jih pokaži pravim potencialnim strankam ali jih preizkusi v majhnih testih in obdrži tisto, ki dobi največ odzivov. Ime, ki pove izid in občinstvo, navadno premaga duhovito.
  5. Naj bo enostaven za uporabo. Malo korakov, malo branja, brez čakanja. Kratek video ali izpolnjena predloga premaga 40 strani PDF-ja, ki ga nihče ne odpre. Če ga ne uporabijo, nikoli ne pridejo do naslednjega problema.
  6. Naj bo res dober. Test je, ali bi ljudje zanj plačali. Če ne, ga izboljšuj, dokler ne bi. Ljudje tvojo plačljivo ponudbo sodijo po brezplačni.
  7. Dodaj jasen poziv k dejanju (call to action). Povej natančno, kaj naj naredijo naslednje in zakaj zdaj, na primer naj rezervirajo posvet, kjer rešita, kar je pokazal pregled. Odličen lead magnet brez naslednjega koraka ustvari hvaležne ljudi, ne strank.

Zakaj »celovito« in »ozko« sodita skupaj

Če je problem ozek, ga lahko brezplačno rešiš do konca, ne da bi podaril svoj posel. Če je širok, ga lahko rešiš le do polovice, polovična rešitev pa deluje kot zvabljanje. Ozko in celovito gradi zaupanje. Široko in delno deluje kot trik.

Podari skrivnosti, prodaj izvedbo

Pogost strah je, da odlična brezplačna stvar odpravi potrebo po nakupu. V praksi več ko ljudje vedo, bolj vidijo, koliko dela je še, in bolj želijo, da ga nekdo naredi zanje. Podariš, kaj narediti. Prodaš izvedbo, hitrejšo izvedbo ali izvedbo z manj tveganja.

Primeri

Primeri so izmišljeni in služijo ponazoritvi.

Pregled kakovosti podatkov v ERP (B2B analitika)

Pred. »Prenesite našo e-knjigo: 10 trendov poslovne inteligence v 2026.«

Po. »Pošljite nam izvoz kupcev in artiklov iz ERP. V 48 urah dobite poročilo, koliko dvojnikov, praznih polj in napačnih šifer imate in koliko faktur je zaradi tega napačno razvrščenih.«

Tip: razkritje problema, dostava kot storitev. E-knjiga ni rešila ničesar in ni dala razloga, da bi koga najeli. Pregled v celoti odgovori na ozko vprašanje (»kako slabi so naši podatki?«) in razkrije naslednji problem: čiščenje podatkov in zanesljivo poročanje, kar je plačljiva ponudba. Večina preverjanj je skriptiranih, zato je vsak pregled poceni za izvedbo.

Pekarna, ki želi naročila pisarn (lokalno podjetje)

Pred. Letak s cenami pogostitev, puščen na recepcijah bližnjih podjetij.

Po. Ob ponedeljkih zjutraj prinesejo brezplačno degustacijsko škatlo za deset ljudi na sestanek ekipe, s kartico: »Naročite redno petkovo dostavo in prvi mesec dobite 15 % ceneje.«

Tip: vzorec, fizična dostava. Letak je od ljudi zahteval, da si izdelek predstavljajo. Škatla da izkušnjo in odpre naslednji problem: kdo bo vsak teden poskrbel za prigrizke? Poziv k dejanju je konkreten in časovno omejen.

Zemljevid procesov za avtomatizacijo (AI svetovanje)

AI svetovalna družba ponuja brezplačno 45-minutno delavnico. Z vodjo oddelka popišejo pet najpogostejših ročnih opravil in ocenijo ure na mesec ter primernost za avtomatizacijo. Stranka odide s tabelo prioritet, ki jo lahko uporabi tudi brez svetovalca. To je prvi korak štiristopenjskega procesa (popis, prototip, produkcija, vzdrževanje). Zaključek: »Prvi proces z vašega seznama postavimo v treh tednih.«

Pogoste napake

  • Preširok problem. »Vse o digitalni preobrazbi« ne reši ničesar. Ozek problem, rešen do konca, premaga širokega, rešenega do polovice.
  • Brez povezave s plačljivo ponudbo. Če naslednji problem ni tisti, ki ga prodajaš, zbiraš hvaležne ljudi, ne strank.
  • Slaba kakovost, ker je zastonj. Povprečen lead magnet sporoča povprečno storitev.
  • Pretežak za uporabo. Dolgi dokumenti, zapleteni obrazci in čakanje pomenijo, da večina nikoli ne pride do vrednosti in ne do naslednjega koraka.
  • Brez poziva k dejanju. Človek je navdušen, nato pa ne ve, kaj naj stori. Vedno daj en naslednji korak in ga olajšaj.

Kako bi to uporabil Alex

  • Dela nazaj od glavne ponudbe. Najprej vpraša, kateri problem rešuje plačljiva ponudba, nato kateri problem pride tik pred njim. Ta prejšnji problem je lead magnet.
  • Dvigne lestvico kakovosti. Njegovo pravilo palca: če ljudje zanj ne bi plačali, ni dovolj dober, da bi ga podaril.
  • Izbere najcenejšo dostavo, ki še navduši. Programska oprema in posneta informacija se skalirata brez stroška, storitev bolje konvertira, a stane čas. Odloči se glede na volumen in maržo.
  • Imena in hooke preizkusi, preden polira. Isti magnet z boljšim imenom lahko pritegne veliko več ljudi, zato jih preizkusi več in obdrži zmagovalca.
  • Lead magnet postavi v vsak kanal. Topla sporočila, objave, hladna e-sporočila in oglasi ponujajo isto brezplačno stvar, zato vsak kanal prinaša zavzete leade, namesto da bi zahteval nakup.
  • Odreže vse, kar upočasni uporabo: dolge uvode, odvečna polja v obrazcih, prenose, ki zahtevajo namestitev.
  • Vprašanja, ki bi jih postavil: Kateri problem rešuje tvoja plačljiva ponudba in kateri problem pride tik pred njim? Bi kdo plačal za tvojo brezplačno stvar? Koliko časa traja, da jo uporabijo? Kaj na koncu ugotovijo, da si želijo več? Kaj natančno jim rečeš, naj naredijo naslednje?

Delovni list

Zasnuj en lead magnet za svojo glavno ponudbo. Odgovori pisno.

  1. Ozek problem: Kateri en boleč problem ima tvoj idealni kupec tik preden potrebuje tvojo plačljivo ponudbo?
  2. Tip: Ga najbolje rešiš z razkritjem problema, vzorcem ali enim korakom procesa? Zakaj?
  3. Dostava: Programska oprema, informacija, storitev ali fizično? Kaj je zanje najlažje in zate dostopno pri velikem številu?
  4. Ime: Napiši tri imena, ki povedo izid in občinstvo. Katero bi preizkusil prvo?
  5. Test kakovosti: Bi kdo zanj plačal? Koliko? Kaj bi dodal, da bi bil odgovor da?
  6. Naslednji korak: Kaj bodo ugotovili po uporabi in kakšen je natančno tvoj poziv k dejanju?
