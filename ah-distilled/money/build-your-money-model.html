Building Your Money ModelSestavi svoj money model

$100M Money Models · Money Model

How to assemble a money model piece by piece, starting from your core offer, so that each new customer pays for their own acquisition within 30 days.Kako money model sestaviš kos za kosom, začenši pri glavni ponudbi, da vsaka nova stranka v 30 dneh sama plača svojo pridobitev.

The gist

A money model is the sequence of offers a customer meets from first contact onwards. You don't design it in one sitting. You start with the thing you already sell well, wrap one offer around it, prove it works, and only then add the next.

Core offer → Attraction (gets them in, covers CAC) → Upsell (at the yes) → Downsell (at the no) → Continuity (keeps them)

Target: 30-day gross profit per customer ≥ 2 × CAC

The yardstick is cash, measured early. What matters is how much gross profit the average new customer produces in the first 30 days compared with what it cost to get them. When that number beats CAC, growth funds itself. When it beats CAC twice, each customer pays for the next two. The aim is a few products sold through many offers. You change how you package and present the products and leave the products themselves mostly alone.

How it works

Step 1. Start from the core offer

Write down what you deliver best and what it costs you to deliver. Every other offer in the model borrows its credibility from this one. If the core offer is weak, fix it first (see $100M Offers). Then measure three numbers you will need for every later decision:

  • CAC: all marketing and sales cost in a period ÷ new customers in that period. Include your own selling time.
  • Gross profit per sale: price minus direct delivery cost.
  • 30-day gross profit today: what an average new customer brings in the first month with your current setup.

Step 2. Add one attraction offer

The attraction offer exists to bring the right customer in at the lowest net cost. Its first job is to cover as much of CAC as possible on day one. It must lead naturally into the core offer: a paid diagnostic before an implementation, a short challenge before a membership. Pick one form and stop there. Common forms include a low-priced entry product, "buy X get Y", a decoy next to a premium option, or a challenge where people earn their money back by hitting a target.

Step 3. Add one upsell at the moment of the yes

The best moment to offer more is right after someone has said yes, because the decision to trust you is already made. A good upsell solves the next problem the first purchase reveals. The audit shows what to fix, so the upsell is fixing it. Make it a natural next step in the same conversation or the same checkout.

Step 4. Add a downsell for the no

Many buyers who say no to the upsell are saying no to the price, size or risk. Give them a smaller step: less scope, a payment plan, or a trial. Take something away or change the terms. Cutting the price of the identical thing teaches people to wait for discounts and punishes those who paid in full.

Step 5. Add continuity for retention

Continuity is anything that bills again: a retainer, a membership, a subscription. It smooths cash flow and raises lifetime value. Offer it when the customer has just felt a result, typically near the end of the first engagement. A bonus for joining, or a waived setup fee, helps people decide.

Step 6. Sequence and timing

Order matters as much as content. Put the offers that collect cash early inside the first 30 days, because that window decides whether you can afford to acquire the next customer. A useful layout:

When Offer Purpose
Day 0 Attraction Get in, cover most of CAC
Day 0 to 14 Upsell, then downsell for the no Push 30-day gross profit past CAC
Day 21 to 30 Continuity Start recurring revenue before the first result fades

Step 7. Test one piece at a time

Change one offer, run it long enough to get a stable take rate, then decide. If you change two things at once you can't tell which one moved the number. Rule of thumb: get the attraction offer working before adding an upsell, and keep adding pieces only while each one raises 30-day gross profit.

Step 8. Keep it simple

More products means more delivery, more training and more ways to fail. Most businesses need only a handful of products. You create new offers by changing price, terms, bundling and timing around the same products.

Examples

Examples are invented for illustration.

Expected gross profit = gross profit per sale × share of new customers who take it. Take rates below are shares of all new customers.

AI automation and BI consultancy (B2B)

Before. One offer: an automation build at €6,000, gross profit €3,600. Cold prospects rarely commit to a large first purchase, so CAC per client is €4,000. 30-day gross profit ÷ CAC = 3,600 ÷ 4,000 = 0.9×. Every new client drains cash.

After. A paid Process Audit becomes the entry point. Far more prospects say yes to it, so CAC per audit client drops to €1,400.

Step Offer Price Gross profit Take rate Expected GP (30 days)
Attraction Process Audit, 2 weeks €1,500 €1,000 100% €1,000
Upsell (at audit readout) First automation build, paid upfront €6,000 €3,600 40% €1,440
Downsell (for the 60% who say no) One quick-win automation €2,500 €1,500 18% (30% of 60%) €270
Continuity (for the 58% who bought a build) Run and improve retainer, billed from signing the build €900/month €600 29% (half of 58%) €174
Total €2,884

30-day gross profit ÷ CAC = 2,884 ÷ 1,400 ≈ 2.06×. The audit alone covers 1,000 ÷ 1,400 ≈ 71% of CAC. The upsell is what pushes the model past break-even. The retainer barely matters in month one but adds €600 of gross profit every month after.

Gym (local business)

Before. Membership at €59/month, gross profit €45. CAC from ads €100. 30-day ratio 45 ÷ 100 = 0.45×. Payback takes 100 ÷ 45 ≈ 2.2 months, assuming nobody leaves.

After. Ads promote a 28-day Kickstart. Membership is offered in week 3, once members see their first results.

Step Offer Price Gross profit Take rate Expected GP (30 days)
Attraction 28-day Kickstart €199 €150 100% €150.00
Upsell (at checkout) Nutrition coaching and meal plan €99 €75 40% €30.00
Downsell (for the 60% who say no) Meal plan only €29 €25 18% (30% of 60%) €4.50
Continuity (offered week 3, first charge day 28) Membership €69/month €52 50% €26.00
Total €210.50

30-day gross profit ÷ CAC = 210.50 ÷ 100 ≈ 2.1×. Each new member now returns enough gross profit within a month to pay for about two more, so the gym can spend more on ads without waiting months for the money to come back.

Both models work for the same reason: the first purchase is easy to say yes to, the next offer arrives when trust is highest, and the recurring revenue starts before the first result fades.

Common mistakes

  • Building everything at once. Four new offers launched together give you no idea which one works. Add and test them one by one.
  • Measuring revenue instead of gross profit. Only gross profit can pay for the next customer. A high-revenue, low-margin offer can look great and still drain cash.
  • Missing the 30-day window. An upsell sold in month four helps lifetime value but does nothing for funding acquisition today.
  • A downsell that is just a discount. It trains buyers to say no first. Remove scope or change terms instead.
  • Too many products. Every new product adds delivery complexity. New offers around existing products are cheaper to test and easier to deliver.

How Alex would apply it

  • He starts with the 30-day number. Before discussing any offer, he wants CAC and 30-day gross profit per customer. Everything else is judged by whether it moves that ratio.
  • He fixes the biggest gap first. If the first sale covers little of CAC, he works on the attraction offer. If the first sale is fine but nothing follows, he adds an upsell. He builds in that order and adds one piece at a time.
  • He puts the upsell where the yes is. He would move the next offer into the same call, the same checkout or the same readout meeting where the customer just agreed.
  • He would rather collect cash upfront. Upfront payments, setup fees and first months bundled into the entry offer pull gross profit into the first 30 days.
  • He cuts products and adds offers. When a business has many products and weak economics, he would trim the product list and package the best few in different ways.
  • He treats the 2× CAC target as the point where growth stops being capped by cash (a rule of thumb). Below 1×, he would hold back on scaling ads until the model is fixed.
  • Questions he would ask: What does a customer cost you, all in? How much gross profit do they bring in the first 30 days? What do people buy right after saying yes? What do the ones who say no actually object to? What keeps a customer paying after the first result? Which one offer would you test next?

Worksheet

Fill this in for your own business, with numbers.

  1. Baseline: What are your CAC and 30-day gross profit per customer today? What is the ratio?
  2. Attraction: Which easy first purchase leads naturally into your core offer, and how much of CAC could it cover?
  3. Upsell: What is the next problem your customer faces right after buying? What would you offer at that moment, and at what price?
  4. Downsell: When people say no, is it price, scope or risk? What smaller step removes that objection?
  5. Continuity: What value could recur every month? When would you offer it, and with what bonus?
  6. Table: Build the 30-day table with honest take rates. Which single offer, added next, would move the ratio most?

Bistvo

Money model je zaporedje ponudb, ki jih stranka sreča od prvega stika naprej. Ne načrtuješ ga v enem kosu. Začneš s tem, kar že dobro prodajaš, okoli tega oviješ eno ponudbo, dokažeš, da deluje, in šele nato dodaš naslednjo.

Glavna ponudba → privabljalna (pripelje stranko, pokrije CAC) → upsell (ob »da«) → downsell (ob »ne«) → naročnina (stranko obdrži)

Cilj: 30-dnevni bruto dobiček na stranko ≥ 2 × CAC

Merilo je gotovina, izmerjena zgodaj. Šteje, koliko bruto dobička povprečna nova stranka ustvari v prvih 30 dneh v primerjavi s tem, koliko je stala njena pridobitev. Ko ta številka preseže CAC, se rast financira sama. Ko ga preseže dvakrat, vsaka stranka plača naslednji dve. Cilj je nekaj izdelkov, prodanih skozi veliko ponudb. Spreminjaš pakiranje in predstavitev, izdelke same pa večinoma pustiš pri miru.

Kako deluje

Korak 1. Začni pri glavni ponudbi

Zapiši, kaj dostaviš najbolje in koliko te dostava stane. Vse druge ponudbe v modelu si od te izposojajo verodostojnost. Če je glavna ponudba šibka, jo najprej popravi (glej $100M Offers). Nato izmeri tri številke, ki jih boš potreboval pri vsaki nadaljnji odločitvi:

  • CAC: vsi stroški marketinga in prodaje v obdobju ÷ nove stranke v istem obdobju. Vštej tudi svoj čas za prodajo.
  • Bruto dobiček na prodajo: cena minus neposredni stroški izvedbe.
  • Današnji 30-dnevni bruto dobiček: koliko povprečna nova stranka prinese v prvem mesecu z obstoječo postavitvijo.

Korak 2. Dodaj eno privabljalno ponudbo (attraction offer)

Privabljalna ponudba obstaja zato, da pripelje pravo stranko ob najnižjem neto strošku. Njena prva naloga je, da že prvi dan pokrije čim več CAC. Naravno mora voditi v glavno ponudbo: plačana diagnostika pred implementacijo, kratek izziv pred članarino. Izberi eno obliko in se tam ustavi. Pogoste oblike so poceni vstopni izdelek, »kupi X, dobi Y«, vaba (decoy) ob dražji možnosti ali izziv, pri katerem ljudje denar dobijo nazaj, če dosežejo cilj.

Korak 3. Dodaj en upsell v trenutku »da«

Najboljši trenutek za ponudbo več je takoj po tem, ko je nekdo rekel da, ker je odločitev, da ti zaupa, že sprejeta. Dober upsell reši naslednji problem, ki ga razkrije prvi nakup. Revizija pokaže, kaj popraviti, zato je upsell popravilo. Naj bo naraven naslednji korak v istem pogovoru ali na isti blagajni.

Korak 4. Dodaj downsell za »ne«

Veliko kupcev, ki upsellu rečejo ne, v resnici zavrne ceno, obseg ali tveganje. Ponudi jim manjši korak: manj obsega, plačilo na obroke ali preizkus. Nekaj odvzemi ali spremeni pogoje. Znižanje cene iste stvari ljudi nauči čakati na popuste in kaznuje tiste, ki so plačali polno ceno.

Korak 5. Dodaj naročnino (continuity) za zadržanje

Naročnina je vse, kar se znova zaračuna: mesečni pavšal (retainer), članarina, naročnina na storitev. Zgladi denarni tok in dvigne življenjsko vrednost. Ponudi jo, ko je stranka ravno občutila rezultat, navadno proti koncu prvega sodelovanja. Bonus za prijavo ali odpis nastavitvene pristojbine pomaga pri odločitvi.

Korak 6. Zaporedje in časovnica

Vrstni red je enako pomemben kot vsebina. Ponudbe, ki zgodaj prinesejo gotovino, postavi v prvih 30 dni, ker to okno odloča, ali si lahko privoščiš pridobitev naslednje stranke. Uporabna razporeditev:

Kdaj Ponudba Namen
Dan 0 Privabljalna Pridobitev, pokritje večine CAC
Dan 0 do 14 Upsell, nato downsell za »ne« 30-dnevni bruto dobiček čez CAC
Dan 21 do 30 Naročnina Ponavljajoči prihodek, preden prvi rezultat zbledi

Korak 7. Testiraj po en kos naenkrat

Spremeni eno ponudbo, jo poganjaj dovolj dolgo, da dobiš stabilen delež sprejetja, nato se odloči. Če hkrati spremeniš dve stvari, ne veš, katera je premaknila številko. Pravilo palca: najprej usposobi privabljalno ponudbo, šele nato dodaj upsell, in kose dodajaj le, dokler vsak dvigne 30-dnevni bruto dobiček.

Korak 8. Ostani preprost

Več izdelkov pomeni več izvedbe, več uvajanja in več načinov, da gre kaj narobe. Večina podjetij potrebuje le peščico izdelkov. Nove ponudbe ustvarjaš s spreminjanjem cene, pogojev, paketov in časa okoli istih izdelkov.

Primeri

Primeri so izmišljeni in služijo ponazoritvi.

Pričakovani bruto dobiček = bruto dobiček na prodajo × delež novih strank, ki ponudbo sprejmejo. Deleži spodaj so deleži vseh novih strank.

Svetovanje za AI avtomatizacijo in BI (B2B)

Pred. Ena ponudba: izdelava avtomatizacije za 6.000 €, bruto dobiček 3.600 €. Hladni potencialni kupci se redko odločijo za velik prvi nakup, zato je CAC na stranko 4.000 €. 30-dnevni bruto dobiček ÷ CAC = 3.600 ÷ 4.000 = 0,9×. Vsaka nova stranka črpa gotovino.

Po. Vstopna točka postane plačana revizija procesov. Zanjo se odloči bistveno več potencialnih kupcev, zato CAC na stranko z revizijo pade na 1.400 €.

Korak Ponudba Cena Bruto dobiček Delež Pričakovani BD (30 dni)
Privabljalna Revizija procesov, 2 tedna 1.500 € 1.000 € 100 % 1.000 €
Upsell (ob predstavitvi revizije) Prva avtomatizacija, plačilo vnaprej 6.000 € 3.600 € 40 % 1.440 €
Downsell (za 60 %, ki rečejo ne) Ena hitra avtomatizacija 2.500 € 1.500 € 18 % (30 % od 60 %) 270 €
Naročnina (za 58 %, ki so kupili izdelavo) Pavšal za delovanje in izboljšave, obračun od podpisa izdelave 900 €/mesec 600 € 29 % (polovica od 58 %) 174 €
Skupaj 2.884 €

30-dnevni bruto dobiček ÷ CAC = 2.884 ÷ 1.400 ≈ 2,06×. Sama revizija pokrije 1.000 ÷ 1.400 ≈ 71 % CAC. Upsell je tisti, ki model potisne čez prag preloma. Pavšal v prvem mesecu skoraj ne šteje, vsak naslednji mesec pa doda 600 € bruto dobička.

Fitnes (lokalno podjetje)

Pred. Članarina 59 € na mesec, bruto dobiček 45 €. CAC iz oglasov 100 €. 30-dnevno razmerje 45 ÷ 100 = 0,45×. Povrnitev traja 100 ÷ 45 ≈ 2,2 meseca, ob predpostavki, da nihče ne odide.

Po. Oglasi promovirajo 28-dnevni Kickstart. Članarino ponudijo v 3. tednu, ko člani vidijo prve rezultate.

Korak Ponudba Cena Bruto dobiček Delež Pričakovani BD (30 dni)
Privabljalna 28-dnevni Kickstart 199 € 150 € 100 % 150,00 €
Upsell (na blagajni) Prehransko svetovanje in jedilnik 99 € 75 € 40 % 30,00 €
Downsell (za 60 %, ki rečejo ne) Samo jedilnik 29 € 25 € 18 % (30 % od 60 %) 4,50 €
Naročnina (ponujena v 3. tednu, prva bremenitev 28. dan) Članarina 69 €/mesec 52 € 50 % 26,00 €
Skupaj 210,50 €

30-dnevni bruto dobiček ÷ CAC = 210,50 ÷ 100 ≈ 2,1×. Vsak nov član zdaj v enem mesecu prinese dovolj bruto dobička za pridobitev približno dveh novih, zato lahko fitnes za oglase porabi več, ne da bi mesece čakal, da se denar vrne.

Oba modela delujeta iz istega razloga: prvemu nakupu je lahko reči da, naslednja ponudba pride, ko je zaupanje najvišje, ponavljajoči prihodek pa se začne, preden prvi rezultat zbledi.

Pogoste napake

  • Vse naenkrat. Štiri nove ponudbe, uvedene skupaj, ti ne povedo, katera deluje. Dodajaj in testiraj jih eno za drugo.
  • Merjenje prihodka namesto bruto dobička. Le bruto dobiček lahko plača naslednjo stranko. Ponudba z visokim prihodkom in nizko maržo je lahko videti odlično in vseeno črpa gotovino.
  • Zamujeno 30-dnevno okno. Upsell, prodan v četrtem mesecu, pomaga življenjski vrednosti, za financiranje današnje pridobitve pa ne naredi nič.
  • Downsell, ki je le popust. Kupce nauči, da najprej rečejo ne. Raje odvzemi obseg ali spremeni pogoje.
  • Preveč izdelkov. Vsak nov izdelek doda zapletenost izvedbe. Nove ponudbe okoli obstoječih izdelkov je ceneje testirati in lažje izvesti.

Kako bi to uporabil Alex

  • Začne pri 30-dnevni številki. Preden se pogovarja o katerikoli ponudbi, hoče CAC in 30-dnevni bruto dobiček na stranko. Vse drugo presoja po tem, ali premakne to razmerje.
  • Najprej zapre največjo vrzel. Če prva prodaja pokrije malo CAC, dela na privabljalni ponudbi. Če je prva prodaja v redu, a ji nič ne sledi, doda upsell. Gradi v tem vrstnem redu in dodaja po en kos.
  • Upsell postavi tja, kjer je »da«. Naslednjo ponudbo bi premaknil v isti klic, na isto blagajno ali na isti sestanek, kjer je stranka ravno privolila.
  • Raje pobere gotovino vnaprej. Plačila vnaprej, nastavitvene pristojbine in prvi meseci, vključeni v vstopno ponudbo, potegnejo bruto dobiček v prvih 30 dni.
  • Krajša seznam izdelkov in dodaja ponudbe. Ko ima podjetje veliko izdelkov in šibko ekonomiko, bi seznam izdelkov skrčil in najboljših nekaj zapakiral na različne načine.
  • Cilj 2 × CAC razume kot točko, kjer rasti ne omejuje več gotovina (pravilo palca). Pod 1× bi z večanjem oglaševanja počakal, dokler model ni popravljen.
  • Vprašanja, ki bi jih postavil: Koliko te stranka stane, vse skupaj? Koliko bruto dobička prinese v prvih 30 dneh? Kaj ljudje kupijo takoj po tem, ko rečejo da? Česa se v resnici branijo tisti, ki rečejo ne? Kaj stranko drži, da plačuje tudi po prvem rezultatu? Katero eno ponudbo bi testiral naslednjo?

Delovni list

Izpolni za svoje podjetje, s številkami.

  1. Izhodišče: Koliko sta danes tvoj CAC in 30-dnevni bruto dobiček na stranko? Kakšno je razmerje?
  2. Privabljalna: Kateri lahek prvi nakup naravno vodi v tvojo glavno ponudbo in koliko CAC bi lahko pokril?
  3. Upsell: Kateri je naslednji problem, s katerim se stranka sreča takoj po nakupu? Kaj bi ji ponudil v tistem trenutku in po kakšni ceni?
  4. Downsell: Ko ljudje rečejo ne, gre za ceno, obseg ali tveganje? Kateri manjši korak odstrani ta ugovor?
  5. Naročnina: Katera vrednost bi se lahko ponavljala vsak mesec? Kdaj bi jo ponudil in s kakšnim bonusom?
  6. Tabela: Sestavi 30-dnevno tabelo s poštenimi deleži sprejetja. Katera ena ponudba, dodana kot naslednja, bi razmerje premaknila najbolj?
