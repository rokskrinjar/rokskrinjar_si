$100M Money Models · Client-Financed Acquisition

When what a new customer pays in the first 30 days covers the cost to acquire and serve them, growth funds itself. At 2×, every customer pays for two more.Ko to, kar nova stranka plača v prvih 30 dneh, pokrije strošek njene pridobitve in izvedbe, se rast financira sama. Pri 2× vsaka stranka plača še dve.

The gist

Every business pays to get a customer before it earns from them. If the money comes back slowly, growth is limited by the cash in the bank, and growing faster means borrowing or raising money. Client-financed acquisition removes that limit. The customer's own payments in the first 30 days cover what it cost to acquire them and to deliver what they bought.

30-day gross profit per customer ≥ CAC, ideally ≥ 2 × CAC

At 1× acquisition pays for itself. At 2× each new customer funds the acquisition of two more. From there the only limits on growth are how many customers you can reach and how many you can serve. The money model is the tool for getting there: it is designed to pull gross profit into the first 30 days.

How it works

The numbers you need

Metric What it is How to calculate
CAC (customer acquisition cost) What it costs to win one new customer All marketing and sales costs in a period ÷ new customers in that period
Gross profit What's left after the direct cost of delivering Revenue − cost of delivery
30-day gross profit Gross profit a new customer generates in their first 30 days Sum of gross profit from all offers bought in the first 30 days
LTGP (lifetime gross profit) Gross profit a customer generates over the whole relationship One-off gross profit + monthly gross profit × average months retained
LTGP:CAC How much a customer is worth relative to what they cost LTGP ÷ CAC
Payback period How long until a customer's gross profit covers their CAC Time until cumulative gross profit ≥ CAC

Count CAC completely

Include everything you spend to win customers: ads, tools, sales salaries or your own selling time, commissions, events. Counting ads alone makes CAC look low and gives a false sense of safety. If you sell yourself, put a value on your hours, or CAC will jump the day you hire a salesperson.

Use gross profit, not revenue

Only gross profit can pay for acquisition. Revenue that goes straight back out to deliver the service funds nothing. €1,000 of revenue at an 80% margin leaves €800 to reinvest. At a 20% margin it leaves €200. This is why all of these metrics are built on gross profit.

LTGP:CAC tells you if a customer is worth it

A common rule of thumb is that LTGP should be at least three times CAC. Below 1:1 every new customer loses money. Around 3:1 there is room for overhead and profit. In a young business the lifetime is still a guess, so estimate it conservatively. For subscriptions a practical estimate is average lifetime ≈ 1 ÷ monthly churn: at 5% churn a month, a customer stays about 20 months.

Payback tells you if you can afford to grow

A good ratio is not enough on its own. A customer can be worth 5× CAC over two years and still sink you if you run out of cash in month three. The faster the payback, the faster you can reinvest. Client-financed acquisition is the case where payback happens within 30 days.

Why 30 days and why 2×

Thirty days lines up with most payment cycles: ads, salaries and card bills come due monthly. If a customer has paid back their CAC before those bills arrive, you never need outside money to grow. At 2× the extra cash funds the next customers. In a simplified view that ignores overhead, a €1,000 ad budget that brings €2,000 of gross profit in 30 days becomes a €2,000 budget next month, then €4,000.

How to get there

You pull gross profit forward with the money model: a paid attraction offer, an upsell right after the first yes, a setup fee, prepayment for a longer term, and continuity that starts in the first month. Raising the price works too, because almost all of a price increase becomes gross profit.

Examples

Examples are invented for illustration.

Managed BI subscription (B2B SaaS / analytics)

A company sells a managed Qlik reporting subscription. In a quarter it spends €10,000 on marketing and sales and wins 5 customers. The subscription is €800 a month with €200 of direct cost. It then adds a paid implementation (€4,000, cost €600) and better onboarding that halves churn. Acquisition spend and customer count stay the same.

Metric Before After
CAC (10,000 ÷ 5) €2,000 €2,000
Monthly gross profit (800 − 200) €600 €600
Implementation gross profit (4,000 − 600) €0 €3,400
30-day gross profit €600 €4,000
30-day gross profit ÷ CAC 0.3× 2.0×
Monthly churn 10% 5%
Average lifetime 10 months 20 months
LTGP €6,000 €15,400
LTGP:CAC 3.0 : 1 7.7 : 1
Payback ≈ 3.3 months within month 1

Arithmetic: LTGP before is 600 × 10 = €6,000. After it is 3,400 + 600 × 20 = €15,400, and 15,400 ÷ 2,000 = 7.7. Payback before is 2,000 ÷ 600 ≈ 3.3 months. Before, the business looked healthy on paper at 3:1 but waited over three months to get its money back on every customer. After, each customer pays for two more within a month.

Home cleaning service (local business)

Offer in the first 30 days Gross profit per buyer Take rate Expected per customer
Before: two regular cleans at €100 (cost €60 each) €80 100% €80
After, attraction: first deep clean €290 (cost €110) €180 100% €180
After, continuity: two regular cleans in month one €80 100% €80
After, upsell: window and oven package €180 (cost €60) €120 50% €60

CAC from local ads is €160. Before, 30-day gross profit is €80, half of CAC (0.5×), so every new customer drains cash for two months. After, it is 180 + 80 + 60 = €320, exactly 2× CAC. The service didn't get cheaper to run. The offers changed what the customer pays in the first month.

Common mistakes

  • Calculating with revenue. Lifetime value on revenue inflates the ratio and hides weak margins. Always use gross profit.
  • Incomplete CAC. Leaving out sales time, tools and commissions makes acquisition look cheap until you try to scale it.
  • A good ratio with slow payback. 5:1 over two years doesn't help if cash runs out in three months.
  • Optimistic lifetime. In a young business retention is a guess. Use a shorter lifetime until the data says otherwise.
  • Blended averages. One overall CAC can hide a channel that loses money. Calculate by channel and by customer type.

How Alex would apply it

  • He looks at 30-day gross profit before the ad budget. When someone says ads are too expensive, he usually concludes the customer is worth too little too late, and fixes that first.
  • He targets 2× in 30 days and treats anything below 1× as a business that can only grow with outside money.
  • He pulls cash forward: setup fees, prepayment, paid attraction offers and upsells in the first visit or call.
  • He raises prices early, because a price increase lands almost entirely in gross profit and improves every ratio at once.
  • He uses LTGP:CAC as a health check and payback as the growth limit. A strong lifetime ratio with slow payback still needs funding.
  • He computes everything on gross profit and full CAC, and is conservative with lifetime estimates until there's real data.
  • Questions he would ask: What does it cost you to get one customer, including your own time? What's your gross margin? How much gross profit do you collect in the first 30 days? How long until a customer pays back their CAC? What could they buy in the first month that they buy later today? What would happen to your numbers if you raised prices 20%?

Worksheet

Use the last three months of data. Where you lack a number, write a conservative estimate and mark it as one.

  1. CAC: What did you spend in total on winning customers, including your own time, and how many new customers did you get?
  2. Gross profit: What does it cost to deliver to one customer? What is your gross margin per offer?
  3. 30-day gross profit: How much gross profit does a new customer bring in the first 30 days? Divide it by CAC.
  4. LTGP: How long does a customer stay and what do they buy in total? Compute LTGP and LTGP:CAC.
  5. Lever: Which one change (price, setup fee, prepayment, first-month upsell, lower delivery cost) would move 30-day gross profit closest to 2× CAC? What will you do this month?

Bistvo

Vsako podjetje za stranko plača, preden z njo zasluži. Če se denar vrača počasi, je rast omejena z denarjem na računu, hitrejša rast pa pomeni posojilo ali zunanji kapital. Pridobivanje, ki ga financira stranka (client-financed acquisition), to omejitev odpravi. Plačila stranke v prvih 30 dneh pokrijejo strošek njene pridobitve in izvedbe tega, kar je kupila.

30-dnevni bruto dobiček na stranko ≥ CAC, idealno ≥ 2 × CAC

Pri 1× se pridobitev plača sama. Pri 2× vsaka nova stranka financira pridobitev še dveh. Od tam naprej rast omejujeta le to, koliko strank lahko dosežeš, in to, koliko jih lahko oskrbiš. Orodje, ki te tja pripelje, je money model: zasnovan je tako, da bruto dobiček potegne v prvih 30 dni.

Kako deluje

Številke, ki jih potrebuješ

Kazalnik Kaj je Kako izračunaš

Bruto dobiček (gross profit) Kar ostane po neposrednih stroških izvedbe Prihodek − strošek izvedbe
30-dnevni bruto dobiček Bruto dobiček nove stranke v prvih 30 dneh Vsota bruto dobička vseh ponudb, kupljenih v prvih 30 dneh
LTGP (življenjski bruto dobiček) Bruto dobiček stranke v celotnem sodelovanju Enkratni bruto dobiček + mesečni bruto dobiček × povprečno število mesecev
LTGP:CAC Koliko je stranka vredna glede na to, koliko stane LTGP ÷ CAC
Doba povrnitve (payback) Koliko časa traja, da bruto dobiček stranke pokrije njen CAC Čas, ko kumulativni bruto dobiček doseže CAC

CAC štej v celoti

Vključi vse, kar porabiš za pridobivanje strank: oglase, orodja, plače prodajalcev ali svoj prodajni čas, provizije, dogodke. Če šteješ samo oglase, je CAC videti nizek in daje lažen občutek varnosti. Če prodajaš sam, ovrednoti svoje ure, sicer bo CAC poskočil tisti dan, ko zaposliš prodajalca.

Računaj z bruto dobičkom, ne s prihodkom

Pridobitev lahko plača samo bruto dobiček. Prihodek, ki gre takoj naprej v izvedbo storitve, ne financira ničesar. 1.000 € prihodka pri 80 % marži pusti 800 € za ponovno vlaganje. Pri 20 % marži jih pusti 200 €. Zato so vsi ti kazalniki zgrajeni na bruto dobičku.

LTGP:CAC pove, ali se stranka splača

Pogosto pravilo palca je, da naj bo LTGP vsaj trikrat večji od CAC. Pod 1:1 vsaka nova stranka izgublja denar. Okoli 3:1 ostane prostor za režijo in dobiček. Pri mladem podjetju je življenjska doba še domneva, zato jo oceni previdno. Za naročnine je praktična ocena povprečna življenjska doba ≈ 1 ÷ mesečni delež odhodov (churn): pri 5 % odhodov na mesec stranka ostane približno 20 mesecev.

Doba povrnitve pove, ali si rast lahko privoščiš

Dobro razmerje samo po sebi ni dovolj. Stranka je lahko v dveh letih vredna 5× CAC, pa te vseeno potopi, če ti denar zmanjka v tretjem mesecu. Hitrejša kot je povrnitev, hitreje lahko denar vložiš nazaj. Pridobivanje, ki ga financira stranka, je primer, ko se povrnitev zgodi v 30 dneh.

Zakaj 30 dni in zakaj 2×

Trideset dni se ujema z večino plačilnih ciklov: oglasi, plače in računi kartic zapadejo mesečno. Če je stranka povrnila svoj CAC, preden ti računi pridejo, za rast nikoli ne potrebuješ zunanjega denarja. Pri 2× dodatni denar financira naslednje stranke. Poenostavljeno, brez upoštevanja režije: 1.000 € proračuna za oglase, ki v 30 dneh prinese 2.000 € bruto dobička, postane naslednji mesec 2.000 € proračuna, nato 4.000 €.

Kako prideš do tja

Bruto dobiček potegneš naprej z money modelom: plačana privabljalna ponudba, upsell takoj po prvem »da«, pristojbina za nastavitev, predplačilo za daljše obdobje in naročnina, ki se začne že v prvem mesecu. Deluje tudi dvig cene, ker skoraj ves dvig postane bruto dobiček.

Primeri

Primeri so izmišljeni in služijo ponazoritvi.

Upravljana BI naročnina (B2B SaaS / analitika)

Podjetje prodaja upravljano naročnino na Qlik poročila. V četrtletju porabi 10.000 € za marketing in prodajo ter pridobi 5 strank. Naročnina je 800 € na mesec z 200 € neposrednih stroškov. Nato doda plačano implementacijo (4.000 €, strošek 600 €) in boljši onboarding, ki prepolovi odhode. Stroški pridobivanja in število strank ostanejo enaki.

Kazalnik Pred Po
CAC (10.000 ÷ 5) 2.000 € 2.000 €
Mesečni bruto dobiček (800 − 200) 600 € 600 €
Bruto dobiček implementacije (4.000 − 600) 0 € 3.400 €
30-dnevni bruto dobiček 600 € 4.000 €
30-dnevni bruto dobiček ÷ CAC 0,3× 2,0×
Mesečni odhodi 10 % 5 %
Povprečna življenjska doba 10 mesecev 20 mesecev
LTGP 6.000 € 15.400 €
LTGP:CAC 3,0 : 1 7,7 : 1
Doba povrnitve ≈ 3,3 meseca v 1. mesecu

Računi: LTGP prej je 600 × 10 = 6.000 €. Potem je 3.400 + 600 × 20 = 15.400 €, in 15.400 ÷ 2.000 = 7,7. Doba povrnitve prej je 2.000 ÷ 600 ≈ 3,3 meseca. Prej je bilo podjetje na papirju zdravo pri 3:1, a je na denar za vsako stranko čakalo več kot tri mesece. Potem vsaka stranka v enem mesecu plača še dve.

Storitev čiščenja domov (lokalno podjetje)

Ponudba v prvih 30 dneh Bruto dobiček na kupca Delež Pričakovano na stranko
Pred: dve redni čiščenji po 100 € (strošek 60 € vsako) 80 € 100 % 80 €
Po, privabljalna: prvo globinsko čiščenje 290 € (strošek 110 €) 180 € 100 % 180 €
Po, naročnina: dve redni čiščenji v prvem mesecu 80 € 100 % 80 €
Po, upsell: paket oken in pečice 180 € (strošek 60 €) 120 € 50 % 60 €

CAC iz lokalnih oglasov je 160 €. Prej je 30-dnevni bruto dobiček 80 €, polovica CAC (0,5×), zato vsaka nova stranka dva meseca črpa denar. Potem je 180 + 80 + 60 = 320 €, natanko 2× CAC. Storitev ni postala cenejša za izvedbo. Ponudbe so spremenile, koliko stranka plača v prvem mesecu.

Pogoste napake

  • Računanje s prihodkom. Življenjska vrednost na prihodku napihne razmerje in skrije šibko maržo. Vedno računaj z bruto dobičkom.
  • Nepopoln CAC. Če izpustiš prodajni čas, orodja in provizije, je pridobivanje videti poceni, dokler ga ne poskusiš skalirati.
  • Dobro razmerje s počasno povrnitvijo. 5:1 v dveh letih ne pomaga, če ti denar zmanjka v treh mesecih.
  • Optimistična življenjska doba. Pri mladem podjetju je zadržanje strank domneva. Računaj s krajšo dobo, dokler podatki ne pokažejo drugače.
  • Mešana povprečja. En skupni CAC lahko skrije kanal, ki izgublja denar. Računaj po kanalih in po tipu stranke.

Kako bi to uporabil Alex

  • 30-dnevni bruto dobiček pogleda pred proračunom za oglase. Ko nekdo reče, da so oglasi predragi, navadno ugotovi, da je stranka vredna premalo in prepozno, in to popravi najprej.
  • Cilja na 2× v 30 dneh, vse pod 1× pa obravnava kot podjetje, ki lahko raste samo z zunanjim denarjem.
  • Denar potegne naprej: pristojbine za nastavitev, predplačila, plačane privabljalne ponudbe in upselli že ob prvem obisku ali klicu.
  • Cene dvigne zgodaj, ker dvig cene skoraj v celoti pristane v bruto dobičku in izboljša vsa razmerja hkrati.
  • LTGP:CAC uporablja kot pregled zdravja, dobo povrnitve pa kot mejo rasti. Močno življenjsko razmerje s počasno povrnitvijo še vedno potrebuje financiranje.
  • Vse računa na bruto dobičku in polnem CAC, pri ocenah življenjske dobe pa je previden, dokler nima pravih podatkov.
  • Vprašanja, ki bi jih postavil: Koliko te stane ena stranka, vključno s tvojim časom? Kakšna je tvoja bruto marža? Koliko bruto dobička pobereš v prvih 30 dneh? Koliko časa traja, da stranka povrne svoj CAC? Kaj bi stranka lahko kupila v prvem mesecu, kar danes kupi pozneje? Kaj bi se zgodilo s številkami, če bi dvignil cene za 20 %?

Delovni list

Uporabi podatke zadnjih treh mesecev. Kjer številke nimaš, zapiši previdno oceno in jo označi kot oceno.

  1. CAC: Koliko si skupaj porabil za pridobivanje strank, vključno s svojim časom, in koliko novih strank si dobil?
  2. Bruto dobiček: Koliko stane izvedba za eno stranko? Kakšna je tvoja bruto marža po ponudbah?
  3. 30-dnevni bruto dobiček: Koliko bruto dobička prinese nova stranka v prvih 30 dneh? Deli ga s CAC.
  4. LTGP: Kako dolgo stranka ostane in koliko vsega kupi? Izračunaj LTGP in LTGP:CAC.
  5. Vzvod: Katera ena sprememba (cena, pristojbina za nastavitev, predplačilo, upsell v prvem mesecu, nižji strošek izvedbe) bi 30-dnevni bruto dobiček najbolj približala 2× CAC? Kaj boš naredil ta mesec?
