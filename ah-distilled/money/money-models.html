Money ModelsMoney modeli

$100M Money Models · Money Model

A deliberate sequence of offers designed to make more money from each customer, faster. The business that can afford to spend the most to acquire a customer wins.Načrtno zaporedje ponudb, ki iz vsake stranke hitreje ustvari več denarja. Zmaga podjetje, ki si lahko za pridobitev stranke privošči porabiti največ.

The gist

Most businesses have one offer and hope the customer comes back. A money model is a planned sequence of offers: what you sell first, what you offer right after a yes, what you offer after a no, and what keeps paying every month. Its job is to make more money from each customer, and to make it sooner.

Attraction (get them in) → Upsell (more, right after yes) → Downsell (save the sale after no) → Continuity (recurring)

This matters because acquisition is where businesses compete. If each customer is worth more to you in the first weeks, you can spend more to get them than your competitors can. A small, simple business with a strong money model can outspend a much bigger one on ads and sales, and still grow on its own cash.

How it works

Attraction offers: get the right customers in

What. The first thing a new customer buys. Its job is to turn strangers into customers at a low, zero or even negative net cost. Common forms include a free or cheap entry service, a "buy one, get one" offer, a cheaper option placed next to a premium one, or a challenge where the customer earns money back for hitting a result.

Why. It lowers the barrier to the first yes. Once someone has bought, the next purchase is far easier.

How. Tie it closely to your core product, so continuing feels like the natural next step. An entry offer that attracts bargain hunters fills the funnel with people who never buy anything else.

Upsell offers: sell more right after a yes

What. An offer made immediately after the customer buys. It solves the next problem the first purchase reveals, or gives them more, better or faster.

Why. Right after a purchase, trust is highest and the decision to spend has already been made. A week later the energy is gone.

How. Use the logic of "you can't have X without Y." You bought the analysis, now you need it implemented. You bought the training plan, now you need the nutrition plan. Offer one clear next step, not a menu of ten.

Downsell offers: save the sale after a no

What. When a customer says no, offer a smaller, cheaper or differently paid version.

Why. A no is often a no to the price, the scope or the risk, not to the outcome. Without a downsell you lose everyone whose problem was the terms.

How. Change something real: less scope, fewer features, a shorter trial, a payment plan. Avoid simply cutting the price of the same thing, because that teaches buyers to say no and punishes those who paid in full.

Continuity offers: make it recur

What. Anything the customer pays for repeatedly: membership, subscription, maintenance, retainer, ongoing support.

Why. It turns one sale into a stream of gross profit and makes revenue predictable. It also gives you a reason to keep delivering value.

How. Build it around value that genuinely recurs: new data each month, regular sessions, monthly reviews, upkeep. A bonus for signing up, a discount for committing longer, or a waived setup fee helps people join.

Simple scales

A money model is many offers around the same core product, not many products. The gym sells the same training as a challenge, a membership and a coaching add-on. The consultant sells the same expertise as a workshop, a project and a retainer. Each new product adds delivery complexity. Each new offer around the core adds money with little extra work. Build the model in order: get the attraction offer working first, then add one piece at a time.

Examples

Examples are invented for illustration.

Gym (local business)

Before, the gym sold only a €99 monthly membership with €70 gross profit. Ads cost €150 per new member, so a new member returned about half their acquisition cost in the first month (70 ÷ 150 ≈ 0.47).

After, a sequence of offers within the first 30 days. Expected gross profit = gross profit per buyer × share of customers who take it.

Offer Gross profit per buyer Take rate Expected per customer
Attraction: 4-week challenge, €300 €240 100% €240
Upsell: nutrition coaching €150 30% €45
Downsell: meal plan guide (half of the 70% who decline) €40 35% €14
Continuity: membership, first month €70 50% €35
30-day gross profit per customer €334
Acquisition cost (CAC) €150

Each new customer now brings in about 2.2 times what it cost to acquire them (334 ÷ 150 ≈ 2.2) within a month. The gym can afford to pay more for ads than the gym down the street and still have cash left over.

Qlik analytics consultancy (B2B)

Before. One offer: a three-month dashboard project. Sales calls end in yes or no. Those who say no are gone. Those who say yes leave when the project ends.

After. Attraction: a paid two-day data review, credited against the project if they sign within 30 days. Upsell: the first dashboard built, offered at the end of the review while the findings are on the screen. Downsell: a single-department pilot or payment in three parts. Continuity: a monthly retainer for data model upkeep, new reports and support.

The review pays for much of the sales effort, so a new client no longer depends on one big decision. A no to the project turns into a pilot instead of a lost lead. The retainer turns a one-off project into income that continues after delivery.

Common mistakes

  • One offer and hope. Selling a single thing and waiting for repeat business leaves most of each customer's value on the table.
  • Attraction offers that attract the wrong people. If the entry offer isn't connected to the core product, you get freebie seekers who never buy again.
  • Upselling at the wrong time. An upsell sent a week later competes with everything else in their inbox. Make it while the yes is fresh.
  • Treating no as the end. Without a downsell, every objection about price, scope or risk becomes a lost customer.
  • More products instead of more offers. Adding new products to increase revenue multiplies delivery work. Package the core product in new ways first.

How Alex would apply it

  • He looks at the money model before the marketing. When growth stalls because ads seem too expensive, he first asks how much each customer is worth in the first 30 days. Often the fix is the sequence of offers.
  • He builds it one offer at a time, starting with the attraction offer. Once that reliably brings in the right customers, he adds the upsell, then the downsell, then continuity.
  • He wants every customer to hear the next offer. An upsell after every yes and a downsell after every no is a process, not a matter of mood.
  • He keeps it simple. Many offers around one core product beat a catalogue. He would cut products before he would cut offers.
  • He measures it with 30-day gross profit against CAC, and aims for the first month to pay for acquiring that customer and ideally two more.
  • He prefers models that pay up front. Setup fees, prepayments and first-month upsells bring cash in when the business needs it to acquire the next customer.
  • Questions he would ask: What's your entry offer, and does it lead naturally to your core product? What do you offer right after someone says yes? What happens to the people who say no? What do customers pay for every month? How much gross profit does a new customer bring in the first 30 days, compared with what they cost you?

Worksheet

Draw your current sequence of offers, then improve it. For each offer, estimate gross profit per buyer and the share of customers who take it.

  1. Core: What is your one core product or service that all offers should be built around?
  2. Attraction: What could you offer that attracts the right customers and is a natural first step to the core?
  3. Upsell and downsell: What next problem does the first purchase reveal? Why do people most often say no, and what could you change in scope or terms to clear that obstacle?
  4. Continuity: What value recurs naturally every month in your business, and how could customers pay for it?
  5. Numbers: Add up the expected 30-day gross profit per new customer and divide by CAC. How far are you from 2×?

Bistvo

Večina podjetij ima eno ponudbo in upa, da se stranka vrne. Money model je načrtovano zaporedje ponudb: kaj prodaš najprej, kaj ponudiš takoj po »da«, kaj ponudiš po »ne« in kaj plačuje vsak mesec. Njegova naloga je, da iz vsake stranke ustvari več denarja in da ga ustvari prej.

Privabljalna ponudba (pripelje noter) → Upsell (več, takoj po »da«) → Downsell (reši prodajo po »ne«) → Naročnina (ponavljajoče)

To je pomembno, ker podjetja tekmujejo pri pridobivanju strank. Če ti je vsaka stranka v prvih tednih vredna več, lahko za njeno pridobitev porabiš več kot konkurenca. Majhno, preprosto podjetje z močnim money modelom lahko za oglase in prodajo porabi več kot precej večje podjetje in še vedno raste z lastnim denarjem.

Kako deluje

Privabljalne ponudbe (attraction offers): pripelji prave stranke

Kaj. Prva stvar, ki jo nova stranka kupi. Njena naloga je spremeniti neznance v stranke ob nizkem, ničelnem ali celo negativnem neto strošku. Pogoste oblike so brezplačna ali poceni vstopna storitev, ponudba »kupi enega, dobiš drugega«, cenejša možnost ob dražji ali izziv, pri katerem stranka ob doseženem rezultatu dobi denar nazaj.

Zakaj. Zniža prag za prvi »da«. Ko je nekdo enkrat kupil, je naslednji nakup veliko lažji.

Kako. Tesno jo poveži z glavnim produktom, da je nadaljevanje naraven naslednji korak. Vstopna ponudba, ki privabi lovce na popuste, napolni lijak z ljudmi, ki nikoli ne kupijo ničesar drugega.

Upsell ponudbe: prodaj več takoj po »da«

Kaj. Ponudba, ki jo daš takoj po nakupu. Reši naslednjo težavo, ki jo razkrije prvi nakup, ali pa da več, boljše ali hitreje.

Zakaj. Takoj po nakupu je zaupanje največje in odločitev za porabo je že sprejeta. Teden dni pozneje je zagon mimo.

Kako. Uporabi logiko »ne moreš imeti X brez Y«. Kupil si analizo, zdaj jo potrebuješ uvedeno. Kupil si plan vadbe, zdaj potrebuješ prehranski načrt. Ponudi en jasen naslednji korak, ne menija z desetimi možnostmi.

Downsell ponudbe: reši prodajo po »ne«

Kaj. Ko stranka reče ne, ponudiš manjšo, cenejšo ali drugače plačano različico.

Zakaj. »Ne« je pogosto »ne« ceni, obsegu ali tveganju, ne izidu. Brez downsella izgubiš vse, katerih težava so bili pogoji.

Kako. Spremeni nekaj resničnega: manjši obseg, manj funkcij, krajši preizkus, plačilo na obroke. Izogibaj se preprostemu nižanju cene iste stvari, ker kupce s tem naučiš reči ne, tiste, ki so plačali polno ceno, pa kaznuješ.

Ponudbe naročnine (continuity offers): naj se ponavlja

Kaj. Vse, kar stranka plačuje večkrat: članarina, naročnina, vzdrževanje, mesečni pavšal (retainer), stalna podpora.

Zakaj. En nakup spremeni v tok bruto dobička in naredi prihodek predvidljiv. Hkrati ti da razlog, da vrednost dostavljaš naprej.

Kako. Zgradi jo okoli vrednosti, ki se res ponavlja: novi podatki vsak mesec, redne vadbe, mesečni pregledi, vzdrževanje. Pri vključitvi pomagajo bonus ob prijavi, popust za daljšo zavezo ali odpis pristojbine za nastavitev.

Preprosto se skalira (simple scales)

Money model je veliko ponudb okoli istega glavnega produkta, ne veliko produktov. Fitnes prodaja isto vadbo kot izziv, članarino in dodatno osebno vodenje. Svetovalec prodaja isto znanje kot delavnico, projekt in mesečni pavšal. Vsak nov produkt doda zahtevnost izvedbe. Vsaka nova ponudba okoli jedra doda denar ob malo dodatnega dela. Model gradi po vrsti: najprej naj deluje privabljalna ponudba, nato dodajaj po en kos.

Primeri

Primeri so izmišljeni in služijo ponazoritvi.

Fitnes (lokalno podjetje)

Prej je fitnes prodajal samo mesečno članarino 99 € z 70 € bruto dobička. Oglasi so stali 150 € na novega člana, zato je nov član v prvem mesecu vrnil približno polovico stroška svoje pridobitve (70 ÷ 150 ≈ 0,47).

Potem zaporedje ponudb v prvih 30 dneh. Pričakovani bruto dobiček = bruto dobiček na kupca × delež strank, ki ponudbo vzamejo.

Ponudba Bruto dobiček na kupca Delež Pričakovano na stranko
Privabljalna: 4-tedenski izziv, 300 € 240 € 100 % 240 €
Upsell: prehransko svetovanje 150 € 30 % 45 €
Downsell: vodnik z jedilniki (polovica od 70 %, ki zavrnejo) 40 € 35 % 14 €
Naročnina: članarina, prvi mesec 70 € 50 % 35 €
30-dnevni bruto dobiček na stranko 334 €
Strošek pridobitve (CAC) 150 €

Vsaka nova stranka zdaj v enem mesecu prinese približno 2,2-kratnik stroška svoje pridobitve (334 ÷ 150 ≈ 2,2). Fitnes si lahko privošči plačati več za oglase kot fitnes na drugi strani ulice in mu še vedno ostane denar.

Svetovanje za Qlik analitiko (B2B)

Pred. Ena ponudba: trimesečni projekt nadzornih plošč. Prodajni pogovori se končajo z da ali ne. Tisti, ki rečejo ne, so izgubljeni. Tisti, ki rečejo da, odidejo, ko se projekt konča.

Po. Privabljalna: plačan dvodnevni pregled podatkov, ki se odšteje od projekta, če ga podpišejo v 30 dneh. Upsell: izdelava prve nadzorne plošče, ponujena ob koncu pregleda, ko so ugotovitve še na zaslonu. Downsell: pilot na enem oddelku ali plačilo v treh delih. Naročnina: mesečni pavšal za vzdrževanje podatkovnega modela, nova poročila in podporo.

Pregled pokrije velik del prodajnega truda, zato nova stranka ni več odvisna od ene velike odločitve. »Ne« projektu postane pilot namesto izgubljenega leada. Pavšal spremeni enkraten projekt v prihodek, ki teče tudi po izvedbi.

Pogoste napake

  • Ena ponudba in upanje. Če prodaš eno stvar in čakaš na ponovne nakupe, ostane večina vrednosti vsake stranke neizkoriščena.
  • Privabljalne ponudbe, ki privabijo napačne ljudi. Če vstopna ponudba ni povezana z glavnim produktom, dobiš lovce na zastonj stvari, ki nikoli več ne kupijo.
  • Upsell ob napačnem času. Upsell, poslan teden dni pozneje, tekmuje z vsem drugim v nabiralniku. Ponudi ga, dokler je »da« še svež.
  • »Ne« kot konec. Brez downsella vsak pomislek glede cene, obsega ali tveganja postane izgubljena stranka.
  • Več produktov namesto več ponudb. Dodajanje novih produktov za več prihodka pomnoži delo pri izvedbi. Najprej glavni produkt zapakiraj na nove načine.

Kako bi to uporabil Alex

  • Money model pogleda pred marketingom. Ko rast zastane, ker so oglasi videti predragi, najprej vpraša, koliko je vsaka stranka vredna v prvih 30 dneh. Pogosto je rešitev v zaporedju ponudb.
  • Gradi ga po eno ponudbo naenkrat, z začetkom pri privabljalni. Ko ta zanesljivo pripelje prave stranke, doda upsell, nato downsell, nato naročnino.
  • Hoče, da vsaka stranka sliši naslednjo ponudbo. Upsell po vsakem »da« in downsell po vsakem »ne« je proces, ne stvar razpoloženja.
  • Ohrani preprostost. Veliko ponudb okoli enega glavnega produkta premaga katalog. Prej bi ukinil produkte kot ponudbe.
  • Meri ga s 30-dnevnim bruto dobičkom proti CAC in cilja na to, da prvi mesec plača pridobitev te stranke in idealno še dveh.
  • Ima raje modele, ki plačajo vnaprej. Pristojbine za nastavitev, predplačila in upselli v prvem mesecu prinesejo denar takrat, ko ga podjetje potrebuje za pridobitev naslednje stranke.
  • Vprašanja, ki bi jih postavil: Kakšna je tvoja vstopna ponudba in ali naravno vodi do glavnega produkta? Kaj ponudiš takoj, ko nekdo reče da? Kaj se zgodi s tistimi, ki rečejo ne? Kaj stranke plačujejo vsak mesec? Koliko bruto dobička prinese nova stranka v prvih 30 dneh v primerjavi s tem, koliko te stane?

Delovni list

Nariši svoje trenutno zaporedje ponudb in ga nato izboljšaj. Za vsako ponudbo oceni bruto dobiček na kupca in delež strank, ki jo vzamejo.

  1. Jedro: Kateri je tvoj en glavni produkt ali storitev, okoli katerega naj bodo zgrajene vse ponudbe?
  2. Privabljalna: Kaj bi lahko ponudil, da privabiš prave stranke, in je hkrati naraven prvi korak do jedra?
  3. Upsell in downsell: Katero naslednjo težavo razkrije prvi nakup? Zakaj ljudje najpogosteje rečejo ne in kaj bi lahko spremenil v obsegu ali pogojih, da odstraniš to oviro?
  4. Naročnina: Katera vrednost se pri tebi naravno ponavlja vsak mesec in kako bi jo stranke lahko plačevale?
  5. Številke: Seštej pričakovani 30-dnevni bruto dobiček na novo stranko in ga deli s CAC. Koliko ti manjka do 2×?
