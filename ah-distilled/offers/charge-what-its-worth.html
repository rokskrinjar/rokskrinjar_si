$100M Offers · Charge What It's Worth (Virtuous Cycle of Price)

Don't compete on price. A premium price funds better delivery, attracts more committed clients and produces better results, which justifies the price again.Ne tekmuj s ceno. Premium cena financira boljšo izvedbo, privabi bolj zavzete stranke in prinese boljše rezultate, ki ceno znova upravičijo.

The gist

Being the cheapest is a weak position. There is always someone willing to go lower, and a low price leaves no money to do the job well. Alex argues the opposite: charge a premium, and use the margin to deliver a result so good that the price feels small next to it.

Higher price → more margin and more committed buyers → better results → proof and referrals → justifies a higher price

This is the virtuous cycle of price. The same loop runs the other way at a low price: thin margins, rushed delivery, clients who care less, weak results, no proof and more pressure to cut. The condition is that value stays well above the price. A high price without value is not premium, it is just expensive.

How it works

The vicious cycle of low price

  1. You price low to win the deal.
  2. Margin is thin, so you cut delivery: less attention, cheaper people, fewer extras.
  3. Clients who paid little invest little attention and effort. Results suffer.
  4. Weak results bring complaints, churn and no case studies.
  5. Without proof, the only way to win the next deal is another low price.

Low prices also attract the buyers who shop hardest on price. They tend to demand the most and leave first when someone cheaper appears.

The virtuous cycle of high price

  1. You price well above competitors and deliver value well above the price.
  2. The margin funds better delivery: more attention, better hires, better tools, more follow-up.
  3. The buyer who paid more is emotionally invested. They show up, do the work and take the advice seriously, so they get better results.
  4. Margin also funds more marketing. You can outspend competitors to acquire each client.
  5. Better results become proof and referrals, which raise perceived likelihood and let you charge more again.

Price is part of the value

Price is a signal. Buyers often read a higher price as higher quality, and they value what cost them something. In the Value Equation, a committed buyer is more likely to achieve the result, so a higher price can raise the actual and perceived likelihood of success. Price from the value of the outcome to the buyer, not from your hours or costs.

Earning the premium

A premium price needs a reason. The usual sources:

  • A narrow market where your offer is clearly made for them (Commit to a Niche).
  • An offer with no direct comparison (Grand Slam Offer).
  • Risk reversal so the buyer feels safe paying more (Guarantees).
  • A market that can pay (Starving Crowd).

Test raising prices

You won't know your ceiling until you test it. Raise the price for new prospects, keep everything else the same and watch close rate and profit, not just close rate. Losing some deals at a higher price is normal and often profitable. Rule of thumb: if almost everyone says yes, you are probably priced too low. Some resistance means you are near the edge of value.

Examples

Examples are invented for illustration.

AI automation retainer (B2B consulting)

Low price Premium price
Monthly fee €1,000 €1,500
Delivery cost per client €600 €600
Margin per client €400 €900
Clients (25% fewer at premium) 20 15
Monthly profit €8,000 €13,500

At the higher price the consultant has fewer clients and 69% more profit (€5,500 on top of €8,000). Fewer clients also means more time for each one, so results improve. Part of the extra margin could pay for a junior hire to speed up delivery, which strengthens the cycle again.

Personal trainer (local business)

Before. €25 per session, sold one at a time. Clients cancel often, skip weeks and rarely reach their goal. The trainer needs 40 clients to make a living and has no time for check-ins.

After. A 12-week transformation programme for €1,200, paid upfront: three sessions a week, a nutrition plan and weekly check-ins. Clients who paid €1,200 rarely miss a session. The trainer needs far fewer clients and uses the time for coaching between sessions.

Thirty-six sessions at €25 would cost the client €900. The programme costs €300 more, but it includes nutrition and check-ins, and the upfront commitment changes behaviour. Better results bring before-and-after proof that sells the next programme.

Qlik dashboard project (B2B analytics)

A consultant moves from €70 an hour to a fixed €9,800 package for one outcome. Prospects who wanted the cheapest hours drop out. Those who stay care about the result, give access quickly and use the dashboards, which produces the case studies that support the price.

Common mistakes

  • Pricing low out of fear. A low price feels safer but attracts the least committed buyers and removes the money for good delivery.
  • Raising price without raising value. If the offer stays a commodity, a higher price only loses deals.
  • Pricing from cost or hours. Your costs are irrelevant to the buyer. Price from what the outcome is worth to them.
  • Keeping the margin instead of reinvesting it. The cycle only works if part of the extra margin goes back into delivery and acquisition.
  • Never testing. Many businesses have never tried a higher price, so they don't know where their ceiling is.

How Alex would apply it

  • He refuses to compete on being cheapest. He sees no lasting advantage in a race to the bottom.
  • He raises value first, then raises the price. Niche, Grand Slam Offer and guarantees come before the higher number.
  • He reinvests the margin in better delivery and more marketing spend per client, which a cheaper competitor cannot match.
  • He wants buyers who are invested. He treats a higher price as a filter that improves client behaviour and results.
  • He tests price increases on new prospects and judges them by total profit, not by close rate alone.
  • He changes the unit of sale from hours or sessions to an outcome, so the price is compared with the value, not with other vendors.
  • Questions he would ask: What is the result worth to the buyer in money? Who is the most expensive competitor, and why are you below them? What is your close rate? If almost everyone says yes, why haven't you raised the price? What would you do for clients with twice the margin?

Worksheet

Answer in writing with real numbers from your business.

  1. Current position: Where is your price compared with competitors? Why?
  2. Value in money: What is the outcome worth to a typical buyer per year? How does your price compare?
  3. Cycle check: Which signs of the vicious cycle do you see today (thin margin, low-commitment clients, no case studies)?
  4. Reinvestment: If your price went up 50%, what exactly would you improve in delivery or marketing?
  5. Test: Choose a higher price for the next ten prospects. Write down the price, the date and the close rate and profit you will compare.

Bistvo

Biti najcenejši je šibek položaj. Vedno se najde nekdo, ki gre še nižje, nizka cena pa ne pusti denarja za dobro opravljeno delo. Alex zagovarja nasprotno: zaračunaj premium ceno, maržo pa porabi za rezultat, ki je tako dober, da je cena ob njem majhna.

Višja cena → več marže in bolj zavzeti kupci → boljši rezultati → dokazi in priporočila → upravičijo višjo ceno

To je krog rasti cene (virtuous cycle of price). Pri nizki ceni se isti krog vrti v nasprotno smer: tanke marže, hitena izvedba, strankam je manj mar, šibki rezultati, ni dokazov in še večji pritisk na znižanje. Pogoj je, da vrednost ostane precej nad ceno. Visoka cena brez vrednosti ni premium, je samo draga.

Kako deluje

Začarani krog nizke cene (vicious cycle)

  1. Ceno postaviš nizko, da dobiš posel.
  2. Marža je tanka, zato varčuješ pri izvedbi: manj pozornosti, cenejši ljudje, manj dodatkov.
  3. Stranke, ki so plačale malo, vložijo malo pozornosti in truda. Rezultati trpijo.
  4. Šibki rezultati prinesejo pritožbe, odhode in nobenih primerov uspeha.
  5. Brez dokazov lahko naslednji posel dobiš le z novo nizko ceno.

Nizke cene privabijo tudi kupce, ki najbolj trdo primerjajo cene. Ti navadno zahtevajo največ in odidejo prvi, ko se pojavi nekdo cenejši.

Krog rasti visoke cene (virtuous cycle)

  1. Ceno postaviš precej nad konkurenco in dostaviš vrednost, ki je precej nad ceno.
  2. Marža financira boljšo izvedbo: več pozornosti, boljše zaposlene, boljša orodja, več spremljanja.
  3. Kupec, ki je plačal več, je čustveno vložen. Pride, opravi svoj del in nasvete jemlje resno, zato doseže boljše rezultate.
  4. Marža financira tudi več marketinga. Za pridobitev vsake stranke lahko porabiš več kot konkurenca.
  5. Boljši rezultati postanejo dokazi in priporočila, ki dvignejo zaznano verjetnost in ti omogočijo, da znova zaračunaš več.

Cena je del vrednosti

Cena je signal. Kupci višjo ceno pogosto razumejo kot višjo kakovost in bolj cenijo tisto, kar jih je nekaj stalo. V enačbi vrednosti je zavzet kupec bolj verjetno uspešen, zato višja cena lahko dvigne dejansko in zaznano verjetnost uspeha. Ceno postavi po vrednosti izida za kupca, ne po svojih urah ali stroških.

Kako si prislužiš premium

Premium cena potrebuje razlog. Običajni viri:

  • Ozek trg, za katerega je tvoja ponudba očitno narejena (Zaveza niši).
  • Ponudba brez neposredne primerjave (Grand Slam ponudba).
  • Prevzem tveganja, da se kupec počuti varno, ko plača več (Garancije).
  • Trg, ki lahko plača (Lačna množica).

Preizkusi dvig cen

Svojega stropa ne poznaš, dokler ga ne preizkusiš. Dvigni ceno za nove potencialne stranke, vse drugo pusti enako in spremljaj delež zaključenih poslov in dobiček, ne le delež zaključenih poslov. Da pri višji ceni izgubiš nekaj poslov, je normalno in pogosto donosno. Pravilo palca: če skoraj vsi rečejo da, je cena verjetno prenizka. Nekaj odpora pomeni, da si blizu meje vrednosti.

Primeri

Primeri so izmišljeni in služijo ponazoritvi.

Mesečni paket za AI avtomatizacijo (B2B svetovanje)

Nizka cena Premium cena
Mesečna cena 1.000 € 1.500 €
Strošek izvedbe na stranko 600 € 600 €
Marža na stranko 400 € 900 €
Stranke (pri premium 25 % manj) 20 15
Mesečni dobiček 8.000 € 13.500 €

Pri višji ceni ima svetovalec manj strank in 69 % več dobička (5.500 € več od 8.000 €). Manj strank pomeni tudi več časa za vsako, zato so rezultati boljši. Del dodatne marže bi lahko plačal mlajšega sodelavca, ki pospeši izvedbo, kar krog še okrepi.

Osebni trener (lokalna storitev)

Pred. 25 € na trening, prodano posamično. Stranke pogosto odpovedujejo, izpuščajo tedne in redko dosežejo cilj. Trener za preživetje potrebuje 40 strank in nima časa za spremljanje.

Po. 12-tedenski program preobrazbe za 1.200 €, plačan vnaprej: trije treningi na teden, prehranski načrt in tedenska preverjanja. Stranke, ki so plačale 1.200 €, redko izpustijo trening. Trener potrebuje veliko manj strank, čas pa porabi za vodenje med treningi.

Šestintrideset treningov po 25 € bi stranko stalo 900 €. Program stane 300 € več, a vključuje prehrano in preverjanja, plačilo vnaprej pa spremeni vedenje. Boljši rezultati prinesejo dokaze »pred in po«, ki prodajo naslednji program.

Projekt Qlik nadzornih plošč (B2B analitika)

Svetovalec preide s 70 € na uro na fiksni paket za 9.800 € za en izid. Potencialne stranke, ki so iskale najcenejše ure, odpadejo. Tiste, ki ostanejo, jim je mar za rezultat, hitro omogočijo dostop in nadzorne plošče res uporabljajo, kar ustvari primere uspeha, ki podprejo ceno.

Pogoste napake

  • Nizka cena iz strahu. Nizka cena se zdi varnejša, a privabi najmanj zavzete kupce in odvzame denar za dobro izvedbo.
  • Dvig cene brez dviga vrednosti. Če ponudba ostane blago (commodity), višja cena prinese le izgubljene posle.
  • Cena po stroških ali urah. Tvoji stroški kupca ne zanimajo. Ceno postavi po tem, koliko mu je vreden izid.
  • Marža ostane v žepu namesto v ponovni naložbi. Krog deluje le, če gre del dodatne marže nazaj v izvedbo in pridobivanje strank.
  • Nikoli ne preizkusiš. Mnoga podjetja višje cene niso nikoli poskusila, zato ne vedo, kje je njihov strop.

Kako bi to uporabil Alex

  • Noče tekmovati v tem, kdo je najcenejši. V tekmi do dna ne vidi trajne prednosti.
  • Najprej dvigne vrednost, nato ceno. Niša, Grand Slam ponudba in garancije pridejo pred višjo številko.
  • Maržo ponovno vloži v boljšo izvedbo in več marketinga na stranko, česar cenejši konkurent ne more dohajati.
  • Želi kupce, ki so vloženi. Višjo ceno obravnava kot filter, ki izboljša vedenje in rezultate strank.
  • Dvige cen preizkuša na novih potencialnih strankah in jih ocenjuje po skupnem dobičku, ne le po deležu zaključenih poslov.
  • Spremeni enoto prodaje z ur ali treningov na izid, da se cena primerja z vrednostjo, ne z drugimi ponudniki.
  • Vprašanja, ki bi jih postavil: Koliko je rezultat kupcu vreden v denarju? Kdo je najdražji konkurent in zakaj si pod njim? Kolikšen je tvoj delež zaključenih poslov? Če skoraj vsi rečejo da, zakaj še nisi dvignil cene? Kaj bi za stranke naredil z dvakrat večjo maržo?

Delovni list

Odgovori pisno z resničnimi številkami iz svojega podjetja.

  1. Trenutni položaj: Kje je tvoja cena v primerjavi s konkurenco? Zakaj?
  2. Vrednost v denarju: Koliko je izid tipičnemu kupcu vreden na leto? Kako se s tem primerja tvoja cena?
  3. Preverjanje kroga: Katere znake začaranega kroga vidiš danes (tanka marža, nezavzete stranke, ni primerov uspeha)?
  4. Ponovna naložba: Če bi se tvoja cena dvignila za 50 %, kaj točno bi izboljšal v izvedbi ali marketingu?
  5. Preizkus: Izberi višjo ceno za naslednjih deset potencialnih strank. Zapiši ceno, datum ter delež zaključenih poslov in dobiček, ki ju boš primerjal.
