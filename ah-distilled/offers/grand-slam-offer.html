$100M Offers · Grand Slam Offer

An offer so valuable for its price, and so specific to one market, that the buyer has nothing to compare it with. It is the way out of competing on price.Ponudba, ki je za svojo ceno tako vredna in tako prilagojena enemu trgu, da je kupec nima s čim primerjati. Je izhod iz tekmovanja s ceno.

The gist

A commodity is anything the buyer can compare line by line with what someone else sells. Same hours, same features, same deliverables. When the buyer can compare everything, the only thing left to decide on is price, and the cheapest seller wins a race nobody profits from.

A Grand Slam Offer is built so that comparison stops working. It solves every problem one specific market has on the way to its dream outcome, it is worth far more than it costs, and it is packaged in a way nobody else packages it. The buyer can't find a second option, so you become a category of one. The test Alex uses is how the buyer feels: the offer should be so good that saying no feels foolish.

Commodity (compared on price) → Differentiated (compared on features) → Grand Slam (no comparison, priced on value)

How it works

The commodity trap

Most businesses describe what they do in the same words as their competitors: "Qlik development at €70 an hour", "personal training sessions", "bookkeeping services". The buyer collects three quotes, lines them up and picks the lowest. Margins shrink, there is no money left to deliver well, results get worse, and the business has to cut price again. The problem isn't the market or the sales team. The problem is that the offer invites comparison.

The ladder

Level What the buyer sees How they decide Your pricing power
Commodity The same thing as everyone else Price None
Differentiated A better version with some extras Features and price Small premium
Grand Slam A solution to their situation that nobody else offers Value against price Price set by value

Differentiation alone is not enough. A "better" offer can still be put in the same spreadsheet as the others. The jump to Grand Slam happens when the offer is so specific to one market and so complete that the spreadsheet no longer has a matching row.

How a Grand Slam Offer is built

The book walks through the build in a fixed order. Each step has its own entry.

  1. Pick the market. Sell to a starving crowd and commit to a niche. An offer is only "unmatched" relative to a specific buyer.
  2. Set the price from value. Decide early that you will charge what it's worth, so you have the margin to deliver an outstanding result.
  3. Define the dream outcome and score it with the Value Equation. Raise the outcome and the perceived likelihood, cut the time delay and the effort.
  4. List every problem and turn it into a solution. Walk the buyer's journey before, during and after the purchase and write down everything that could stop them. Each problem becomes a "how to get X without Y" solution (Problems into Solutions).
  5. Choose delivery vehicles. For each solution, decide how it reaches the buyer: one-on-one or group, done-for-you or do-it-yourself, call, recording, template, software (Delivery Vehicles).
  6. Trim and stack. Cut what is costly and low-value, keep what is cheap and highly valuable, and bundle the rest into one package where every piece is named (Trim & Stack).
  7. Enhance. Add scarcity, urgency, bonuses, guarantees and a strong name. These raise demand without changing the core delivery.

Why it works

The bundle solves problems the competitors never listed, so they have nothing to match it with. Because each piece removes a reason to hesitate, the buyer's perceived risk drops. And because many of the pieces are cheap to deliver (templates, recordings, checklists, reusable software), the value goes up much faster than your cost. That gap between value and cost is what lets you charge a premium and still deliver more than the buyer expected.

Examples

Examples are invented for illustration.

Manufacturing analytics (B2B)

Before. "Qlik app development at an hourly rate. Estimate: 120 hours." The buyer collects three quotes and picks the lowest rate.

After. "OEE and scrap under control in 30 days": connection to MES and ERP, master-data clean-up, three ready-made apps for production, a 30-minute weekly review with shift leads, a board-ready checklist and one month of support after launch.

Each piece answers an obstacle the buyer has: "our data is messy" (clean-up), "people won't use it" (weekly review), "the board won't trust the numbers" (checklist), "we'll be on our own after handover" (support). The ready-made apps cost little to reuse. With no hourly rate, there is nothing to put next to another quote.

Hair salon (local business)

Obstacle Solution in the package
"I don't know which style suits me" Trial style six weeks before, with photos
"The morning will be stressful" Home visit and an exact timetable for the bride and bridesmaids
"What if it rains?" Touch-up kit and instructions for the maid of honour
"My hair won't last until midnight" Hair treatment programme in the four weeks before

Instead of "hairstyle €60", the salon sells "a stress-free wedding morning and hair that lasts all night". The salon down the street doesn't have this package, so comparing on the price of one hairstyle no longer makes sense.

AI invoice automation (consulting)

Dream outcome: accounting closes the month two days earlier. Obstacles: fear of AI errors, an auditor who won't trust it, no time for onboarding. Solutions: two weeks of parallel running with a variance report, auditor documentation as a ready template, five 15-minute recordings instead of a workshop, chat support for the first month. Cut: long on-site training, which costs a lot and the client doesn't need.

Common mistakes

  • Calling a better commodity a Grand Slam. Lower price, faster delivery or one extra feature still leaves you in the same comparison. If the buyer can line you up with a competitor, you are not there yet.
  • Building for everyone. An offer can only be unmatched for a specific buyer. Without a narrow market the problem list stays generic and so does the offer.
  • Stacking filler. Bonuses that solve no real problem make the package look padded. Every piece has to remove an obstacle or move a lever in the Value Equation.
  • Skipping the trim. Promising one-on-one attention for everything makes the offer impossible to deliver profitably. Each piece has to survive the cost test.
  • Describing it in commodity words. If the name and structure still say "hours", "sessions" or "licences", buyers will compare on those units.

How Alex would apply it

  • He diagnoses commoditisation first. When a business complains about price pressure, he assumes the offer is comparable and fixes that before touching ads or sales scripts.
  • He builds from the buyer's problems, not from the product. His starting point is a long list of everything that could go wrong for the buyer, and the offer is the answer to that list.
  • He changes the unit of sale. He moves away from hours, sessions or seats toward an outcome with a time frame, so the price can no longer be divided and compared.
  • He favours cheap, high-value pieces. Templates, recordings, checklists and software raise value with little extra delivery cost, so margin grows as the offer improves.
  • He raises the price once the value is clearly higher. The point of the work is to charge more than competitors and still have the buyer feel they got the better deal.
  • He keeps the core simple and enhances around it. Scarcity, urgency, bonuses and guarantees come last, on top of a solid core.
  • Questions he would ask: Who exactly is this for? What could the buyer compare you with today? What are all the things that could stop them getting the result? Which of those does nobody else solve? What would it take for them to feel stupid saying no?

Worksheet

Fill this in writing for one offer. Keep the order.

  1. Comparison check: Write down three offers a buyer could put next to yours. On what units would they compare you (hours, price per user, sessions)?
  2. Ladder: Is your offer today a commodity, differentiated or a Grand Slam? Give one sentence of evidence.
  3. Problems: List at least 15 obstacles your buyer faces before, during and after buying.
  4. Unique solutions: Which of those obstacles does no competitor address? How would you solve each one?
  5. New unit: Rewrite the offer as an outcome with a time frame, without hours or seats.
  6. Final test: Who could the buyer still compare you with directly? If anyone, what is missing?

Bistvo

Blago (commodity) je vse, kar lahko kupec postavko za postavko primerja s tem, kar prodaja nekdo drug. Iste ure, iste funkcije, isti izdelki. Ko lahko kupec primerja vse, mu za odločitev ostane le cena, najcenejši pa zmaga v tekmi, v kateri nihče ne zasluži.

Grand Slam ponudba (Grand Slam Offer) je zgrajena tako, da primerjava ne deluje več. Reši vse probleme, ki jih ima en specifičen trg na poti do sanjskega izida, vredna je bistveno več, kot stane, in je zapakirana tako, kot je ne zapakira nihče drug. Kupec ne najde druge možnosti, zato postaneš kategorija s samo enim ponudnikom (category of one). Alexov test je občutek kupca: ponudba mora biti tako dobra, da se ob zavrnitvi počuti neumno.

Blago (primerjava po ceni) → Diferencirana ponudba (primerjava po funkcijah) → Grand Slam (brez primerjave, cena po vrednosti)

Kako deluje

Past blaga (commodity trap)

Večina podjetij opisuje svoje delo z enakimi besedami kot konkurenca: »razvoj Qlik aplikacij po 70 € na uro«, »ure osebnega treninga«, »računovodske storitve«. Kupec zbere tri ponudbe, jih postavi eno ob drugo in izbere najnižjo. Marže se krčijo, za dobro izvedbo ni več denarja, rezultati so slabši, podjetje pa mora ceno znova znižati. Težava ni v trgu ali prodajni ekipi. Težava je v tem, da ponudba vabi k primerjavi.

Lestev

Raven Kaj kupec vidi Kako se odloči Tvoja moč pri ceni
Blago Isto kot pri vseh drugih Po ceni Nobene
Diferencirana Boljšo različico z nekaj dodatki Po funkcijah in ceni Majhna premija
Grand Slam Rešitev za njegovo situacijo, ki je nihče drug ne ponuja Vrednost proti ceni Ceno določa vrednost

Samo razlikovanje ni dovolj. »Boljšo« ponudbo je še vedno mogoče vpisati v isto preglednico kot druge. Skok na raven Grand Slam se zgodi, ko je ponudba tako specifična za en trg in tako celovita, da v preglednici ni več vrstice, ki bi se ji ujemala.

Kako se zgradi Grand Slam ponudba

Knjiga gre skozi gradnjo v točno določenem vrstnem redu. Vsak korak ima svoj vnos.

  1. Izberi trg. Prodajaj lačni množici (starving crowd) in se zavezi niši. Ponudba je »brez primerjave« le glede na določenega kupca.
  2. Ceno postavi po vrednosti. Že zgodaj se odloči, da boš zaračunal, kar je vredno, da imaš maržo za izjemen rezultat.
  3. Določi sanjski izid in ga oceni z enačbo vrednosti. Dvigni izid in zaznano verjetnost, skrajšaj časovni zamik in zmanjšaj trud.
  4. Naštej vse probleme in vsakega spremeni v rešitev. Prehodi pot kupca pred nakupom, med njim in po njem ter zapiši vse, kar bi ga lahko ustavilo. Vsak problem postane rešitev v obliki »kako doseči X brez Y« (Iz problemov v rešitve).
  5. Izberi načine dostave. Za vsako rešitev določi, kako pride do kupca: individualno ali v skupini, naredimo namesto vas (done-for-you) ali naredite sami (do-it-yourself), klic, posnetek, predloga, programska oprema (Načini dostave).
  6. Odreži in zloži. Odreži, kar je drago in malo vredno, obdrži, kar je poceni in zelo vredno, ostalo pa zloži v en paket, v katerem ima vsak kos ime (Odreži in zloži).
  7. Ojačaj. Dodaj pomanjkanje (scarcity), nujnost (urgency), bonuse, garancije in močno ime. Ti dvignejo povpraševanje, ne da bi spremenili jedro izvedbe.

Zakaj deluje

Paket rešuje probleme, ki jih konkurenti nikoli niso zapisali, zato nimajo s čim odgovoriti. Ker vsak kos odstrani en razlog za oklevanje, zaznano tveganje kupca pade. In ker je veliko kosov poceni za dostavo (predloge, posnetki, kontrolni seznami, ponovno uporabna programska oprema), vrednost raste veliko hitreje kot tvoj strošek. Ta razlika med vrednostjo in stroškom ti omogoča premium ceno, pri tem pa še vedno dostaviš več, kot je kupec pričakoval.

Primeri

Primeri so izmišljeni in služijo ponazoritvi.

Analitika za proizvodnjo (B2B)

Pred. »Razvoj Qlik aplikacij po urni postavki. Ocena: 120 ur.« Kupec zbere tri ponudbe in izbere najnižjo urno postavko.

Po. »OEE in izmet pod nadzorom v 30 dneh«: priklop na MES in ERP, čiščenje šifrantov, tri vnaprej pripravljene aplikacije za proizvodnjo, 30-minutni tedenski pregled z vodji izmen, kontrolni seznam za upravo in mesec podpore po zagonu.

Vsak kos odgovori na oviro kupca: »podatki so neurejeni« (čiščenje), »ljudje tega ne bodo uporabljali« (tedenski pregled), »uprava ne bo verjela številkam« (kontrolni seznam), »po predaji bomo sami« (podpora). Pripravljene aplikacije je poceni ponovno uporabiti. Urne postavke ni več, zato ponudbe ni mogoče postaviti ob drugo.

Frizerski salon (lokalna storitev)

Ovira Rešitev v paketu
»Ne vem, katera pričeska mi pristaja« Poskusna pričeska šest tednov prej, s fotografijami
»Zjutraj bo stres« Prihod na dom in natančen urnik za nevesto in družice
»Kaj, če bo deževalo?« Komplet za popravke in navodila za pričo
»Lasje ne bodo zdržali do polnoči« Program nege las v štirih tednih pred poroko

Namesto »pričeska 60 €« salon prodaja »brezskrbno poročno jutro in lase, ki zdržijo vso noč«. Salon na drugi strani ulice takega paketa nima, zato primerjava po ceni ene pričeske nima več smisla.

AI avtomatizacija računov (svetovanje)

Sanjski izid: računovodstvo zapre mesec dva dni prej. Ovire: strah pred napakami AI, revizor, ki ne bo zaupal, ni časa za uvajanje. Rešitve: dva tedna vzporednega delovanja s poročilom o odstopanjih, dokumentacija za revizorja kot pripravljena predloga, pet 15-minutnih posnetkov namesto delavnice, podpora prek chata prvi mesec. Odrezano: dolgo šolanje na lokaciji, ki veliko stane, stranka pa ga ne potrebuje.

Pogoste napake

  • Boljše blago imenuješ Grand Slam. Nižja cena, hitrejša dobava ali ena funkcija več te pusti v isti primerjavi. Če te kupec lahko postavi ob konkurenta, še nisi tam.
  • Gradiš za vse. Ponudba je lahko brez primerjave le za določenega kupca. Brez ozkega trga ostane seznam problemov splošen, z njim pa tudi ponudba.
  • Zlagaš polnilo. Bonusi, ki ne rešujejo nobenega resničnega problema, paket le napihnejo. Vsak kos mora odstraniti oviro ali premakniti vzvod v enačbi vrednosti.
  • Preskočiš rezanje. Če obljubiš individualno pozornost za vse, ponudbe ni mogoče dobičkonosno izvesti. Vsak kos mora preživeti test stroška.
  • Opisuješ jo z besedami blaga. Če ime in struktura še vedno govorita o »urah«, »terminih« ali »licencah«, te bodo kupci primerjali po teh enotah.

Kako bi to uporabil Alex

  • Najprej diagnosticira, ali je ponudba postala blago. Ko se podjetje pritožuje nad pritiskom na ceno, predpostavi, da je ponudba primerljiva, in to popravi, preden se dotakne oglasov ali prodajnih skript.
  • Gradi iz problemov kupca, ne iz izdelka. Izhodišče je dolg seznam vsega, kar lahko gre kupcu narobe, ponudba pa je odgovor na ta seznam.
  • Spremeni enoto prodaje. Odmakne se od ur, terminov ali sedežev k izidu s časovnim okvirom, tako da cene ni več mogoče razdeliti in primerjati.
  • Daje prednost poceni, zelo vrednim kosom. Predloge, posnetki, kontrolni seznami in programska oprema dvignejo vrednost z malo dodatnega stroška izvedbe, zato marža raste skupaj s ponudbo.
  • Ceno dvigne, ko je vrednost jasno višja. Smisel vsega dela je, da zaračunaš več kot konkurenca, kupec pa ima še vedno občutek, da je dobil boljši posel.
  • Jedro ohrani preprosto in ga ojača od zunaj. Pomanjkanje, nujnost, bonusi in garancije pridejo na koncu, na trdno jedro.
  • Vprašanja, ki bi jih postavil: Za koga točno je to? S čim bi te kupec danes lahko primerjal? Kaj vse bi ga lahko ustavilo na poti do rezultata? Katerih od teh ovir ne rešuje nihče drug? Kaj bi moralo biti res, da bi se ob zavrnitvi počutil neumno?

Delovni list

Za eno ponudbo izpolni pisno. Drži se vrstnega reda.

  1. Preverjanje primerjave: Zapiši tri ponudbe, ki bi jih kupec lahko postavil ob tvojo. Po katerih enotah bi vas primerjal (ure, cena na uporabnika, termini)?
  2. Lestev: Je tvoja ponudba danes blago, diferencirana ali Grand Slam? Dokaz v enem stavku.
  3. Problemi: Naštej vsaj 15 ovir, ki jih ima kupec pred nakupom, med njim in po njem.
  4. Edinstvene rešitve: Katerih ovir ne rešuje noben konkurent? Kako bi rešil vsako od njih?
  5. Nova enota: Ponudbo prepiši kot izid s časovnim okvirom, brez ur ali sedežev.
  6. Končni test: S kom bi te kupec še lahko neposredno primerjal? Če s komer koli, kaj manjka?
