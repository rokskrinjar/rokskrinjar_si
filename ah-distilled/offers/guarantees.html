$100M Offers · Guarantees (Risk Reversal)

A promise that moves the risk of buying from the buyer to the seller. It raises perceived likelihood of success and usually wins more sales than it costs in refunds.Obljuba, ki tveganje nakupa prenese s kupca na prodajalca. Dvigne zaznano verjetnost uspeha in navadno prinese več prodaj, kot stane vračil.

The gist

Every purchase carries risk, and by default the buyer holds all of it. They pay first and hope it works. A guarantee flips that. The seller takes on the risk, so the buyer's biggest fear (paying and getting nothing) is gone. This raises the perceived likelihood of achievement in the value equation, which raises value directly.

Strong guarantee = If you do X (the actions that create success) and do not get Y (the specific result) by when, we will do Z (refund, extra work, payment back).

Most sellers fear that guarantees will be abused. In practice the extra buyers who say yes usually outnumber those who ask for refunds. And if a guarantee would bankrupt you, that tells you more about your delivery than about the guarantee.

How it works

Unconditional guarantee

Money back, no questions asked, within a time window. It is the simplest and most persuasive type, because the buyer does not need to prove anything. It fits offers where the buyer's effort is small and the cost of delivery is low, or where your results are so reliable that refunds are rare. A variation is a guarantee that pays back more than the price, which shows extreme confidence.

Conditional guarantee

A refund or extra work if the buyer does their part and still does not get the result. The conditions should be the exact actions that produce success: attend the sessions, supply the data, use the system weekly. This does two things. It protects you from buyers who did nothing, and it pushes buyers to do what makes them succeed. A conditional guarantee can promise more than an unconditional one, because it is tied to effort.

Two common forms:

  • Refund conditional: "Do the steps, and if it doesn't work, you get your money back."
  • Service conditional: "Do the steps, and if it doesn't work, we keep working with you for free until it does." This often costs you less than a refund and keeps the client.

Anti-guarantee

An explicit "all sales are final", with a clear reason. It works when what you deliver cannot be taken back once given: confidential methods, custom work started on day one, access to private material. The reason is what makes it work. Stated well, it signals exclusivity and seriousness: "Because you get our full internal playbooks on day one, we cannot offer refunds." Without a reason, it just sounds like you don't trust your product.

Implied guarantee (performance)

You are only paid, or paid more, when the result happens. Forms include pay per result, a share of revenue or savings, or a low base fee plus a performance bonus. The guarantee is implied because the buyer only pays for outcomes. It works when the result is measurable and agreed in advance, and when you have enough control over delivery to make it happen.

Choosing a type

Type Fits when Watch out for
Unconditional Low price, little buyer effort, reliable results Cost of refunds if delivery is uneven
Conditional Result depends on what the buyer does Too many conditions read as distrust
Anti-guarantee What you give cannot be returned Needs a believable reason
Implied / performance Result is measurable, you control delivery Disputes about how to measure

Stacking guarantees

You can combine guarantees. For example: an unconditional 14-day "love it or leave it" guarantee on the first phase, plus a conditional guarantee on the final result. Or a service guarantee on the timeline plus a performance fee on savings. Each one removes a different fear.

Name the guarantee and make it specific

A named guarantee is remembered and repeated: "the Go-Live Guarantee", "the 30-Day Board-Ready Promise". Be precise about what, by when and what happens. Vague guarantees ("satisfaction guaranteed") add little, because nobody knows what they mean.

Examples

Examples are invented for illustration.

Qlik reporting project (B2B)

Before. "We will deliver the agreed reports according to the specification." The CFO worries about paying €15,000 for reports nobody uses.

After. "The Go-Live Guarantee: if you give us database access in week one and your controller joins two one-hour reviews, your margin reports are live in 30 days. If not, we keep working at no cost until they are. Plus the Usage Promise: if fewer than five people use the app weekly after 60 days, we run a free adoption workshop."

Two stacked conditional guarantees. The conditions are exactly what makes the project succeed: access and review time. The CFO's two fears, delay and low usage, each have their own answer.

Plumber (local business)

Before. "Repairs done professionally."

After. "On-Time or Free: if we arrive more than 30 minutes late, the call-out fee is waived. Fixed-Once Guarantee: if the same leak comes back within 12 months, we fix it free."

One unconditional guarantee on punctuality, one conditional guarantee on the repair. Both are named and specific, and both target what homeowners fear most.

AI automation (performance)

An AI consultant charges €3,000 up front plus 20% of the value of measured hours saved in the first six months, at an agreed internal rate. If the automation saves 400 hours at €40 an hour, that is €16,000 saved and a €3,200 performance fee. If it saves nothing, the client is out only the €3,000. The measurement method is written into the contract before work starts.

Common mistakes

  • No guarantee out of fear. Assuming everyone will abuse it. Most buyers don't, and the extra sales usually more than cover the refunds.
  • Conditions nobody can meet. A guarantee buried in fine print reads as distrust and lowers likelihood instead of raising it.
  • Conditions unrelated to success. Conditions should be the actions that create the result. Random hoops just look defensive.
  • Vague promises. "Satisfaction guaranteed" does not say what, by when or what happens. Specific guarantees are believed.
  • An anti-guarantee without a reason. "No refunds" alone sounds like you expect the product to fail.

How Alex would apply it

  • He wants a guarantee on almost every offer, and usually the strongest one the business can survive.
  • He ties conditions to the behaviours that create success, so the guarantee doubles as a way to make clients do the work.
  • He prefers service guarantees ("we keep working until you get it") when a refund would hurt, because they cost less and keep the client.
  • He stacks guarantees to remove several fears at once, and gives each one a name.
  • He treats refund rates as a delivery signal. If a guarantee would be claimed often, he fixes the product before he weakens the guarantee.
  • He uses performance deals when results are measurable, because they make the decision almost risk-free for the buyer and can pay the seller more.
  • Questions he would ask: What is the buyer's biggest fear about buying? What must they do to succeed, and could that be your condition? What would happen if 10% asked for their money back? Can you promise more work instead of money? What would you name it so people remember it?

Worksheet

Take your main offer and design its guarantee in writing.

  1. Fear: What is the buyer most afraid of: losing money, wasting time, looking bad to their boss?
  2. Success actions: List the three to five things a client must do to get the result. These become your conditions.
  3. Type: Which type fits: unconditional, conditional, anti-guarantee or performance? Why?
  4. Sentence: Write it as "If you do X and don't get Y by [when], we will Z."
  5. Stack and name: Add a second guarantee for a different fear. Give both a name.
  6. Stress test: If 10% of clients claimed it, what would it cost you? What would you fix in delivery so they don't?

Bistvo

Vsak nakup nosi tveganje, in privzeto ga v celoti nosi kupec. Najprej plača in upa, da bo delovalo. Garancija to obrne. Tveganje prevzame prodajalec, zato kupčev največji strah (plačam in ne dobim nič) izgine. To dvigne zaznano verjetnost uspeha v enačbi vrednosti, s tem pa neposredno vrednost.

Močna garancija = Če narediš X (dejanja, ki ustvarijo uspeh) in do kdaj ne dobiš Y (konkreten rezultat), bomo naredili Z (vračilo, dodatno delo, povrnitev plačila).

Večina prodajalcev se boji, da bodo garancijo zlorabili. V praksi dodatni kupci, ki rečejo da, navadno presežejo tiste, ki zahtevajo vračilo. In če bi te garancija spravila v stečaj, to pove več o tvoji izvedbi kot o garanciji.

Kako deluje

Brezpogojna garancija (unconditional)

Vračilo denarja brez vprašanj v določenem časovnem oknu. Je najpreprostejša in najbolj prepričljiva vrsta, ker kupcu ni treba ničesar dokazovati. Ustreza ponudbam, kjer je kupčev trud majhen in strošek izvedbe nizek, ali kjer so tvoji rezultati tako zanesljivi, da so vračila redka. Različica je garancija, ki vrne več od cene, kar kaže izjemno samozavest.

Pogojna garancija (conditional)

Vračilo ali dodatno delo, če kupec opravi svoj del in rezultata vseeno ni. Pogoji naj bodo natančno tista dejanja, ki prinesejo uspeh: udeležba na srečanjih, dostava podatkov, tedenska uporaba sistema. To naredi dvoje. Zaščiti te pred kupci, ki niso naredili ničesar, in kupce potisne, da naredijo, kar jih pripelje do uspeha. Pogojna garancija lahko obljubi več od brezpogojne, ker je vezana na trud.

Dve pogosti obliki:

  • Pogojno vračilo: »Opravi korake, in če ne deluje, dobiš denar nazaj.«
  • Pogojna storitev: »Opravi korake, in če ne deluje, delamo s tabo brezplačno naprej, dokler ne deluje.« To te pogosto stane manj od vračila in obdrži stranko.

Anti-garancija (anti-guarantee)

Izrecno »vsi nakupi so dokončni«, z jasnim razlogom. Deluje, ko tega, kar dostaviš, ni mogoče vzeti nazaj, ko je enkrat dano: zaupne metode, delo po meri, ki se začne prvi dan, dostop do zasebnih gradiv. Delovati jo naredi prav razlog. Dobro povedana sporoča ekskluzivnost in resnost: »Ker že prvi dan dobite vse naše interne priročnike, vračil ne moremo ponuditi.« Brez razloga zveni, kot da ne zaupaš svojemu izdelku.

Implicitna garancija (implied, performance)

Plačan si samo, ali plačan več, ko rezultat pride. Oblike so plačilo po rezultatu, delež prihodka ali prihranka, ali nizko osnovno plačilo z bonusom za uspešnost. Garancija je implicitna, ker kupec plača samo za rezultate. Deluje, ko je rezultat merljiv in vnaprej dogovorjen ter ko imaš dovolj nadzora nad izvedbo, da ga dosežeš.

Izbira vrste

Vrsta Ustreza, ko Pazi na
Brezpogojna Nizka cena, malo kupčevega truda, zanesljivi rezultati Strošek vračil, če je izvedba neenakomerna
Pogojna Rezultat je odvisen od tega, kar naredi kupec Preveč pogojev deluje kot nezaupanje
Anti-garancija Tega, kar daš, ni mogoče vrniti Potrebuje verjeten razlog
Implicitna / po uspešnosti Rezultat je merljiv, izvedbo nadzoruješ ti Spori o načinu merjenja

Zlaganje garancij (stacking)

Garancije lahko kombiniraš. Na primer: brezpogojna 14-dnevna garancija »všeč ali vračilo« na prvi fazi in pogojna garancija na končni rezultat. Ali garancija storitve na rok in plačilo po uspešnosti na prihranke. Vsaka odstrani drug strah.

Poimenuj garancijo in jo naredi konkretno

Poimenovano garancijo si ljudje zapomnijo in jo ponavljajo: »Garancija zagona«, »30-dnevna obljuba za upravo«. Bodi natančen glede tega, kaj, do kdaj in kaj se zgodi. Nejasne garancije (»zadovoljstvo zagotovljeno«) dodajo malo, ker nihče ne ve, kaj pomenijo.

Primeri

Primeri so izmišljeni in služijo ponazoritvi.

Qlik projekt poročanja (B2B)

Pred. »Dostavili bomo dogovorjena poročila po specifikaciji.« Finančni direktor se boji, da bo plačal 15.000 € za poročila, ki jih nihče ne bo uporabljal.

Po. »Garancija zagona: če nam v prvem tednu omogočite dostop do baze in se vaš kontroler udeleži dveh enournih pregledov, so vaša poročila o marži v uporabi v 30 dneh. Če niso, delamo brezplačno naprej, dokler niso. Poleg tega Obljuba uporabe: če po 60 dneh aplikacijo tedensko uporablja manj kot pet ljudi, brezplačno izvedemo delavnico za uvajanje.«

Dve zloženi pogojni garanciji. Pogoji so točno tisto, kar projekt pripelje do uspeha: dostop in čas za preglede. Oba strahova finančnega direktorja, zamuda in slaba uporaba, imata vsak svoj odgovor.

Vodoinštalater (lokalni posel)

Pred. »Strokovno opravljena popravila.«

Po. »Pravočasno ali zastonj: če zamudimo več kot 30 minut, ne plačate prihoda. Garancija enkratnega popravila: če se isto puščanje vrne v 12 mesecih, ga popravimo brezplačno.«

Ena brezpogojna garancija na točnost, ena pogojna na popravilo. Obe sta poimenovani in konkretni, obe ciljata na to, česar se lastniki hiš najbolj bojijo.

AI avtomatizacija (po uspešnosti)

AI svetovalec zaračuna 3.000 € vnaprej in 20 % vrednosti izmerjenih prihranjenih ur v prvih šestih mesecih, po dogovorjeni interni urni postavki. Če avtomatizacija prihrani 400 ur po 40 € na uro, je to 16.000 € prihranka in 3.200 € plačila po uspešnosti. Če ne prihrani ničesar, stranka izgubi le 3.000 €. Način merjenja je zapisan v pogodbi, preden se delo začne.

Pogoste napake

  • Brez garancije iz strahu. Predpostavka, da jo bodo vsi zlorabili. Večina kupcev je ne, dodatne prodaje pa navadno več kot pokrijejo vračila.
  • Pogoji, ki jih nihče ne more izpolniti. Garancija, skrita v drobnem tisku, deluje kot nezaupanje in verjetnost zniža, namesto da bi jo dvignila.
  • Pogoji, ki niso povezani z uspehom. Pogoji naj bodo dejanja, ki ustvarijo rezultat. Naključne ovire delujejo obrambno.
  • Nejasne obljube. »Zadovoljstvo zagotovljeno« ne pove, kaj, do kdaj in kaj se zgodi. Konkretnim garancijam ljudje verjamejo.
  • Anti-garancija brez razloga. Samo »brez vračil« zveni, kot da pričakuješ, da izdelek ne bo deloval.

Kako bi to uporabil Alex

  • Garancijo želi skoraj pri vsaki ponudbi, in navadno najmočnejšo, ki jo posel preživi.
  • Pogoje veže na vedenja, ki ustvarijo uspeh, tako da garancija hkrati služi kot način, da stranke opravijo delo.
  • Ima raje garancije storitve (»delamo naprej, dokler ne dosežete«), kadar bi vračilo bolelo, ker stanejo manj in obdržijo stranko.
  • Garancije zlaga, da hkrati odstrani več strahov, in vsaki da ime.
  • Delež vračil jemlje kot signal izvedbe. Če bi garancijo pogosto uveljavljali, najprej popravi izdelek, preden garancijo oslabi.
  • Dogovore po uspešnosti uporabi, ko so rezultati merljivi, ker kupcu odločitev naredijo skoraj brez tveganja, prodajalcu pa lahko prinesejo več.
  • Vprašanja, ki bi jih postavil: Česa se kupec pri nakupu najbolj boji? Kaj mora narediti za uspeh, in ali je to lahko tvoj pogoj? Kaj bi se zgodilo, če bi 10 % zahtevalo denar nazaj? Lahko namesto denarja obljubiš več dela? Kako bi jo poimenoval, da si jo ljudje zapomnijo?

Delovni list

Vzemi svojo glavno ponudbo in pisno oblikuj njeno garancijo.

  1. Strah: Česa se kupec najbolj boji: izgube denarja, izgubljenega časa, slabe podobe pred šefom?
  2. Dejanja za uspeh: Naštej tri do pet stvari, ki jih mora stranka narediti za rezultat. To postanejo tvoji pogoji.
  3. Vrsta: Katera vrsta ustreza: brezpogojna, pogojna, anti-garancija ali po uspešnosti? Zakaj?
  4. Stavek: Zapiši jo kot »Če narediš X in do [kdaj] ne dobiš Y, bomo naredili Z.«
  5. Zlaganje in ime: Dodaj drugo garancijo za drug strah. Obema daj ime.
  6. Stresni test: Če bi jo uveljavilo 10 % strank, koliko bi te stalo? Kaj bi popravil v izvedbi, da je ne bi?
