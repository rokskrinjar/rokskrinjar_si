ScarcityPomanjkanje

$100M Offers · Scarcity

Limiting how many people can get the offer makes it more desirable. In B2B your capacity is already limited, so stating it honestly is both true and persuasive.Če omejiš, koliko ljudi lahko dobi ponudbo, postane bolj zaželena. V B2B je tvoja kapaciteta že omejena, zato je pošteno sporočanje te omejitve hkrati resnično in prepričljivo.

The gist

People want more of what they can't easily get. When an offer is available to anyone at any time, there is no cost to waiting, so buyers wait. When only a few spots exist, saying "maybe later" carries the risk of losing the chance, and the fear of missing out pushes the decision forward.

Scarcity = a limit on quantity (how many can buy)

Urgency = a limit on time (until when they can buy)

Scarcity raises demand without changing what you deliver. It only works over time if it is real: if the "last three spots" are still open next month, buyers learn to ignore you. In a service business this is rarely a problem, because your hours, team and attention are genuinely limited.

How it works

Why it raises demand

Three effects work together. Limited things look more valuable, because others seem to want them too. A limit creates a cost to delaying, which breaks the default of "I'll think about it". And a selective offer signals quality: a provider who can turn clients away looks like one worth hiring. Scarcity often lets you raise the price as well, because demand exceeds supply.

Three ways to limit supply

Type What it means Example
Total limit Only a fixed number will ever exist or be served at once. "We work with at most eight manufacturing clients at a time."
Growth limit A cap on how many new clients you take per period. "We onboard two new implementation clients per month."
Cohort limit Clients start in groups, each with a fixed number of places. "The spring cohort has twelve places and starts on 3 March."

A related form is limiting a part of the offer instead of the whole: only the first five buyers get a bonus, such as a free data audit. The core offer stays open, but the extra is scarce.

Picking the right type

  • Total limit suits high-touch, premium services where quality depends on your personal involvement. It is the strongest signal, and it can raise price significantly.
  • Growth limit suits most B2B services. It reflects how onboarding actually works: new clients need the most attention in their first weeks, so you can only absorb a few at a time. It also works permanently, since there is always a next month.
  • Cohort limit suits programmes, training and group delivery. It combines scarcity (places) with urgency (start date).

Using it honestly

The limit must be true and you must stick to it. Practical rules:

  1. Calculate the real number. Start from your delivery capacity: hours per client in onboarding, people available, quality you want to keep. The cap comes from that, not from what sounds impressive.
  2. Explain the reason. "Each new client takes about forty hours of my time in the first month, so I start two per month" is believable because it's specific.
  3. Show the state honestly. Tell buyers how many spots are left in the current period, and keep a waiting list when they are gone.
  4. Enforce it. If you say two per month and sign a third, the limit stops meaning anything. Put the extra buyer on next month's start date.
  5. Never fake it. Fake countdowns or "only two left" that never change damage trust, and in many markets they also break consumer protection rules.

Examples

Examples are invented for illustration.

Qlik implementation partner (B2B analytics)

Before. "We can start whenever suits you." Prospects postponed for months, then several signed in the same week, the team was overloaded and two projects started late.

After. "We start two implementations per month, because the first four weeks need a senior developer almost full time. November is booked; December has one place left. If it fills, the next start is January." The waiting list is now a normal part of the sales process.

The growth limit is real (it comes from onboarding effort), it smooths the workload, and it gives prospects a concrete reason to decide now instead of next quarter.

Pilates studio (local business)

Before After
Offer Open drop-in classes, any time 8-week beginner cohort, max 10 people per group
Start Whenever Fixed start date each month
Reason given None Small group so the instructor can correct each person's form
Result Irregular attendance, many no-shows Groups fill before the start date, members stay together

The cohort limit is honest because form correction really does need small groups. It also adds a start date, so scarcity and urgency work together.

Common mistakes

  • Fake scarcity. Limits that never run out or reset every week teach buyers that your words mean nothing. One exposed fake costs more than any sale it brought.
  • No reason given. "Only three spots" sounds like a sales trick. "Only three spots because each needs a dedicated developer" sounds like a fact.
  • A cap that doesn't fit capacity. A limit far above what you can deliver doesn't create scarcity; a limit far below it leaves money on the table.
  • Breaking your own limit. Taking one more client "just this once" removes the credibility of the next limit you announce.
  • Confusing scarcity with urgency. Scarcity limits how many; urgency limits until when. Use both deliberately, not by accident.

How Alex would apply it

  • He starts from real capacity. The first question is how many clients the business can serve without results dropping, and the limit follows from that.
  • He prefers the growth limit for services, because it reflects onboarding reality and can be used every month without wearing out.
  • He states the reason and the remaining count openly, and treats the waiting list as proof of demand that also justifies raising prices.
  • He uses scarcity as a pricing signal: when spots consistently sell out, the right move is a higher price, not more spots squeezed in.
  • He combines it with urgency (cohorts with start dates, bonuses for the first few) to give buyers both a reason to want it and a reason to act now.
  • He refuses fake limits, because his business depends on long-term reputation and referrals more than on one more sale.
  • Questions he would ask: How many new clients can you onboard each month without delivery suffering? What happens today when several sign at once? Do you have a waiting list? When did you last turn someone away? Are you full often enough that the price should go up?

Worksheet

Work out a limit you can state honestly.

  1. Capacity: How many hours and which people does a new client need in the first month? How many can you absorb at once?
  2. Type: Which fits your delivery best: total limit, growth limit or cohort limit?
  3. Number and reason: Write the limit and the one-sentence reason you will give buyers.
  4. Visibility: How will buyers see how many spots are left, and what happens when they are gone?
  5. Price signal: If you fill every spot for three periods in a row, how much will you raise the price?

Bistvo

Ljudje si bolj želijo tistega, česar ne morejo zlahka dobiti. Ko je ponudba na voljo vsakomur kadarkoli, čakanje nič ne stane, zato kupci čakajo. Ko obstaja le nekaj mest, nosi »morda kasneje« tveganje, da priložnost izgubijo, strah pred zamujeno priložnostjo pa odločitev potisne naprej.

Pomanjkanje (scarcity) = omejitev količine (koliko jih lahko kupi)

Nujnost (urgency) = omejitev časa (do kdaj lahko kupijo)

Pomanjkanje dvigne povpraševanje, ne da bi spremenil, kar dostaviš. Dolgoročno deluje le, če je resnično: če so »zadnja tri mesta« naslednji mesec še vedno prosta, se kupci naučijo, da te ignorirajo. V storitvenem podjetju je to redko težava, ker so tvoje ure, ekipa in pozornost resnično omejene.

Kako deluje

Zakaj dvigne povpraševanje

Skupaj delujejo trije učinki. Omejene stvari so videti vrednejše, ker se zdi, da si jih želijo tudi drugi. Omejitev ustvari strošek odlašanja, kar prekine privzeti »bom še razmislil«. Selektivna ponudba pa sporoča kakovost: ponudnik, ki lahko stranke zavrne, je videti kot nekdo, ki ga je vredno najeti. Pomanjkanje ti pogosto omogoči tudi višjo ceno, ker povpraševanje presega ponudbo.

Trije načini omejevanja ponudbe

Vrsta Kaj pomeni Primer
Skupna omejitev (total limit) Obstaja ali se hkrati streže le fiksno število. »Hkrati delamo z največ osmimi proizvodnimi strankami.«
Omejitev rasti (growth limit) Omejitev, koliko novih strank sprejmeš na obdobje. »Na mesec uvedemo dve novi stranki za implementacijo.«
Omejitev kohorte (cohort) Stranke začnejo v skupinah, vsaka ima fiksno število mest. »Pomladna kohorta ima dvanajst mest in začne 3. marca.«

Sorodna oblika je omejitev dela ponudbe namesto celote: bonus, na primer brezplačno revizijo podatkov, dobi le prvih pet kupcev. Osnovna ponudba ostane odprta, dodatek pa je omejen.

Izbira prave vrste

  • Skupna omejitev ustreza premium storitvam z veliko osebnega dela, kjer je kakovost odvisna od tvoje osebne vpletenosti. Je najmočnejši signal in lahko ceno občutno dvigne.
  • Omejitev rasti ustreza večini B2B storitev. Odraža, kako uvajanje dejansko poteka: nove stranke potrebujejo največ pozornosti v prvih tednih, zato jih lahko naenkrat sprejmeš le nekaj. Deluje tudi trajno, saj vedno pride naslednji mesec.
  • Omejitev kohorte ustreza programom, izobraževanjem in skupinski dostavi. Združuje pomanjkanje (mesta) z nujnostjo (datum začetka).

Pošteno ravnanje s pomanjkanjem

Omejitev mora biti resnična in se je moraš držati. Praktična pravila:

  1. Izračunaj resnično številko. Začni pri kapaciteti dostave: ure na stranko pri uvajanju, razpoložljivi ljudje, kakovost, ki jo želiš ohraniti. Omejitev izhaja iz tega, ne iz tega, kar zveni impresivno.
  2. Pojasni razlog. »Vsaka nova stranka v prvem mesecu vzame okoli štirideset ur mojega časa, zato začnem dve na mesec« je verjetno, ker je konkretno.
  3. Pošteno pokaži stanje. Kupcem povej, koliko mest je še prostih v tekočem obdobju, in vodi čakalni seznam, ko jih zmanjka.
  4. Drži se je. Če rečeš dve na mesec in podpišeš tretjo, omejitev ne pomeni več ničesar. Dodatnega kupca postavi na začetek naslednjega meseca.
  5. Nikoli je ne ponaredi. Lažni odštevalniki ali »le še dva«, ki se nikoli ne spremeni, škodijo zaupanju, na mnogih trgih pa kršijo tudi pravila varstva potrošnikov.

Primeri

Primeri so izmišljeni in služijo ponazoritvi.

Qlik implementacijski partner (B2B analitika)

Pred. »Začnemo lahko, kadar vam ustreza.« Potencialne stranke so odlašale mesece, nato jih je več podpisalo v istem tednu, ekipa je bila preobremenjena in dva projekta sta začela pozno.

Po. »Na mesec začnemo dve implementaciji, ker prvi štirje tedni zahtevajo seniorja skoraj s polnim delovnim časom. November je zaseden, december ima še eno mesto. Če se zapolni, je naslednji začetek januarja.« Čakalni seznam je zdaj običajen del prodajnega procesa.

Omejitev rasti je resnična (izhaja iz napora pri uvajanju), zgladi obremenitev in potencialnim strankam da konkreten razlog, da se odločijo zdaj, ne naslednje četrtletje.

Pred Po

Omejitev kohorte je poštena, ker popravljanje drže res zahteva majhne skupine. Doda tudi datum začetka, tako da pomanjkanje in nujnost delujeta skupaj.

Pogoste napake

  • Lažno pomanjkanje. Omejitve, ki nikoli ne pošlejo ali se ponastavijo vsak teden, kupce naučijo, da tvoje besede ne pomenijo ničesar. En razkrit ponaredek stane več, kot je prinesla katerakoli prodaja.
  • Brez razloga. »Le tri mesta« zveni kot prodajni trik. »Le tri mesta, ker vsako potrebuje svojega razvijalca« zveni kot dejstvo.
  • Omejitev, ki se ne ujema s kapaciteto. Omejitev daleč nad tem, kar lahko dostaviš, ne ustvari pomanjkanja; omejitev daleč pod tem pusti denar na mizi.
  • Kršenje lastne omejitve. Če sprejmeš še eno stranko »samo tokrat«, izgubi verodostojnost naslednja omejitev, ki jo napoveš.
  • Mešanje pomanjkanja in nujnosti. Pomanjkanje omejuje koliko, nujnost do kdaj. Uporabi oboje namerno, ne po naključju.

Kako bi to uporabil Alex

  • Začne pri resnični kapaciteti. Prvo vprašanje je, koliko strank lahko podjetje streže, ne da bi rezultati padli, omejitev pa sledi iz tega.
  • Za storitve ima raje omejitev rasti, ker odraža resničnost uvajanja in jo lahko uporablja vsak mesec, ne da bi se obrabila.
  • Razlog in število prostih mest pove odkrito, čakalni seznam pa obravnava kot dokaz povpraševanja, ki upraviči tudi dvig cen.
  • Pomanjkanje uporablja kot cenovni signal: ko se mesta redno razprodajo, je pravi korak višja cena, ne več stisnjenih mest.
  • Kombinira ga z nujnostjo (kohorte z datumi začetka, bonusi za prve) in kupcem da razlog, da si ponudbo želijo, in razlog, da ukrepajo zdaj.
  • Lažne omejitve zavrača, ker je njegovo poslovanje bolj odvisno od dolgoročnega ugleda in priporočil kot od še ene prodaje.
  • Vprašanja, ki bi jih postavil: Koliko novih strank lahko vsak mesec uvedeš, ne da bi dostava trpela? Kaj se danes zgodi, ko jih več podpiše hkrati? Imaš čakalni seznam? Kdaj si nazadnje koga zavrnil? Si dovolj pogosto poln, da bi morala cena gor?

Delovni list

Določi omejitev, ki jo lahko pošteno sporočiš.

  1. Kapaciteta: Koliko ur in katere ljudi potrebuje nova stranka v prvem mesecu? Koliko jih lahko sprejmeš naenkrat?
  2. Vrsta: Kaj najbolj ustreza tvoji dostavi: skupna omejitev, omejitev rasti ali omejitev kohorte?
  3. Število in razlog: Zapiši omejitev in razlog v enem stavku, ki ga boš povedal kupcem.
  4. Vidnost: Kako bodo kupci videli, koliko mest je še prostih, in kaj se zgodi, ko jih zmanjka?
  5. Cenovni signal: Če tri obdobja zapored zapolniš vsa mesta, za koliko boš dvignil ceno?
