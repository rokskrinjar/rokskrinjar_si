$100M Offers · Starving Crowd

Pick a market that is already hungry for what you sell. The right market makes an average offer work, and the wrong one makes a great offer fail.Izberi trg, ki je že lačen tega, kar prodajaš. Pravi trg poskrbi, da deluje tudi povprečna ponudba, napačen pa pokoplje odlično.

The gist

Before you improve an offer, check who you are selling it to. If you sell food to a crowd that is starving, you don't need to be a brilliant salesperson or have the best recipe. If you sell to people who aren't hungry or can't pay, no copy and no script will save you.

Market > Offer > Persuasion

This is the order of importance. Most people invest the other way round: they polish sales scripts and ads first, tweak the offer second, and almost never question the market. Alex starts at the top. A good market forgives a mediocre offer and mediocre selling. A bad market punishes even excellent work.

How it works

The four criteria

A starving crowd meets four conditions. Score candidate markets on each one before you commit.

Criterion Question Weak sign Strong sign
Massive pain Must they solve this? "It would be nice to have" They already pay for bad workarounds
Purchasing power Can they pay, and can the person decide? Enthusiastic but broke Budget plus an owner who signs
Easy to target Can you find them by name? "All SMEs" A list from an association or registry
Growing Is demand rising? Shrinking industry New regulation, technology or habits pushing them

1. Massive pain

The buyer has to need the problem solved, not just like the idea. Pain is a cost they are already paying: lost orders, overtime, fines, embarrassment in front of the board. The best test is whether they already try to solve it themselves, badly and expensively, with spreadsheets, extra staff or contractors. If they do, the pain is real. Pain also drives price: the bigger the problem, the more a solution is worth.

2. Purchasing power

A market can be in great pain and have no money. People who want to start a business but have no income are a classic case: hungry, but unable to pay much. Purchasing power has two parts. They need the money, and the person you talk to must be able to decide. In B2B, a manager who feels the pain but has no budget authority is not yet a buyer.

3. Easy to target

You have to know where they gather and how to reach them: associations, trade fairs, online groups, industry registries, newsletters they read. Easy to target means you can build a list of real names, rather than hoping they find you. "Small businesses" is not targetable. "Plastics manufacturers in Slovenia with 50 or more employees" is.

4. Growing

In a growing market the number of buyers and their willingness to pay rise without any extra skill on your part. In a shrinking one, you fight harder every year for a smaller slice. A growing market is like swimming with the current. Look for forces outside your control that push demand up: new regulation, new technology, demographic change.

The three core markets and drilling down

Almost everything people pay for falls under one of three core markets: health, wealth and relationships. These needs never go away, which makes them a safe starting point. The money is not at that level, though. You drill down until you reach a specific group with a specific problem:

  1. Wealth
  2. B2B services
  3. Business analytics
  4. Analytics for manufacturers
  5. OEE and scrap reporting for mid-sized manufacturers

Each step narrows the market, makes the pain more specific and makes the buyer easier to find. You stop at the level where you can name the buyer, describe their problem in their words and build a list of the first hundred prospects. The commitment to that level is covered in Commit to a Niche.

When nothing works

If leads don't convert and the offer seems fine, go back up the hierarchy. Check the market first: are they in pain, can they pay, can you reach them, is demand growing? Fix the offer only after that, and the sales process last.

Examples

Examples are invented for illustration.

AI consulting: comparing three markets (B2B)

Scores from 1 to 5.

Market Pain Money Reach Growth Total
"Companies that want AI" 3 3 1 5 12
Micro businesses and sole traders 3 1 3 4 11
Distributors with 30 to 200 staff entering orders by hand 5 4 4 4 17

The first market is growing but can't be found by name and its pain is vague. The second is reachable but can't pay. The third is narrower, yet it has sharp pain (order errors and delays), a budget and clear places to find it (chamber of commerce, trade fairs, business registers). A consultant who commits to it can charge more because they speak exactly this buyer's language.

Dentist (local business)

Before. A private practice advertises "general dentistry for the whole family" and competes on price with publicly funded clinics.

After. "Implants for people over 50 who hide their smile." Massive pain (shame, trouble chewing), purchasing power (savings), easy to target (local media, retiree associations), and a market that grows as the population ages.

The practice still treats everyone, but builds its messaging, proof and offers around one group. Comparison with general clinics falls away, and revenue per patient rises.

Reporting SaaS

A generic reporting tool at €30 per user is compared with Power BI. The same platform sold as "monthly reporting for gym franchise owners", with sources pre-connected, industry KPIs and benchmarks across locations, sells at €300 per location. The market is smaller but hungrier, and it has no direct comparison.

Common mistakes

  • Fixing persuasion before the market. Rewriting ads and scripts for a market that isn't hungry wastes time. Check the market first.
  • Confusing enthusiasm with purchasing power. The warmest feedback often comes from people who can't pay. Interest is not budget.
  • Picking a market you can't find. If you can't list 100 specific companies or people, the market isn't defined well enough to target.
  • Staying at the top level. "Health" or "business owners" is too broad to have a specific pain you can speak to.
  • Fighting a shrinking market. Skill can't outrun a market where buyers are disappearing every year.

How Alex would apply it

  • He checks the market before the offer. When someone asks why their sales are slow, his first suspicion is the market, not the copy.
  • He wants all four criteria, and he weighs pain and money most. Rule of thumb: a market that scores badly on pain or purchasing power is rarely worth saving with a better offer.
  • He drills down from health, wealth or relationships until he can name the buyer and their exact problem.
  • He prefers buyers already paying for a bad solution. Existing spending proves both pain and money.
  • He prefers business buyers when he wants high prices. Companies usually have more money and a clearer return on a solved problem than individuals.
  • He picks one market and commits rather than spreading the same offer across several.
  • Questions he would ask: Who exactly is this for? What does the problem cost them today? How are they solving it now? Who signs the cheque? Where can you find the first hundred of them? Why will this market be bigger in three years?

Worksheet

Write down two or three candidate markets and score each from 1 to 5 on the four criteria.

  1. Pain: What does the unsolved problem cost this market today? How are they solving it themselves now?
  2. Money: Who approves the purchase, and what budget do they have for this kind of problem?
  3. Reach: Where can you find the first 100 names? Name three concrete sources.
  4. Growth: Why will this market be bigger, or need your solution more, in three years?
  5. Drill down: Write the path from health, wealth or relationships down to your market in four or five steps. Where do you stop, and why?

Bistvo

Preden izboljšuješ ponudbo, preveri, komu jo prodajaš. Če prodajaš hrano množici, ki strada, ne potrebuješ vrhunske prodajne veščine ne najboljšega recepta. Če prodajaš ljudem, ki niso lačni ali ne morejo plačati, te ne reši nobeno besedilo in nobena skripta.

Trg > Ponudba > Prepričevanje

To je vrstni red pomembnosti. Večina ljudi vlaga obratno: najprej loščijo prodajne skripte in oglase, nato popravljajo ponudbo, trga pa skoraj nikoli ne postavijo pod vprašaj. Alex začne na vrhu. Dober trg odpusti povprečno ponudbo in povprečno prodajo. Slab trg kaznuje tudi odlično delo.

Kako deluje

Štirje kriteriji

Lačna množica (starving crowd) izpolnjuje štiri pogoje. Preden se zavežeš, vsak možni trg oceni po vseh štirih.

Kriterij Vprašanje Šibek znak Močan znak
Velika bolečina Ali morajo to rešiti? »Bilo bi lepo imeti« Že plačujejo za slabe zasilne rešitve
Kupna moč Ali lahko plačajo in ali sogovornik odloča? Navdušeni, a brez denarja Proračun in lastnik, ki podpiše
Lahko dosegljivi Ali jih najdeš poimensko? »Vsa MSP« Seznam iz združenja ali registra
Rastoč trg Ali povpraševanje raste? Panoga se krči Nova regulativa, tehnologija ali navade jih potiskajo

1. Velika bolečina (massive pain)

Kupec mora problem nujno rešiti, ne le všeč mu mora biti ideja. Bolečina je strošek, ki ga že plačuje: izgubljena naročila, nadure, kazni, zadrega pred upravo. Najboljši test je, ali ga že poskuša rešiti sam, slabo in drago, s preglednicami, dodatnimi ljudmi ali zunanjimi izvajalci. Če ga, je bolečina resnična. Bolečina žene tudi ceno: večji ko je problem, več je vredna rešitev.

2. Kupna moč (purchasing power)

Trg ima lahko veliko bolečino in nič denarja. Ljudje, ki bi radi zagnali posel, pa nimajo prihodkov, so tipičen primer: lačni, a ne morejo plačati veliko. Kupna moč ima dva dela. Imeti morajo denar, oseba, s katero govoriš, pa mora imeti možnost odločiti. V B2B vodja, ki čuti bolečino, nima pa pooblastila za proračun, še ni kupec.

3. Lahko dosegljivi (easy to target)

Vedeti moraš, kje se zbirajo in kako jih doseči: združenja, sejmi, spletne skupine, panožni registri, novice, ki jih berejo. Lahko dosegljivi pomeni, da lahko sestaviš seznam resničnih imen, namesto da upaš, da bodo oni našli tebe. »Mala podjetja« niso dosegljiva. »Proizvajalci plastike v Sloveniji s 50 ali več zaposlenimi« so.

4. Rastoč trg (growing)

Na rastočem trgu število kupcev in njihova pripravljenost plačati rasteta brez dodatne spretnosti na tvoji strani. Na upadajočem se vsako leto bolj boriš za manjši kos. Rastoč trg je kot plavanje s tokom. Išči sile zunaj tvojega nadzora, ki dvigujejo povpraševanje: nova regulativa, nova tehnologija, demografske spremembe.

Trije osnovni trgi in spuščanje navzdol

Skoraj vse, za kar ljudje plačajo, sodi v enega od treh osnovnih trgov: zdravje (health), bogastvo (wealth) in odnosi (relationships). Te potrebe nikoli ne izginejo, zato so varno izhodišče. Denar pa ni na tej ravni. Spuščaš se navzdol, dokler ne prideš do specifične skupine s specifičnim problemom:

  1. Bogastvo
  2. B2B storitve
  3. Poslovna analitika
  4. Analitika za proizvajalce
  5. Poročanje o OEE in izmetu za srednje velike proizvajalce

Vsak korak zoži trg, naredi bolečino bolj specifično in kupca lažje najdljivega. Ustaviš se na ravni, kjer lahko kupca poimenuješ, opišeš njegov problem z njegovimi besedami in sestaviš seznam prvih sto potencialnih strank. Zaveza tej ravni je opisana v vnosu Zaveza niši.

Ko nič ne deluje

Če leadi ne kupujejo, ponudba pa se zdi v redu, pojdi nazaj po hierarhiji navzgor. Najprej preveri trg: ali jih boli, ali lahko plačajo, ali jih dosežeš, ali povpraševanje raste? Šele nato popravljaj ponudbo, prodajni proces pa na koncu.

Prvi trg raste, a ga ni mogoče poimensko najti, bolečina pa je nejasna. Drugi je dosegljiv, a ne more plačati. Tretji je ožji, vendar ima ostro bolečino (napake in zamude pri naročilih), proračun in jasna mesta, kjer ga najdeš (zbornica, sejmi, poslovni registri). Svetovalec, ki se mu zaveže, lahko zaračuna več, ker govori jezik prav tega kupca.

Zobozdravnik (lokalna storitev)

Pred. Zasebna ordinacija oglašuje »splošno zobozdravstvo za vso družino« in tekmuje s ceno koncesijskih ambulant.

Po. »Implantati za ljudi nad 50 let, ki skrivajo nasmeh.« Velika bolečina (sram, težave pri žvečenju), kupna moč (prihranki), lahka dosegljivost (lokalni mediji, društva upokojencev) in trg, ki raste s staranjem prebivalstva.

Ordinacija še vedno sprejme vse, a sporočila, dokaze in ponudbe gradi okoli ene skupine. Primerjava s splošnimi ambulantami odpade, prihodek na pacienta pa naraste.

SaaS za poročanje

Splošno orodje za poročila po 30 € na uporabnika se primerja s Power BI. Ista platforma, prodana kot »mesečno poročanje za lastnike franšiz fitnes centrov«, z vnaprej povezanimi viri, panožnimi KPI-ji in primerjavo med lokacijami, se prodaja po 300 € na lokacijo. Trg je manjši, a lačnejši in brez neposredne primerjave.

Pogoste napake

  • Popravljanje prepričevanja pred trgom. Prepisovanje oglasov in skript za trg, ki ni lačen, je izguba časa. Najprej preveri trg.
  • Mešanje navdušenja s kupno močjo. Najtoplejše odzive pogosto dajo ljudje, ki ne morejo plačati. Zanimanje ni proračun.
  • Trg, ki ga ne znaš najti. Če ne moreš našteti 100 konkretnih podjetij ali ljudi, trg ni dovolj definiran, da bi ga ciljal.
  • Ostajanje na najvišji ravni. »Zdravje« ali »lastniki podjetij« je preširoko, da bi imelo specifično bolečino, ki jo lahko nagovoriš.
  • Boj z upadajočim trgom. Spretnost ne more prehiteti trga, na katerem kupci vsako leto izginjajo.

Kako bi to uporabil Alex

  • Trg preveri pred ponudbo. Ko ga nekdo vpraša, zakaj prodaja stoji, najprej posumi na trg, ne na besedilo.
  • Želi vse štiri kriterije, največjo težo pa da bolečini in denarju. Pravilo palca: trga, ki je slab pri bolečini ali kupni moči, se redko splača reševati z boljšo ponudbo.
  • Spušča se od zdravja, bogastva ali odnosov navzdol, dokler ne zna poimenovati kupca in njegovega točnega problema.
  • Raje ima kupce, ki že plačujejo za slabo rešitev. Obstoječa poraba dokazuje bolečino in denar hkrati.
  • Za visoke cene raje izbere poslovne kupce. Podjetja imajo navadno več denarja in jasnejši donos rešenega problema kot posamezniki.
  • Izbere en trg in se mu zaveže, namesto da bi isto ponudbo razpršil na več trgov.
  • Vprašanja, ki bi jih postavil: Za koga točno je to? Koliko jih problem stane danes? Kako ga rešujejo zdaj? Kdo podpiše? Kje najdeš prvih sto? Zakaj bo ta trg čez tri leta večji?

Delovni list

Zapiši dva ali tri možne trge in vsakega oceni od 1 do 5 po štirih kriterijih.

  1. Bolečina: Koliko ta trg danes stane, ker problem ni rešen? Kako ga zdaj rešujejo sami?
  2. Denar: Kdo odobri nakup in kakšen proračun ima za tak problem?
  3. Dosegljivost: Kje najdeš prvih 100 imen? Navedi tri konkretne vire.
  4. Rast: Zakaj bo ta trg čez tri leta večji ali bolj potreboval tvojo rešitev?
  5. Spust: Zapiši pot od zdravja, bogastva ali odnosov do svojega trga v štirih ali petih korakih. Kje se ustaviš in zakaj?
