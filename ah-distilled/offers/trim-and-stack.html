$100M Offers · Trim & Stack

Score every solution and delivery option by value to the buyer and cost to you, cut the costly low-value ones, and stack what remains into one offer whose total value clearly exceeds the price.Vsako rešitev in način dostave oceni po vrednosti za kupca in strošku zate, odreži drage z nizko vrednostjo, preostanek pa zloži v eno ponudbo, katere skupna vrednost jasno presega ceno.

The gist

After listing problems and brainstorming delivery vehicles, you have far more possible components than any offer should contain. Trim and stack is the editing step. You judge every combination of solution and delivery on two things only: how much the buyer values it and how much it costs you to deliver.

Trim: cut high cost / low value · keep low cost / high value · keep the few critical high cost / high value pieces

Stack: bundle what remains, name and value each piece, show the total value next to the price

The result is one offer made of many named components. When the buyer sees each piece with its own value, the sum is much larger than the price, and comparing it with a competitor's single line item stops making sense.

How it works

The 2×2: value versus cost

Place every candidate component in one of four boxes.

Low cost to you High cost to you
High value to buyer Keep, always. Templates, recordings, checklists, automations. The core of your margin. Keep only if critical. Done-for-you work, 1:1 time, fast access. Use where it changes the outcome; consider a premium tier.
Low value to buyer Optional. Keep only if it doesn't clutter the offer. Often better left out. Cut. Costs you a lot and the buyer barely notices it.

"Value" here is value as the buyer perceives it, measured with the value equation: does the piece make the outcome bigger, more likely, faster or easier? A piece that moves none of those is noise.

How to trim

  1. Put every solution and delivery option from the previous steps in one list.
  2. Score each one on value (1 to 5) and cost (1 to 5). Use real delivery cost: hours, people, tools.
  3. Remove everything high cost and low value first. This is often what you do today out of habit (long kick-off workshops, weekly status meetings, custom reports nobody reads).
  4. Keep all low cost / high value pieces.
  5. From the high cost / high value box, keep only what the outcome really depends on. Move the rest to an upgrade.
  6. Check for gaps: does every major obstacle from your list still have at least one solution in the offer?

How to stack

  1. Group the kept pieces into a clear sequence, usually following the buyer's timeline (start, delivery, after).
  2. Name each piece so it describes the result it gives, not the activity ("Board-ready first month" instead of "presentation support").
  3. Assign a value to each piece. Base it on what the buyer would reasonably pay for it alone, or what solving that problem is worth to them. Keep it defensible: inflated numbers lower trust.
  4. Show the total next to the price. The gap between total value and price is what makes saying no feel unreasonable.

Why stacking works

A single price for a single thing invites comparison: hourly rate against hourly rate. A stack of named solutions has no direct equivalent on the market. The buyer can't find someone else offering the same ten pieces, so they judge the offer on value instead of price.

Examples

Examples are invented for illustration.

Month-end reporting offer (B2B analytics)

Trimming the candidate list:

Component Value Cost Decision
Three ready-made finance apps (reused template) High Low Keep
Recorded how-to library Medium-high Low Keep
Monthly group clinic Medium-high Low Keep
ERP connection and data model High High Keep (critical)
Master-data clean-up High High Keep (critical)
Weekly 1:1 status call Low High Cut
Two-day on-site kick-off workshop Low High Cut
Any new chart built within 24 hours Very high Very high Premium tier

The stack shown to the buyer:

Component Value
ERP connection and finance data model €6,000
Master-data clean-up €3,000
Three ready-made apps: margin, cash, month-end €7,500
Week-one reconciliation against your Excel €1,500
Six months of monthly group clinics €1,800
Library of recorded how-tos €1,200
Board-ready first month presentation template €1,000
90 days of support, next business day response €2,000
Total value €24,000
Price €9,800

Cutting the status calls and the on-site workshop removed a large share of delivery hours. The kept pieces solve more obstacles than the old offer, and the buyer sees €24,000 of value for €9,800.

Bakery wedding cakes (local business)

Before. "Wedding cake, three tiers, from €450." Plus a free one-hour tasting session in the shop for every enquiry, which cost the owner a lot of Saturday time and converted poorly.

After. The tasting became a pre-packed tasting box couples collect (low cost, high value). The bakery added a photo catalogue to pick a design, delivery and set-up at the venue, a matching dessert table option and a printed cake-cutting guide. Each piece has its own value in the offer, and the total is shown next to the price.

The expensive, low-converting piece was replaced by a cheaper one that couples liked more. The venue set-up stayed because it is the thing couples worry about most.

Common mistakes

  • Keeping things because you always did them. Habitual pieces such as weekly status meetings often land in the high cost / low value box. Check them first.
  • Stacking filler. Pieces that solve no real obstacle make the offer look padded and lower trust.
  • Inflated values. Claiming that a checklist is worth €5,000 makes the buyer doubt every other number. Values should be ones the buyer would accept.
  • Cutting the critical expensive piece. If the outcome depends on a done-for-you step, removing it to save cost breaks the result and the guarantee.
  • Stacking without showing. A long list of components with one lump price hides the value. Each piece needs a name and a number.

How Alex would apply it

  • He cuts before he adds. His first pass looks for what costs the most and moves the outcome least, because that improves margin and value at the same time.
  • He hunts for low cost / high value pieces and multiplies them: templates, recordings, scripts, checklists, software.
  • He protects the pieces the outcome depends on, even when they are expensive, and raises the price to fund them instead of removing them.
  • He moves expensive extras into a higher tier rather than giving them to everyone.
  • He presents the stack in the buyer's order of concern and names each piece after the result it delivers.
  • He wants a clear gap between total value and price, as a rule of thumb large enough that the price feels small next to it, but every number must be believable.
  • Questions he would ask: What do you deliver today that clients never mention? Which piece costs you the most hours? Which cheap piece do clients thank you for? If you removed one component, which would they miss? Does every major obstacle still have a solution in the stack?

Worksheet

Use your delivery options from the previous step.

  1. Score: Rate every component on value to buyer and cost to you, 1 to 5.
  2. Cut: List everything high cost / low value. What would you save by removing it?
  3. Protect: Which high cost / high value pieces does the outcome truly depend on?
  4. Upgrade: Which expensive pieces could move to a premium tier?
  5. Stack: Write the final list with a result-based name and a defensible value for each piece. Add up the total.
  6. Compare: Put total value next to your price. Is the gap large enough that saying no feels unreasonable?

Bistvo

Ko si naštel probleme in zbral ideje za načine dostave, imaš veliko več možnih sestavnih delov, kot jih sme vsebovati katerakoli ponudba. Odreži in zloži (trim & stack) je korak urejanja. Vsako kombinacijo rešitve in dostave presodiš le po dveh stvareh: koliko jo ceni kupec in koliko te stane, da jo dostaviš.

Odreži: odstrani visok strošek / nizko vrednost · obdrži nizek strošek / visoko vrednost · obdrži nekaj ključnih kosov z visokim stroškom in visoko vrednostjo

Zloži: preostanek zloži v paket, vsak kos poimenuj in ovrednoti, skupno vrednost pokaži ob ceni

Rezultat je ena ponudba iz veliko poimenovanih sestavnih delov. Ko kupec vidi vsak kos z njegovo vrednostjo, je vsota veliko večja od cene, primerjava s konkurentovo eno samo postavko pa izgubi smisel.

Kako deluje

Matrika 2×2: vrednost proti strošku

Vsak kandidat za sestavni del postavi v eno od štirih polj.

Nizek strošek zate Visok strošek zate
Visoka vrednost za kupca Vedno obdrži. Predloge, posnetki, kontrolni seznami, avtomatizacije. Jedro tvoje marže. Obdrži le, če je ključno. Delo done-for-you, 1:1 čas, hiter dostop. Uporabi tam, kjer spremeni izid; razmisli o premium nadgradnji.
Nizka vrednost za kupca Neobvezno. Obdrži le, če ne zamegli ponudbe. Pogosto je bolje izpustiti. Odreži. Stane te veliko, kupec pa ga komaj opazi.

»Vrednost« je tu vrednost, kot jo zaznava kupec, merjena z enačbo vrednosti: ali kos naredi izid večji, verjetnejši, hitrejši ali lažji? Kos, ki ne premakne ničesar od tega, je šum.

Kako odrezati

  1. Vse rešitve in možnosti dostave iz prejšnjih korakov zberi na enem seznamu.
  2. Vsako oceni po vrednosti (od 1 do 5) in strošku (od 1 do 5). Uporabi resnični strošek dostave: ure, ljudi, orodja.
  3. Najprej odstrani vse z visokim stroškom in nizko vrednostjo. Pogosto je to tisto, kar danes delaš iz navade (dolge uvodne delavnice, tedenski statusni sestanki, poročila po meri, ki jih nihče ne bere).
  4. Obdrži vse kose z nizkim stroškom in visoko vrednostjo.
  5. Iz polja visok strošek / visoka vrednost obdrži le tisto, od česar je izid res odvisen. Ostalo prestavi v nadgradnjo.
  6. Preveri vrzeli: ali ima vsaka večja ovira s tvojega seznama v ponudbi še vedno vsaj eno rešitev?

Kako zložiti

  1. Razvrsti obdržane kose v jasno zaporedje, navadno po kupčevi časovnici (začetek, izvedba, potem).
  2. Poimenuj vsak kos tako, da opiše rezultat, ki ga da, ne dejavnosti (»Prvi mesec, pripravljen za upravo« namesto »podpora pri predstavitvi«).
  3. Pripiši vrednost vsakemu kosu. Osnova je to, kar bi kupec zanj razumno plačal samostojno, ali koliko mu je vredna rešitev tega problema. Naj bo zagovorljiva: napihnjene številke znižajo zaupanje.
  4. Pokaži skupno vrednost ob ceni. Razlika med skupno vrednostjo in ceno je tisto, zaradi česar se zavrnitev zdi nerazumna.

Zakaj zlaganje deluje

Ena cena za eno stvar vabi k primerjavi: urna postavka proti urni postavki. Sklad poimenovanih rešitev na trgu nima neposrednega ekvivalenta. Kupec ne najde nikogar, ki bi ponujal istih deset kosov, zato ponudbo presoja po vrednosti, ne po ceni.

Poročne torte v pekarni (lokalna storitev)

Pred. »Poročna torta, tri nadstropja, od 450 €.« Poleg tega brezplačna enourna degustacija v trgovini za vsako povpraševanje, ki je lastnico stala veliko sobotnega časa in se je slabo spreminjala v naročila.

Po. Degustacija je postala vnaprej pripravljena degustacijska škatla, ki jo par prevzame (nizek strošek, visoka vrednost). Pekarna je dodala foto katalog za izbiro dizajna, dostavo in postavitev na prizorišču, možnost ujemajoče se sladice mize in natisnjen vodič za rezanje torte. Vsak kos ima v ponudbi svojo vrednost, skupna vrednost pa je prikazana ob ceni.

Drag kos z nizko konverzijo je zamenjal cenejši, ki je bil parom bolj všeč. Postavitev na prizorišču je ostala, ker je to tisto, kar pare najbolj skrbi.

Pogoste napake

  • Ohranjanje stvari, ker si jih vedno delal. Kosi iz navade, kot so tedenski statusni sestanki, pogosto pristanejo v polju visok strošek / nizka vrednost. Preveri jih najprej.
  • Zlaganje polnila. Kosi, ki ne rešujejo nobene resnične ovire, naredijo ponudbo napihnjeno in znižajo zaupanje.
  • Napihnjene vrednosti. Trditev, da je kontrolni seznam vreden 5.000 €, povzroči, da kupec podvomi v vse druge številke. Vrednosti naj bodo take, ki bi jih kupec sprejel.
  • Rezanje ključnega dragega kosa. Če je izid odvisen od koraka done-for-you, njegova odstranitev zaradi prihranka uniči rezultat in garancijo.
  • Zlaganje brez prikaza. Dolg seznam sestavnih delov z eno skupno ceno skrije vrednost. Vsak kos potrebuje ime in številko.

Kako bi to uporabil Alex

  • Najprej reže, potem dodaja. Pri prvem prehodu išče tisto, kar stane največ in izid premakne najmanj, ker to hkrati izboljša maržo in vrednost.
  • Lovi kose z nizkim stroškom in visoko vrednostjo in jih množi: predloge, posnetke, skripte, kontrolne sezname, programsko opremo.
  • Varuje kose, od katerih je izid odvisen, tudi ko so dragi, in raje dvigne ceno, da jih financira, kot da bi jih odstranil.
  • Drage dodatke prestavi v višji paket, namesto da bi jih dal vsem.
  • Sklad predstavi v vrstnem redu kupčevih skrbi in vsak kos poimenuje po rezultatu, ki ga prinese.
  • Želi jasno razliko med skupno vrednostjo in ceno, po pravilu palca dovolj veliko, da je cena ob njej videti majhna, vendar mora biti vsaka številka verjetna.
  • Vprašanja, ki bi jih postavil: Kaj danes dostavljaš, česar stranke nikoli ne omenijo? Kateri kos te stane največ ur? Za kateri poceni kos se ti stranke zahvaljujejo? Če bi odstranil en sestavni del, katerega bi pogrešale? Ali ima vsaka večja ovira v skladu še vedno rešitev?

Delovni list

Uporabi svoje možnosti dostave iz prejšnjega koraka.

  1. Oceni: Vsak sestavni del oceni po vrednosti za kupca in strošku zate, od 1 do 5.
  2. Odreži: Naštej vse z visokim stroškom in nizko vrednostjo. Koliko bi prihranil z odstranitvijo?
  3. Zavaruj: Od katerih kosov z visokim stroškom in visoko vrednostjo je izid res odvisen?
  4. Nadgradnja: Katere drage kose bi lahko prestavil v premium paket?
  5. Zloži: Zapiši končni seznam z imenom po rezultatu in zagovorljivo vrednostjo za vsak kos. Seštej skupno vrednost.
  6. Primerjaj: Skupno vrednost postavi ob ceno. Je razlika dovolj velika, da se zavrnitev zdi nerazumna?
