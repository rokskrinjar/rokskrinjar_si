The Value EquationEnačba vrednosti

$100M Offers · Value Equation

How a buyer decides, in their head, whether something is worth its price. Push two levers up and two down.Kako kupec v glavi oceni, ali je nekaj vredno svoje cene. Dva vzvoda dvigneš, dva spustiš.

The gist

People don't buy a product, they buy a change. An offer is worth as much as that change is big and believable, divided by how long it takes and how much pain it costs to get there.

Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)

Most sellers only compete on the top line: a bigger promise. The bigger opportunity is usually the bottom line. If the time to a result and the work for the buyer get close to zero, value grows very large, and so does what you can charge.

How it works

Dream Outcome ↑

The result the buyer actually wants, in their words and emotions, not your features. It is often about status: how others will see them afterwards. "A dashboard" is not a dream outcome. "You answer every board question in ten seconds" is.

Perceived Likelihood of Achievement ↑

How strongly the buyer believes it will work for them. The key word is perceived: it is not enough that it works, they have to believe it. Proof (client results, numbers), guarantees, specificity ("for manufacturers with 50 to 200 employees") and a clearly described process all raise it.

Time Delay ↓

The time from paying to the first felt result, not the final one. People pay far more for speed. You often can't speed up the end result, but you can design a quick win in the first days that proves the decision was right.

Effort & Sacrifice ↓

Everything the buyer has to do or give up: learning, their time, new habits, and the emotional cost of trying and failing. This is why done-for-you costs more than do-it-yourself even when the knowledge is identical.

Why the bottom line matters most

The two top levers have a ceiling: a promise can only grow so big before it stops being believable. The two bottom levers can be pushed toward zero. Removing work and waiting is often cheaper for you to deliver than a bigger result, and buyers value it more.

Examples

Examples are invented for illustration.

BI dashboard implementation (B2B analytics)

Before. "We'll build your Qlik dashboards. The project takes three months. We need system access and your people in workshops."

After. "In ten days you'll have your margin-by-customer report, the one you now build by hand in Excel. Your team spends four hours in total. If it isn't in use within ten days, you don't pay for phase one."

The dream outcome is concrete (a report they already know), time delay drops from three months to ten days, effort drops from workshops to four hours, and a conditional guarantee raises likelihood.

Personal trainer (local service)

Before. "Monthly membership with gym access and a workout plan."

After. "Down 6 kg by summer. Weekly shopping list and recipes, three 30-minute workouts, a weekly weigh-in with your coach. First change on the scale within 14 days."

Recipes and the list cut effort, 30 minutes cuts time sacrifice, weigh-ins raise likelihood, and the 14-day marker shortens the perceived delay.

Common mistakes

  • Inflating the promise. A bigger outcome without proof lowers likelihood. A promise the buyer doesn't believe is worth nothing.
  • Selling the process instead of the result. Hours, modules and features are costs to the buyer. More steps usually means more effort.
  • Ignoring the denominator. Everyone competes on outcome. A faster start and less work for the client is often cheaper for you and worth more to them.
  • Confusing actual and perceived likelihood. Great results nobody knows about don't raise the value of your offer. Proof has to be shown.

How Alex would apply it

  • He starts from the bottom of the fraction. His first question is rarely "how do we promise more?" but "how do we make this faster and easier for them?"
  • He looks for the fastest felt win and builds the first week of delivery around it, because early proof lowers refunds and raises referrals.
  • He sells the outcome in the buyer's words and moves the mechanics (hours, deliverables, process) to the back.
  • He raises likelihood with proof before he raises the promise: case studies, specific numbers, a narrower target customer, and a guarantee.
  • He prices from value, not cost. When value goes up on all four levers, he expects the price to go up too.
  • Questions he would ask: What does the client really want, and how will others see them when they get it? What would make a sceptic believe it? What can they get in week one? What do they have to do that you could do for them?

Worksheet

Take your main offer and answer in writing. Score each lever 1 to 10 as it stands today.

  1. Outcome: How would the client describe what they want, in their own words? How will others see them afterwards?
  2. Likelihood: Which three proofs would most help a sceptic believe? Which do you already have?
  3. Delay: What is the fastest visible win the client can get in week one?
  4. Effort: List everything the client has to do. Which of it can you take over or remove?
  5. Rewrite: Write a one-sentence version of the offer that improves the two weakest levers.

Bistvo

Ljudje ne kupujejo izdelka, kupujejo spremembo. Ponudba je vredna toliko, kolikor je ta sprememba velika in verjetna, deljeno s tem, koliko časa traja in koliko muke stane, da do nje pridejo.

Vrednost = (sanjski izid × zaznana verjetnost uspeha) ÷ (časovni zamik × trud in žrtvovanje)

Večina prodajalcev tekmuje samo v števcu: z večjo obljubo. Večja priložnost je navadno v imenovalcu. Če se čas do rezultata in delo za kupca približata nič, vrednost zelo zraste, z njo pa tudi to, kar lahko zaračunaš.

Kako deluje

Sanjski izid (Dream Outcome) ↑

Rezultat, ki si ga kupec v resnici želi, z njegovimi besedami in čustvi, ne s tvojimi funkcijami. Pogosto gre za status: kako ga bodo drugi videli potem. »Nadzorna plošča« ni sanjski izid. »Na vsako vprašanje uprave odgovoriš v desetih sekundah« je.

Zaznana verjetnost uspeha (Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) ↑

Kako močno kupec verjame, da bo pri njem delovalo. Ključna beseda je zaznana: ni dovolj, da deluje, kupec mora to verjeti. Dvigujejo jo dokazi (rezultati strank, številke), garancije, specifičnost (»za proizvodna podjetja s 50 do 200 zaposlenimi«) in jasno opisan proces.

Časovni zamik (Time Delay) ↓

Čas od plačila do prvega občutenega rezultata, ne do končnega. Ljudje za hitrost plačajo bistveno več. Končnega rezultata pogosto ne moreš pospešiti, lahko pa načrtuješ hitro zmago v prvih dneh, ki dokaže, da je bila odločitev pravilna.

Trud in žrtvovanje (Effort & Sacrifice) ↓

Vse, kar mora kupec narediti ali čemur se mora odpovedati: učenje, njegov čas, nove navade in čustveni strošek poskusa, ki bi lahko spodletel. Zato »naredimo namesto vas« (done-for-you) stane več kot »naredite sami« (do-it-yourself), čeprav je znanje enako.

Zakaj je imenovalec najpomembnejši

Vzvoda v števcu imata strop: obljuba lahko zraste le do točke, ko ji ljudje še verjamejo. Vzvoda v imenovalcu lahko potisneš proti nič. Odstranjevanje dela in čakanja je zate pogosto cenejše od večjega rezultata, kupec pa ga ceni bolj.

Primeri

Primeri so izmišljeni in služijo ponazoritvi.

Implementacija BI nadzorne plošče (B2B analitika)

Pred. »Postavimo vam Qlik nadzorne plošče. Projekt traja tri mesece. Potrebujemo dostop do sistemov in vaše ljudi na delavnicah.«

Po. »V desetih dneh imate poročilo o marži po kupcih, ki ga zdaj delate ročno v Excelu. Vaša ekipa porabi skupaj štiri ure. Če v desetih dneh ni v uporabi, prve faze ne plačate.«

Sanjski izid je konkreten (poročilo, ki ga že poznajo), časovni zamik pade s treh mesecev na deset dni, trud z delavnic na štiri ure, verjetnost pa dviguje pogojna garancija.

Osebni trener (lokalna storitev)

Pred. »Mesečna članarina z dostopom do fitnesa in planom vadbe.«

Po. »Do poletja 6 kg manj. Tedenski nakupovalni seznam in recepti, tri 30-minutne vadbe, tedensko tehtanje s trenerjem. Prva sprememba na tehtnici v 14 dneh.«

Recepti in seznam znižajo trud, 30 minut zniža žrtvovanje časa, tehtanja dvigujejo verjetnost, mejnik 14 dni pa skrajša zaznani zamik.

Pogoste napake

  • Napihovanje obljube. Večji izid brez dokazov zniža verjetnost. Obljuba, ki ji kupec ne verjame, nima vrednosti.
  • Prodaja procesa namesto rezultata. Ure, moduli in funkcije so za kupca strošek. Več korakov navadno pomeni več truda.
  • Ignoriranje imenovalca. Vsi tekmujejo na izidu. Hitrejši začetek in manj dela za stranko je zate pogosto cenejše, zanjo pa vrednejše.
  • Mešanje dejanske in zaznane verjetnosti. Odlični rezultati, za katere nihče ne ve, ne dvignejo vrednosti ponudbe. Dokaze moraš pokazati.

Kako bi to uporabil Alex

  • Začne pri imenovalcu. Njegovo prvo vprašanje je redko »kako obljubimo več?«, ampak »kako to zanje naredimo hitrejše in lažje?«.
  • Poišče najhitrejšo občuteno zmago in okoli nje zgradi prvi teden izvedbe, ker zgodnji dokaz zniža vračila in poveča priporočila.
  • Izid proda z besedami kupca, mehaniko (ure, dobavljive, proces) pa prestavi na konec.
  • Verjetnost dvigne z dokazi, preden dvigne obljubo: primeri strank, konkretne številke, ožja ciljna stranka in garancija.
  • Ceno postavi po vrednosti, ne po stroških. Ko vrednost zraste na vseh štirih vzvodih, pričakuje tudi višjo ceno.
  • Vprašanja, ki bi jih postavil: Kaj si stranka res želi in kako jo bodo videli drugi, ko to dobi? Kaj bi prepričalo skeptika? Kaj lahko dobi v prvem tednu? Kaj mora narediti sama, kar bi lahko naredil ti?

Delovni list

Vzemi svojo glavno ponudbo in odgovori pisno. Vsak vzvod oceni od 1 do 10, kot je danes.

  1. Izid: Kako bi stranka z lastnimi besedami opisala, kaj želi? Kako jo bodo drugi videli potem?
  2. Verjetnost: Kateri trije dokazi bi skeptiku najbolj pomagali verjeti? Katere imaš že danes?
  3. Zamik: Katera je najhitrejša vidna zmaga, ki jo stranka lahko dobi v prvem tednu?
  4. Trud: Naštej vse, kar mora stranka narediti. Kaj od tega lahko prevzameš ali odpraviš?
  5. Prepis: Napiši ponudbo v enem stavku, ki izboljša dva najšibkejša vzvoda.
