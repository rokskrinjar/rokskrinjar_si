Alex's Playbook · Business Math (LTGP, LTV:CAC, payback)

The handful of numbers Alex runs in his head on every business, and the benchmarks he compares them to. Choosing between options becomes a math problem.Peščica številk, ki jih Alex na pamet izračuna za vsako podjetje, in mejniki, s katerimi jih primerja. Izbira med možnostmi postane matematični problem.

The gist

Alex treats business math as a language: you can only think as clearly about a business as your vocabulary for its numbers allows. In conversation he runs the same small set of calculations before he advises anyone: what a customer is worth in gross profit, what it costs to get one, how fast that cost comes back, and what the goal implies in leads, calls and budget.

There are only two ways to grow: more customers, or more value per customer. He keeps finding that the second is easier. Since acquisition cost for a given customer is roughly the same for every competitor in a market, the lasting advantage goes to whoever makes a customer worth the most.

LTGP = (price ÷ monthly churn) × gross margin · Max CAC = LTGP · Client-financed when 30-day cash collected > CAC + cost to deliver

How it works

Two growth levers

Every improvement should move one of a few levers: more customers, more lifetime gross profit per customer, or lower risk that the earnings continue. In another talk he names four: traffic, conversion, price and churn, and says a task that does not ladder up to one of them should not be done.

His "genie" question makes the point. If you could double new customers, price, or purchases per customer, which would you pick? At a 20% margin, doubling customers doubles profit, while doubling price gives about six times the profit, because the extra revenue is all margin. He also prefers doubling retention over doubling new customers: profit ends up similar, but the higher lifetime value lets you outspend competitors for the next customer.

Lifetime gross profit, not revenue

He insists that lifetime value means lifetime gross profit (LTGP): price minus the cost to deliver, times the number of purchases or months. Revenue overstates it. Fixed overhead such as rent and non-delivery staff stays out. One example he uses: a $10 meal that costs $9 to deliver, bought with $5 of acquisition cost, looks like 2:1 on revenue and is really $5 paid to earn $1.

His back-of-napkin version:

  1. Churn = cancellations this month ÷ active customers.
  2. LTV = price ÷ churn.
  3. LTGP = LTV × gross margin.
  4. Revenue cap = new sales per month × LTV. Growth flattens as churn eats new sales.

A young business can estimate it from one month: if 9 of 10 customers stay, churn is 10% and LTV is price ÷ 0.10. He tells owners to calculate it conservatively and round down.

Acquisition cost and the ratio

CAC is marketing payroll plus media plus sales commissions over a period, divided by new customers, ideally over a full year. The ratio he wants depends on how many humans sit in lead generation, sales and delivery: about 3:1 if all three are automated, rising to 6:1, 9:1 and 12:1 or more as people are added, because new hires ramp slowly and CAC only rises over time. He optimises the ratio rather than the cheapest lead: $5,000 LTV at $500 CAC beats $1,000 at $200.

30-day cash vs CAC

His payback target is 30 days. That is about as long as a small business can carry the cost, and it matches interest-free card terms. When gross profit collected in the first 30 days exceeds the cost to acquire and deliver, growth stops being limited by cash. If payback is longer, he looks for setup fees, prepaid terms, bundled products or upsells inside the first month.

Backward math from the goal

He works targets backwards. In one worked example: 300 members at 10% monthly churn means 30 replacements a month just to stay flat. At 50% front-end conversion that is 60 front-end sales, at a 50% close rate 120 conversations, at a 50% show rate about 240 bookings, which took roughly 15 leads a day. At about $10 a lead the budget comes to around $4,500 a month, and growth means doubling it.

Benchmarks he states

These are his rules of thumb from many videos, with the context they came in. He also warns against industry averages as targets and asks instead whether physics forbids a better result.

Metric His rule of thumb
Close rate 80%+ means underpriced (he says by 3–4x); 30–40% is about right with pre-call education; under 30% means fix the avatar and sales process before touching price
Show rate ~25%+ for a cold webinar; a small paid upfront fee often gives 70–80%; ~80% means lead handling is fine
Opt-in ~30% on a lead-magnet page; qualified pages 20–50%
Webinar booking ~20% of attendees with a book-a-call CTA
Gross margin 80%+ for services; he will not invest below that; delivery cost about a fifth of price
Retention monthly churn 20%+ in months 1–3, about 10% after day 90, about 2% after month six; B2B annual retention above 80%
Payback CAC back within 30 days; let a new ad campaign spend up to 2x allowable CAC before cutting it

ROAS vs absolute profit

He optimises for absolute profit. A lower return ratio on much larger spend can net far more money, so a falling ratio is no reason to stop. In one hotseat with search ads returning 9 to 24 times spend, he said he would keep raising spend until it reached about 5:1.

Examples

Cases are paraphrased and anonymised from Alex's videos. Any example marked as invented is for illustration only.

Subscription service at $1,000 a month

The owner had 380 clients, 100 new sales a month and 50 cancellations, and did not know churn. Alex: 50 ÷ 380 is about 13% churn, LTV about $7,700, LTGP about $7,000 at a 90% margin. Cap: 100 × $7,700 is about $770,000 a month. Cutting churn to about 3% lifts LTV to roughly $33,000 and the cap to about $3.3M a month, which is far easier than ten times the lead flow.

Duct-cleaning service, 38% net margin

Before. About $1.25M revenue, about $479,000 profit, 82% close rate.

After. A 10% price rise adds about $125,000 that falls straight to profit: roughly 25% more net profit, held as long as close rate stays above 65%.

Thin or middling margins make price the strongest lever, which is why low margins excite him.

Analytics retainer, invented

A BI firm sells a €3,000/month retainer with €900 delivery cost (70% gross margin, below his 80% rule). Churn is 4% a month: LTV €75,000, LTGP €52,500, rounded down to €45,000. CAC is €9,000, so the ratio is 5:1, while human sales and human delivery call for 9–12:1. First-month gross profit is €2,100 against €9,000 CAC. Moves: a €6,000 onboarding fee plus a prepaid quarter brings in €15,000 within 30 days, above CAC plus first-quarter delivery (€9,000 + €2,700 = €11,700), and cutting delivery to €600 lifts margin to 80%.

Common mistakes

  • Using revenue as LTV. The ratio looks healthy while each sale loses money.
  • Judging spend by ROAS alone. Stopping a channel because the ratio fell, while absolute profit was still rising.
  • Not knowing churn. Without it you cannot see where revenue will cap.
  • Cutting price to fix sales. A 20% cut on a 40% margin needs twice the sales just to break even.
  • Copying industry averages. He benchmarks against winners and limits, not against the median.

How Alex would apply it

  • He calculates in the conversation. Churn, LTV, LTGP, cap and payback are worked out on the spot, and he expects the owner to know them without a finance report.
  • Value per customer before more customers. Price, churn and upsells usually move profit more, and with less effort, than doubling acquisition.
  • Gross margin is the first health check. A service under 80% gets fixed through price or delivery before anything is scaled.
  • Cash forward until CAC pays back in 30 days. Setup fees, prepay and early upsells come before debt or investors.
  • Decisions by math. Pricing and offer choices are settled by conversion × price × LTV per option, never by opinion.
  • Spend while the math holds. If a dollar reliably returns several, the budget is "as much as possible", judged on absolute profit.
  • Questions he would ask: What do you charge, and what does it cost to deliver? How many cancel each month? What is a customer worth in gross profit? What does one cost to acquire, and how fast do you get it back? What is your close rate against your price? How many leads a day does your goal need?

Worksheet

Use real numbers from the last 12 months.

  1. LTGP: Price ÷ monthly churn × gross margin. Round it down.
  2. CAC: Marketing payroll + media + commissions ÷ new customers. What ratio do you have, and what does your human count call for?
  3. Payback: What gross profit does a new customer bring in the first 30 days? Is it above CAC plus delivery cost?
  4. Benchmarks: Put your close, show and opt-in rates, gross margin and churn next to his rules of thumb. Which is furthest off?
  5. Backward math: From your target customer count, work out replacements per month, then sales, calls, bookings, leads per day and budget.

Bistvo

Alex poslovno matematiko obravnava kot jezik: o podjetju lahko razmišljaš le tako jasno, kot ti dopušča besedišče za njegove številke. V pogovoru, preden komur koli svetuje, izvede isti majhen nabor izračunov: koliko je stranka vredna v bruto dobičku, koliko stane, da jo dobiš, kako hitro se ta strošek vrne in kaj cilj pomeni v leadih, klicih in proračunu.

Rasti je mogoče le na dva načina: več strank ali večja vrednost na stranko. Vedno znova ugotavlja, da je drugi lažji. Ker je strošek pridobitve določene stranke za vse konkurente na trgu približno enak, trajno prednost dobi tisti, pri katerem je stranka vredna največ.

LTGP = (cena ÷ mesečni odliv) × bruto marža · Najvišji CAC = LTGP · Financira stranka, ko je denar, zbran v 30 dneh, večji od CAC + stroška izvedbe

Kako deluje

Dva vzvoda rasti

Vsaka izboljšava naj premakne enega od nekaj vzvodov: več strank, več življenjskega bruto dobička na stranko ali manjše tveganje, da se zaslužek ne bo nadaljeval. V drugem predavanju jih imenuje štiri: promet, konverzija, cena in odliv, in pravi, da naloge, ki ne vodi k enemu od njih, ne bi smeli delati.

Bistvo pokaže njegovo vprašanje o »duhu iz svetilke«. Če bi lahko podvojil nove stranke, ceno ali nakupe na stranko, kaj bi izbral? Pri 20-odstotni marži podvojitev strank podvoji dobiček, podvojitev cene pa ga poveča približno za šestkrat, ker je ves dodatni prihodek marža. Raje ima tudi podvojitev zadrževanja kot podvojitev novih strank: dobiček je na koncu podoben, višja življenjska vrednost pa ti omogoči, da za naslednjo stranko ponudiš več od konkurence.

Življenjski bruto dobiček, ne prihodek

Vztraja, da življenjska vrednost pomeni življenjski bruto dobiček (lifetime gross profit, LTGP): cena minus strošek izvedbe, pomnoženo s številom nakupov ali mesecev. Prihodek jo precenjuje. Fiksni stroški, kot sta najemnina in osebje zunaj izvedbe, ostanejo zunaj. Primer, ki ga uporablja: obrok za 10 dolarjev, katerega izvedba stane 9, pridobljen s 5 dolarji stroška pridobitve, je na prihodku videti kot 2 : 1, v resnici pa plačaš 5 dolarjev, da zaslužiš 1.

Njegova različica »na hrbtu prtička«:

  1. Odliv (churn) = odpovedi ta mesec ÷ aktivne stranke.
  2. LTV = cena ÷ odliv.
  3. LTGP = LTV × bruto marža.
  4. Strop prihodkov = nove prodaje na mesec × LTV. Rast se splošči, ko odliv požre nove prodaje.

Mlado podjetje ga lahko oceni iz enega meseca: če od 10 strank ostane 9, je odliv 10 % in LTV je cena ÷ 0,10. Lastnikom svetuje, naj računajo konzervativno in zaokrožijo navzdol.

Strošek pridobitve in razmerje

CAC je plača marketinga plus mediji plus prodajne provizije v obdobju, deljeno z novimi strankami, najbolje za celo leto. Razmerje, ki ga želi, je odvisno od tega, koliko ljudi je vpletenih v pridobivanje leadov, prodajo in izvedbo: okoli 3 : 1, če je vse troje avtomatizirano, nato 6 : 1, 9 : 1 in 12 : 1 ali več, ko dodajaš ljudi, ker se novi zaposleni počasi uvajajo, CAC pa sčasoma le raste. Optimizira razmerje, ne najcenejšega leada: 5.000 dolarjev LTV pri 500 dolarjih CAC je boljše od 1.000 pri 200.

Denar v 30 dneh proti CAC

Njegov cilj za povračilo je 30 dni. Toliko časa majhno podjetje približno zdrži strošek, in to se ujema z brezobrestnim obdobjem kreditnih kartic. Ko bruto dobiček, zbran v prvih 30 dneh, preseže strošek pridobitve in izvedbe, rasti ne omejuje več denar. Če je povračilo daljše, išče pristojbine za vzpostavitev, predplačila, priložene izdelke ali dodatne prodaje (upsell) v prvem mesecu.

Računanje nazaj od cilja

Cilje računa nazaj. V enem izračunanem primeru: 300 članov pri 10-odstotnem mesečnem odlivu pomeni 30 nadomestitev na mesec samo zato, da ostaneš na mestu. Pri 50-odstotni konverziji vstopne ponudbe je to 60 vstopnih prodaj, pri 50-odstotnem zaključku 120 pogovorov, pri 50-odstotni udeležbi okoli 240 rezervacij, za kar je bilo potrebnih približno 15 leadov na dan. Pri okoli 10 dolarjih na lead znese proračun okoli 4.500 dolarjev na mesec, rast pa pomeni njegovo podvojitev.

Mejniki, ki jih navaja

To so njegova pravila palca iz mnogih videov, s kontekstom, v katerem so nastala. Hkrati svari pred panožnimi povprečji kot cilji in raje vpraša, ali boljšemu rezultatu nasprotuje fizika.

Metrika Njegovo pravilo palca
Stopnja zaključka 80 % in več pomeni prenizko ceno (po njegovem za 3- do 4-krat); 30–40 % je približno prav ob izobraževanju pred klicem; pod 30 % najprej popravi ciljno stranko (avatar) in prodajni proces, šele nato razmišljaj o ceni

ROAS proti absolutnemu dobičku

Optimizira absolutni dobiček. Nižje razmerje donosa pri precej večjem vložku lahko prinese bistveno več denarja, zato padajoče razmerje ni razlog za ustavitev. V hotseatu, kjer so iskalni oglasi vračali 9- do 24-kratnik vložka, je rekel, da bi vložek dvigoval, dokler razmerje ne pade na približno 5 : 1.

Primeri

Primeri so povzeti in anonimizirani iz Alexovih videov. Primeri, označeni kot izmišljeni, so samo za ponazoritev.

Naročniška storitev po 1.000 dolarjev na mesec

Lastnik je imel 380 strank, 100 novih prodaj na mesec in 50 odpovedi, odliva pa ni poznal. Alex: 50 ÷ 380 je približno 13 % odliva, LTV okoli 7.700 dolarjev, LTGP okoli 7.000 pri 90-odstotni marži. Strop: 100 × 7.700 je okoli 770.000 dolarjev na mesec. Znižanje odliva na okoli 3 % dvigne LTV na približno 33.000 dolarjev in strop na okoli 3,3 milijona na mesec, kar je precej lažje od desetkratnika leadov.

Čiščenje prezračevalnih kanalov, 38 % neto marže

Pred. Okoli 1,25 milijona dolarjev prihodka, okoli 479.000 dobička, 82 % zaključka.

Po. 10-odstotni dvig cene doda okoli 125.000 dolarjev, ki gredo naravnost v dobiček: približno 25 % več neto dobička, dokler stopnja zaključka ostane nad 65 %.

Pri tankih ali srednjih maržah je cena najmočnejši vzvod, zato ga nizke marže navdušujejo.

Analitični retainer, izmišljeno

BI podjetje prodaja mesečni retainer za 3.000 € s 900 € stroška izvedbe (70 % bruto marže, pod njegovim pravilom 80 %). Odliv je 4 % na mesec: LTV 75.000 €, LTGP 52.500 €, zaokroženo navzdol na 45.000 €. CAC je 9.000 €, torej je razmerje 5 : 1, človeška prodaja in človeška izvedba pa zahtevata 9–12 : 1. Bruto dobiček prvega meseca je 2.100 € proti 9.000 € CAC. Poteze: 6.000 € pristojbine za uvedbo in vnaprej plačano četrtletje prineseta 15.000 € v 30 dneh, kar je več od CAC in izvedbe prvega četrtletja (9.000 € + 2.700 € = 11.700 €), znižanje stroška izvedbe na 600 € pa dvigne maržo na 80 %.

Pogoste napake

  • Prihodek kot LTV. Razmerje je videti zdravo, vsaka prodaja pa izgublja denar.
  • Presojanje vložka samo po ROAS. Ustavitev kanala, ker je razmerje padlo, čeprav je absolutni dobiček še rasel.
  • Nepoznavanje odliva. Brez njega ne vidiš, kje se bo prihodek ustavil.
  • Nižanje cene za večjo prodajo. 20-odstotno znižanje pri 40-odstotni marži zahteva dvakrat več prodaje samo za izenačenje.
  • Kopiranje panožnih povprečij. Primerja se z zmagovalci in mejami, ne z mediano.

Kako bi to uporabil Alex

  • Računa med pogovorom. Odliv, LTV, LTGP, strop in povračilo izračuna sproti in pričakuje, da jih lastnik pozna brez poročila računovodstva.
  • Vrednost na stranko pred več strankami. Cena, odliv in dodatne prodaje navadno bolj in z manj truda premaknejo dobiček kot podvojitev pridobivanja.
  • Bruto marža je prvi zdravstveni pregled. Storitev pod 80 % popravi s ceno ali izvedbo, preden karkoli skalira.
  • Denar naprej, dokler se CAC ne povrne v 30 dneh. Pristojbine za vzpostavitev, predplačila in zgodnje dodatne prodaje pridejo pred dolgom ali investitorji.
  • Odločitve z matematiko. Izbire cene in ponudbe reši s konverzijo × ceno × LTV za vsako možnost, nikoli z mnenjem.
  • Vlagaj, dokler matematika zdrži. Če dolar zanesljivo vrne večkratnik, je proračun »kolikor je mogoče«, presojan po absolutnem dobičku.
  • Vprašanja, ki bi jih postavil: Koliko zaračunaš in koliko stane izvedba? Koliko jih vsak mesec odpove? Koliko je stranka vredna v bruto dobičku? Koliko stane njena pridobitev in kako hitro se ti vrne? Kakšna je stopnja zaključka glede na ceno? Koliko leadov na dan zahteva tvoj cilj?

Delovni list

Uporabi resnične številke zadnjih 12 mesecev.

  1. LTGP: Cena ÷ mesečni odliv × bruto marža. Zaokroži navzdol.
  2. CAC: Plača marketinga + mediji + provizije ÷ nove stranke. Kakšno razmerje imaš in kakšnega zahteva število ljudi v procesu?
  3. Povračilo: Koliko bruto dobička prinese nova stranka v prvih 30 dneh? Je to več od CAC in stroška izvedbe?
  4. Mejniki: Svoje stopnje zaključka, udeležbe in prijave, bruto maržo in odliv postavi ob njegova pravila palca. Kaj najbolj odstopa?
  5. Računanje nazaj: Iz ciljnega števila strank izračunaj nadomestitve na mesec, nato prodaje, klice, rezervacije, leade na dan in proračun.
