Alex's Playbook · Hotseat cases

Real hotseat situations from e-commerce brands, DTC labels, consumer goods and physical-product makers, with what Alex diagnosed and told the owner to do.Resnične situacije s hotseatov e-trgovin, DTC blagovnih znamk, potrošniškega blaga in proizvajalcev fizičnih izdelkov, z Alexovo diagnozo in nasveti lastniku.

The gist

Product owners usually arrive asking for more traffic or a new line. In most of these cases Alex finds the traffic is fine or under-tested, and the real gaps sit elsewhere: prices set by cost-plus habit, too many products or avatars competing for the same attention, no email or repeat-purchase back end, broken attribution, and a business that buys clicks profitably without ever becoming a brand anyone would pay a premium for or acquire.

His prescriptions follow a steady order. First subtract: cut the losing location, the side SKUs, the new venture that is really an escape. Then fix money per customer: test higher prices, add repeat-purchase offers and email flows, and make the most profitable segment easy to buy from. Only then scale acquisition, and when he does, he pushes owners to spend more and test more than feels comfortable on the channel that already returns well, treating profit as an experiment budget.

Two longer-term themes recur. Unbranded direct-response winners get copied and margins collapse as spend rises, so he steers owners with traction toward one product they believe in and a real brand, often built through creators and affiliates. And when an owner wants to bolt on something new (a SaaS, a character universe, an acquisition), he asks whether the existing engine is exhausted and who carries the risk; usually the answer is to keep the engine running and fund the new thing from its profits, or not do it at all.

Cases

Hair-extension wholesaler with a salon · ~$3.5M/yr combined · wants to build salon-booking software

  • Situation. Extensions wholesale and e-commerce at about $2.6M and 30% margin, growing fast, plus a salon at about $900k and 50% margin. The owner planned to spend about $500k building booking software for salons.
  • Diagnosis. An escape from solvable problems (margin and acquisition) into a much harder business. Extensions are already a repeat purchase.
  • Advice. Do not build the software. Improve margins and acquisition in the core: content, ads aimed at stylists, a front-end offer that helps salons earn more by selling extensions, and separate hunter and farmer reps.
  • Why. Software means years without profit against focused, funded competitors; the current business is cash-positive and calmer.
  • Source. Helping E-Commerce Business Owners Scale (19:11)

Custom trophy and awards manufacturer · ~$1.7M/yr, 6% net · cost-plus pricing and no focus

  • Situation. Multi-generation maker with 40% gross margin, about $110k profit, two locations (one barely breaking even), and sales spread across its own site, SEO, marketplaces and a viral gag-gift line on a video-shopping platform.
  • Diagnosis. Mispricing, two very different avatars (price-sensitive repeat B2B buyers and impulse gift buyers) and no email back end.
  • Advice. Close the losing location. Move to value-based pricing with regular tests, starting on gag gifts. Max out SEO and add paid search, including brand terms. Commit to the gag-gift media path with one to three posts a day, affiliates and ads behind organic winners. Add an in-box repeat offer, email flows and a weekly nurture.
  • Why. At thin margins a ~6% price rise can roughly double profit, and e-commerce profit comes from repeat purchases.
  • Source. Building a $3,500,000 Business for a Stranger in 51 Mins (17:31)

E-commerce railing maker · ~$2.5M/yr, 15% net · attribution broken, best segment under-monetised

  • Situation. About $384k profit, 81% of customers from Google, about 70% DIY buyers and 30% custom, around 10% repeat. Reported LTV:CAC was 1:1 while dashboards showed 0.41.
  • Diagnosis. With attribution broken nothing can be optimised. The least price-sensitive segment was under-monetised and the custom option was hidden.
  • Advice. Fix attribution first. Reorganise around DIY plus custom, build a call funnel for custom orders with a VSL and qualifying texts, raise prices, show the custom option on every page and send email nurture twice a week.
  • Why. Doubling close rate and margin per customer compounds. A year later revenue was reported up 44%, custom orders were half the business and budget had moved from break-even campaigns to winners.
  • Source. Building a $1,000,000 Business for a Stranger in 26 Minutes (11:19)

Luxury gaming-table maker · ~$2.5M/yr, 20% margin · extra ad spend brought worse leads

  • Situation. Google Ads at $15–20k a month; the owner wanted 4x growth, but raising spend lowered lead quality.
  • Diagnosis. The market was far from saturated. The real limits were keyword and media-buying skill and a fear of spending; ads only reached the most aware buyers.
  • Advice. Treat profit as an experiment budget and accept losing a few hundred thousand while testing keywords to find more winners. Move up in awareness with broader keywords and advertorial bridge pages.
  • Why. The keywords already found returned around 10:1, so more winners likely exist, and colder clicks are cheaper and far more numerous.
  • Source. Helping E-Commerce Business Owners Scale (01:36)

Direct-response e-commerce operator · ~$3M run rate after four months · four unbranded SKUs, founder buried in chores

  • Situation. Four products under separate brands, aiming for $15M and a portfolio exit. The founder was overwhelmed by small tasks.
  • Diagnosis. The chores are a complaint; the constraint lies elsewhere. The model itself caps out: pure media arbitrage tends to stall near $10M with collapsing margins and nothing a buyer wants.
  • Advice. Hand niche tasks to contractors and assistants. Pick the one product the team truly believes in, cut the rest, and build a brand through influencer-affiliates before worrying about legal protection.
  • Why. He calls it the direct-response doom loop: revenue up, margins down, team becoming a liability. Buyers pay for brands, and copycats kill unbranded winners.
  • Source. Helping E-Commerce Business Owners Scale (09:38)

DTC menswear brand · ~$10M/yr, ~$3M profit · considering womenswear and formal lines

  • Situation. Contract-manufactured fashion brand in a large Asian market, strong at buying traffic, thinking about new categories.
  • Diagnosis. Excellent at media arbitrage, but without a brand.
  • Advice. Stay in menswear and aim for $100M there. Build a real brand, for example around an endorser, instead of imitating fast fashion.
  • Why. A brand creates premium pricing and makes the company saleable; new categories would split focus before the first one is exhausted.
  • Source. 7 Millionaires Asked Me How to Get Rich(er) (30:33)

Live-shopping reseller of designer accessories · ~$6M/yr · wants ~$38M

  • Situation. Sells designer bags and sunglasses on a live-shopping app, limited to about five live hours a day, runs its own fulfilment and keeps hitting cash crunches.
  • Diagnosis. The constraint is on-camera sellers. The warehouse is a commodity, and cash runs short because all gross profit goes into inventory.
  • Advice. Add two more on-camera sellers paid a share of profit, recruited from creators already live-selling and signed to exclusivity. Hand the warehouse to a capable leader, hire a finance person for forecasting, train sellers in concrete behaviours and keep the star seller selling.
  • Why. Each show is profitable, so more selling hours scale revenue directly; the founder's leverage is teaching others to sell.
  • Source. Helping E-Commerce Business Owners Scale (28:48)

Solo board-game publisher · $1.3M from one crowdfunded game · four new games started before the first shipped

  • Situation. About $150k cash left, another $150k already spent developing four more games, the first game not yet delivered, and the owner overwhelmed.
  • Diagnosis. Self-made complexity. Starting four games before finishing one removed priorities and created the anxiety.
  • Advice. Raise cash by offering past buyers a buy-one-get-one deal on upcoming games. Make delivering game one the only objective. Hire an assistant. Learn ads with a fixed learning budget split roughly 70/20/10.
  • Why. Feedback from the first delivery will change the later games, and the business itself is simple: ship a box.
  • Source. "Should He Walk Away From His $1.4M Business To Start Over?" | Alex Hormozi Answers Live (39:49)

Premium canned-cocktail brand · ~$1.5M/yr, zero profit · wants a billion-dollar brand

  • Situation. In about 1,000 stores with weak sell-through, founder alone after letting staff go, no branding or direct-response experience. She also ran a proven property-development business on the side.
  • Diagnosis. No repeat-purchase data, no capital for a celebrity-style brand play and no appetite for a 10–15 year regional grind.
  • Advice. One last focused bet: go all in on travel retail contracts (airlines, trains, cruise lines), where a small can is an advantage. If no contract closes, shut it down. Otherwise put her time into property and learn to hire and train people.
  • Why. It is the only path that needs no capital and fits her skills; learning fast beats losing three more years.
  • Source. Your Business Is NOT What You Think It Is (24:48)

Robotic vending-machine seller · ~$15k machines at ~50% margin · outbound works but is tiny

  • Situation. Entrepreneurs want the machines but lack locations. Cold email gets two or three replies per 100 and nearly all of them close. Four closers, one setter; paid ads had not worked.
  • Diagnosis. A validated, very profitable outbound motion that has never been scaled.
  • Advice. Go from about 100 to about 5,000 emails a day using several warmed domains, AI personalisation and automated sends in reps' names. Route replies straight to closers, who also keep prospecting. Consider SDRs or AI dialers.
  • Why. The unit economics are extreme. Once a process works, the limit is volume and headcount.
  • Source. How to Build a Business That Runs Without You (1:39:58)

Licensed-drop apparel brand · ~$6M/yr, ~10% net · conversion swings 5–25% by license

  • Situation. Drops with TV and anime licenses, all traffic from paid social to waitlists; the owner wanted a 4–5x EBITDA exit.
  • Diagnosis. No predictive data on which license will perform. The exit thesis is weak: large acquirers rarely write cheques this small, and the model is easy to copy.
  • Advice. Build quantitative predictors of drop performance. Create an ambassador program of niche creators who push every drop and get drops of their own, and use their pull data to choose license partners.
  • Why. Creators bring audiences without ad spend, negotiate softly and give early signal on who really moves product.
  • Source. How to Build a Business That Runs Without You (1:08:02)

E-commerce clothing brand with a community · size not stated · apparel or an original character universe?

  • Situation. A brand with about 14,000 community members, torn between scaling apparel and building an original character universe.
  • Diagnosis. A false either/or.
  • Advice. Keep the clothing engine running. Fund the universe from its profits and treat it as marketing. Measure whether it brings more buyers, and study how large studios monetise characters.
  • Why. Building intellectual property costs capital. Someone's money has to be at risk, and ideally it comes from your own working engine.
  • Source. 10 Millionaires Asked Me How to Get Richer (33:11)

Custom railings shipped nationwide · ~$420k/yr · wants architects and contractors to refer big projects

  • Situation. Inbound comes through SEO. The owner wanted trade partners to send projects of $50k and more.
  • Diagnosis. Hunting large B2B deals is a strategy change with real risk, and partners need a compelling reason to refer.
  • Advice. Start with the lowest-risk moves: add paid search and more SEO articles. For partners, use direct outreach and ideally let them resell and keep the margin; otherwise offer a pool (for example 20%) split between partner cut and client discount. Raise direct prices.
  • Why. Generic kickbacks do not change behaviour; what you give away simply becomes your acquisition cost.
  • Source. $100M Investor Answers The Most Important Business Questions for 1 Hour (16:58)

Trophy manufacturer eyeing an acquisition · target ~2x its size · 70% of target revenue from one customer

  • Situation. The same awards maker considered buying a local competitor about twice its revenue, whose owner was retiring.
  • Diagnosis. Key-person risk from the retiring owner on top of severe customer concentration, and no clear strategic reason for the deal.
  • Advice. Do not buy. If the supplier relationships are the attraction, try to get them another way.
  • Why. If the retiring owner holds the big relationship, the buyer could lose most of the revenue the day the deal closes.
  • Source. Building a $3,500,000 Business for a Stranger in 51 Mins (13:33)

Patterns

  • Subtract before adding (about 7 cases). Losing location, extra SKUs, unfinished games, new categories, a SaaS escape: his first move is usually to cut or refuse something.
  • The new venture is an escape (about 4 cases). Software, womenswear, a character universe, an acquisition. He checks whether the core is exhausted; it rarely is.
  • Prices set by habit (about 4 cases). Cost-plus or below-market pricing; he prescribes value-based tests and direct price rises, noting that small increases move thin-margin profit a lot.
  • Missing back end (about 4 cases). No email flows, no repeat offer, past buyers never re-sold. E-commerce profit comes from the second and third purchase.
  • Under-spending and under-testing the winning channel (about 4 cases). Paid search at 10:1 that the owner is afraid to push, PPC never tried, outbound left at 100 emails a day.
  • Media arbitrage without a brand (about 3 cases). Profitable click-buying that caps out, gets copied and is hard to sell. Pick one product and build a brand, often through creators.
  • Creators and partners as a channel (about 4 cases). Influencer-affiliates, ambassador programs, trade partners who resell; give partners something they can earn on.
  • Data before optimisation (about 2 cases). Broken attribution or no predictive model means spend cannot be steered.
  • Cash and inventory traps (about 3 cases). Gross profit locked in stock or prototypes; he raises cash from past buyers and refocuses on shipping.

How Alex would apply it

First questions. 1. What are gross margin and net profit, and how were prices set? 2. What share of customers buy again, and what do you send them after the first order? 3. Which single channel and product make most of the profit, and can you prove it with attribution? 4. What happens to returns if you double spend on the best channel? 5. Is this a brand people would pay more for, or a product you sell because the ads work? 6. What new thing are you about to start, and why now?

Default moves by situation. - Margin under ~10% or cost-plus pricing: run price tests first, starting with the least price-sensitive line; close or cut anything losing money. - No repeat purchases: add in-box offers, email flows and a weekly nurture before buying more traffic. - A channel returns well but growth stalls: raise spend and widen targeting deliberately, with profit set aside as a learning budget; move into less-aware audiences with broader terms and bridge content. - Attribution broken: fix it before any other optimisation. - Several SKUs or brands on media arbitrage: keep the one the team believes in and build a brand around it, using creators or affiliates. - Wanting a new line, a SaaS or an acquisition: default answer is no. If the itch is real, keep the core engine running and fund the new thing from its profits as a marketing bet; refuse acquisitions with key-person risk or one-customer concentration. - Cash squeeze: sell to past buyers first (pre-orders, bundles), narrow to delivering the current product, and outsource or lease what is commoditised. - A validated outbound or selling motion: scale its volume and headcount before changing strategy.

Bistvo

Lastniki izdelčnih podjetij navadno pridejo s prošnjo za več prometa ali novo linijo. V večini teh primerov Alex ugotovi, da je promet v redu ali premalo testiran, prave vrzeli pa so drugje: cene, postavljene po navadi "strošek plus", preveč izdelkov ali ciljnih kupcev, ki tekmujejo za isto pozornost, brez e-pošte ali ponovnih nakupov v back endu, pokvarjena atribucija in podjetje, ki sicer donosno kupuje klike, a nikoli ne postane blagovna znamka, za katero bi kdo plačal več ali jo kupil.

Njegovi recepti sledijo stalnemu vrstnemu redu. Najprej odvzemanje: zapri lokacijo, ki izgublja, odreži stranske izdelke, opusti novi projekt, ki je v resnici beg. Nato uredi denar na kupca: testiraj višje cene, dodaj ponudbe za ponovni nakup in e-poštne tokove ter poskrbi, da najdonosnejši segment zlahka kupi. Šele nato skaliraj pridobivanje kupcev, in ko to stori, lastnike potiska, naj na kanalu, ki že dobro vrača, porabijo in testirajo več, kot jim je prijetno, dobiček pa obravnavajo kot proračun za eksperimente.

Ponavljata se še dve dolgoročni temi. Zmagovalne izdelke brez blagovne znamke, prodane z direct response oglasi, drugi skopirajo, marže pa z rastjo porabe padajo, zato lastnike z vlečno silo usmerja k enemu izdelku, v katerega verjamejo, in k pravi blagovni znamki, pogosto zgrajeni prek ustvarjalcev in affiliatov. Ko pa lastnik želi dodati nekaj novega (SaaS, svet likov, prevzem), vpraša, ali je obstoječi motor izčrpan in kdo nosi tveganje; odgovor je navadno, naj motor teče naprej in novo financira iz njegovega dobička, ali pa naj tega sploh ne počne.

Primeri

Veleprodaja podaljškov za lase s salonom · ~3,5 mio $/leto skupaj · želi zgraditi programsko opremo za rezervacije v salonih

  • Situacija. Veleprodaja in e-trgovina s podaljški pri okoli 2,6 mio $ in 30 % marži, hitra rast, poleg tega salon pri okoli 900 tisoč $ in 50 % marži. Lastnica je nameravala porabiti okoli 500 tisoč $ za programsko opremo za rezervacije.
  • Diagnoza. Beg pred rešljivimi težavami (marža in pridobivanje kupcev) v veliko težji posel. Podaljški so že ponovni nakup.
  • Nasvet. Programske opreme ne gradi. Izboljšaj marže in pridobivanje v jedru: vsebine, oglasi, usmerjeni na frizerje, vstopna ponudba, ki salonom pomaga več zaslužiti s prodajo podaljškov, ter ločeni prodajalci za lov in za vzdrževanje strank (hunter in farmer).
  • Zakaj. Programska oprema pomeni leta brez dobička proti osredotočenim, dobro financiranim tekmecem; sedanji posel je denarno pozitiven in mirnejši.

Proizvajalec pokalov in priznanj · ~1,7 mio $/leto, 6 % neto · cene "strošek plus" in brez fokusa

  • Situacija. Družinsko podjetje v več generacijah s 40 % bruto maržo, okoli 110 tisoč $ dobička, dvema lokacijama (ena komaj pokriva stroške) in prodajo, razpršeno med lastno stran, SEO, spletne tržnice in viralno linijo šaljivih daril na platformi za video nakupovanje.
  • Diagnoza. Napačne cene, dva zelo različna ciljna kupca (cenovno občutljivi ponavljajoči se B2B kupci in impulzivni kupci daril) in brez e-poštnega back enda.
  • Nasvet. Zapri lokacijo, ki izgublja. Preidi na cene po vrednosti z rednimi testi, začni pri šaljivih darilih. Izkoristi SEO do konca in dodaj plačano iskanje, tudi za lastno znamko. Zaveži se medijski poti šaljivih daril z eno do tremi objavami na dan, affiliati in oglasi za organskimi zmagovalci. Dodaj ponudbo v paketu, e-poštne tokove in tedensko negovanje.
  • Zakaj. Pri tankih maržah lahko ~6 % višja cena dobiček približno podvoji, dobiček v e-trgovini pa prihaja iz ponovnih nakupov.

Spletni proizvajalec ograj · ~2,5 mio $/leto, 15 % neto · pokvarjena atribucija, najboljši segment premalo izkoriščen

  • Situacija. Okoli 384 tisoč $ dobička, 81 % kupcev iz Googla, okoli 70 % kupcev, ki montirajo sami, in 30 % naročil po meri, okoli 10 % ponovnih nakupov. Poročani LTV:CAC je bil 1:1, nadzorne plošče pa so kazale 0,41.
  • Diagnoza. S pokvarjeno atribucijo ni mogoče ničesar optimizirati. Cenovno najmanj občutljivi segment je bil premalo izkoriščen, možnost izdelave po meri pa skrita.
  • Nasvet. Najprej popravi atribucijo. Preuredi posel okrog samomontaže in izdelave po meri, zgradi lijak s klicem za naročila po meri z VSL in kvalifikacijskimi sporočili, dvigni cene, pokaži možnost po meri na vsaki strani in pošiljaj e-pošto dvakrat na teden.
  • Zakaj. Podvojena stopnja zaključkov in marža na kupca se seštevata. Leto pozneje so poročali o 44 % višjih prihodkih, naročila po meri so bila polovica posla, proračun pa se je preselil s kampanj na pragu donosnosti k zmagovalnim.

Proizvajalec luksuznih igralnih miz · ~2,5 mio $/leto, 20 % marža · več oglasov je prineslo slabše povpraševanje

  • Situacija. Google Ads za 15–20 tisoč $ na mesec; lastnik je želel 4-kratno rast, a višja poraba je znižala kakovost povpraševanj.
  • Diagnoza. Trg še zdaleč ni nasičen. Prave meje so bile znanje o ključnih besedah in zakupu medijev ter strah pred porabo; oglasi so dosegali le najbolj ozaveščene kupce.
  • Nasvet. Dobiček obravnavaj kot proračun za eksperimente in sprejmi, da boš pri testiranju ključnih besed izgubil nekaj sto tisoč, da najdeš nove zmagovalce. Pomakni se k manj ozaveščenim kupcem s širšimi ključnimi besedami in vmesnimi stranmi v slogu članka (advertorial).
  • Zakaj. Že najdene ključne besede so vračale okoli 10:1, zato verjetno obstaja še več zmagovalcev, hladnejši kliki pa so cenejši in jih je veliko več.

Direct response e-trgovina · ~3 mio $ letne stopnje po štirih mesecih · štirje izdelki brez znamke, ustanovitelj zasut z opravili

  • Zakaj. Temu reče direct response začarani krog: prihodki gor, marže dol, ekipa postane breme. Kupci plačajo za blagovne znamke, kopije pa ubijejo zmagovalce brez znamke.

Premium koktajli v pločevinki · ~1,5 mio $/leto, nič dobička · želi milijardno blagovno znamko

  • Situacija. V okoli 1.000 trgovinah s šibko prodajo s polic, ustanoviteljica sama po odpustu zaposlenih, brez izkušenj z blagovno znamko ali direct response trženjem. Ob strani je vodila preverjen posel z razvojem nepremičnin.
  • Diagnoza. Ni podatkov o ponovnih nakupih, ni kapitala za igro z znano osebnostjo in ni pripravljenosti na 10–15 let regionalnega garanja.
  • Nasvet. Ena zadnja osredotočena stava: vse na pogodbe v potovalni prodaji (letalske družbe, vlaki, križarke), kjer je majhna pločevinka prednost. Če ne zapre nobene pogodbe, naj posel zapre. Sicer naj čas vloži v nepremičnine in se nauči zaposlovati in usposabljati ljudi.
  • Zakaj. To je edina pot, ki ne potrebuje kapitala in ustreza njenim veščinam; hitro učenje je boljše od izgube še treh let.
  • Vir. Your Business Is NOT What You Think It Is (24:48)

Prodajalec robotskih prodajnih avtomatov · avtomati po ~15 tisoč $ z ~50 % maržo · outbound deluje, a je majhen

  • Situacija. Podjetniki avtomate želijo, nimajo pa lokacij. Hladna e-pošta prinese dva ali tri odgovore na 100, in skoraj vsi se zaključijo. Štirje zaključevalci, en setter; plačani oglasi niso delovali.
  • Diagnoza. Preverjen, zelo donosen outbound postopek, ki ga nikoli niso skalirali.
  • Nasvet. S približno 100 pojdi na okoli 5.000 e-poštnih sporočil na dan z več ogretimi domenami, personalizacijo z UI in samodejnim pošiljanjem v imenu prodajalcev. Odgovore pošlji neposredno zaključevalcem, ki tudi sami iščejo stranke. Razmisli o SDR-jih ali klicanju z UI.
  • Zakaj. Ekonomika enote je izjemna. Ko postopek deluje, sta meja obseg in število ljudi.
  • Vir. How to Build a Business That Runs Without You (1:39:58)

Oblačila z licenčnimi izdajami · ~6 mio $/leto, ~10 % neto · konverzija niha med 5 in 25 % glede na licenco

  • Situacija. Izdaje z licencami TV serij in animejev, ves promet iz plačanih družbenih omrežij na čakalne sezname; lastnik je želel izstop pri 4–5-kratniku EBITDA.
  • Diagnoza. Ni napovednih podatkov, katera licenca bo uspela. Teza za izstop je šibka: veliki kupci redko pišejo tako majhne čeke, model pa je lahko posnemati.
  • Nasvet. Zgradi kvantitativne napovednike uspeha izdaj. Ustvari ambasadorski program nišnih ustvarjalcev, ki promovirajo vsako izdajo in dobijo svoje, njihove podatke o vplivu pa uporabi za izbiro licenčnih partnerjev.
  • Zakaj. Ustvarjalci prinesejo občinstvo brez oglaševalskih stroškov, se mehko pogajajo in zgodaj pokažejo, kdo res proda izdelek.
  • Vir. How to Build a Business That Runs Without You (1:08:02)

Spletna trgovina z oblačili in skupnostjo · velikost ni navedena · oblačila ali lasten svet likov?

  • Situacija. Znamka z okoli 14.000 člani skupnosti, razpeta med skaliranjem oblačil in gradnjo lastnega sveta likov.
  • Diagnoza. Lažna izbira "ali-ali".
  • Nasvet. Naj motor z oblačili teče naprej. Svet likov financiraj iz njegovega dobička in ga obravnavaj kot trženje. Meri, ali prinaša več kupcev, in preuči, kako veliki studii monetizirajo like.
  • Zakaj. Gradnja intelektualne lastnine stane kapital. Nečiji denar mora biti v tveganju, najbolje iz lastnega delujočega motorja.
  • Vir. 10 Millionaires Asked Me How to Get Richer (33:11)

Ograje po meri z dostavo po vsej državi · ~420 tisoč $/leto · želi priporočila arhitektov in izvajalcev za velike projekte

  • Situacija. Povpraševanje prihaja prek SEO. Lastnik je želel, da mu partnerji iz stroke pošiljajo projekte od 50 tisoč $ naprej.
  • Diagnoza. Lov na velike B2B posle je sprememba strategije z resničnim tveganjem, partnerji pa potrebujejo prepričljiv razlog za priporočanje.
  • Nasvet. Začni z najmanj tveganimi potezami: dodaj plačano iskanje in več SEO člankov. Pri partnerjih uporabi neposreden outreach in jim po možnosti dovoli preprodajo z lastno maržo; sicer ponudi skupni delež (na primer 20 %), razdeljen med provizijo partnerja in popust stranki. Dvigni neposredne cene.
  • Zakaj. Splošne provizije ne spremenijo vedenja; kar daš, postane preprosto tvoj strošek pridobitve kupca.
  • Vir. $100M Investor Answers The Most Important Business Questions for 1 Hour (16:58)

Proizvajalec pokalov razmišlja o prevzemu · tarča ~2-krat večja · 70 % prihodkov tarče od ene stranke

  • Situacija. Isti proizvajalec priznanj je razmišljal o nakupu lokalnega tekmeca s približno dvakratnimi prihodki, katerega lastnik se je upokojeval.
  • Diagnoza. Tveganje ključne osebe zaradi upokojitve lastnika, povrhu hude koncentracije strank, in brez jasnega strateškega razloga za posel.
  • Nasvet. Ne kupi. Če so privlačni odnosi z dobavitelji, jih poskusi dobiti drugače.
  • Zakaj. Če ima upokojeni lastnik v rokah veliko stranko, lahko kupec ob zaključku posla izgubi večino prihodkov.

Vzorci

  • Najprej odvzemi, nato dodajaj (približno 7 primerov). Lokacija z izgubo, dodatni izdelki, nedokončane igre, nove kategorije, beg v SaaS: njegova prva poteza je navadno, da nekaj odreže ali zavrne.
  • Novi projekt je beg (približno 4 primeri). Programska oprema, ženska oblačila, svet likov, prevzem. Preveri, ali je jedro izčrpano; redko je.
  • Cene iz navade (približno 4 primeri). Cene "strošek plus" ali pod trgom; predpiše teste cen po vrednosti in neposredne dvige, ker majhna povišanja pri tankih maržah močno premaknejo dobiček.
  • Manjkajoč back end (približno 4 primeri). Brez e-poštnih tokov, brez ponudbe za ponovni nakup, preteklim kupcem nihče ne proda znova. Dobiček e-trgovine prihaja iz drugega in tretjega nakupa.
  • Premalo porabe in testov na zmagovalnem kanalu (približno 4 primeri). Plačano iskanje pri 10:1, ki ga lastnik ne upa potisniti, nikoli preizkušen PPC, outbound pri 100 sporočilih na dan.
  • Medijska arbitraža brez blagovne znamke (približno 3 primeri). Donosno kupovanje klikov, ki doseže strop, ga drugi skopirajo in ga je težko prodati. Izberi en izdelek in zgradi znamko, pogosto prek ustvarjalcev.
  • Ustvarjalci in partnerji kot kanal (približno 4 primeri). Influencerji-affiliati, ambasadorski programi, partnerji, ki preprodajajo; partnerjem daj nekaj, na čemer lahko zaslužijo.
  • Podatki pred optimizacijo (približno 2 primera). Pokvarjena atribucija ali brez napovednega modela pomeni, da porabe ni mogoče usmerjati.
  • Pasti gotovine in zalog (približno 3 primeri). Bruto dobiček, ujet v zalogi ali prototipih; denar zbere pri preteklih kupcih in fokus vrne na dobavo.

Kako bi to uporabil Alex

Prva vprašanja. 1. Kakšni sta bruto marža in neto dobiček in kako so bile postavljene cene? 2. Kolikšen delež kupcev kupi znova in kaj jim pošljete po prvem naročilu? 3. Kateri en kanal in izdelek prinašata večino dobička in ali to lahko dokažete z atribucijo? 4. Kaj se zgodi z donosom, če podvojite porabo na najboljšem kanalu? 5. Je to blagovna znamka, za katero ljudje plačajo več, ali izdelek, ki ga prodajate, ker oglasi delujejo? 6. Kaj novega nameravate začeti in zakaj ravno zdaj?

Privzete poteze glede na situacijo. - Marža pod ~10 % ali cene "strošek plus": najprej testi cen, začni pri cenovno najmanj občutljivi liniji; zapri ali odreži vse, kar izgublja. - Ni ponovnih nakupov: preden kupiš več prometa, dodaj ponudbe v paketu, e-poštne tokove in tedensko negovanje. - Kanal dobro vrača, rast pa stoji: načrtno povečaj porabo in razširi ciljanje, del dobička pa nameni proračunu za učenje; s širšimi izrazi in vmesnimi vsebinami pojdi k manj ozaveščenim kupcem. - Pokvarjena atribucija: popravi jo pred vsako drugo optimizacijo. - Več izdelkov ali znamk na medijski arbitraži: obdrži tisto, v kar ekipa verjame, in okrog nje zgradi blagovno znamko z ustvarjalci ali affiliati. - Želja po novi liniji, SaaS-u ali prevzemu: privzeti odgovor je ne. Če je želja resnična, naj jedrni motor teče naprej in novo financiraj iz njegovega dobička kot tržno stavo; zavrni prevzeme s tveganjem ključne osebe ali odvisnostjo od ene stranke. - Stiska z gotovino: najprej prodajaj preteklim kupcem (prednaročila, paketi), zoži fokus na dobavo trenutnega izdelka, zamenljive stvari pa oddaj zunanjim izvajalcem ali najemi. - Preverjen outbound ali prodajni postopek: pred spremembo strategije skaliraj njegov obseg in število ljudi.
