Delegation & the Owner's RoleDelegiranje in vloga lastnika

Alex's Playbook · Buy back your time

Alex sees the founder as the default bottleneck. He buys back time in a fixed order and delegates through a written system, until the business keeps growing while the owner is away.Alex ima ustanovitelja za privzeto ozko grlo. Čas odkupuje v stalnem vrstnem redu in delegira prek zapisanega sistema, dokler podjetje ne raste tudi takrat, ko lastnika ni.

The gist

Early on, a business is a suit of armour that multiplies the owner but cannot move without them. Alex's goal is a business that creates value without you: only then is it worth something to someone else, and only then can you step away. Two companies with the same profit are worth very different amounts if one needs its owner eighty hours a week.

Delegation = time study → task list → process → decision rules with limits → KPIs → graduation test (about 80% as good) → shadow, supervise, support

He treats this as a sequence of giving up control. At each stage, the thing you hold on to is the next thing you must hand off, and it always feels uncomfortable. Handing over control never hands over accountability: you stay responsible for the result and close enough to know where the problems are.

How it works

The buyback order

Across several lectures the order is consistent:

  1. Low-skill support and delivery tasks.
  2. Admin: billing, collections, contracts. Then money tracking, once someone tracks it better than you (you still look at it).
  3. Selling. He puts the ceiling for founder-led selling around $1M a year.
  4. Running the sales team. He places this roughly between $3M and $10M.
  5. Leading leaders. From about $10M to $30M you lead a small group, and culture travels through them.

The rule for each task: if the market will do it for a fraction of what your hour is worth, hand it off, even if you believe nobody does it as well. Freed time must go into higher-value work. Founders who free time and then fill it with nothing see margins fall.

The delegation system

  1. Time study. Log what you do every 15 minutes for a week. List every task as granularly as possible. He uses the same study on any key employee the business depends on too much.
  2. Person, process or project. Next to each task, write whether it needs a person, a process or a one-off project. Rate it green (can hand off now), yellow (you need to build a process first) or red (you lack the skill or the person). Clear greens first, then yellows, then reds.
  3. Decision rules. Document decisions as well as tasks: if this happens, do that. The more repetitive the role, the more standard the rules. Give each person spending authority without approval; his examples include under $500 per decision with a $5,000 monthly cap, or an operator who can spend up to 1,000 without calling him. A monthly financial review catches waste.
  4. KPIs. Ask each person how their role makes the company money. If they cannot answer, help them, for example by reversing the costs of doing the job badly into KPIs.
  5. Graduation test. Can they get 100% of the result about 80% as well as you? At handoff he accepts 60 to 80% if there is a clear path to improve, and judges people by how fast they get better.
  6. Handoff. They shadow you, then you supervise them, then you are available but not involved.

Document, demonstrate, duplicate

His training method recurs in hotseats and sales talks: write the checklist, do the task in front of them using only the checklist, then have them do it in front of you while you correct immediately. Keep the steps simple enough for someone with no experience. If you have no written process, record yourself doing the work: the structure is already there.

Know where the bodies are buried

Delegation succeeds only when the function's results hold or improve after the handoff. If they drop, you abdicated. He wants owners able to name each department's current problems. If a department only brings good news, you are too far away. The fix is more visibility, never taking control back.

Owner-independence tests

  • You are off the company chat, one person can reach you, you meet that person once a week, and the company keeps growing.
  • Basic pass: a month away and nothing burns down. Real pass: three months away (or six months in another video) and the business grows. He recommends actually taking the time off, fixing what breaks and repeating.
  • A new operator should leave the owner with more time. If they mainly create tasks for the owner, it is not working.
  • To train judgement, ask staff "what do you think I would say?" and tell them to do that.

When work isn't getting done

Two lectures give the same triangle. Before judging the person, check:

Cause Question Fix
Expectation (what) Did I state it clearly, in writing, and is it in their reporting? Write it down with a deadline; review it often
Training (how) Do they know how, given their workload? Do it together, record it, define how to report back
Motivation (why) Do they know why it matters to the company and to them? Explain the why; check the comp plan rewards it

He starts from "this is probably my fault" and checks expectation first. If communication and training are fixed and nothing changes, he names it as motivation and has the direct conversation. Moving the person to another role is usually wrong.

The founder hides the real constraint

His sharpest observation: founders keep working on what they enjoy and are good at, and hand off what they dislike. Their strengths become the bottleneck. A media person optimises media; a closer studies closing. If you dislike people work, recruiting and managing are probably your constraint. Growth requires an identity change: from best salesperson or best coach to the person who teaches and then builds the system. "I have trust issues" is not an acceptable reason in his view.

Examples

Cases are paraphrased and anonymised from Alex's videos. Any example marked as invented is for illustration only.

Field-service owner who is in everything

Before. A high-end field-service company, about $1.5M revenue and $700K net. Each technician brings $180–300K a year. The owner insists on being involved in everything and has stopped recruiting.

After. A one-week time study, the bottom half of his activities delegated, and the freed time spent recruiting technicians nationally with a budget of about $50K per hire.

Half the owner's time back could double the business, because a technician is worth far more than the cost to recruit one.

Dental practice with one expert doing every step

Before. A founder-doctor handles every high-ticket case, capped at about 25–30 patients a month, with a booking delay of weeks.

After. Associates take the simpler half of the visits. The founder becomes the architect and teacher. Combined with price and payment changes, the follow-up showed average ticket up from about $28K to $44K and booking within a week.

Halving the expert's workload doubles capacity. The founder's identity moves from best practitioner to best teacher.

Owner who closes best

An event company owner closed about 40% while two reps underperformed. Alex turned the reps into appointment setters for the owner, still on commission, with daily morning and end-of-day check-ins, until the process was documented well enough to hand back.

Analytics consultancy founder (invented)

A founder of a ten-person BI consultancy does presales, solution design and code reviews. A time study shows 12 hours a week on reviews. He writes a review checklist (green), records himself doing three reviews (document, demonstrate), then has a senior consultant review while he watches (duplicate). After a month the senior handles all reviews at about 80% of his quality, with a rule: escalate only changes to shared data models. The 12 hours go into presales.

Common mistakes

  • Abdication. Handing off and never looking again. Results drop, and the owner concludes nobody can do it.
  • Delegating only what you dislike. You keep the tasks you enjoy, and those become the bottleneck.
  • Handing over tasks without decisions. Without if-then rules and spending limits, every exception still comes back to you.
  • Waiting for 100%. Demanding your own quality on day one means you never hand anything off.
  • Wasting the freed time. Time bought back has to go into higher-value work or margins suffer.

How Alex would apply it

  • He starts with a time study, never with a guess. The log shows where the hours go.
  • He hands off from the bottom of the value list and keeps going in the fixed order: support, admin, selling, the sales team, leaders.
  • He wants decisions on paper, with if-then rules and a spending limit, so people can act without calling the owner.
  • He sets a graduation bar at roughly 80% of the owner's quality and accepts less at first if improvement is visible.
  • He diagnoses before he blames: expectation, then training, then motivation.
  • He tests independence for real by having the owner leave for a month, then longer, and fixing what breaks.
  • Questions he would ask: What did your last week look like in 15-minute blocks? Which tasks could someone do for a fraction of your hourly value? Can you name each department's current problems? Would the sales model still work if reps closed half as well as you? Did they know it was expected, do they know how, and do they know why? What are you holding on to because you enjoy it?

Worksheet

Run this over the next two weeks.

  1. Time study: Log one week in 15-minute blocks. Which ten tasks took most of your time?
  2. Colour code: Mark each task green, yellow or red, with a person, process or project next to it.
  3. Rules: For your most frequent green task, write five if-then rules and a spending limit.
  4. Bar: Define what 80% as good looks like and how you will measure it.
  5. Absence: Pick a date for a week away. What must exist before then so nothing breaks?

Bistvo

Na začetku je podjetje oklep, ki lastnika pomnoži, a se brez njega ne premakne. Alexov cilj je podjetje, ki ustvarja vrednost brez tebe: šele takrat je vredno nekaj komu drugemu in šele takrat se lahko umakneš. Dve podjetji z enakim dobičkom sta vredni zelo različno, če eno potrebuje lastnika osemdeset ur na teden.

Delegiranje = časovna študija → seznam opravil → proces → odločitvena pravila z omejitvami → KPI-ji → test zrelosti (približno 80 % tvoje kakovosti) → senčenje, nadzor, podpora

To razume kot zaporedje odpovedovanja nadzoru. Na vsaki stopnji je tisto, česar se oklepaš, naslednje, kar moraš predati, in vedno je neprijetno. Predaja nadzora nikoli ne pomeni predaje odgovornosti: za rezultat ostaneš odgovoren in dovolj blizu, da veš, kje so problemi.

Kako deluje

Vrstni red odkupa časa

V več predavanjih je vrstni red enak:

  1. Nizkokvalificirana podpora in izvedbena opravila.
  2. Administracija: računi, izterjava, pogodbe. Nato spremljanje denarja, ko ga nekdo spremlja bolje od tebe (ti ga še vedno gledaš).
  3. Prodaja. Strop prodaje, ki jo vodi ustanovitelj, postavi pri okoli milijonu dolarjev letno.
  4. Vodenje prodajne ekipe. To umesti približno med 3 in 10 milijonov.
  5. Vodenje voditeljev. Od približno 10 do 30 milijonov vodiš majhno skupino, kultura pa potuje prek nje.

Pravilo za vsako opravilo: če ga trg naredi za delček vrednosti tvoje ure, ga predaj, tudi če verjameš, da ga nihče ne naredi tako dobro. Sproščeni čas mora iti v delo z večjo vrednostjo. Ustanovitelji, ki čas sprostijo in ga nato ne zapolnijo z ničimer, vidijo padec marž.

Sistem delegiranja

  1. Časovna študija. Teden dni beleži, kaj delaš, vsakih 15 minut. Vsako opravilo zapiši čim bolj podrobno. Isto študijo uporabi pri vsakem ključnem zaposlenem, od katerega je podjetje preveč odvisno.
  2. Oseba, proces ali projekt. Ob vsako opravilo zapiši, ali potrebuje osebo, proces ali enkraten projekt. Oceni ga zeleno (lahko predaš takoj), rumeno (najprej moraš zgraditi proces) ali rdeče (manjka ti veščina ali človek). Najprej počisti zelena, nato rumena, nato rdeča.
  3. Odločitvena pravila. Dokumentiraj tudi odločitve, ne le opravila: če se zgodi to, naredi tisto. Bolj ko je vloga ponavljajoča, bolj standardna so pravila. Vsakemu daj pooblastilo za porabo brez odobritve; njegovi primeri so do 500 dolarjev na odločitev z mesečno omejitvijo 5.000 ali operativec, ki lahko porabi do 1.000, ne da bi ga klical. Mesečni finančni pregled ujame zapravljanje.
  4. KPI-ji. Vsakega vprašaj, kako njegova vloga podjetju prinaša denar. Če ne zna odgovoriti, mu pomagaj, na primer tako, da stroške slabo opravljenega dela obrneš v KPI-je.
  5. Test zrelosti. Ali doseže 100 % rezultata pri približno 80 % tvoje kakovosti? Ob predaji sprejme 60 do 80 %, če je pot izboljšanja jasna, ljudi pa ocenjuje po tem, kako hitro napredujejo.
  6. Predaja. Najprej te senčijo, nato jih nadziraš, nato si na voljo, a nisi vpleten.

Dokumentiraj, pokaži, podvoji (document, demonstrate, duplicate)

Njegova metoda učenja se ponavlja v hotseatih in prodajnih predavanjih: napiši kontrolni seznam, opravilo naredi pred njimi samo po seznamu, nato ga oni naredijo pred tabo, ti pa takoj popravljaš. Koraki naj bodo dovolj preprosti za nekoga brez izkušenj. Če nimaš zapisanega procesa, se posnemi pri delu: struktura je že tam.

Vedi, kje so zakopana trupla (know where the bodies are buried)

Delegiranje uspe le, če rezultati funkcije po predaji ostanejo enaki ali se izboljšajo. Če padejo, si odgovornost odvrgel (abdication). Od lastnika pričakuje, da zna našteti trenutne probleme vsakega oddelka. Če oddelek prinaša samo dobre novice, si predaleč. Rešitev je več vpogleda, nikoli vračanje nadzora k sebi.

Testi neodvisnosti od lastnika

  • Nisi v podjetniškem klepetu, doseže te lahko ena oseba, s to osebo se dobiš enkrat na teden, podjetje pa raste.
  • Osnovni prehod: mesec dni odsotnosti in nič ne zgori. Pravi prehod: tri mesece odsotnosti (v drugem videu šest) in podjetje raste. Priporoča, da si čas res vzameš, popraviš, kar se pokvari, in ponoviš.
  • Nov operativec mora lastniku prinesti več časa. Če mu predvsem ustvarja nova opravila, ne deluje.
  • Za učenje presoje vprašaj zaposlene »kaj misliš, da bi rekel jaz?« in jim reci, naj naredijo to.

Ko delo ni opravljeno

Dve predavanji dajeta isti trikotnik. Preden presodiš človeka, preveri:

Vzrok Vprašanje Rešitev
Pričakovanje (kaj) Sem ga jasno izrazil, pisno, in je v njegovem poročanju? Zapiši ga z rokom; pogosto ga preverjaj
Usposobljenost (kako) Ali ve, kako, glede na svojo obremenitev? Naredita skupaj, posnemi, določi način poročanja
Motivacija (zakaj) Ali ve, zakaj je to pomembno za podjetje in zanj? Pojasni zakaj; preveri, ali ga plačni načrt nagrajuje

Začne pri »verjetno sem kriv jaz« in najprej preveri pričakovanje. Če sta komunikacija in usposabljanje urejena in se nič ne spremeni, to poimenuje kot motivacijo in opravi neposreden pogovor. Premestitev človeka v drugo vlogo je navadno napaka.

Ustanovitelj skriva pravo ozko grlo

Njegovo najostrejše opažanje: ustanovitelji še naprej delajo to, kar imajo radi in v čemer so dobri, predajo pa, česar ne marajo. Njihove prednosti postanejo ozko grlo. Človek iz medijev optimizira medije, closer študira zapiranje prodaje. Če ne maraš dela z ljudmi, je tvoje ozko grlo verjetno zaposlovanje in vodenje. Rast zahteva spremembo identitete: od najboljšega prodajalca ali trenerja do človeka, ki uči in nato zgradi sistem. »Ne zaupam ljudem« po njegovem ni sprejemljiv razlog.

Primeri

Primeri so povzeti in anonimizirani iz Alexovih videov. Primeri, označeni kot izmišljeni, so samo za ponazoritev.

Lastnik terenskega servisa, ki je v vsem

Pred. Vrhunsko podjetje za terenske storitve, okoli 1,5 milijona dolarjev prihodkov in 700 tisoč neto. Vsak tehnik prinese 180–300 tisoč letno. Lastnik vztraja, da je vpleten v vse, in je nehal zaposlovati.

Po. Enotedenska časovna študija, spodnja polovica njegovih aktivnosti predana, sproščeni čas pa gre v iskanje tehnikov po vsej državi s proračunom okoli 50 tisoč na zaposlitev.

Polovica lastnikovega časa nazaj lahko podvoji podjetje, ker je tehnik vreden veliko več, kot stane, da ga najdeš.

Zobna ordinacija, kjer en strokovnjak dela vse

Pred. Zdravnik ustanovitelj sam vodi vse drage primere, omejen na približno 25–30 pacientov na mesec, čakalna doba pa je več tednov.

Po. Sodelavci prevzamejo preprostejšo polovico obiskov. Ustanovitelj postane arhitekt in učitelj. Skupaj s spremembami cene in plačil je spremljanje pokazalo dvig povprečnega posla s približno 28 na 44 tisoč dolarjev in termin v tednu dni.

Če strokovnjaku prepoloviš obremenitev, se kapaciteta podvoji. Ustanoviteljeva identiteta se premakne od najboljšega izvajalca k najboljšemu učitelju.

Lastnik, ki najbolje zapira prodajo

Lastnik podjetja za dogodke je zaprl okoli 40 % pogovorov, dva prodajalca pa sta zaostajala. Alex je prodajalca spremenil v setterja za lastnika, še vedno na proviziji, z dnevnimi jutranjimi in večernimi preverjanji, dokler proces ni bil dovolj dokumentiran, da ga je lahko vrnil njima.

Ustanovitelj analitskega svetovanja (izmišljeno)

Ustanovitelj BI svetovalnega podjetja z desetimi ljudmi dela predprodajo, načrtovanje rešitev in preglede kode. Časovna študija pokaže 12 ur na teden za preglede. Napiše kontrolni seznam za preglede (zeleno), posname se pri treh pregledih (dokumentiraj, pokaži), nato višji svetovalec pregleduje, on pa gleda (podvoji). Po mesecu višji svetovalec opravi vse preglede pri okoli 80 % njegove kakovosti, s pravilom: eskaliraj samo spremembe skupnih podatkovnih modelov. 12 ur gre v predprodajo.

Pogoste napake

  • Odvrženje odgovornosti. Predaš in nikoli več ne pogledaš. Rezultati padejo, lastnik pa sklene, da tega nihče ne zna.
  • Predajaš samo, česar ne maraš. Obdržiš opravila, ki jih imaš rad, in ta postanejo ozko grlo.
  • Predaja opravil brez odločitev. Brez pravil »če, potem« in omejitev porabe se vsaka izjema vrne k tebi.
  • Čakanje na 100 %. Če prvi dan zahtevaš svojo kakovost, ne predaš ničesar.
  • Zapravljen sproščeni čas. Odkupljeni čas mora iti v delo z večjo vrednostjo, sicer trpijo marže.

Kako bi to uporabil Alex

  • Začne s časovno študijo, nikoli z ugibanjem. Dnevnik pokaže, kam gredo ure.
  • Predaja od dna seznama vrednosti in nadaljuje v stalnem vrstnem redu: podpora, administracija, prodaja, prodajna ekipa, voditelji.
  • Odločitve hoče na papirju, s pravili »če, potem« in omejitvijo porabe, da lahko ljudje ukrepajo brez klica lastniku.
  • Postavi prag zrelosti pri približno 80 % lastnikove kakovosti in na začetku sprejme manj, če je napredek viden.
  • Najprej postavi diagnozo, šele nato krivi: pričakovanje, nato usposobljenost, nato motivacija.
  • Neodvisnost preizkusi zares: lastnik gre za mesec dni stran, nato za dlje, in popravi, kar se pokvari.
  • Vprašanja, ki bi jih postavil: Kako je bil videti tvoj zadnji teden v 15-minutnih blokih? Katera opravila bi lahko kdo naredil za delček vrednosti tvoje ure? Znaš našteti trenutne probleme vsakega oddelka? Bi prodajni model deloval, če bi prodajalci zapirali pol tako dobro kot ti? So vedeli, da se to pričakuje, znajo to narediti in vedo, zakaj? Česa se oklepaš, ker te veseli?

Delovni list

To izpelji v naslednjih dveh tednih.

  1. Časovna študija: En teden beleži v 15-minutnih blokih. Katerih deset opravil ti je vzelo največ časa?
  2. Barve: Vsako opravilo označi zeleno, rumeno ali rdeče in zraven zapiši osebo, proces ali projekt.
  3. Pravila: Za najpogostejše zeleno opravilo zapiši pet pravil »če, potem« in omejitev porabe.
  4. Prag: Določi, kako je videti 80 % tvoje kakovosti in kako jo boš meril.
  5. Odsotnost: Izberi datum za teden dni odsotnosti. Kaj mora obstajati do takrat, da se nič ne pokvari?
