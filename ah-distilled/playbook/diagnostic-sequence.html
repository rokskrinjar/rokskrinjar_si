The Diagnostic SequenceZaporedje diagnoze

Alex's Playbook · Diagnostic Sequence

The fixed order of questions Alex runs on any business before he gives advice. Numbers first, then the one constraint, then two or three moves.Stalni vrstni red vprašanj, ki jih Alex postavi vsakemu podjetju, preden da nasvet. Najprej številke, nato eno ozko grlo, nato dve ali tri poteze.

The gist

Alex diagnoses almost every business the same way. He asks for the numbers before he listens to the story, because the problem the owner brings is rarely the problem the numbers show. "We need more leads" turns out to be an offer problem, "ads stopped working" turns out to be a lifetime value problem, and "my team is the issue" turns out to be a broken model.

Then he sorts the business into one of two buckets: it cannot get enough customers (demand) or it cannot serve more customers (supply). Inside that bucket he finds the single stage that sits furthest below a healthy benchmark, names it as a "how do I…" question, and limits his advice to the two or three moves that attack it.

Numbers → Supply or demand? → Weakest stage vs benchmark → One "how do I…" question → 2–3 moves, then the owner's real goal decides the plan

How it works

1. The fixed order of questions

In his live hotseats the questions come in nearly the same order every time: what do you sell, to whom, at what price, how do customers find you, how many sales a month, what is the close rate, what is a customer worth over their lifetime, what does it cost to get one, how many leave, what is the gross margin, how much capacity is left. Only then does he ask what the owner actually wants.

He expects owners to know these by heart. In one walkthrough he reduces it to six numbers: new sales per month, current revenue, price, churn, lifetime value and gross margin, and notes that most owners cannot name the last three. In another he says plainly that he breaks down unit economics before asking about payroll or anything else, because that is where the problem shows up.

2. Distrust the stated problem

A recurring pattern across many videos: the owner names a symptom, the numbers point somewhere else. A practice with three "separate" problems (low margin, pricing set by insurers, one channel) had one model problem. A founder listing six problems was usually missing around three senior people. An owner who wants to start a new business after a plateau usually does not know how to grow the current one. Alex treats the stated problem as a hypothesis and checks it against the numbers.

He also separates a complaint from a constraint: if you know more spend would double the business and you are not doing it, your chores are not the constraint.

3. Supply or demand triage

His first real question in most hotseats: is this business supply-constrained or demand-constrained? The test is concrete. If leads, ad spend or outreach doubled tomorrow, would sales double, or would you create a mess? A mess means supply. He says you can only have one of the two at a time, and the constraint swings back and forth like an accordion. Owners stay stuck for years because they keep working on the side they already solved.

If the answer is… The work is… What he refuses
Demand (capacity is free) Offer, price, conversion, creative, then more spend Fixing delivery or hiring ahead of growth
Supply (doubling would break delivery) Raise price, change the delivery ratio, productise, hire More marketing; a second business "to market more"

On the supply side he goes further: tolerate mediocre marketing while capacity is full, because improving it would make the business worse.

4. Compare each stage to a benchmark

Inside the funnel he walks the stages (click, opt-in, booking, show, close, repeat purchase) and compares each to the numbers he knows. He fixes the stage furthest below its benchmark, because that is where a 2–4x is possible. Another version of the same idea: add the same small percentage to every stage and see which one moves output the most. That is usually the weakest stage.

He normally optimises acquisition front to back, since early stages touch the most people. The exception is one stage far below the norm, such as a 5% show rate where 60% is normal. A 60% show rate cannot triple; a 1% click-through can.

5. Turn the constraint into a "how do I…" question

Once he has the constraint, he rewrites it as a solvable skill question: "how do I recruit, train and manage a sales team", "how do I get customers from a channel I am allowed to use". In one story he refused to discuss pricing, messaging or lifetime gross profit with an owner until the named constraint was solved.

6. Limit the advice to two or three moves

Hotseats end with a short, ordered list. He writes the easy, high-impact moves first and the big bets last, and repeatedly leaves alone the parts that already work (a converting offer, a sales process with healthy close rates). In one case he trimmed his plan to two steps the owner would actually carry out. He also orders the fixes so that cheap conversion gains come before more spend, since they may already push the business into a supply constraint.

7. Then the owner's real goal

Before he commits to a plan he asks what the owner wants: double it, sell it, or simply make money. The answer changes the advice. If the owner only wants cash, he avoids complexity and repeats the working playbook. If they want to sell, concentration risk and owner dependence move up the list. In some talks he asks the goal first, together with current state, desired state and the obstacle between them.

Examples

Cases are paraphrased and anonymised from Alex's videos. Any example marked as invented is for illustration only.

Duct-cleaning service, about $1.25M revenue

Before. The owner asks for more and better leads. Numbers: 38% net margin, 99% show rate, 82% close rate, ads returning roughly 13 to 1, one active referral partner.

After. Triage: demand-constrained, nothing badly broken, so incremental moves. A 10% price rise first (an 82% close rate signals underpricing), then a dedicated landing page, more ad creative and spend, reactivation emails, and outbound last.

Price and conversion fixes come before spend, so the new van and staff are not swamped.

Online weight-loss program, about $4M, webinar funnel

The owner was nervous about launching a big new lead magnet. Alex compared each stage to his numbers: show rate at 30% was already above benchmark, opt-in sat at 5% where 20% or more is possible. All testing went to the opt-in page and the lead magnet's name, because the stage furthest below benchmark held the largest multiple.

Business at about $150K a month

Many things could have been improved, including the pricing model and messaging. Alex named one constraint (he had never built a sales team), rewrote it as "how do I recruit, manage and train salespeople", and declined to talk about anything else on the follow-up call.

Qlik consultancy, invented

A two-person BI consultancy says it needs more leads. Numbers: 6 proposals a month, 5 signed (83% close), both consultants booked for the next four months. Triage answer: doubling leads would break delivery, so it is supply. The question becomes "how do I deliver the same result with less senior time?" Two moves: raise the day rate on new work, and split delivery so a junior builds the data model while seniors handle design and sign-off.

Common mistakes

  • Starting from the story. Advice given before the numbers usually treats a symptom.
  • Working the solved side. Buying more leads while delivery is full, or hiring while capacity sits idle.
  • Fixing everything at once. A list of ten fixes gets none of them done; he keeps it to two or three.
  • Touching what works. Rewriting an offer or script that converts well, instead of attacking the weak stage.
  • Skipping the goal. The right plan for a cash-flow owner differs from the plan for someone building to sell.

How Alex would apply it

  • Numbers before opinions. He asks for price, volume, close rate, churn, lifetime value, acquisition cost and gross margin before he says anything about strategy.
  • Supply or demand first. If doubling customers would break delivery, he works on capacity and price. If not, it is a pure demand problem, and he works on offer and traffic.
  • The weakest stage gets everything. He benchmarks each funnel stage and puts all testing on the one furthest below its norm.
  • One constraint, phrased as a skill. He turns it into a "how do I…" question and refuses side topics until it is solved.
  • Short ordered list. Two or three moves, cheapest and highest impact first, working parts left alone.
  • A new venture is a red flag. If the owner would drop the new idea once the current business tripled, it is an escape from the constraint.
  • Questions he would ask: What do you sell, to whom, at what price? Where do customers come from? What are your show and close rates? What is a customer worth and what does one cost? If customers doubled, what breaks? Why aren't you spending ten times more on ads? What do you want: double it, sell it, or take cash out?

Worksheet

Answer with numbers, not impressions.

  1. The numbers: Write down new sales per month, price, close rate, monthly churn, gross margin and acquisition cost. Mark the ones you had to guess.
  2. Triage: If leads doubled next month, what would break first? Is that a supply or a demand answer?
  3. Benchmarks: List your funnel stages with today's rate next to each. Which is furthest below a healthy level?
  4. The question: Rewrite your constraint as one "how do I…" sentence.
  5. The moves: Pick at most three moves that attack it, in order. What are you deliberately leaving untouched?
  6. The goal: Do you want to grow it, sell it, or take cash out? Does the plan still fit?

Bistvo

Alex skoraj vsako podjetje diagnosticira na enak način. Za številke vpraša, preden posluša zgodbo, ker problem, s katerim pride lastnik, le redko ustreza problemu, ki ga pokažejo številke. »Rabimo več leadov« se izkaže za problem ponudbe, »oglasi ne delujejo več« za problem življenjske vrednosti stranke, »težava je ekipa« pa za pokvarjen poslovni model.

Nato podjetje razvrsti v enega od dveh košev: ne more dobiti dovolj strank (povpraševanje, demand) ali ne more postreči več strankam (ponudba oziroma kapaciteta, supply). Znotraj koša poišče eno fazo, ki je najdlje pod zdravim mejnikom, jo ubesedi kot vprašanje »kako naj…« in nasvet omeji na dve ali tri poteze, ki jo napadejo.

Številke → Kapaciteta ali povpraševanje? → Najšibkejša faza proti mejniku → Eno vprašanje »kako naj…« → 2–3 poteze, načrt pa nato določi lastnikov resnični cilj

Kako deluje

1. Stalni vrstni red vprašanj

V hotseatih v živo si vprašanja skoraj vedno sledijo v istem zaporedju: kaj prodajaš, komu, po kakšni ceni, kako te stranke najdejo, koliko prodaj na mesec, kolikšna je stopnja zaključka (close rate), koliko je stranka vredna v svoji življenjski dobi, koliko stane, da jo dobiš, koliko jih odide, kolikšna je bruto marža, koliko kapacitete še ostane. Šele nato vpraša, kaj lastnik v resnici želi.

Pričakuje, da lastnik te številke zna na pamet. V kratki razlagi jih skrči na šest: nove prodaje na mesec, trenutni prihodek, cena, odliv (churn), življenjska vrednost (LTV) in bruto marža, in pripomni, da večina lastnikov zadnjih treh ne zna povedati. Drugje jasno reče, da najprej razstavi ekonomiko enote (unit economics), preden vpraša o plačah ali čemer koli drugem, ker se tam pokaže problem.

2. Ne zaupaj navedenemu problemu

Vzorec, ki se ponavlja v mnogih videih: lastnik poimenuje simptom, številke pa kažejo drugam. Ambulanta s tremi »ločenimi« problemi (nizka marža, cene, ki jih določajo zavarovalnice, en sam kanal) je imela en problem modela. Ustanovitelj, ki našteje šest problemov, navadno pogreša približno tri višje kadre. Lastnik, ki po zastoju želi začeti novo podjetje, običajno ne zna rasti obstoječega. Alex navedeni problem obravnava kot hipotezo in jo preveri s številkami.

Loči tudi pritožbo od ozkega grla. Če že veš, da bi z večjim vložkom podvojil posel, pa tega preprosto ne narediš, administrativna opravila, nad katerimi tarnaš, niso tisto, kar omejuje rast.

3. Triaža: kapaciteta ali povpraševanje

Njegovo prvo pravo vprašanje v večini hotseatov: je to podjetje omejeno s kapaciteto ali s povpraševanjem? Test je konkreten. Če bi se jutri podvojili leadi, oglaševalski proračun ali outreach, bi se podvojila prodaja ali bi nastala zmeda? Zmeda pomeni kapaciteto. Pravi, da imaš lahko naenkrat samo eno od obojega, ozko grlo pa niha sem in tja kot harmonika. Lastniki obtičijo za leta, ker še naprej delajo na strani, ki so jo že rešili.

Če je odgovor… Delo je… Česa ne naredi
Povpraševanje (kapaciteta je prosta) Ponudba, cena, konverzija, kreative, nato večji vložek Popravljanje izvedbe ali zaposlovanje pred rastjo
Kapaciteta (podvojitev bi zlomila izvedbo) Dvig cene, sprememba razmerja izvedbe, produktizacija, zaposlovanje Več marketinga; drugo podjetje »za več marketinga«

Pri kapaciteti gre še dlje: dokler je kapaciteta polna, prenašaj povprečen marketing, ker bi njegovo izboljšanje podjetju škodilo.

4. Vsako fazo primerjaj z mejnikom

V lijaku gre po fazah (klik, prijava, rezervacija, udeležba, zaključek, ponoven nakup) in vsako primerja s številkami, ki jih pozna. Popravi fazo, ki je najdlje pod svojim mejnikom, ker je tam mogoč 2- do 4-kratnik. Druga različica iste ideje: vsaki fazi dodaj enak majhen odstotek in poglej, katera najbolj premakne izhod. Navadno je to najšibkejša faza.

Pridobivanje strank običajno optimizira od spredaj nazaj, ker zgodnje faze dosežejo največ ljudi. Izjema je faza, ki je daleč pod normo, na primer 5-odstotna udeležba (show rate), kjer je normalno 60 %. Opozori tudi, da faza ne more preseči 100 %: 60-odstotne udeležbe ne moreš potrojiti, 1-odstotni klikovni delež pa lahko.

5. Ozko grlo spremeni v vprašanje »kako naj…«

Ko ima ozko grlo, ga prepiše v rešljivo vprašanje o veščini: »kako naj zaposlim, izurim in vodim prodajno ekipo«, »kako naj dobim stranke iz kanala, ki ga smem uporabljati«. V eni zgodbi z lastnikom ni hotel govoriti o cenah, sporočilih ali življenjskem bruto dobičku, dokler poimenovano ozko grlo ni bilo rešeno.

6. Nasvet omeji na dve ali tri poteze

Hotseati se končajo s kratkim, razvrščenim seznamom. Najprej napiše lahke poteze z velikim učinkom, velike stave na konec, in vedno znova pusti pri miru tisto, kar že deluje (ponudbo, ki konvertira, prodajni proces z zdravo stopnjo zaključka). V enem primeru je načrt skrajšal na dva koraka, ki ju bo lastnik dejansko izvedel. Popravke tudi razvrsti tako, da poceni izboljšave konverzije pridejo pred večjim vložkom, ker lahko podjetje že same potisnejo v omejitev kapacitete.

7. Nato lastnikov resnični cilj

Preden se zaveže načrtu, vpraša, kaj lastnik želi: podvojiti, prodati ali preprosto služiti. Odgovor spremeni nasvet. Če lastnik želi le denarni tok, se izogne kompleksnosti in ponavlja delujoči recept. Če želi prodati, se tveganje koncentracije in odvisnost od lastnika pomakneta višje na seznamu. V nekaterih predavanjih cilj vpraša najprej, skupaj s trenutnim stanjem, želenim stanjem in oviro med njima.

Primeri

Primeri so povzeti in anonimizirani iz Alexovih videov. Primeri, označeni kot izmišljeni, so samo za ponazoritev.

Čiščenje prezračevalnih kanalov, okoli 1,25 milijona dolarjev prihodka

Pred. Lastnik prosi za več in boljše leade. Številke: 38 % neto marže, 99 % udeležbe, 82 % zaključka, oglasi vračajo približno 13 proti 1, en aktiven partner za priporočila.

Po. Triaža: omejitev s povpraševanjem, nič hudo pokvarjenega, torej postopne poteze. Najprej 10-odstotni dvig cene (82-odstotni zaključek kaže na prenizko ceno), nato namenska pristajalna stran, več oglasnih kreativ in večji proračun, e-pošta za reaktivacijo, outbound na koncu.

Vrstni red je pomemben: popravki cene in konverzije pridejo pred večjim vložkom, da novi kombi in ljudje niso preplavljeni.

Spletni program hujšanja, okoli 4 milijonov dolarjev, lijak z webinarjem

Lastnica se je bala lansirati velik nov lead magnet. Alex je vsako fazo primerjal s svojimi številkami: udeležba pri 30 % je bila že nad mejnikom, prijava pa pri 5 %, kjer je mogočih 20 % ali več. Vse testiranje je šlo na prijavno stran in ime lead magneta, ker je faza, ki je najdlje pod mejnikom, skrivala največji multiplikator.

Podjetje pri okoli 150 tisoč dolarjih na mesec

Izboljšati bi se dalo marsikaj, tudi cenovni model in sporočila. Alex je poimenoval eno ozko grlo (lastnik nikoli ni zgradil prodajne ekipe), ga prepisal v »kako naj zaposlim, vodim in izurim prodajalce« in na naslednjem klicu ni hotel govoriti o ničemer drugem.

Qlik svetovalno podjetje, izmišljeno

Dvočlansko BI svetovalno podjetje pravi, da rabi več leadov. Številke: 6 ponudb na mesec, 5 podpisanih (83 % zaključka), oba svetovalca zasedena za naslednje štiri mesece. Triaža: podvojitev leadov bi zlomila izvedbo, torej gre za kapaciteto. Vprašanje postane »kako naj isti rezultat izvedem z manj seniorskega časa?«. Dve potezi: dvig dnevne postavke za nove projekte in delitev izvedbe, pri kateri junior zgradi podatkovni model, seniorja pa skrbita za zasnovo in prevzem.

Pogoste napake

  • Začetek pri zgodbi. Nasvet pred številkami navadno zdravi simptom.
  • Delo na že rešeni strani. Kupovanje leadov, ko je izvedba polna, ali zaposlovanje, ko kapaciteta stoji.
  • Popravljanje vsega hkrati. Od seznama desetih popravkov se ne izvede noben; on ostane pri dveh ali treh.
  • Poseganje v to, kar deluje. Prepisovanje ponudbe ali skripte, ki dobro konvertira, namesto napada na šibko fazo.
  • Preskok cilja. Pravi načrt za lastnika, ki želi denarni tok, je drugačen od načrta za tistega, ki gradi za prodajo.

Kako bi to uporabil Alex

  • Številke pred mnenji. Za ceno, obseg, stopnjo zaključka, odliv, življenjsko vrednost, strošek pridobitve in bruto maržo vpraša, preden reče karkoli o strategiji.
  • Najprej kapaciteta ali povpraševanje. Če bi podvojitev strank zlomila izvedbo, dela na kapaciteti in ceni. Če ne, gre za čisti problem povpraševanja in dela na ponudbi in prometu.
  • Najšibkejša faza dobi vse. Vsako fazo lijaka primerja z mejnikom in vse testiranje usmeri v tisto, ki je najdlje pod normo.
  • Eno ozko grlo, ubesedeno kot veščina. Spremeni ga v vprašanje »kako naj…« in stranskih tem ne odpira, dokler ni rešeno.
  • Kratek razvrščen seznam. Dve ali tri poteze, najcenejše in najučinkovitejše najprej, deli, ki delujejo, ostanejo pri miru.
  • Novo podjetje je opozorilni znak. Če bi lastnik novo idejo opustil, ko bi se obstoječe podjetje potrojilo, je to beg pred ozkim grlom.
  • Vprašanja, ki bi jih postavil: Kaj prodajaš, komu, po kakšni ceni? Od kod pridejo stranke? Kolikšni sta udeležba in stopnja zaključka? Koliko je vredna stranka in koliko stane? Če se stranke podvojijo, kaj se zlomi? Zakaj ne vlagaš desetkrat več v oglase? Kaj želiš: podvojiti, prodati ali vzeti denar ven?

Delovni list

Odgovarjaj s številkami, ne z vtisi.

  1. Številke: Zapiši nove prodaje na mesec, ceno, stopnjo zaključka, mesečni odliv, bruto maržo in strošek pridobitve stranke. Označi tiste, ki si jih moral oceniti.
  2. Triaža: Če bi se leadi naslednji mesec podvojili, kaj bi se prvo zlomilo? Je to odgovor kapacitete ali povpraševanja?
  3. Mejniki: Naštej faze svojega lijaka in ob vsako zapiši današnji delež. Katera je najdlje pod zdravo ravnjo?
  4. Vprašanje: Svoje ozko grlo prepiši v en stavek »kako naj…«.
  5. Poteze: Izberi največ tri poteze, ki ga napadejo, po vrsti. Česa namenoma ne boš spreminjal?
  6. Cilj: Želiš rasti, prodati ali vzeti denar ven? Ali načrt še ustreza?
