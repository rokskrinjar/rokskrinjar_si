Focus & SubtractionFokus in odvzemanje

Alex's Playbook · Focus, 1-1-1, subtraction

Alex's default fix for a stuck or overwhelmed owner is to take things away. One business, one avatar, one channel and one priority, held long enough to compound.Alexov privzeti recept za obstalega ali preobremenjenega lastnika je odvzemanje. En posel, en avatar, en kanal in ena prioriteta, dovolj dolgo, da se učinek sešteva.

The gist

Across the hotseats, the most common thing Alex prescribes is removal. Owners arrive with two businesses, three offers, five channels, a side software product or a partner deal on the side, and describe the result as overwhelm or a plateau. He reads both as self-made complexity. Focus, in his usage, is something you get by subtracting, and he judges it by the number and quality of things you say no to.

1-1-1: one product, one avatar, one channel until about $1M a year. Then: one priority at a time, and subtract before you add.

The logic is economic. An owner's attention is the scarcest resource in a small company, and he treats strategy as the allocation of limited time, money and people across unlimited options. Every split of attention and every change has a cost, usually higher than the owner thinks.

How it works

1-1-1 until the first $1M

In at least two talks he states the rule directly: one product, one avatar and one channel until roughly $1M a year. He ranks avatar clarity in one Q&A: one product for one avatar is best, one product for many people is next, everything for everyone is worst. He only accepts a new avatar once the current one is exhausted, and when an owner itches for something new, he would rather add a channel than a customer type.

Subtract first, then add

Before anyone adds a venture, he wants a list of what can be removed from the current one, and he only lets them proceed if the hours left over are realistic. In one live session he estimates that halving attention to hedge against downside can push year-two results out to year four, because you compete against people who are fully committed.

The recurring subtractions:

  • Kill the vampire. An unprofitable line that drains cash and founder time from the cash cow gets cut (a side software product, a money-losing course, a second retail location that barely breaks even).
  • Exit side deals and partnerships as fast as possible. You can own several companies, but he treats being CEO of more than one as the usual failure. His litmus test: if a problem in it rings your phone, you are running it.
  • Fire-sell the weaker business when the other one is an order of magnitude better, so you get full attention back.
  • Keep passive investments passive. Other holdings only distract if they change what you do each day.

The woman in the red dress

He uses this image (also "girl in the red dress") for the shiny new opportunity that looks greener only because you have not seen its problems yet. It appears in at least four videos: a wholesale owner who wants to build salon software, a B2C coaching company eyeing enterprise clients, a whale pulling a firm off its avatar, and founders who start another business to avoid a new kind of problem. His diagnosis is the same each time: the new venture is an escape from a solvable problem, and the skill gap that caused the plateau will follow you.

Every change costs a dip

In one hotseat he gives his own anecdotal estimate: any change to a process (a sales script, onboarding, a leadership routine) causes a temporary drop of about 20% while the team adapts. So he only makes a change when the risk-adjusted upside is about 20% or more, for example 40% upside at 50/50 odds. He treats changes like a limited punch card: roughly one test a week on automated assets such as landing pages, and perhaps one real process change per department per quarter. Ideas go onto a private list with estimated upside and probability, and are pulled only when the team has bandwidth and the idea beats everything else in the queue.

One priority, and let small fires burn

He asks for the one thing that, if true twelve months from now, would change everything (an owned lead channel, a talent pipeline, faster cash conversion). If the CEO cannot name it, he says the CEO has failed. Once chosen, the bet gets patience: no second solution because the first has not paid off in 30 days. Small fires are allowed to burn, because very little kills a business within hours, and the first four to six hours of the day go to the highest-leverage work.

Simplify offers and features

The same subtraction runs through the product. He calls overwhelm the main churn driver after price: paying for five things and using one feels like 20% value, while paying the same for one thing feels like 100%. His moves are to remove deliverables, ask members what they would fight to keep and what they would not miss, and design a simple start-here path. In marketing he looks for the single most compelling point. In sales he usually cuts about half of a script. His shorthand is that simple scales and fancy fails.

Examples

Cases are paraphrased and anonymised from Alex's videos. Any example marked as invented is for illustration only.

Solo legal practice with two side products

Before. A solo attorney serving creative professionals sells hourly services, a low-priced course and a software subscription off one video audience. The services make money, the software burns cash, and together the side lines lose about $100K a year.

After. Kill the software or sell it outright. Make the education free and use it as nurture. Route every call to action to one channel and one sales call, and sell an outcome package at a flat fee instead of hours.

The services already had more demand than supply. Removing the vampire stopped the losses, and pointing all traffic at one monetisation path made the remaining business simpler to grow.

Founder with four ventures

A founder running writing retreats, a publishing house, a TV show and a business school keeps starting things because she gets bored. Alex tells her to follow where the market already pays (the books), stop pushing the school, use the show as media to sell the core offer, and put her appetite for novelty into marketing, where change is cheap.

Contractor with a better second business

An owner runs an established construction firm (about $11M at roughly 18% margin) and a young elevator-service company (about $3M at roughly 30% in year one, recurring). The safe route is to spend profit on leaders to run construction. Alex's own route is to sell the construction firm quickly, even at a discount, and go all in on the recurring business, because in his view one focused quarter of recurring growth there could equal the enterprise value of the old firm.

Common mistakes

  • Hedging by splitting. Running the old and new business side by side feels safe. He sees it as the default way to fail at both.
  • Calling an escape a strategy. A new product line, avatar or company started at a plateau usually avoids the skill gap that caused the plateau.
  • Changing many things at once. Each change costs a dip and attribution is lost. Five parallel changes stack five dips.
  • Adding to fix churn. More features and more content widen the gap between what is paid for and what is used.
  • Keeping a losing unit out of loyalty. A location or line that drains cash and attention from the winner gets closed unless something concrete requires it.

How Alex would apply it

  • He asks which unit actually makes money, and where sales really happen, then points everything there and closes the other doors.
  • He makes the owner list subtractions before any addition and checks whether the freed hours are enough for the new thing.
  • He names the red dress out loud when a new venture, avatar or whale shows up at a plateau, and redirects to the constraint in the current business.
  • He treats change as expensive: one real change at a time, a risk-adjusted 20% bar, real-time metrics to see the dip, and a queue for every other idea.
  • He picks one priority for the year and protects it, letting small fires burn rather than starting parallel fixes after 30 days.
  • He simplifies the product and the message: fewer deliverables, one core promise, the shortest script that works.
  • Questions he would ask: Which line makes the money? If a friend had one business everyone wanted plus four slower side ventures, what would you tell them? If you only did this one thing, would you hit your goal? What can we delete and still have it be as good or better? Would you still want this new venture if the current business tripled?

Worksheet

Answer in writing, with numbers where you have them.

  1. Units: List every product, avatar and channel. Next to each, write revenue, profit and the hours it takes from you each week.
  2. Vampires: Which unit loses money or attention to the cash cow? What would happen if you closed it this month?
  3. 1-1-1: Write your one product, one avatar and one channel for the next twelve months.
  4. One thing: What single result, if true in twelve months, would make most other problems irrelevant?
  5. Change budget: Which one change will you make this quarter, and what upside and odds justify the dip?
  6. Simplify: What can you remove from your offer that customers would not miss?

Bistvo

V hotseatih Alex najpogosteje predpiše odvzemanje. Lastniki pridejo z dvema podjetjema, tremi ponudbami, petimi kanali, stranskim programskim izdelkom ali partnerskim poslom ob strani, posledico pa opišejo kot preobremenjenost ali plato. Oboje razume kot kompleksnost, ki so si jo ustvarili sami. Fokus v njegovem pomenu dosežeš z odvzemanjem, meri pa ga s številom in kakovostjo stvari, ki jim rečeš ne.

1-1-1: en izdelek, en avatar, en kanal do približno 1 milijona dolarjev letno. Nato: ena prioriteta naenkrat in najprej odvzemi, potem dodaj.

Logika je ekonomska. Pozornost lastnika je v majhnem podjetju najredkejši vir, strategijo pa Alex razume kot razporejanje omejenega časa, denarja in ljudi med neomejene možnosti. Vsaka razdelitev pozornosti in vsaka sprememba ima ceno, navadno višjo, kot si lastnik misli.

Kako deluje

1-1-1 do prvega milijona

Vsaj v dveh nastopih pravilo pove neposredno: en izdelek, en avatar in en kanal do približno milijona dolarjev letno. V enem Q&A jasnost avatarja tudi razvrsti: najboljše je en izdelek za en avatar, sledi en izdelek za veliko ljudi, najslabše je vse za vse. Nov avatar sprejme šele, ko je trenutni izčrpan. Če lastnika mika nekaj novega, raje doda kanal kot nov tip stranke.

Najprej odvzemi, nato dodaj

Preden kdo doda nov posel, Alex zahteva seznam stvari, ki jih lahko odstrani iz obstoječega, in ga pusti naprej le, če je preostalih ur realno dovolj. V eni oddaji v živo oceni, da razpolovljena pozornost, s katero se lastnik zavaruje pred slabim izidom, lahko rezultate drugega leta prestavi v četrto leto, ker tekmuješ z ljudmi, ki so zavezani v celoti.

Odvzemanja, ki se ponavljajo:

  • Ubij vampirja (kill the vampire). Nedonosna linija, ki kravi molznici jemlje denar in čas ustanovitelja, gre stran (stranski programski izdelek, tečaj z izgubo, druga trgovina, ki komaj pokriva stroške).
  • Izstopi iz stranskih poslov in partnerstev, čim hitreje. Lastiš si lahko več podjetij, vodenje več kot enega kot direktor pa ima za običajen vzrok neuspeha. Njegov test: če te zaradi težave v njem pokličejo, ga vodiš ti.
  • Šibkejše podjetje prodaj hitro, tudi pod ceno, kadar je drugo za red velikosti boljše, da dobiš nazaj vso pozornost.
  • Pasivne naložbe naj ostanejo pasivne. Druge naložbe motijo le, če spremenijo tvoj vsakdan.

Ženska v rdeči obleki (woman in the red dress)

To podobo (tudi »girl in the red dress«) uporablja za bleščečo novo priložnost, ki se zdi boljša samo zato, ker še ne vidiš njenih težav. Pojavi se v vsaj štirih videih: lastnik veleprodaje, ki bi rad razvil programsko opremo za salone, B2C coaching podjetje, ki ga mikajo korporativne stranke, velika stranka (whale), ki podjetje odvleče od avatarja, in ustanovitelji, ki začnejo nov posel, da bi se izognili novi vrsti problema. Diagnoza je vsakič enaka: novi posel je beg pred rešljivim problemom, vrzel v znanju, ki je povzročila plato, pa gre s tabo.

Vsaka sprememba stane padec

V enem hotseatu poda svojo izkustveno oceno: vsaka sprememba procesa (prodajne skripte, uvajanja, vodstvene rutine) povzroči začasen padec za okoli 20 %, dokler se ekipa ne prilagodi. Zato spremembo naredi le, kadar je pričakovani dobiček, prilagojen tveganju, okoli 20 % ali več, na primer 40 % potenciala pri verjetnosti 50/50. Spremembe obravnava kot omejeno luknjalno kartico (punch card): približno en test na teden na avtomatiziranih elementih, kot so pristajalne strani, in morda ena prava sprememba procesa na oddelek na četrtletje. Ideje gredo na zasebni seznam z ocenjenim potencialom in verjetnostjo, iz njega pa jih vzame le, ko ima ekipa kapaciteto in ideja premaga vse ostale v vrsti.

Ena prioriteta in naj majhni požari gorijo

Sprašuje po eni stvari, ki bi, če bi čez dvanajst mesecev držala, spremenila vse (lasten kanal za leade, dotok talentov, hitrejša pretvorba v denar). Če je direktor ne zna poimenovati, po njegovem ni opravil svojega dela. Ko je izbrana, stava dobi potrpežljivost: nobene druge rešitve samo zato, ker se prva v 30 dneh še ni obrestovala. Majhni požari lahko gorijo (let small fires burn), ker podjetje v nekaj urah ubije le malo stvari, prve štiri do šest ur dneva pa gredo delu z največjim vzvodom.

Poenostavi ponudbe in funkcije

Isto odvzemanje velja za izdelek. Preobremenjenost ima za glavni vzrok odhodov strank (churn) takoj za ceno: če plačaš za pet stvari in uporabljaš eno, se zdi kot 20 % vrednosti, če za isti denar dobiš eno stvar, se zdi kot 100 %. Njegove poteze so odstraniti dele storitve, članov vprašati, za kaj bi se borili in česa ne bi pogrešali, ter zasnovati preprosto pot »začni tukaj«. V marketingu išče eno samo najmočnejšo točko. Prodajno skripto navadno skrajša za polovico. Njegova kratica: preprosto se skalira, razkošno propade (simple scales, fancy fails).

Primeri

Primeri so povzeti in anonimizirani iz Alexovih videov. Primeri, označeni kot izmišljeni, so samo za ponazoritev.

Samostojna odvetniška praksa z dvema stranskima izdelkoma

Pred. Samostojni odvetnik, ki dela za ustvarjalce, iz ene video publike prodaja storitve po urah, poceni tečaj in naročnino na programsko opremo. Storitve prinašajo denar, programska oprema ga žge, stranski liniji skupaj izgubljata okoli 100.000 dolarjev na leto.

Po. Programsko opremo ugasni ali jo prodaj v celoti. Izobraževanje naj bo brezplačno in služi negovanju leadov. Vsak poziv k dejanju naj vodi v en kanal in en prodajni klic, prodaja pa naj bo paket z izidom po fiksni ceni namesto ur.

Storitve so že imele več povpraševanja kot zmogljivosti. Odstranitev vampirja je ustavila izgube, usmeritev vsega prometa v eno pot do prihodka pa je preostali posel naredila lažji za rast.

Ustanoviteljica s štirimi posli

Ustanoviteljica vodi pisateljske umike, založbo, TV oddajo in poslovno šolo, nove stvari pa začenja, ker se dolgočasi. Alex ji svetuje, naj gre tja, kjer trg že plačuje (knjige), neha potiskati šolo, oddajo uporabi kot medij za prodajo osrednje ponudbe, željo po novostih pa preusmeri v marketing, kjer je sprememba poceni.

Gradbinec z boljšim drugim podjetjem

Lastnik ima uveljavljeno gradbeno podjetje (okoli 11 milijonov dolarjev pri marži okoli 18 %) in mlado servisno podjetje za dvigala (okoli 3 milijone v prvem letu pri okoli 30 %, ponavljajoči se prihodki). Varna pot je, da dobiček porabi za vodje, ki bi vodili gradbeništvo. Alexova pot je, da gradbeno podjetje hitro proda, tudi s popustom, in gre v celoti v ponavljajoči se posel, ker bi po njegovem eno osredotočeno četrtletje rasti tam lahko doseglo vrednost starega podjetja.

Pogoste napake

  • Zavarovanje z delitvijo. Vodenje starega in novega posla hkrati se zdi varno. Alex v tem vidi običajen način, da propadeta oba.
  • Beg, ki se mu reče strategija. Nova linija, avatar ali podjetje, začeto na platoju, se navadno izogne vrzeli v znanju, ki je plato povzročila.
  • Veliko sprememb hkrati. Vsaka sprememba stane padec, izgubi pa se tudi pripisovanje učinka. Pet vzporednih sprememb pomeni pet padcev.
  • Dodajanje kot zdravilo za odhode. Več funkcij in več vsebine poveča razkorak med tem, kar stranka plača, in tem, kar uporabi.
  • Ohranjanje enote z izgubo iz zvestobe. Lokacija ali linija, ki zmagovalcu jemlje denar in pozornost, se zapre, razen če jo kaj konkretnega zahteva.

Kako bi to uporabil Alex

  • Vpraša, katera enota dejansko služi, in kje se prodaja v resnici zgodi, nato vse usmeri tja in zapre druga vrata.
  • Pred vsakim dodajanjem zahteva seznam odvzemanj in preveri, ali je sproščenih ur dovolj za novo stvar.
  • Rdečo obleko poimenuje na glas, ko se na platoju pojavi nov posel, avatar ali velika stranka, in pogovor vrne k ozkemu grlu v obstoječem poslu.
  • Spremembo obravnava kot drago: ena prava sprememba naenkrat, prag 20 % ob upoštevanju tveganja, metrike v realnem času, da se vidi padec, in čakalna vrsta za vse druge ideje.
  • Izbere eno prioriteto za leto in jo varuje; majhne požare pusti goreti, namesto da bi po 30 dneh začel vzporedne popravke.
  • Poenostavi izdelek in sporočilo: manj delov storitve, ena osrednja obljuba, najkrajša skripta, ki deluje.
  • Vprašanja, ki bi jih postavil: Katera linija služi denar? Če bi imel prijatelj en posel, ki ga hočejo vsi, in štiri počasnejše stranske, kaj bi mu rekel? Če bi delal samo to eno stvar, bi dosegel cilj? Kaj lahko izbrišemo, pa bo še vedno enako dobro ali boljše? Bi si ta novi posel še vedno želel, če bi se obstoječi potrojil?

Delovni list

Odgovori pisno, s številkami, kjer jih imaš.

  1. Enote: Naštej vse izdelke, avatarje in kanale. Ob vsakem zapiši prihodek, dobiček in ure, ki ti jih vzame vsak teden.
  2. Vampirji: Katera enota kravi molznici jemlje denar ali pozornost? Kaj bi se zgodilo, če bi jo zaprl ta mesec?
  3. 1-1-1: Zapiši svoj en izdelek, en avatar in en kanal za naslednjih dvanajst mesecev.
  4. Ena stvar: Kateri en rezultat bi, če bi držal čez dvanajst mesecev, naredil večino drugih problemov nepomembnih?
  5. Proračun sprememb: Katero eno spremembo boš naredil to četrtletje in kakšen potencial in verjetnost upravičujeta padec?
  6. Poenostavi: Kaj lahko odstraniš iz ponudbe, pa tega stranke ne bi pogrešale?
