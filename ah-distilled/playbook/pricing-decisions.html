Alex's Playbook · Pricing (raise, test, never discount the core)

Price is the lever Alex reaches for first: it costs nothing to change, falls straight to profit, and most owners are underpriced. He tests it by conversion × price and almost never lowers it.Cena je vzvod, po katerem Alex seže najprej: spremeniti jo ne stane nič, razlika gre naravnost v dobiček, večina lastnikov pa je prepoceni. Preizkuša jo z zmnožkom konverzija × cena in je skoraj nikoli ne zniža.

The gist

Across his hotseats, Alex treats price as the first and cheapest lever. Changing it means changing a number or a sentence, with almost no operational drag, yet owners fear it more than anything else. His recurring diagnosis is that small owners price from their own wallet: they cannot imagine anyone paying more than they would, and they discount whatever comes easily to them.

The math is why he cares. On thin margins, a small price rise multiplies profit, while more customers only add to it. In one keynote he walks through a business with a 20% margin: doubling the number of customers doubles profit, but doubling the price makes profit six times larger, because the extra revenue carries no extra cost.

Test price by close rate × price (revenue per sales conversation), or total gross profit. Stop raising when that number falls, not when the first buyer says no.

How it works

Signals that you are underpriced

These come up again and again in the notes:

  • Very high close rates. In his business-math lecture he gives a rule-of-thumb table: closing 80% or more means you are probably 3–4x underpriced, 60–80% means 2–3x, 50–60% means 1.5–2x, 40–50% means 1.25–1.5x. Around 30–40% is about right if prospects are educated before the call. Below 30% he looks at the avatar and the sales process before ever touching price.
  • Full capacity. If demand exceeds supply, his first move is to raise price, before changing the delivery ratio or hiring. Fewer clients at a higher price means more money and freed time to build capacity.
  • Thin margin at full volume. If a business barely profits even on repeat and word-of-mouth customers (free acquisition), either fixed costs are too high or prices are wrong. For services he wants at least 80% gross margin, with delivery at about one fifth of price.
  • You can't afford acquisition. In his view, many "we need cheaper leads" problems are really problems of not making enough per customer.
  • Long time since the last increase. A raise after three or four years is just catching up with inflation.

Testing price

He wants price answered by tests, not opinions. Edge the price up until people stop saying yes, then back off slightly. Test live on calls, moving from low to high, because going down means crediting recent buyers. Test the less price-sensitive segment first. Expect to make more money before you make less: in one example a 50% raise with 10% fewer sales is clearly worth keeping. The point of maximum profit sits beyond the point of maximum conversion.

Never discount the core offer

This is one of his most consistent rules across the sales trainings. When asked for a discount:

  1. Say you could do it for more. This resets the anchor upward.
  2. Change the terms (payment plan, prepay) or the components (remove a feature, downsell to a lighter version).
  3. Add a bonus instead of a discount. He suggests giving sales teams a list of valued bonuses to use in place of price cuts.
  4. If you do lower price, trade it for something: a longer commitment, a testimonial, reviews, a referral. That keeps you in control and makes the difference defensible to other customers.

Unplanned price cuts sound like begging and lower trust. Price tests are planned in advance, not improvised on the call.

Anchors and premium tiers

Always present the premium tier first and step down only if the buyer says they are on a budget. Keep a high anchor even if few take it: a visible top tier lifts the perceived value of everything below it. His arithmetic: if 10% take an anchor priced at 10x, revenue roughly doubles. One-on-one should cost about 5x one-to-many; if everyone pays the premium, raise it. If the anchor isn't meant to sell, make it nearly identical to the core with one or two extra features.

Surge pricing and fees

Supply and demand apply to small businesses too. Charge more at peak times, or frame it as an off-peak discount. Sell speed: a same-day or priority fee is pure margin on the same work (he suggests 10% rising to 20% in one hotseat). Pass on card-processing fees, which in a restaurant case could add about 15% to profit. Name fees after what customers accept (setup, equipment) and price them to what you need.

Competitors and the worst customers

Never cut price to fight a competitor who undercuts you or copies the cheap part of your model. Be in the value-surpassing game: show buyers variables they hadn't considered (risk, ease, speed) so the comparison is no longer apples to apples. Accept that a raise loses some customers. When he tripled prices at his gym and lost about a third of members, revenue roughly doubled and staff nicknamed it "the culling", because it cleared out the chronic complainers. Price complaints from non-ideal customers mean the price is filtering correctly.

Examples

Cases are paraphrased and anonymised from Alex's videos. Any example marked as invented is for illustration only.

Local moving company, ~$300K a year

Before. Hourly pricing near the top of the market, losing about 40% of quotes on price. The owner thinks he needs more leads.

After. A VIP tier presented first (master movers, packing, materials, insurance, a price cap about 50% above the estimate), standard as the fallback, and a same-day surcharge starting at 10%.

Alex diagnosed a commodity offer, not a lead problem. Selling by the hour invites comparison, so he broke the offer into elements buyers hadn't thought about.

Custom awards manufacturer, ~$1.7M revenue, 6% net

Prices were set cost-plus. With net margin near 6%, a roughly 6% price rise about doubles profit. His advice: move to value-based pricing, split-test higher prices on top sellers, start with the impulse-gift segment rather than price-sensitive B2B buyers, and judge by gross profit × units.

Solo attorney billing $250 an hour

Average client value was about $1,500 and the close rate was very high. He moved her to a $5,800 flat fee plus a small performance share, with a $25K no-commission anchor and a $2–3K do-it-yourself downsell close enough in price to make the core look best.

Common mistakes

  • Pricing from your own wallet. You underprice because you can't imagine paying it yourself. Price from the value of the problem solved and the buyer's wealth.
  • Cost-plus or market-average pricing. Your costs are irrelevant to what the buyer will pay, and the market average is mostly small businesses that are struggling.
  • Judging a raise by unit sales. Fewer units at a higher price often means more profit. Look at conversion × price.
  • Cutting price to close or to fight a rival. It lowers trust and trains discount seekers. Change terms, components or bonuses instead.
  • Cutting price to fix churn. In one case a cut from about 3,000 to 2,500 a month changed nothing, and in his own business a cut didn't move churn either.

How Alex would apply it

  • He checks price before demand. Thin margin, full calendar, very high close rate, or unaffordable acquisition all point him to price first, ahead of new channels or hires.
  • He runs the numbers in the room. Margin, close rate and units turn the decision into arithmetic: a 20% cut at a 40% margin needs twice the sales to break even.
  • He raises, then backs it with value. If the owner can't justify a higher price, he improves the offer (faster, easier, more certain) and adds a guarantee with teeth, then raises 20–40%.
  • He refuses to discount the core. Terms, components and bonuses can move; the core price does not. Any discount is traded for something.
  • He builds a ladder of tiers. Premium first, a 10x anchor above, a downsell below, priced so the core looks best.
  • He accepts losing the worst customers. Watch what customers do, not what they say: if they keep buying while complaining, ignore the complaints.
  • Questions he would ask: How do you set prices today, and who set them? What is your close rate at this price? When did you last raise prices? What is your gross margin? Are you full? Which segment is least price-sensitive? What revenue per call do you get at price A versus price B?

Worksheet

Answer for your main offer, with real numbers from the last three months.

  1. Close rate: What share of qualified sales conversations close? Where does that put you in his table?
  2. Margin: What is your gross margin per client? Is delivery at or below one fifth of price?
  3. Test plan: Which next price will you quote on the next ten calls, and what revenue per call would make it a win?
  4. Discount script: What will you say instead of a discount? List three bonuses and one term change you can offer.
  5. Anchor: What would a version at 5–10x the price include? Which of those items cost you little?
  6. Fees and speed: Where could you charge for priority, peak times or setup?

Bistvo

V svojih hotseatih Alex obravnava ceno kot prvi in najcenejši vzvod. Spremeniti jo pomeni spremeniti številko ali stavek, skoraj brez operativnega bremena, a lastniki se je bojijo bolj kot česar koli drugega. Njegova ponavljajoča se diagnoza: mali lastniki postavljajo cene iz lastne denarnice. Ne morejo si predstavljati, da bi kdo plačal več, kot bi plačali sami, in podcenjujejo vse, kar jim gre zlahka.

Zakaj mu je to tako pomembno, pokaže matematika. Pri tankih maržah majhen dvig cene dobiček pomnoži, več strank pa ga le poveča. Na enem predavanju pokaže podjetje z 20-odstotno maržo: podvojitev števila strank podvoji dobiček, podvojitev cene pa ga poveča za šestkrat, ker dodatni prihodek ne prinese dodatnih stroškov.

Ceno preizkušaj z zmnožkom stopnja zaprtja × cena (prihodek na prodajni pogovor) ali s skupnim bruto dobičkom. Nehaj dvigovati, ko ta številka pade, ne ko prvi kupec reče ne.

Kako deluje

Znaki, da si prepoceni

V zapiskih se vedno znova ponavljajo:

  • Zelo visoka stopnja zaprtja (close rate). V predavanju o poslovni matematiki da tabelo kot pravilo palca: če zapreš 80 % ali več, si verjetno 3- do 4-krat prepoceni, pri 60–80 % 2- do 3-krat, pri 50–60 % 1,5- do 2-krat, pri 40–50 % 1,25- do 1,5-krat. Okoli 30–40 % je približno prav, če so kupci pred klicem poučeni. Pod 30 % najprej pogleda ciljno stranko (avatar) in prodajni proces, preden se sploh dotakne cene.
  • Polna zasedenost. Če povpraševanje presega zmogljivost, je njegova prva poteza dvig cene, pred spremembo razmerja izvedbe ali zaposlovanjem. Manj strank po višji ceni pomeni več denarja in sproščen čas za gradnjo zmogljivosti.
  • Tanka marža pri polnem obsegu. Če podjetje komaj ustvarja dobiček celo s ponavljajočimi se strankami in priporočili (brezplačno pridobivanje), so fiksni stroški previsoki ali pa so cene napačne. Pri storitvah želi vsaj 80 % bruto marže, izvedba pa naj stane približno petino cene.
  • Pridobivanja strank si ne moreš privoščiti. Po njegovem je veliko težav tipa »potrebujemo cenejše leade« v resnici težav, da na stranko ne zaslužiš dovolj.
  • Dolgo od zadnjega dviga. Dvig po treh ali štirih letih je le dohitevanje inflacije.

Preizkušanje cene

Ceno hoče določati s testi, ne z mnenji. Ceno počasi dviguj, dokler ljudje ne nehajo reči ja, nato jo malo spusti. Testiraj v živo na klicih in pojdi od nižje k višji, ker znižanje pomeni dobropis nedavnim kupcem. Najprej testiraj segment, ki je manj občutljiv na ceno. Pričakuj, da boš zaslužil več, preden boš zaslužil manj: v enem primeru je dvig za 50 % ob 10 % manj prodajah jasno vreden obdržati. Točka največjega dobička leži onkraj točke največje konverzije.

Nikoli ne popuščaj pri ceni jedrne ponudbe

To je eno njegovih najdoslednejših pravil v prodajnih treningih. Ko kupec prosi za popust:

  1. Reci, da bi lahko naredil za več. To sidro postavi višje.
  2. Spremeni pogoje (obročno plačilo, predplačilo) ali sestavine (odstrani funkcijo, ponudi lažjo različico).
  3. Namesto popusta dodaj bonus. Prodajni ekipi predlaga seznam ovrednotenih bonusov, ki jih uporablja namesto znižanj.
  4. Če ceno res znižaš, jo zamenjaj za nekaj: daljšo zavezo, pričevanje, ocene, priporočilo. Tako ostaneš ti tisti, ki postavlja pogoje, razliko pa lahko upravičiš drugim strankam.

Nenačrtovana znižanja zvenijo kot prosjačenje in znižajo zaupanje. Cenovni testi se načrtujejo vnaprej, ne improvizirajo na klicu.

Sidra in premium paketi

Vedno najprej predstavi premium paket in pojdi navzdol le, če kupec reče, da ima omejen proračun. Visoko sidro obdrži, tudi če ga le redki vzamejo: viden vrhnji paket dvigne zaznano vrednost vsega pod njim. Njegov račun: če 10 % kupcev vzame sidro po 10-kratni ceni, se prihodek približno podvoji. Delo ena na ena naj stane približno 5-krat več kot delo ena z mnogimi; če vsi plačajo premium, ga dvigni. Če sidro ni namenjeno prodaji, naj bo skoraj enako jedru, z eno ali dvema dodatnima funkcijama.

Konične cene in pristojbine

Ponudba in povpraševanje veljata tudi za mala podjetja. V konicah zaračunaj več ali to predstavi kot popust izven konice. Prodajaj hitrost: doplačilo za isti dan ali prednost je čista marža na isto delo (v enem hotseatu predlaga 10 % in nato 20 %). Prenesi stroške kartičnih provizij na kupca, kar bi v primeru restavracije dobiček povečalo za okoli 15 %. Pristojbine poimenuj po tem, kar kupci sprejmejo (nastavitev, oprema), ceno pa jim določi po tem, kar potrebuješ.

Konkurenti in najslabše stranke

Nikoli ne nižaj cene, da bi se boril s konkurentom, ki te spodbija ali kopira poceni del tvojega modela. Igraj igro preseganja vrednosti: kupcu pokaži spremenljivke, na katere ni pomislil (tveganje, enostavnost, hitrost), da primerjava ni več jabolka z jabolki. Sprejmi, da boš ob dvigu izgubil nekaj strank. Ko je v svojem fitnesu cene potrojil in izgubil približno tretjino članov, se je prihodek približno podvojil, zaposleni pa so temu rekli »čiščenje« (the culling), ker je odstranilo kronične pritoževalce. Pritožbe nad ceno od neidealnih strank pomenijo, da cena pravilno filtrira.

Primeri

Primeri so povzeti in anonimizirani iz Alexovih videov. Primeri, označeni kot izmišljeni, so samo za ponazoritev.

Lokalno selitveno podjetje, ~300.000 $ na leto

Pred. Urna postavka blizu vrha trga, zaradi cene izgubijo okoli 40 % ponudb. Lastnik misli, da potrebuje več leadov.

Po. Najprej predstavljen VIP paket (mojstri selitev, pakiranje, material, zavarovanje, cenovna kapica približno 50 % nad oceno), standardni paket kot rezerva in doplačilo za isti dan, ki začne pri 10 %.

Alex je diagnosticiral blagovno (commodity) ponudbo, ne težave z leadi. Prodaja po urah vabi k primerjanju, zato je ponudbo razstavil na elemente, na katere kupci niso pomislili.

Proizvajalec pokalov po meri, ~1,7 mio $ prihodka, 6 % neto

Cene so bile postavljene po načelu strošek plus pribitek. Pri neto marži okoli 6 % dvig cene za približno 6 % dobiček skoraj podvoji. Njegov nasvet: preklop na cene po vrednosti, split testi višjih cen na najbolj prodajanih izdelkih, začetek pri segmentu impulzivnih daril namesto pri cenovno občutljivih B2B kupcih, presoja po bruto dobičku × količina.

Samostojna odvetnica z urno postavko 250 $

Povprečna vrednost stranke je bila okoli 1.500 $, stopnja zaprtja pa zelo visoka. Prestavil jo je na fiksnih 5.800 $ in majhen delež od uspeha, s sidrom 25.000 $ brez provizije in različico »naredi sam« za 2.000–3.000 $, ki je cenovno dovolj blizu, da je jedro videti najboljša izbira.

Pogoste napake

  • Cena iz lastne denarnice. Ceno postaviš prenizko, ker si ne predstavljaš, da bi jo plačal sam. Ceno izpelji iz vrednosti rešenega problema in premoženja kupca.
  • Strošek plus pribitek ali tržno povprečje. Tvoji stroški kupca ne zanimajo, tržno povprečje pa večinoma sestavljajo mala podjetja, ki se borijo.
  • Presoja dviga po številu prodaj. Manj prodaj po višji ceni pogosto pomeni več dobička. Glej zmnožek konverzija × cena.
  • Nižanje cene za zaprtje posla ali boj s konkurentom. Zniža zaupanje in vzgaja lovce na popuste. Raje spremeni pogoje, sestavine ali bonuse.
  • Nižanje cene, da bi zmanjšal odliv. V enem primeru znižanje s približno 3.000 na 2.500 na mesec ni spremenilo ničesar, tudi v njegovem podjetju znižanje odliva ni premaknilo.

Kako bi to uporabil Alex

  • Ceno preveri pred povpraševanjem. Tanka marža, poln koledar, zelo visoka stopnja zaprtja ali nedosegljivo pridobivanje strank ga vsi najprej pripeljejo do cene, pred novimi kanali ali zaposlitvami.
  • Številke izračuna kar na mestu. Marža, stopnja zaprtja in količina odločitev spremenijo v aritmetiko: 20-odstotno znižanje pri 40-odstotni marži zahteva dvakrat več prodaj samo za izenačenje.
  • Ceno dvigne in jo podpre z vrednostjo. Če lastnik višje cene ne zna upravičiti, izboljša ponudbo (hitreje, lažje, bolj zanesljivo) in doda garancijo z zobmi, nato ceno dvigne za 20–40 %.
  • Jedrne ponudbe ne poceni. Pogoji, sestavine in bonusi se lahko premaknejo, cena jedra ne. Vsak popust je zamenjan za nekaj.
  • Zgradi lestev paketov. Najprej premium, nad njim sidro po 10-kratni ceni, pod njim cenejša različica, vse tako, da je jedro videti najboljše.
  • Sprejme, da izgubi najslabše stranke. Glej, kaj stranke delajo, ne kaj govorijo: če kupujejo naprej in se pritožujejo nad ceno, pritožbe prezri.
  • Vprašanja, ki bi jih postavil: Kako danes določaš cene in kdo jih je določil? Kakšna je tvoja stopnja zaprtja pri tej ceni? Kdaj si nazadnje dvignil cene? Kakšna je bruto marža? Si polno zaseden? Kateri segment je najmanj občutljiv na ceno? Kakšen prihodek na klic dobiš pri ceni A in kakšnega pri ceni B?

Delovni list

Odgovori za svojo glavno ponudbo, z dejanskimi številkami zadnjih treh mesecev.

  1. Stopnja zaprtja: Kolikšen delež kvalificiranih prodajnih pogovorov zapreš? Kam te to postavi v njegovi tabeli?
  2. Marža: Kakšna je tvoja bruto marža na stranko? Je izvedba pri petini cene ali manj?
  3. Načrt testa: Katero naslednjo ceno boš ponudil na naslednjih desetih klicih in kakšen prihodek na klic bi pomenil zmago?
  4. Odgovor na popust: Kaj boš rekel namesto popusta? Naštej tri bonuse in eno spremembo pogojev, ki jih lahko ponudiš.
  5. Sidro: Kaj bi vsebovala različica po 5- do 10-kratni ceni? Kateri od teh elementov te stanejo malo?
  6. Pristojbine in hitrost: Kje bi lahko zaračunal prednost, konico ali nastavitev?
