Prioritising MovesRazvrščanje potez

Alex's Playbook · More, Better, New; ICE (impact, confidence, ease)

How Alex orders the work once he knows the constraint. Squeeze what already works, rank everything else by impact, confidence and ease, and give the owner only two or three moves.Kako Alex razvrsti delo, ko pozna ozko grlo. Izžmi, kar že deluje, vse drugo razvrsti po učinku, zaupanju in lahkosti, lastniku pa daj samo dve ali tri poteze.

The gist

Most hotseats end the same way. Alex writes a short numbered list, with quick, likely, high-impact moves at the top and heavy strategic bets at the bottom. Before anything new appears on that list, he asks why the owner cannot simply do more of what already works. New ideas have to prove they beat spending more on the existing winner.

More of what works → make it better → only then something new. Rank candidates by Impact × Confidence × Ease, and hand over two or three.

The ordering is how he controls risk. A move that adds volume to a proven channel has known economics. A move that changes the offer, the avatar or the channel resets the learning curve. He picks up the obvious money first and lets the cash and confidence from those wins fund the harder bets later.

How it works

More, then better, then new

He states the default order in several talks: first ask why you cannot do more of what works; if you cannot do more, ask whether you can make it better; only then try something new. He uses it as a quarterly plan too ("more, better", repeated until something blocks it). In one Q&A he explains why small owners stall: they find something that works, stop doing it, and chase several new things.

Typical "more" moves from the cases: going from a few networking events a week to several a day, from one stage talk a year to one a week, doubling the ad spend on a funnel that already returns about 4x, or sending 20 partner emails a day instead of a handful. A channel that works but has never been scaled is, in his view, usually the biggest win in the business.

Rank by impact, confidence and ease

He names ICE (impact, confidence, ease) as his prioritisation tool and in one hotseat splits ease into speed and effort, so the ranking mirrors the value equation. The practical rules he attaches to it:

  • Light first, heavy later, unless a later step depends on an earlier one.
  • Cheapest to heaviest. In one case the sequence was better creative, then the right pixel event, then a high-ticket one-time offer, and only then a sales team selling a large back-end package.
  • Low operational risk when execution is weak. If the team is unlikely to execute a complex change well, he prefers the move that still works when done badly.
  • Low operational drag. Price is his favourite example: you change a number or a sentence, and nothing in delivery has to change.
  • Can these people execute it? He checks the team before finalising the list.

His order of operations

When several areas need work, he follows a fairly stable sequence across videos:

Step What he fixes Why it comes here
1 Offer and price Highest leverage, lowest drag; frees cash
2 Sales motion and conversion Fixes leaks before you pay for more traffic
3 Demand on existing channels Spend and volume on what is proven
4 New channels, avatars, products Highest risk, funded by steps 1–3

He puts it as: do not push more demand through thin margins, and do not buy traffic into a leaky bucket. For a stalled local practice he ordered it as pricing and packaging, then attribution, then ads, then content. He adds that cheap conversion fixes can double leads on their own and push a business into a supply constraint. The supply-versus-demand triage itself comes before all of this (see the diagnostic sequence).

Never switch off what works

He repeats this rule often. Do not turn off a channel that makes money to test a new one; add the new one on top. Do not let the main channel drop while building a second. Do not put a new person on a new channel: hand the existing channel to someone, confirm it holds, then put a proven person on the new one. When testing a new lead source, route it to one proven closer so the only variables are the source, the offer and the process. If budget has to move, he moves it gradually (for example 30%, then 50%).

Do not change what customers love

If demand exceeds capacity, he raises price or adds capacity rather than changing the product, because pricing is easy to change and delivery is hard. He warns that products get worse through addition, that a working offer in a large company touches billing, sales, ads and fulfilment at once, and that a sales process with healthy close rates should be left alone. He also refuses to fix problems that are not showing up in the metrics: a narrative about a threat, with no rise in churn, is no reason to rebuild.

The biggest obvious pile of money first

In a keynote he says most businesses grow most by picking up the biggest obvious pile of money in front of them. Elsewhere he asks owners which single thing would double the business this year, and tells them to drop whatever is not that. In one case he refused to discuss anything except the main constraint until the owner stopped asking about secondary optimisations.

Two or three moves only

He deliberately limits advice to the two or three highest-impact moves so the owner actually executes them, and in one hotseat simplifies a plan to two steps.

Examples

Cases are paraphrased and anonymised from Alex's videos. Any example marked as invented is for illustration only.

Home-service company with nothing broken

A duct-cleaning business with about $1.25M revenue, 38% profit, a 99% show rate and an 82% close rate asked for more leads. Alex's ordered list:

# Move Logic
1 Raise price about 10%, hold while close rate stays above ~65% About +25% net profit, no operational change
2 Keep paying down debt Lower risk
3 Dedicated ad landing page, all buttons to it Cheap conversion fix
4 More creative, organic winners as ads, then more spend Ads already returned about 13:1
5–7 Retargeting, reactivation emails, partner offer More from the existing base and partners
8 Outbound to new segments Newest and heaviest, so last

Coach about to switch away from a working funnel

Before. A weight-loss coach at about $288K needs $600K. National ads to DMs run at about 4x return. She is considering a local in-person model as well.

After. Double the ad spend on the 4x funnel and add DM setters. Do not run both models.

Alex first suggested the local hybrid, then corrected himself on the call: when something works, put more into it.

BI consultancy (invented)

Before. Most clients come from one ERP partner. The owner plans a new AI product, a webinar series and a price review, all this quarter.

After. 1) Raise the project price on new quotes. 2) Turn the partner referrals into a fixed monthly routine with a report-back after each project. 3) Ask the partner for introductions to its other regional offices. The AI product waits on the idea list.

Steps 1 and 2 are fast, likely and nearly free. Step 3 is still "more" of the proven channel. The new product carries the most risk, so it comes last.

Common mistakes

  • Starting with "new". A new channel or product feels like progress, while the working channel stays under-exploited.
  • Fixing the loudest problem instead of the biggest one. He looks for the move that changes throughput most and ignores secondary optimisations until it is done.
  • Turning off a winner to test. Replacing a working channel removes the cash and the benchmark you need to judge the test.
  • Handing over a long list. Ten good ideas executed badly lose to two executed fully.
  • Improving what customers already love. Changing the core product or a healthy sales process risks the part that works.

How Alex would apply it

  • He asks "why can't you just do more of this?" before discussing anything new, and treats the answer as the next constraint.
  • He writes a numbered list in ICE order: quick, likely, high-impact items first, structural bets last, dependencies respected.
  • He fixes offer and price first, then conversion, then volume on proven channels, and only then new channels.
  • He keeps every working channel running while testing, and isolates new variables (one closer, one source, one change).
  • He leaves alone what customers love and what the metrics say is healthy.
  • He cuts the plan to two or three moves the owner will actually carry out.
  • Questions he would ask: Where do your best customers come from, and have you scaled that? If you could do only one thing this year and it had to double the business, what would it be? What happened when you increased spend? What breaks if we do more? Is this new idea better than simply spending more on what works?

Worksheet

List every move you are considering, then work through these.

  1. More: Which channel or offer already works, and what would 2x or 10x of it look like?
  2. Better: Which step of that working path is furthest below where it should be?
  3. Score: Give each move 1–10 for impact, confidence and ease. Multiply and sort.
  4. Order: Re-sort so offer and price come before conversion, conversion before volume, and volume before anything new.
  5. Protect: Write what you will not touch (working channels, the core product, a healthy sales process).
  6. Cut: Keep the top two or three moves. Put the rest on a dated idea list.

Bistvo

Večina hotseatov se konča enako. Alex napiše kratek oštevilčen seznam: na vrhu hitre, verjetne poteze z velikim učinkom, na dnu težke strateške stave. Preden se na seznamu pojavi kar koli novega, vpraša, zakaj lastnik ne more preprosto delati več tega, kar že deluje. Nove ideje morajo dokazati, da so boljše od tega, da več vložiš v obstoječega zmagovalca.

Več tega, kar deluje → naredi bolje → šele nato nekaj novega. Kandidate razvrsti po učinek × zaupanje × lahkost (ICE) in jih predaj dva ali tri.

S tem vrstnim redom nadzoruje tveganje. Poteza, ki doda obseg preverjenemu kanalu, ima znano ekonomiko. Poteza, ki spremeni ponudbo, avatar ali kanal, učno krivuljo postavi na začetek. Najprej pobere očiten denar, denar in samozavest iz teh zmag pa kasneje financirata težje stave.

Kako deluje

Več, nato bolje, nato novo

Privzeti vrstni red pove v več nastopih: najprej vprašaj, zakaj ne moreš delati več tega, kar deluje; če ne moreš več, vprašaj, ali lahko narediš bolje; šele nato poskusi nekaj novega. Uporablja ga tudi kot četrtletni načrt (»več, bolje«, ponavljano, dokler kaj ne zablokira). V enem Q&A razloži, zakaj mali lastniki obstanejo: najdejo nekaj, kar deluje, s tem nehajo in lovijo več novih stvari.

Tipične poteze »več« iz primerov: z nekaj mreženjskih dogodkov na teden na več na dan, z enega predavanja na odru na leto na enega na teden, podvojitev oglaševalskega proračuna na lijaku, ki že vrača okoli 4-kratnik, ali 20 e-mailov partnerjem na dan namesto peščice. Kanal, ki deluje, a ga nihče ni skaliral, je po njegovem navadno največja zmaga v podjetju.

Razvrsti po učinku, zaupanju in lahkosti

ICE (impact, confidence, ease) imenuje kot svoje orodje za razvrščanje, v enem hotseatu pa lahkost razdeli na hitrost in trud, tako da razvrstitev zrcali enačbo vrednosti. Praktična pravila, ki jih doda:

  • Lahko najprej, težko pozneje, razen če je poznejši korak odvisen od zgodnejšega.
  • Od najcenejšega do najtežjega. V enem primeru je bil vrstni red: boljši oglasni material, nato pravi dogodek za piksel, nato enkratna dražja ponudba in šele nato prodajna ekipa, ki prodaja velik paket v nadaljevanju.
  • Nizko operativno tveganje, kadar je izvedba šibka. Če ekipa kompleksne spremembe verjetno ne bo izvedla dobro, izbere potezo, ki deluje tudi slabo izvedena.
  • Malo operativnega trenja. Najljubši primer je cena: spremeniš številko ali stavek, v izvedbi storitve pa se ne spremeni nič.
  • Ali to ti ljudje zmorejo izvesti? Ekipo preveri, preden seznam zaključi.

Njegov vrstni red operacij

Kadar je treba popraviti več področij, v videih sledi dokaj stalnemu zaporedju:

Korak Kaj popravi Zakaj je tu
1 Ponudbo in ceno Največji vzvod, najmanj trenja; sprosti denar
2 Prodajni proces in konverzijo Zamaši luknje, preden plačaš več prometa
3 Povpraševanje na obstoječih kanalih Proračun in obseg na preverjenem
4 Nove kanale, avatarje, izdelke Največje tveganje, financirano s koraki 1–3

Pove takole: ne potiskaj več povpraševanja skozi tanke marže in ne kupuj prometa v vedro z luknjo. Za lokalno prakso, ki je stala na mestu, je vrstni red postavil tako: cene in paketi, nato pripisovanje virov, nato oglasi, nato vsebina. Doda, da lahko poceni popravki konverzije sami podvojijo leade in podjetje porinejo v ozko grlo ponudbe (supply constraint). Triaža ponudba ali povpraševanje pride pred vsem tem (glej zaporedje diagnoze).

Nikoli ne ugasni, kar deluje

To pravilo pogosto ponovi. Ne ugašaj kanala, ki služi, da bi testiral novega; novega dodaj na vrh. Ne dovoli, da glavni kanal pade, medtem ko gradiš drugega. Ne postavljaj novega človeka na nov kanal: obstoječi kanal predaj nekomu, preveri, da zdrži, nato na novega postavi preverjeno osebo. Ko testiraš nov vir leadov, ga usmeri k enemu preverjenemu prodajalcu, da ostanejo spremenljivke samo vir, ponudba in proces. Če je treba proračun premakniti, ga premika postopoma (na primer 30 %, nato 50 %).

Ne spreminjaj tega, kar stranke imajo rade

Če povpraševanje presega zmogljivost, dvigne ceno ali doda zmogljivost, izdelka pa ne spreminja, ker je ceno lahko spremeniti, izvedbo pa težko. Opozarja, da se izdelki z dodajanjem slabšajo, da delujoča ponudba v velikem podjetju hkrati zadene obračun, prodajo, oglase in izvedbo, in da prodajni proces z zdravimi stopnjami zaključevanja pustiš pri miru. Prav tako noče reševati problemov, ki se ne kažejo v metrikah: zgodba o grožnji brez rasti odhodov strank ni razlog za prenovo.

Najprej največji očiten kup denarja

V enem glavnem predavanju (keynote) pravi, da večina podjetij najbolj zraste, ko najprej pobere največji očiten kup denarja pred sabo. Drugje lastnike vpraša, katera ena stvar bi letos podvojila posel, in jim reče, naj opustijo vse, kar to ni. V enem primeru ni hotel govoriti o ničemer razen o glavnem ozkem grlu, dokler lastnik ni nehal spraševati o drugotnih optimizacijah.

Samo dve ali tri poteze

Nasvet namerno omeji na dve ali tri poteze z največjim učinkom, da jih lastnik res izvede, v enem hotseatu pa načrt poenostavi na dva koraka.

Primeri

Primeri so povzeti in anonimizirani iz Alexovih videov. Primeri, označeni kot izmišljeni, so samo za ponazoritev.

Storitveno podjetje, v katerem nič ni pokvarjeno

Podjetje za čiščenje prezračevalnih kanalov s približno 1,25 milijona dolarjev prihodka, 38 % dobička, 99 % udeležbo na terminih in 82 % stopnjo zaključevanja je prosilo za več leadov. Alexov razvrščeni seznam:

# Poteza Logika
1 Dvig cene za okoli 10 %, obdrži, dokler stopnja zaključevanja ostaja nad ~65 % Okoli +25 % čistega dobička, brez operativne spremembe
2 Nadaljuj z odplačevanjem dolga Manjše tveganje
3 Namenska pristajalna stran za oglase, vsi gumbi vodijo nanjo Poceni popravek konverzije
4 Več oglasnega materiala, uspešne organske objave kot oglasi, nato večji proračun Oglasi so že vračali okoli 13 : 1
5–7 Ponovno ciljanje, e-maili za reaktivacijo, ponudba za partnerje Več iz obstoječe baze in partnerjev
8 Outbound v nove segmente Najnovejše in najtežje, zato zadnje

Coach tik pred opustitvijo delujočega lijaka

Pred. Coachinja za hujšanje ima okoli 288.000 dolarjev, potrebuje 600.000. Nacionalni oglasi, ki vodijo v DM pogovore, vračajo okoli 4-kratnik. Razmišlja še o lokalnem modelu v živo.

Po. Podvoji proračun za oglase na lijaku s 4-kratnikom in dodaj ljudi, ki v DM dogovarjajo termine (setterje). Ne poganjaj obeh modelov.

Alex je najprej predlagal lokalni hibrid, nato pa se med klicem popravil: ko nekaj deluje, vloži več vanj.

BI svetovanje (izmišljeno)

Pred. Večina strank pride od enega ERP partnerja. Lastnik to četrtletje načrtuje nov AI izdelek, serijo webinarjev in pregled cen.

Po. 1) Dvigni ceno projektov v novih ponudbah. 2) Priporočila partnerja spremeni v stalno mesečno rutino s poročilom po vsakem projektu. 3) Partnerja prosi za predstavitve njegovim drugim regionalnim pisarnam. AI izdelek čaka na seznamu idej.

Koraka 1 in 2 sta hitra, verjetna in skoraj brezplačna. Korak 3 je še vedno »več« preverjenega kanala. Nov izdelek nosi največ tveganja, zato pride zadnji.

Pogoste napake

  • Začetek pri »novem«. Nov kanal ali izdelek daje občutek napredka, delujoči kanal pa ostaja premalo izkoriščen.
  • Popravljanje najglasnejšega problema namesto največjega. Alex išče potezo, ki najbolj spremeni pretok, drugotne optimizacije pa ignorira, dokler ta ni opravljena.
  • Ugašanje zmagovalca zaradi testa. Zamenjava delujočega kanala odvzame denar in merilo, ki ju potrebuješ za presojo testa.
  • Predaja dolgega seznama. Deset dobrih idej, slabo izvedenih, izgubi proti dvema, izvedenima do konca.
  • Izboljševanje tega, kar stranke imajo rade. Spreminjanje osrednjega izdelka ali zdravega prodajnega procesa ogrozi del, ki deluje.

Kako bi to uporabil Alex

  • Vpraša »zakaj ne moreš preprosto delati več tega?«, preden se pogovarja o čem novem, odgovor pa vzame kot naslednje ozko grlo.
  • Napiše oštevilčen seznam po ICE: hitro, verjetno, z velikim učinkom najprej, strukturne stave zadnje, ob upoštevanju odvisnosti.
  • Najprej popravi ponudbo in ceno, nato konverzijo, nato obseg na preverjenih kanalih in šele nato nove kanale.
  • Med testiranjem pusti teči vse delujoče kanale in izolira nove spremenljivke (en prodajalec, en vir, ena sprememba).
  • Pri miru pusti, kar stranke imajo rade in kar metrike kažejo kot zdravo.
  • Načrt skrči na dve ali tri poteze, ki jih bo lastnik res izvedel.
  • Vprašanja, ki bi jih postavil: Od kod pridejo tvoje najboljše stranke in si ta vir skaliral? Če bi letos lahko naredil samo eno stvar in bi morala podvojiti posel, katera bi bila? Kaj se je zgodilo, ko si povečal proračun? Kaj se zlomi, če naredimo več? Je ta nova ideja boljša od tega, da preprosto več vložiš v to, kar deluje?

Delovni list

Naštej vse poteze, o katerih razmišljaš, nato pojdi skozi ta vprašanja.

  1. Več: Kateri kanal ali ponudba že deluje in kako bi izgledal 2-kratnik ali 10-kratnik tega?
  2. Bolje: Kateri korak te delujoče poti je najbolj pod tem, kjer bi moral biti?
  3. Ocena: Vsaki potezi daj oceno 1–10 za učinek, zaupanje in lahkost. Zmnoži in razvrsti.
  4. Vrstni red: Ponovno razvrsti tako, da ponudba in cena prideta pred konverzijo, konverzija pred obsegom in obseg pred čim novim.
  5. Zaščiti: Zapiši, česa se ne boš dotaknil (delujoči kanali, osrednji izdelek, zdrav prodajni proces).
  6. Odreži: Obdrži dve ali tri najboljše poteze. Ostale daj na seznam idej z datumom.
