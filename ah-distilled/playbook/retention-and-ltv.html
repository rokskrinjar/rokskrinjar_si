Retention & Lifetime ValueZadrževanje in življenjska vrednost

Alex's Playbook · Retention, LTV and LTGP

If Alex could give a business one advantage, it would be revenue retention. He fixes churn before scaling acquisition, and he reads churn as an onboarding, usage or expectation problem far more often than a sign the product is bad.Če bi Alex podjetju lahko dal eno samo prednost, bi bila to zadržanost prihodka (revenue retention). Odliv popravi, preden poveča pridobivanje strank, in ga veliko pogosteje razume kot težavo uvajanja, uporabe ali pričakovanj kot pa znak slabega izdelka.

The gist

In several lectures Alex calls retention the most valuable property a business can have. Retained customers stack year on year and growth compounds. Without retention you are permanently in the business of replacing customers, and a business that depends only on new sales dies when its channel breaks. Since the cost of reaching a given customer is roughly the same for every competitor, the lasting edge is the lifetime value you build on top.

His order is consistent: plug the leaks, then pour in more. Scaling acquisition into a leaking bucket just scales the problems. And when he digs into churn, the cause is usually upstream of the product: weak onboarding, customers who don't use what they bought, or a sales promise that didn't match reality.

Churn = cancels this month ÷ active clients · LTV = price ÷ churn · LTGP (lifetime gross profit) = LTV × gross margin Ceiling: new sales per month × LTV is roughly where revenue stops growing.

How it works

Why it is worth more than acquisition

  • The ceiling moves. With 100 new sales a month and an LTV of about $7.7K, revenue tops out around $770K a month in one of his worked examples. Halve churn and the ceiling doubles without one extra sale.
  • You can pay more per customer. At a fixed LTV:CAC target, doubling LTV doubles what you can afford to acquire a customer. In one portfolio company, LTV rising from about $2,000 to $3,800 took the business from about $2M a month to about $2M a week.
  • It is often easier. He frequently finds dropping churn far easier than multiplying acquisition. He would rather keep the same 300 customers than sell 900 a year to stay at 300.

Fix the leak first, but read it correctly

If clients drop after a couple of quarters, fix that before spending more on the front end. Two cautions he adds: young programmes (under 90 days or six months) show inflated churn, so don't overhaul everything because month one looks bad; and if retention is already strong, put the effort into acquisition rather than reinventing the product.

Where churn happens

Across a large membership platform he describes a stable shape: over 20% churn in month one, a second drop around month three (about 10%), and about 2% a month after month six. So he reframes churn work as getting people past day 30, past day 90 and past month six:

Window What has to happen
Month one Activation, onboarding, expectations set, the right promises made
Day 90 A real outcome or a visible change in behaviour
Month six Customers connected to each other

Activation and onboarding

The activation point is what your longest-staying customers did early. If you don't know it, study them and point the whole early experience at that behaviour. His examples: a first $2,000 sale within seven days for a gym licensee, 5+ lbs lost in week one, a trainer's first five clients. If there is no onboarding at all, he adds it first: one-on-one where possible, restate the customer's goal and why they want it, deliver a quick win within 24 hours, introduce one named contact. In the lecture built on a SaaS case, he says onboarding can add two to three months to LTV in recurring businesses.

Churn as consumption and expectations

  • Usage predicts cancellation. Low renewal is usually preceded by low consumption. Track usage and act on the first drop.
  • Overwhelm drives churn. Paying for five things and using one feels like 20% value. Removing unused inclusions raises perceived value.
  • Promises set the gap. High promises raise close rates and lower retention. Set modest expectations and over-deliver; fix the gap by rescripting sales rather than adding delivery.
  • Stated exit reasons mislead. "We already got the value" usually hides poor onboarding and communication. He prescribes frequent updates that tell clients what you did for them, without asking for anything.

Billing cadence

At each charge, customers judge value over the period since the last charge. Longer billing periods smooth over bad months. He cites a study with about 10.7% monthly churn on monthly billing, about 5% on quarterly and about 2% on annual. So he makes annual the default (about 16% off, with status perks), charges the one-time value up front and keeps the recurring fee small ("big head, long tail"), and prices the recurring fee at what customers would still pay in their worst month.

Talk to the people who left and the people who stayed

Message every canceller and tag the reason (price, overwhelm, not using, missing feature, not as expected). Every ~20 cancels, fix the top one or two; don't react to a single answer. Ask the longest-tenured customers why they stay: that answer is your retention engine. Tell customers at signup that cancelling includes an exit conversation, then offer to credit what they've paid toward staying, toward a tier that fixes their real problem, or a lighter version without the feature they don't use.

Referral ask and upsell at the moment of need

Ask for referrals at the activation point. The customer's gratitude fades quickly while you remember the favour as bigger than it was, so ask early. After activation he wants a testimonial, a referral, then ascension. Selling customers the next thing is itself a retention tactic: if you don't meet their urge to buy again, someone else will. The two upsells that work almost anywhere are more of what they bought or more help with it, offered right after purchase. Renewals get pitched at several points (week four, milestones, halfway, last chance).

Benchmarks he states

Treat these as his rules of thumb, each from its own context: B2B annual client retention above 80% (accounting firms, private equity buyers); 80% annual retention ≈ five years of revenue per customer, 50% ≈ two; best-in-class prosumer 12-month retention 60%+; if fewer than 7 in 10 members stay each month, retention is your highest-return lever; service churn rarely goes below 1–2% a month; SMB services at $1,500–3,000 a month stick about four to six months.

Examples

Cases are paraphrased and anonymised from Alex's videos. Any example marked as invented is for illustration only.

Portfolio SaaS company, churn rising after a launch

Before. Group onboarding with about 25 customers per call. Complaints such as "too techy", "not enough support" and "too slow" pointed to a gap between what sales promised and what customers got.

After. One-on-one onboarding that restates the goal and fixes issues live, rescripted sales expectations, a separate escalations team, commission for support staff on saves.

He reports 61% fewer cancellations, ascensions up from 20% to 48% with one-on-one onboarding, and about $2M saved in six months. Lowering the promises did not hurt close rates.

Online communities and gyms

On his community platform, one group that deleted content cut monthly churn from about 30% to 5%. His own community cut three weekly calls to one after overwhelm topped the cancel reasons. In his gyms, seven stacked tactics (onboarding, unlockables, exit interviews, attendance tracking and others) took churn from 11% to 6% a month.

B2B consultancy with retainer churn

A ~$1.1M bar consultancy lost retainer clients after a couple of quarters. His diagnosis: value wasn't being shown. Fix churn first by showing clients their numbers every month, before putting more into the front end.

Invented: Qlik managed analytics service

30 manufacturing clients at €1,500 a month started the year; 18 were still paying twelve months later. That is 60% annual retention, about 2.5 years and €45,000 revenue per client. Interviews show the stayers ran a management meeting from the dashboard within the first month. Onboarding is rebuilt around that meeting, with the referral ask right after it. At 80% retention, lifetime revenue per client would be about €90,000.

Common mistakes

  • Scaling a leaking bucket. More ad spend into high churn just buys more people to lose.
  • Measuring LTV as revenue. He wants lifetime gross profit. Revenue LTV can make an unprofitable channel look healthy.
  • Cutting price to stop churn. His own cut didn't change churn, and in one case a recurring price drop of about a sixth changed nothing.
  • Adding features to keep people. Unused inclusions widen the gap between paid and consumed and raise cancellations.

How Alex would apply it

  • He calculates churn and LTV first. Most owners don't know either. He works out the plateau before talking about growth.
  • He fixes retention before acquisition, unless retention is already strong, in which case he sends the effort to acquisition.
  • He starts with onboarding. If there is none, it is the first fix; then unlockables, exit interviews and usage tracking, one tactic at a time.
  • He defines activation from the best customers and points the first 30 days at it.
  • He makes annual the default and separates one-time value from the recurring fee.
  • He places the referral ask and the upsell at the moment of need, right after activation and right after purchase.
  • Questions he would ask: How many customers cancel each month, out of how many active? What is your price and gross margin? How many of last year's customers are still paying, and how much of last year's revenue? What does onboarding look like? What is your activation point? Why did the last 20 leave, and why do your longest customers stay? Where exactly does the referral ask happen?

Worksheet

Use your client list from the past 12 months.

  1. Numbers: Monthly churn, LTV and LTGP. What is your revenue ceiling at today's new-sales rate?
  2. Cohort: Of clients active a year ago, what share still pays? Compare with his 80% B2B rule of thumb.
  3. Activation: What did your five longest-staying clients do in their first 30 days?
  4. Leavers: List the reasons of your last 20 cancellations. Which one or two will you fix first?
  5. Billing: Could annual or quarterly billing become the default for new clients?
  6. Ask: At which moment will you ask for a referral and offer the next purchase?

Bistvo

V več predavanjih Alex imenuje zadrževanje strank najdragocenejšo lastnost, ki jo lahko ima podjetje. Zadržane stranke se iz leta v leto seštevajo in rast se obrestuje. Brez zadrževanja si za vedno v poslu nadomeščanja strank, podjetje, ki živi samo od novih prodaj, pa umre, ko se mu kanal pokvari. Ker je strošek dosega določene stranke za vse konkurente približno enak, je trajna prednost v življenjski vrednosti, ki jo zgradiš nad tem.

Njegov vrstni red je dosleden: najprej zamaši luknje, nato nalij več. Če pridobivanje strank širiš v vedro, ki pušča, širiš le težave. Ko pa odliv razišče, je vzrok navadno pred izdelkom: šibko uvajanje (onboarding), stranke, ki ne uporabljajo tega, kar so kupile, ali prodajna obljuba, ki se ni ujemala z resničnostjo.

Odliv (churn) = odpovedi v mesecu ÷ aktivne stranke · LTV = cena ÷ odliv · LTGP (življenjski bruto dobiček) = LTV × bruto marža Strop: nove prodaje na mesec × LTV je približno raven, kjer prihodek neha rasti.

Kako deluje

Zakaj je vredno več kot pridobivanje

  • Strop se premakne. Pri 100 novih prodajah na mesec in LTV okoli 7.700 $ prihodek v enem od njegovih izračunov obstane pri približno 770.000 $ na mesec. Prepolovi odliv in strop se podvoji brez ene same dodatne prodaje.
  • Na stranko lahko plačaš več. Pri fiksnem ciljnem razmerju LTV:CAC podvojen LTV podvoji, koliko si lahko privoščiš za pridobitev stranke. V enem podjetju iz njegovega portfelja je LTV zrasel s približno 2.000 $ na 3.800 $, posel pa s približno 2 mio $ na mesec na približno 2 mio $ na teden.
  • Pogosto je lažje. Pogosto ugotovi, da je znižati odliv veliko lažje kot pomnožiti pridobivanje. Raje obdrži istih 300 strank, kot da jih proda 900 na leto, da ostane pri 300.

Najprej zamaši luknjo, a jo pravilno preberi

Če stranke odidejo po nekaj četrtletjih, to popravi, preden vložiš več v začetni del lijaka. Dodaja dve opozorili: mladi programi (manj kot 90 dni ali šest mesecev) kažejo napihnjen odliv, zato ne prenavljaj vsega, ker je prvi mesec slab; in če je zadrževanje že dobro, trud usmeri v pridobivanje strank, ne v prenovo izdelka.

Kje nastane odliv

Na veliki platformi za članstva opisuje stabilno obliko: več kot 20 % odliva v prvem mesecu, drugi padec okoli tretjega meseca (približno 10 %) in okoli 2 % na mesec po šestem mesecu. Zato delo na odlivu preoblikuje v vprašanje, kako ljudi spraviti čez 30. dan, čez 90. dan in čez šesti mesec:

Obdobje Kaj se mora zgoditi
Prvi mesec Aktivacija, uvajanje, postavljena pričakovanja, prave obljube
90. dan Resničen rezultat ali vidna sprememba vedenja
Šesti mesec Stranke so povezane med seboj

Aktivacija in uvajanje

Točka aktivacije (activation point) je tisto, kar so tvoje najdlje zadržane stranke naredile zgodaj. Če je ne poznaš, jih preuči in celotno zgodnjo izkušnjo usmeri v to vedenje. Njegovi primeri: prva prodaja za 2.000 $ v sedmih dneh za licenciranega lastnika fitnesa, več kot 5 funtov manj v prvem tednu, prvih pet strank za trenerja. Če uvajanja sploh ni, ga doda najprej: kjer je mogoče ena na ena, ponovi cilj stranke in zakaj ga želi, hitra zmaga v 24 urah, predstavitev ene imenovane kontaktne osebe. V predavanju, zgrajenem na primeru SaaS podjetja, pravi, da lahko uvajanje v naročniških podjetjih LTV podaljša za dva do tri mesece.

Odliv kot vprašanje uporabe in pričakovanj

  • Uporaba napove odpoved. Nizkemu podaljševanju navadno sledi nizka uporaba. Spremljaj uporabo in ukrepaj ob prvem padcu.
  • Preobremenjenost poganja odliv. Plačati za pet stvari in uporabljati eno se zdi kot 20 % vrednosti. Odstranitev neuporabljenih vsebin dvigne zaznano vrednost.
  • Obljube postavijo vrzel. Visoke obljube dvignejo stopnjo zaprtja in znižajo zadrževanje. Postavi zmerna pričakovanja in jih preseži; vrzel zapri s prepisom prodajnega scenarija, ne z dodajanjem izvedbe.
  • Navedeni razlogi za odhod zavajajo. »Vrednost smo že dobili« navadno skriva slabo uvajanje in komunikacijo. Predpiše pogosta sporočila, ki stranki povedo, kaj si naredil zanjo, brez kakršne koli prošnje.

Ritem zaračunavanja

Ob vsakem plačilu stranka presoja vrednost za obdobje od zadnjega plačila. Daljša obdobja zgladijo slabe mesece. Navaja študijo z okoli 10,7 % mesečnega odliva pri mesečnem zaračunavanju, okoli 5 % pri četrtletnem in okoli 2 % pri letnem. Zato naredi letni paket za privzeto izbiro (okoli 16 % popusta, s statusnimi ugodnostmi), enkratno vrednost zaračuna vnaprej in ponavljajočo se naročnino drži nizko (»velika glava, dolg rep«, big head, long tail), ponavljajočo se ceno pa postavi tako, da bi jo stranka plačala tudi v svojem najslabšem mesecu.

Govori s tistimi, ki so odšli, in s tistimi, ki so ostali

Vsakemu, ki odpove, pošlji sporočilo in označi razlog (cena, preobremenjenost, ne uporablja, manjkajoča funkcija, ni bilo, kot je pričakoval). Na vsakih približno 20 odpovedi popravi enega ali dva najpogostejša; ne odzivaj se na en sam odgovor. Najdlje zadržane stranke vprašaj, zakaj ostajajo: ta odgovor je tvoj motor zadrževanja. Strankam že ob podpisu povej, da odpoved vključuje izstopni pogovor, nato ponudi, da plačano upošteješ kot dobropis za nadaljevanje, za paket, ki reši njihov pravi problem, ali za lažjo različico brez funkcije, ki je ne uporabljajo.

Prošnja za priporočilo in nadgradnja v trenutku potrebe

Za priporočila prosi ob točki aktivacije. Hvaležnost stranke hitro zbledi, ti pa se uslugo spominjaš kot večjo, kot je bila, zato prosi zgodaj. Po aktivaciji želi pričevanje, priporočilo in nato nadgradnjo (ascension). Prodaja naslednje stvari je sama po sebi taktika zadrževanja: če ne potešiš želje po ponovnem nakupu, jo bo nekdo drug. Dve nadgradnji, ki delujeta skoraj povsod, sta več tega, kar so kupili, ali več pomoči pri tem, ponujeni takoj po nakupu. Podaljšanja ponudi v več točkah (četrti teden, mejniki, polovica, zadnja priložnost).

Mejniki, ki jih navaja

To so njegova pravila palca, vsako iz svojega konteksta: letno zadrževanje B2B strank nad 80 % (računovodska podjetja, kupci iz zasebnega kapitala); 80 % letnega zadrževanja ≈ pet let prihodka na stranko, 50 % ≈ dve leti; najboljši »prosumer« izdelki obdržijo 60 % ali več po 12 mesecih; če vsak mesec ostane manj kot 7 od 10 članov, je zadrževanje tvoj vzvod z najvišjim donosom; odliv pri storitvah redko pade pod 1–2 % na mesec; storitve za mala podjetja po 1.500–3.000 $ na mesec stranke zadržijo približno štiri do šest mesecev.

Primeri

Primeri so povzeti in anonimizirani iz Alexovih videov. Primeri, označeni kot izmišljeni, so samo za ponazoritev.

SaaS podjetje iz portfelja, odliv narašča po lansiranju

Pred. Skupinsko uvajanje s približno 25 strankami na klic. Pritožbe, kot so »preveč tehnično«, »premalo podpore« in »prepočasno«, so kazale na vrzel med tem, kar je obljubila prodaja, in tem, kar so stranke dobile.

Po. Uvajanje ena na ena, ki ponovi cilj in težave reši sproti, prepisana prodajna pričakovanja, ločena ekipa za eskalacije, provizija za podporo za rešene odpovedi.

Poroča o 61 % manj odpovedih, nadgradnjah, ki so se z uvajanjem ena na ena dvignile z 20 % na 48 %, in okoli 2 mio $ prihranka v šestih mesecih. Nižje obljube niso znižale stopnje zaprtja.

Spletne skupnosti in fitnesi

Na njegovi platformi za skupnosti je ena skupina z brisanjem vsebin znižala mesečni odliv s približno 30 % na 5 %. Njegova lastna skupnost je tri tedenske klice skrčila na enega, ko je bila preobremenjenost najpogostejši razlog za odpoved. V njegovih fitnesih je sedem naloženih taktik (uvajanje, odklepanja, izstopni pogovori, spremljanje obiska in druge) znižalo odliv z 11 % na 6 % na mesec.

B2B svetovanje z odlivom retainerjev

Svetovalno podjetje za bare s približno 1,1 mio $ prihodka je retainer stranke izgubljalo po nekaj četrtletjih. Njegova diagnoza: vrednost ni bila prikazana. Odliv popravi najprej, tako da strankam vsak mesec pokažeš njihove številke, preden vložiš več v začetni del lijaka.

Izmišljeno: upravljana Qlik analitika kot storitev

Leto je začelo 30 proizvodnih podjetij po 1.500 € na mesec; dvanajst mesecev pozneje jih je plačevalo še 18. To je 60 % letnega zadrževanja, približno 2,5 leta in 45.000 € prihodka na stranko. Pogovori pokažejo, da so stranke, ki so ostale, že v prvem mesecu vodile sestanek vodstva iz nadzorne plošče. Uvajanje je prenovljeno okoli tega sestanka, prošnja za priporočilo pa sledi takoj po njem. Pri 80 % zadrževanja bi bil življenjski prihodek na stranko okoli 90.000 €.

Pogoste napake

  • Širjenje vedra, ki pušča. Več oglaševanja ob visokem odlivu samo kupi več ljudi, ki jih boš izgubil.
  • LTV, merjen kot prihodek. Hoče življenjski bruto dobiček. LTV iz prihodka lahko nedonosen kanal pokaže kot zdrav.
  • Nižanje cene, da bi ustavil odliv. Njegovo lastno znižanje odliva ni spremenilo, v enem primeru tudi znižanje ponavljajoče se cene za približno šestino ni spremenilo ničesar.
  • Dodajanje funkcij, da bi zadržal ljudi. Neuporabljene vsebine povečajo vrzel med plačanim in porabljenim ter dvignejo število odpovedi.

Kako bi to uporabil Alex

  • Najprej izračuna odliv in LTV. Večina lastnikov ne pozna ne enega ne drugega. Strop izračuna, preden začne govoriti o rasti.
  • Zadrževanje popravi pred pridobivanjem, razen če je zadrževanje že dobro; takrat trud usmeri v pridobivanje.
  • Začne pri uvajanju. Če ga ni, je to prvi popravek; nato odklepanja (unlockables), izstopni pogovori in spremljanje uporabe, ena taktika naenkrat.
  • Aktivacijo določi iz najboljših strank in prvih 30 dni usmeri vanjo.
  • Letni paket naredi za privzeto izbiro in loči enkratno vrednost od ponavljajoče se naročnine.
  • Prošnjo za priporočilo in nadgradnjo postavi v trenutek potrebe, takoj po aktivaciji in takoj po nakupu.
  • Vprašanja, ki bi jih postavil: Koliko strank odpove vsak mesec in od koliko aktivnih? Kakšna sta cena in bruto marža? Koliko lanskih strank še plačuje in kolikšen del lanskega prihodka? Kako je videti uvajanje? Kaj je tvoja točka aktivacije? Zakaj je odšlo zadnjih 20 in zakaj ostajajo najdlje zadržane stranke? Kje točno v procesu prosiš za priporočilo?

Delovni list

Uporabi seznam strank iz zadnjih 12 mesecev.

  1. Številke: Mesečni odliv, LTV in LTGP. Kje je tvoj strop prihodka pri današnjem tempu novih prodaj?
  2. Kohorta: Kolikšen delež strank, aktivnih pred enim letom, še plačuje? Primerjaj z njegovim pravilom palca 80 % za B2B.
  3. Aktivacija: Kaj je tvojih pet najdlje zadržanih strank naredilo v prvih 30 dneh?
  4. Odhodi: Naštej razloge zadnjih 20 odpovedi. Katerega ali katera dva boš popravil najprej?
  5. Zaračunavanje: Bi letno ali četrtletno zaračunavanje lahko postalo privzeto za nove stranke?
  6. Prošnja: V katerem trenutku boš prosil za priporočilo in ponudil naslednji nakup?
