Risk & BetsTveganje in stave

Alex's Playbook · Risk-adjusted bets; push vs pivot

Alex prices every move as payoff times probability, prefers the surer bet, keeps pushing while the core assumptions hold, and treats fear-driven under-spending, debt and premature hiring as the risks that actually hurt owners.Alex vsako potezo oceni kot izplačilo krat verjetnost, raje izbere zanesljivejšo stavo, vztraja, dokler osnovne predpostavke držijo, za resnična tveganja pa ima premajhno vlaganje iz strahu, dolg in zaposlovanje pred rastjo.

The gist

Alex talks about risk in two directions at once. He thinks owners overestimate the downside of the right moves (raising price, spending more on a channel that pays, hiring salespeople when leads exist) and underestimate the downside of the wrong ones (starting a second business, taking on debt, adding staff ahead of growth, changing course on a bad week).

Bet value = payoff × probability At equal value, choose the higher probability, then string sure wins together. Push while the first-principles assumptions hold. Pivot only when data disproves one.

He frames risk as personal and usually overestimated: for most founders the real downside is bankruptcy and starting over. The scarce resource is time, since a founder only gets a handful of multi-year attempts in a career.

How it works

Payoff × probability, and the career punch card

In a short lecture on risk he runs a thought experiment: about 35 working years at about five years per serious attempt gives roughly seven bets. Big-bet logic (10% odds at 100x) suits venture funds with unlimited capital and attempts. A founder who loses three such bets in a row is fifteen years in with nothing. So when two options have similar risk-adjusted value, he takes the more probable one and compounds a chain of sure wins. He adds a test: assume none of the good things you expect happen; if you still win, take it.

In another talk he stresses the other side: you are paid for the risk you are seen to take, and risk is often mispriced because people overestimate downside. The two fit together: seek bets that look riskier to others than they are for you.

For changes inside a running business he uses a concrete bar in one hotseat: make a change only when the risk-adjusted upside is about 20% or more (for example 40% upside at 50/50 odds), because every change costs a dip.

Idea risk versus execution risk

He separates two kinds of risk and says he only takes the second. Idea risk asks whether people want it. Execution risk asks whether you can run it well. He picks businesses with proven demand, so the only variable is execution. With proven demand, a failure tells you the problem was you. With idea risk you cannot tell, which makes push-or-pivot decisions nearly impossible.

Push or pivot

His rule, repeated in several videos: pivot only when a core assumption about demand or monetisation has been disproven by data. If demand is real and things are simply hard, keep solving one problem at a time. He calls this "good hard". Write the assumptions down before investing (the buyers are on this platform, they can be reached profitably), so you know what would count as disproof. Losses from testing an assumption that turned out false are the price of learning, which he calls an ignorance tax. In one lecture he puts the ratio bluntly: push nine times out of ten, and pivot when the market structurally does not allow repeat purchase.

Do not start a new venture to escape a plateau

His test for the urge: would you still want the new venture if the current business tripled? If not, it is an escape. A new business caps at the founder's current skill level, so changing the vehicle does not lift the ceiling. Years in one business pay down your ignorance debt; a new field puts you back at the bottom.

Owners self-capping spend out of fear

Often the owner's fear is the limiter: capped budgets on profitable funnels, too few creatives, no new salespeople despite lead flow. His counter-arguments:

  • If a dollar reliably returns several, the right budget is as much as the channel will take.
  • Testing spend is investment. Budget a known loss to find the few keywords, creatives or channels that scale. One rule of thumb he gives: let a new campaign spend up to about 2x the allowable acquisition cost before shutting it off. A new channel costing $10K a month for six months before its first sale can still be a good investment if it then returns 5–10x.
  • Reversible downside. If spending more fails, you can go back to what you did before.
  • Check fear against cash flow. If cash is fine, the hesitation is irrational.

He also sizes bets. In one turnaround he notes that even a high-expected-value bet is dangerous when you only have enough capital for two hands, and that most founders are under-capitalised relative to the odds.

Debt, expenses and hiring ahead of growth

His view of debt is conservative: it adds risk, and over a long horizon risk tends to come due, so he leans toward paying it off. He prefers growing debt-free through offer design (upfront cash, prepay, anchors). Expenses are downside risk, so he does not hire ahead of growth: get the growth and let it pay for the people. He recalls hiring 25 support reps in anticipation of growth, and later four people handled more volume. His exception is commission-based sales reps. He also takes profit out regularly, since being asset rich and cash poor degrades decisions.

Slow down when emotional

In a lecture on what nearly killed his business he names the signal: you flip-flop on the same data from day to day. When he senses emotion or urgency, he slows down, sleeps on big decisions for 24 hours, treats "must decide today" as usually fictitious, and only makes big multivariable calls from a rested state. Once decided, he sticks with the call through the predictable difficulties. This sits alongside his view that a company grows at the speed its leader makes decisions. One way to hold both: decide small things promptly and big emotional ones slowly.

Examples

Cases are paraphrased and anonymised from Alex's videos. Any example marked as invented is for illustration only.

Telehealth owner planning a consultancy

Before. A telehealth and clinic business at about $550K a month has stalled because the product cannot be advertised on the major social platforms. The owner wants to start a consultancy teaching other physicians.

After. Drop the new venture. Restate the problem as "how do I acquire customers from a channel I am allowed to use", and put all effort into that.

The consultancy would enter a saturated market with no track record. The existing engine already works, and its real constraint would follow the owner into any new business.

Education business capping its own ads

An online craft-education membership (about $800K a year, 30–35% profit) ran four ads and limited spend at about $37 per acquisition. Alex told the owner to make 80 or more ads and accept up to about $100 per acquisition, because back-end value recovered it within the first month.

Consumer brand with a defined last test

A founder of a premium drinks brand had about $1.5M in sales across many stores, zero profit and no repeat-purchase data. Alex proposed one focused bet on travel-retail contracts, where the small can was an advantage. If she could not close one, she should shut it down and put her time into the property development she had already proven on the side. The rule: learn fast rather than spend three more years on an unproven assumption.

Common mistakes

  • Treating test spend as lost money. The search for a scalable channel always includes losers.
  • Pivoting on a bad month. Without written assumptions, frustration gets mistaken for disproof.
  • Betting the career on long odds. A few multi-year attempts cannot absorb a string of 10% bets.
  • Hiring for the growth you expect. Fixed costs taken on early become the risk that sinks you if growth is late.
  • Deciding while upset. Flip-flopping on the same data is the sign to wait.

How Alex would apply it

  • He asks for the payoff and the odds and prefers the surer path when the products are close.
  • He chooses execution risk over idea risk and wants proven demand before any new venture.
  • He pushes while the written assumptions hold and pivots only on data, not on fatigue.
  • He runs the escape test before discussing any new business: would you still want it if the current one tripled?
  • He removes fear-based caps on spend and sales hiring when unit economics and cash flow support it, and frames testing as an investment with a budget.
  • He keeps fixed costs and debt low, pays down debt, takes profit out, and does not hire ahead of growth (commission reps excepted).
  • Questions he would ask: Has a fundamental assumption about demand been disproven? How much are you spending, can you spend more, and why don't you? If your return were 10:1 instead of 3:1, would you spend more? What is your monthly break-even and debt service? Would you still want this if the current business tripled?

Worksheet

Pick one decision you are facing and work through it.

  1. Value: Estimate payoff and probability for each option. Which has the higher product, and which is surer?
  2. Risk type: Is demand proven, or are you betting on whether people want it?
  3. Assumptions: Write the two or three assumptions that must hold. What data would disprove each?
  4. Escape test: Would you still choose this if your current business tripled?
  5. Spend: Where are you capping spend or hiring out of fear? What do unit economics and cash flow say?
  6. State: Are you deciding rested and calm? If not, set a date 24 hours out.

Bistvo

Alex o tveganju govori v dveh smereh hkrati. Meni, da lastniki precenjujejo slabo stran pravih potez (dvig cene, več vlaganja v kanal, ki se izplača, zaposlitev prodajalcev, ko leadi obstajajo) in podcenjujejo slabo stran napačnih (začetek drugega posla, zadolževanje, zaposlovanje pred rastjo, menjava smeri po slabem tednu).

Vrednost stave = izplačilo × verjetnost Pri enaki vrednosti izberi večjo verjetnost, nato niži zanesljive zmage eno za drugo. Vztrajaj (push), dokler temeljne predpostavke držijo. Spremeni smer (pivot) šele, ko podatki eno ovržejo.

Tveganje vidi kot osebno in navadno precenjeno: za večino ustanoviteljev je resnična slaba stran stečaj in začetek od začetka. Redki vir je čas, saj ima ustanovitelj v karieri le peščico večletnih poskusov.

Kako deluje

Izplačilo × verjetnost in kariera kot luknjalna kartica

V kratkem predavanju o tveganju naredi miselni poskus: okoli 35 delovnih let pri okoli petih letih na resen poskus da približno sedem stav. Logika velikih stav (10 % verjetnost za 100-kratnik) ustreza skladom tveganega kapitala z neomejenim kapitalom in poskusi. Ustanovitelj, ki izgubi tri take stave zapored, je petnajst let v karieri brez ničesar. Zato, kadar imata dve možnosti podobno vrednost ob upoštevanju tveganja, izbere verjetnejšo in sestavlja verigo zanesljivih zmag. Doda test: predpostavi, da se nič od dobrega, kar pričakuješ, ne zgodi; če še vedno zmagaš, vzemi.

V drugem nastopu poudari drugo stran: plačan si za tveganje, ki ga drugi vidijo, da ga prevzemaš, tveganje pa je pogosto napačno ovrednoteno, ker ljudje precenjujejo slabo stran. Oboje se ujema: išči stave, ki so drugim videti bolj tvegane, kot so zate.

Za spremembe v delujočem podjetju v enem hotseatu uporabi konkreten prag: spremembo naredi le, kadar je pričakovani dobiček, prilagojen tveganju, okoli 20 % ali več (na primer 40 % potenciala pri 50/50), ker vsaka sprememba stane padec.

Tveganje ideje in tveganje izvedbe

Loči dve vrsti tveganja in pravi, da prevzema samo drugo. Tveganje ideje (idea risk) sprašuje, ali ljudje to sploh hočejo. Tveganje izvedbe (execution risk) sprašuje, ali to znaš dobro voditi. Izbira posle z dokazanim povpraševanjem, tako da ostane edina spremenljivka izvedba. Pri dokazanem povpraševanju ti neuspeh pove, da je bil problem v tebi. Pri tveganju ideje tega ne veš, zato je odločitev med vztrajanjem in menjavo smeri skoraj nemogoča.

Vztrajaj ali spremeni smer

Njegovo pravilo, ponovljeno v več videih: smer spremeni samo, ko so podatki ovrgli osnovno predpostavko o povpraševanju ali monetizaciji. Če je povpraševanje resnično in je samo težko, rešuj en problem za drugim. Temu reče »dobro težko« (good hard). Predpostavke zapiši pred vlaganjem (kupci so na tej platformi, dosežemo jih lahko z dobičkom), da veš, kaj bi štelo kot ovržba. Izgube pri testiranju predpostavke, ki se izkaže za napačno, so cena učenja, ki ji reče davek na nevednost (ignorance tax). V enem predavanju razmerje pove naravnost: vztrajaj devetkrat od desetih, smer spremeni, kadar trg strukturno ne dopušča ponovnega nakupa.

Ne začenjaj novega posla, da bi pobegnil s platoja

Njegov test za to željo: bi si novi posel še vedno želel, če bi se obstoječi potrojil? Če ne, gre za beg. Nov posel se ustavi pri ustanoviteljevi trenutni ravni znanja, zato menjava vozila ne dvigne stropa. Leta v enem poslu odplačajo tvoj dolg nevednosti; novo področje te vrne na dno.

Lastniki, ki si iz strahu sami omejijo vlaganje

Pogosto je omejitev lastnikov strah: omejeni proračuni na dobičkonosnih lijakih, premalo oglasov, nobenih novih prodajalcev kljub dotoku leadov. Njegovi protiargumenti:

  • Če dolar zanesljivo vrne več dolarjev, je pravi proračun toliko, kolikor kanal sprejme.
  • Denar za testiranje je naložba. Vnaprej predvidi znano izgubo, da najdeš tistih nekaj ključnih besed, oglasov ali kanalov, ki se skalirajo. Eno njegovih pravil: nova kampanja lahko porabi do približno 2-kratnika dovoljenega stroška pridobitve stranke, preden jo ugasneš. Nov kanal, ki šest mesecev stane 10.000 dolarjev na mesec do prve prodaje, je lahko še vedno dobra naložba, če nato vrača 5- do 10-kratnik.
  • Popravljiva slaba stran. Če večje vlaganje ne uspe, se lahko vrneš k temu, kar si delal prej.
  • Strah preveri z denarnim tokom. Če je denarni tok v redu, je obotavljanje iracionalno.

Stave tudi dimenzionira. Pri enem reševanju podjetja opozori, da je celo stava z visoko pričakovano vrednostjo nevarna, če imaš kapitala samo za dve igri, in da ima večina ustanoviteljev premalo kapitala glede na verjetnosti.

Dolg, stroški in zaposlovanje pred rastjo

Do dolga je konzervativen: dodaja tveganje, tveganje pa na dolgi rok rado pride na vrsto, zato se nagiba k odplačilu. Raje raste brez dolga, z zasnovo ponudbe (denar vnaprej, predplačila, sidrne ponudbe). Stroški so slaba stran, zato ne zaposluje pred rastjo: najprej pridobi rast, nato naj ta plača ljudi. Spominja se, da je v pričakovanju rasti zaposlil 25 ljudi za podporo strankam, kasneje pa so štirje obdelali večji obseg. Izjema so prodajalci na proviziji. Dobiček redno tudi dvigne iz podjetja, ker stanje »bogat s premoženjem, reven z gotovino« slabša odločitve.

Upočasni, ko si čustven

V predavanju o tem, kaj je skoraj uničilo njegov posel, poimenuje znak: na istih podatkih iz dneva v dan menjaš mnenje. Ko zazna čustva ali nujnost, upočasni, o velikih odločitvah prespi 24 ur, »odločiti se moram danes« ima navadno za izmišljeno, velike odločitve z več spremenljivkami pa sprejema le spočit. Ko se odloči, odločitve ne spreminja ob predvidljivih težavah, ki sledijo. To stoji ob njegovem mnenju, da podjetje raste s hitrostjo, s katero se odloča vodja. Oboje lahko združiš tako: o majhnih stvareh odločaj hitro, o velikih čustvenih počasi.

Primeri

Primeri so povzeti in anonimizirani iz Alexovih videov. Primeri, označeni kot izmišljeni, so samo za ponazoritev.

Lastnik telemedicine, ki načrtuje svetovanje

Pred. Podjetje za telemedicino in klinike s približno 550.000 dolarji na mesec je obstalo, ker izdelka ni mogoče oglaševati na glavnih družbenih omrežjih. Lastnik bi rad ustanovil svetovanje, ki bi učilo druge zdravnike.

Po. Opusti novi posel. Problem preoblikuj v »kako pridobim stranke iz kanala, ki ga smem uporabljati« in ves trud usmeri vanj.

Svetovanje bi vstopilo na nasičen trg brez dosežkov. Obstoječi stroj že deluje, njegovo resnično ozko grlo pa bi lastnik odnesel v vsak nov posel.

Izobraževalni posel, ki si sam omejuje oglase

Spletno članstvo za ročne spretnosti (okoli 800.000 dolarjev na leto, 30–35 % dobička) je poganjalo štiri oglase in vlaganje omejilo pri okoli 37 dolarjih na pridobitev. Alex je lastniku naročil, naj naredi 80 ali več oglasov in sprejme do okoli 100 dolarjev na pridobitev, ker je vrednost v nadaljevanju to povrnila v prvem mesecu.

Potrošniška blagovna znamka z določenim zadnjim testom

Ustanoviteljica premium znamke pijač je imela okoli 1,5 milijona dolarjev prodaje v številnih trgovinah, nič dobička in nobenih podatkov o ponovnih nakupih. Alex je predlagal eno osredotočeno stavo na pogodbe s potovalno prodajo, kjer je bila majhna pločevinka prednost. Če ne bi zaprla nobene, naj posel zapre in čas posveti razvoju nepremičnin, ki ga je ob strani že dokazala. Pravilo: raje se nauči hitro, kot da porabiš še tri leta za nedokazano predpostavko.

Pogoste napake

  • Denar za teste kot izgubljen denar. Iskanje kanala, ki se skalira, vedno vključuje poražence.
  • Menjava smeri po slabem mesecu. Brez zapisanih predpostavk frustracijo zamenjaš za ovržbo.
  • Kariera, stavljena na dolge kvote. Nekaj večletnih poskusov ne prenese niza stav z 10 % verjetnostjo.
  • Zaposlovanje za rast, ki jo pričakuješ. Fiksni stroški, prevzeti prezgodaj, postanejo tveganje, ki te potopi, če rast zamuja.
  • Odločanje v razburjenju. Menjavanje mnenja na istih podatkih je znak, da počakaš.

Kako bi to uporabil Alex

  • Vpraša po izplačilu in verjetnosti in izbere zanesljivejšo pot, ko sta produkta blizu.
  • Raje izbere tveganje izvedbe kot tveganje ideje in pred vsakim novim poslom zahteva dokazano povpraševanje.
  • Vztraja, dokler zapisane predpostavke držijo, in smer spremeni le na podlagi podatkov, ne utrujenosti.
  • Izvede test bega, preden se pogovarja o kakršnem koli novem poslu: bi si to še želel, če bi se obstoječi posel potrojil?
  • Odstrani omejitve iz strahu pri vlaganju in zaposlovanju prodajalcev, kadar to podpirata ekonomika enote in denarni tok, testiranje pa obravnava kot naložbo s proračunom.
  • Fiksne stroške in dolg drži nizko, odplačuje dolg, dviguje dobiček in ne zaposluje pred rastjo (razen prodajalcev na proviziji).
  • Vprašanja, ki bi jih postavil: Je bila katera temeljna predpostavka o povpraševanju ovržena? Koliko vlagaš, lahko vlagaš več in zakaj ne? Če bi bil donos 10 : 1 namesto 3 : 1, bi vložil več? Kakšna sta tvoja mesečna točka preloma in odplačilo dolga? Bi si to še želel, če bi se obstoječi posel potrojil?

Delovni list

Izberi eno odločitev, pred katero stojiš, in jo predelaj.

  1. Vrednost: Za vsako možnost oceni izplačilo in verjetnost. Katera ima večji produkt in katera je zanesljivejša?
  2. Vrsta tveganja: Je povpraševanje dokazano ali staviš na to, ali ljudje to sploh hočejo?
  3. Predpostavke: Zapiši dve ali tri predpostavke, ki morajo držati. Kateri podatki bi ovrgli vsako od njih?
  4. Test bega: Bi to še vedno izbral, če bi se tvoj obstoječi posel potrojil?
  5. Vlaganje: Kje iz strahu omejuješ vlaganje ali zaposlovanje? Kaj pravita ekonomika enote in denarni tok?
  6. Stanje: Se odločaš spočit in miren? Če ne, določi rok čez 24 ur.
