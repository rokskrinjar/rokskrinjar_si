Alex's Playbook · Sales team system (speed to lead, feed the killers, drill and review)

How Alex builds selling into a system: what surrounds the call, who gets which lead, a question-led call, a planned answer to every objection, and a team trained by daily drills and recorded calls.Kako Alex prodajo spremeni v sistem: kaj obdaja klic, kdo dobi kateri lead, klic, ki ga vodijo vprašanja, vnaprej pripravljen odgovor na vsak ugovor in ekipa, ki jo trenirajo dnevne vaje in posnetki klicev.

The gist

Alex treats sales as a machine with many small levers, each raising the odds that someone buys. His most repeated claim across the sales trainings is that what happens around the call (availability, speed to lead, booking, reminders, lead routing) usually moves revenue more than what is said on it. He fixes those first because they are mostly one-time setup work.

Sales = opportunities (availability, speed, show rate) × conversion (diagnosis, short pitch, objections) × consistency (training, review, standards)

The call itself follows the CLOSER framework, which this entry does not repeat. Here the focus is the system around it: lead routing, rules for questions and objections, the handoff into delivery, and how he trains, ranks, pays and recruits reps.

How it works

Around the call: availability, speed, show rate

A rep's job has three parts: maximise opportunities, convert them, sustain it. Opportunities come first.

  • Speed to lead. Contact every new lead within about 60 seconds, and call again if they don't pick up. In several hotseats he prescribes one person whose only job is calling new leads.
  • Availability. Sell seven days a week, offer start times every 15 minutes, and offer only same-day or next-day slots. If that leaves you short of slots, hire another rep rather than open later dates. In his scheduling-software data, open slots predicted throughput more strongly than any close-rate change.
  • Pull appointments forward. When someone books for later, call straight away and offer a slot today. One rep who did this outsold the next best about 4x.
  • Reminders. Manual messages 24 hours before, the morning of, and an hour before, with something showing prep you did for them. He says this personal prep can lift show rates by about 30%.
  • Calendar load. Keep reps around 70–75% booked. Overbooked pushes appointments out, underbooked breeds desperation.
  • Pre-call education. A short video that answers the common questions. Deals that need three or four calls signal too little education beforehand.

Every rep gets two SOPs: what to say on calls and what to do between them. Every meeting ends with the next one booked.

Lead scoring and routing

Score leads on the one or two factors that predict a sale, then "feed the killers": the best leads go to the best closers, the worst to new or weak reps. His recurring story is a timeshare company where this lifted the top rep's income more than tenfold and, rolled out nationally, multiplied the business. New reps practise on poor leads, where losses are smallest. Complaints about lead quality go to the manager privately, never to the group.

The call: questions, diagnosis, short pitch

  • Question-led scripts. About 12 questions with one or two follow-ups each. No paragraphs, because reps read statements aloud. A closer says only three things: questions, restatements, and short stories that break one belief.
  • Diagnosis before prescription. Current state, desired state, the obstacle, and every past attempt, in the prospect's words. He does not pitch until he expects a yes. The prospect should talk about two-thirds of the time.
  • Three pillars as analogies. Chunk the solution into three components and show that each past attempt lacked at least one. Each pillar gets a metaphor or a 30-second story, and the whole pitch runs about two to three minutes. Reps memorise only these stories and the objection handles.

Objection theory

Objections fall into three groups: circumstances (time, money, fit), other people (spouse, partner) and self (self-doubt). The deeper the group, the closer you are to the real reason.

They say It usually means His direction
No time Not a priority yet Ask what changed since they said they could commit; put a number on waiting
No money They don't see the value Resourcefulness, not resources; check rep conviction
Spouse or partner Wants support, not permission Use past agreements: the partner already dislikes the problem
Detail questions Preference: "your result, my way" Ask what answer they need before giving one
"Let me think" Avoiding the decision Ask the main concern, then what they fear happening

Raise likely obstacles before the price, when the stakes are low. After the price they become objections. On each one he acknowledges, associates (often through a past client as a third party) and asks the next question. His most-used close plays out best case and worst case.

Step-downs instead of discounts

He starts from the belief that everyone buys something. He never cuts price to close. Instead he moves down a planned ladder: prepayment discount, third-party financing, partial prepay, payment plan, then a smaller commitment. A lower price is traded for something (longer term, testimonial, referral), or a bonus is added. If about six step-downs fail, he concludes the problem was never money and the prospect checked out earlier.

The ethics guardrail

He frames selling as transferring conviction across a bridge of trust. If they would not buy knowing everything you know, a yes is deception. He turns away people the offer won't help. With a qualified prospect and honest facts, he considers asking again an obligation.

Handshake to customer success

Within the hour after the close, the next person sends a personal video restating the buyer's goal. It cuts cold feet. Sales and customer success review recorded calls together weekly so promises match delivery.

Training: drills, recordings, one fix at a time

  • Daily role-play of one short script section, corrected immediately, repeated right several times.
  • Script adherence first: if nobody follows it, you can't tell whether the script is the problem.
  • Record every call. The manager listens at double speed, picks the one or two fixes with the biggest effect on close rate, gives feedback briefly, then role-plays the fix. One fix at a time.
  • Conviction drives tone: testimonials read aloud at huddles, ideally by the rep who closed that client.

Leaderboards, cuts, pay ladders, recruiting

A daily leaderboard and competitions every four to six weeks, run in small teams so reps share what works. Cut the bottom performers regularly (he cites about 10% a quarter) after a short improvement plan. Pay attracts reps but rarely changes behaviour, except paying on cash collected. A visible career ladder (setter, qualifier, closer, with objective targets and relegation) gives entry roles a future, and a high top earner becomes the recruiting ad. Hire through group interviews and a coachability test, prefer believers over seasoned closers, and don't make the best closer the manager.

Examples

Cases are paraphrased and anonymised from Alex's videos. Any example marked as invented is for illustration only.

Webinar-funnel coaching business (hotseat, high six figures a month)

Before. Webinar show rate about 18%, close rate 14% after the webinar, CAC about $5,800.

After. Reminders across every medium, honest scarcity, a scripted call opening, role-play and per-rep metrics. From the same ad spend, booked calls went from 58 to 102, close rate to 35% and CAC to about $2,900.

The ads did not change. The gains came from shows, the call opening and training.

Multi-location service chain (portfolio company, 14 locations)

Staff sold one-off sessions. After secret-shopping the sales, Alex wrote a diagnostic process: a pre-sale questionnaire, the card taken while "setting up the profile", the customer choosing their own goal, the price set for the path to that goal, and a prepay step-down ladder built into the point-of-sale system. Revenue per customer rose from about $200 to about $800, and the chain grew to 32 locations in about 18 months. The service itself stayed the same.

Analytics consultancy (invented B2B example)

A Qlik consultancy answers demo requests the next day and books calls a week out. The fix: call within minutes, offer a slot today or tomorrow, send a 5-minute video of a finished dashboard. The pitch has three pillars (trusted data, one model for everyone, answers in seconds), each with an analogy. Price objections go down a ladder: prepaid project, phased payments, then a paid two-week pilot.

Common mistakes

  • Polishing the pitch while leads wait. Slow response, week-out slots and no reminders lose more sales than weak closing.
  • Spreading leads evenly. Equal routing wastes the best leads on the weakest reps.
  • Improvised discounts. Unplanned price cuts sound like begging and lower trust.
  • Training by handing out scripts. Without daily drills and call review, reps drift and nobody can diagnose the script.

How Alex would apply it

  • He fixes around the call first. Speed to lead, same-day slots and reminders come before script changes.
  • He treats show rate and close rate as equal. A 20% lift in either is worth the same, so he picks whichever is easier from where you are.
  • He routes leads deliberately: best to the killers, worst to the trainees, no group complaints.
  • He never cuts price to close. Terms or components change, never the price, and price tests are planned in advance.
  • He coaches one fix at a time and judges reps on sub-metrics, not close rate alone.
  • He makes the founder sell until about $1M, then checks the model still works when reps close at half his rate.
  • Questions he would ask: How fast do you call new leads? What percentage book, show, get an offer and close? How booked are the calendars? Is the team following the script word for word? Which one fix would move this rep's close rate most? Would one more rep earn more than they cost?

Worksheet

Answer in writing using last month's numbers.

  1. Speed: What is the median time from opt-in to first call? Who owns it?
  2. Funnel: What share of leads booked, showed, received an offer and closed? Which step is furthest below the rest?
  3. Routing: Which one or two lead traits predict a sale? Who gets the best leads today?
  4. Pitch: What are your three pillars, and which analogy explains each?
  5. Objections: List your three most common objections and the step-down ladder you use instead of discounts.
  6. Training: When did you last record, review and role-play a call, and what was the single fix?

Bistvo

Alex prodajo obravnava kot stroj z veliko majhnimi vzvodi, od katerih vsak malo dvigne verjetnost nakupa. Njegova najpogosteje ponovljena trditev v prodajnih treningih je, da tisto, kar se dogaja okoli klica (razpoložljivost, hitrost odziva, rezervacija termina, opomniki, razporejanje leadov), navadno premakne prihodke bolj kot tisto, kar je povedano na klicu. To popravi najprej, ker gre večinoma za enkratno nastavitev.

Prodaja = priložnosti (razpoložljivost, hitrost, odstotek prihodov) × konverzija (diagnoza, kratek pitch, ugovori) × doslednost (trening, pregled klicev, standardi)

Sam klic sledi okviru CLOSER, ki ga tu ne ponavljamo. Ta vnos govori o sistemu okoli njega: razporejanje leadov, pravila za vprašanja in ugovore, predaja v izvedbo ter kako Alex trenira, razvršča, plačuje in zaposluje prodajalce.

Kako deluje

Okoli klica: razpoložljivost, hitrost, prihodi

Prodajalčevo delo ima tri dele: čim več priložnosti, njihova konverzija in vzdrževanje tega skozi čas. Priložnosti so prve.

  • Hitrost odziva (speed to lead). Vsak nov lead pokliči v približno 60 sekundah in pokliči še enkrat, če se ne oglasi. V več hotseatih predpiše eno osebo, katere edino delo je klicanje novih leadov.
  • Razpoložljivost. Prodajaj sedem dni na teden, ponudi začetke terminov na 15 minut in ponujaj samo termine za danes ali jutri. Če ti terminov zmanjka, zaposli še enega prodajalca, namesto da odpreš kasnejše datume. V podatkih njegove programske opreme za rezervacije je število prostih terminov napovedovalo obseg prodaje bolj kot katera koli sprememba odstotka zaprtih.
  • Premik termina naprej. Ko nekdo rezervira za kasneje, ga takoj pokliči in ponudi termin še danes. Prodajalec, ki je to delal, je prodal približno 4-krat več od drugega najboljšega.
  • Opomniki. Ročna sporočila 24 ur prej, zjutraj na dan klica in eno uro prej, s čim, kar pokaže, da si se nanje pripravil. Pravi, da lahko ta osebna priprava dvigne odstotek prihodov za približno 30 %.
  • Zasedenost koledarja. Prodajalci naj bodo zasedeni približno 70–75 %. Prepoln koledar potiska termine v prihodnost, prazen pa rodi obup.
  • Izobraževanje pred klicem. Kratek video, ki odgovori na pogosta vprašanja. Posli, ki potrebujejo tri ali štiri klice, kažejo, da je bilo pred prvim klicem premalo izobraževanja.

Vsak prodajalec dobi dva SOP-a: kaj reči na klicu in kaj delati med klici. Vsak sestanek se konča z že rezerviranim naslednjim.

Točkovanje in razporejanje leadov

Leade točkuj po enem ali dveh dejavnikih, ki napovedujeta nakup, nato »nahrani ubijalce« (feed the killers): najboljši leadi gredo najboljšim closerjem, najslabši novim ali šibkim prodajalcem. Njegova ponavljajoča se zgodba je podjetje za timeshare, kjer je to najboljšemu prodajalcu dvignilo dohodek za več kot desetkrat, uvedba po vsej državi pa je podjetje pomnožila. Novi prodajalci vadijo na slabih leadih, kjer so izgube najmanjše. Pritožbe o kakovosti leadov gredo vodji na štiri oči, nikoli pred skupino.

Klic: vprašanja, diagnoza, kratek pitch

  • Skripte iz vprašanj. Približno 12 vprašanj z enim ali dvema podvprašanjema. Brez odstavkov, ker prodajalci izjave preberejo na glas. Closer izreče samo tri stvari: vprašanja, povzetke in kratke zgodbe, ki razbijejo eno prepričanje.
  • Diagnoza pred receptom. Trenutno stanje, želeno stanje, ovira in vsak pretekli poskus, z besedami potencialne stranke. Ne pitcha, dokler ne pričakuje »da«. Potencialna stranka naj govori približno dve tretjini časa.
  • Trije stebri kot analogije. Rešitev razdeli na tri sestavne dele in pokaži, da je vsakemu preteklemu poskusu manjkal vsaj eden. Vsak steber dobi metaforo ali 30-sekundno zgodbo, celoten pitch pa traja približno dve do tri minute. Prodajalci se na pamet naučijo samo teh zgodb in odgovorov na ugovore.

Teorija ugovorov

Ugovori spadajo v tri skupine: okoliščine (čas, denar, ustreznost), drugi ljudje (zakonec, partner) in jaz (dvom vase). Globlja kot je skupina, bližje si pravemu razlogu.

Rečejo Navadno pomeni Njegova smer
Nimam časa Še ni prioriteta Vprašaj, kaj se je spremenilo, odkar so rekli, da lahko vložijo čas; ovrednoti ceno čakanja
Nimam denarja Ne vidijo vrednosti Iznajdljivost, ne viri (resourcefulness, not resources); preveri prepričanje prodajalca
Zakonec ali partner Želi podporo, ne dovoljenja Oprimi se preteklih dogovorov: partnerju problem že zdaj ni všeč
Vprašanja o podrobnostih Preferenca: »tvoj rezultat, na moj način« Najprej vprašaj, kakšen odgovor potrebujejo
»Bom premislil« Izogibanje odločitvi Vprašaj za glavni pomislek, nato česa se bojijo

Verjetne ovire odpri pred ceno, ko so vložki še nizki. Po ceni postanejo ugovori. Pri vsakem Alex pritrdi, poveže (pogosto prek pretekle stranke kot tretje osebe) in postavi naslednje vprašanje. Njegov najpogosteje uporabljen close odigra najboljši in najslabši možni izid.

Stopnice navzdol namesto popustov

Izhaja iz prepričanja, da vsak nekaj kupi. Za zaprtje posla nikoli ne zniža cene. Namesto tega gre po vnaprej pripravljeni lestvici navzdol (step-down): popust za predplačilo, financiranje prek tretje osebe, delno predplačilo, obročno plačilo, nato manjša zaveza. Nižjo ceno zamenja za nekaj v zameno (daljša vezava, mnenje stranke, priporočilo) ali doda bonus. Če približno šest stopnic ne zaleže, sklepa, da problem ni bil denar in da je stranka odklopila že prej v klicu.

Etična varovalka

Prodajo opisuje kot prenos prepričanja čez most zaupanja. Če stranka ne bi kupila, ko bi vedela vse, kar veš ti, je njen »da« prevara. Ljudi, ki jim ponudba ne bo pomagala, zavrne. S kvalificirano stranko in poštenimi dejstvi pa ponovno vprašati zanj pomeni obveznost.

Stisk roke ob predaji v uspeh strank

V eni uri po zaprtju naslednja oseba pošlje oseben video, ki ponovi kupčev cilj. To zmanjša kupčev dvom po nakupu. Prodaja in uspeh strank (customer success) vsak teden skupaj pregledata posnete klice, da se obljube ujemajo z izvedbo.

Trening: vaje, posnetki, en popravek naenkrat

  • Dnevno igranje vlog za en kratek del skripte, takojšen popravek in več pravilnih ponovitev zapored.
  • Najprej doslednost skripti: če se je nihče ne drži, ne moreš vedeti, ali je problem v skripti.
  • Snemaj vsak klic. Vodja posluša na dvojni hitrosti, izbere enega ali dva popravka z največjim učinkom na odstotek zaprtih, da kratko povratno informacijo in nato popravek vadi v igri vlog. En popravek naenkrat.
  • Prepričanje poganja ton: mnenja strank, prebrana na glas na jutranjem sestanku, najbolje s strani prodajalca, ki je to stranko zaprl.

Lestvice, rezi, plačilne lestvice, zaposlovanje

Dnevno posodobljena lestvica uspešnosti in tekmovanja vsakih štiri do šest tednov, v majhnih ekipah, da si prodajalci delijo, kaj deluje. Najslabše redno odreži (omenja približno 10 % na četrtletje), po kratkem načrtu izboljšave. Plačilo privabi prodajalce, redko pa spremeni njihovo vedenje, razen plačila po pobranem denarju. Vidna karierna lestvica (setter, kvalifikator, closer, z objektivnimi cilji in spuščanjem nazaj) daje vstopnim vlogam prihodnost, visok zaslužek najboljšega pa postane oglas za zaposlovanje. Zaposluj prek skupinskih razgovorov in testa sprejemanja povratne informacije, raje vzemi ljudi, ki verjamejo v izdelek, kot izkušene closerje in najboljšega closerja ne postavi za vodjo.

Primeri

Primeri so povzeti in anonimizirani iz Alexovih videov. Primeri, označeni kot izmišljeni, so samo za ponazoritev.

Coaching podjetje z webinarskim lijakom (hotseat, visoke šestmestne številke na mesec)

Pred. Odstotek prihodov na webinar približno 18 %, po webinarju zaprtih 14 %, CAC približno 5.800 $.

Po. Opomniki po vseh kanalih, pošteno pomanjkanje, napisan uvod v klic, igranje vlog in metrike po prodajalcu. Ob enaki porabi za oglase je število rezerviranih klicev zraslo z 58 na 102, odstotek zaprtih na 35 %, CAC pa je padel na približno 2.900 $.

Oglasi se niso spremenili. Rast je prišla iz prihodov, uvoda v klic in treninga.

Veriga storitvenih poslovalnic (podjetje iz portfelja, 14 lokacij)

Zaposleni so prodajali posamezne termine. Po skrivnostnem nakupu (secret shopping) je Alex napisal diagnostični proces: vprašalnik pred prodajo, kartico vzeto med »nastavljanjem profila«, stranko, ki sama izbere svoj cilj, ceno, postavljeno za pot do tega cilja, in lestvico predplačil navzdol, vgrajeno v blagajniški sistem. Prihodek na stranko je zrasel s približno 200 $ na približno 800 $, veriga pa je v približno 18 mesecih zrasla na 32 lokacij. Storitev sama je ostala enaka.

Analitično svetovanje (izmišljen B2B primer)

Qlik svetovalno podjetje na zahteve za demo odgovori naslednji dan in klice rezervira teden dni vnaprej. Popravek: pokliči v nekaj minutah, ponudi termin danes ali jutri, pošlji 5-minutni video končane nadzorne plošče. Pitch ima tri stebre (zanesljivi podatki, en model za vse, odgovori v sekundah), vsakega z analogijo. Ugovori o ceni gredo po lestvici navzdol: predplačan projekt, plačilo po fazah, nato plačan dvotedenski pilot.

Pogoste napake

  • Piljenje pitcha, medtem ko leadi čakajo. Počasen odziv, termini čez teden dni in brez opomnikov stanejo več prodaj kot šibko zapiranje.
  • Enakomerno deljenje leadov. Enakomerna razporeditev zapravi najboljše leade na najšibkejših prodajalcih.
  • Improvizirani popusti. Nenačrtovana znižanja cene zvenijo kot prosjačenje in znižajo zaupanje.
  • Trening z deljenjem skript. Brez dnevnih vaj in pregledov klicev prodajalci zdrsnejo, skripte pa nihče ne more diagnosticirati.

Kako bi to uporabil Alex

  • Najprej popravi tisto okoli klica. Hitrost odziva, termini za isti dan in opomniki pridejo pred spremembami skripte.
  • Odstotek prihodov in odstotek zaprtih obravnava enako. 20-odstotni dvig katerega koli je vreden enako, zato izbere tistega, ki je s tvojega izhodišča lažji.
  • Leade razporeja premišljeno: najboljše ubijalcem, najslabše pripravnikom, brez pritoževanja pred skupino.
  • Za zaprtje nikoli ne zniža cene. Spremenijo se pogoji ali sestavni deli, cena nikoli, testi cene pa so načrtovani vnaprej.
  • Trenira en popravek naenkrat in prodajalce presoja po podmetrikah, ne le po odstotku zaprtih.
  • Ustanovitelj naj prodaja do približno 1 M $, nato preveri, ali model še deluje, ko prodajalci zapirajo s polovico njegovega odstotka.
  • Vprašanja, ki bi jih postavil: Kako hitro pokličeš nove leade? Kolikšen delež rezervira, pride, dobi ponudbo in kupi? Kako zasedeni so koledarji? Ali se ekipa skripte drži od besede do besede? Kateri en popravek bi temu prodajalcu najbolj dvignil odstotek zaprtih? Bi en prodajalec več zaslužil več, kot stane?

Delovni list

Odgovori pisno s številkami zadnjega meseca.

  1. Hitrost: Kolikšna je mediana časa od prijave do prvega klica? Kdo je zanj odgovoren?
  2. Lijak: Kolikšen delež leadov je rezerviral, prišel, dobil ponudbo in kupil? Kateri korak najbolj zaostaja za ostalimi?
  3. Razporejanje: Katera ena ali dve lastnosti leada napovedujeta nakup? Kdo danes dobi najboljše leade?
  4. Pitch: Kateri so tvoji trije stebri in katera analogija pojasni vsakega?
  5. Ugovori: Naštej tri najpogostejše ugovore in lestvico navzdol, ki jo uporabiš namesto popustov.
  6. Trening: Kdaj si nazadnje posnel, pregledal in v igri vlog vadil klic, in kateri je bil tisti en popravek?
