Selling & Scaling · CLOSER Framework

Six steps that turn a sales call into a diagnosis the buyer walks through themselves. Understand first, show the destination second, clear doubts last.Šest korakov, ki prodajni klic spremenijo v diagnozo, skozi katero kupec gre sam. Najprej razumeš, nato pokažeš cilj, na koncu odpraviš dvome.

The gist

A good sales call is a diagnosis. The buyer arrives with a problem, a reason it matters now, and a history of failed attempts to fix it. Your job is to make all three visible to them, then show them where they could be, then help them deal with what still holds them back.

Clarify why they're here · Label them with a problem · Overview past pain · Sell the vacation · Explain away concerns · Reinforce the decision

The order matters more than any single line. The first three letters are entirely about the buyer. Only once they have confirmed the problem in their own words and admitted that past attempts didn't work does it make sense to talk about your offer. A seller who jumps straight to S is pitching to someone who hasn't yet bought the problem.

How it works

C: Clarify why they're here

People don't book a call for no reason. Something happened: a lost customer, a new demand from the board, another missed deadline. That trigger drives the whole conversation. Open with a question about it ("What made you reach out now?") and keep asking until you hear the real reason, which is rarely the first polite answer. If the buyer doesn't know why they came, they won't know why they should buy.

L: Label them with a problem they confirm

When you have heard enough, sum the problem up in one sentence and end with a question: "So the issue is that you have the data but can't trust it in time for board meetings. Is that right?" The buyer must confirm it. Once they say "yes, exactly", the problem belongs to them. If they correct you, that is useful too: you now have a sharper picture. A label is a mirror you hold up. It is never a diagnosis you push on them.

O: Overview past pain

Ask what they have already tried, how long it took, what it cost and why it didn't work. This does three things:

  1. The buyer admits, to themselves, that their current approaches won't solve it.
  2. You learn what not to offer, because it has already failed.
  3. You can separate the problem from blame: the past solution failed because it was the wrong fit, which keeps the buyer's pride intact.

S: Sell the vacation, not the plane flight

Nobody books a holiday for the flight. They want the beach. Describe what the buyer's week or month looks like once the problem is solved, in their own words from C and L. Mention the process only as far as it makes the promise believable. Every extra detail about the plane (methodology, meetings, architecture) is another thing that can feel tiring or risky, which raises effort and lowers perceived likelihood in the Value Equation.

E: Explain away concerns

Concerns are normal and usually mean "I'm not sure yet". In Alex's teaching they commonly come down to a few groups:

Group What it sounds like
Circumstances "I don't have time right now", "It's too expensive"
Other people "I need to ask my partner / the director"
Self-doubt "It won't work for us", "I'm not sure I can do it"

A practical sequence: repeat the concern back, ask whether it is the only thing holding them back, then return to what they told you in C, L and O. Their reason, their problem and their failed attempts are your best arguments, because they are theirs. Don't argue. Arguing makes people defend their position.

R: Reinforce the decision

Right after buying, many people feel buyer's remorse. The sale doesn't end at payment. Congratulate them, tell them exactly what happens next and when, and get them to a first contact or a first small win as fast as possible. Reinforcement cuts cancellations and refunds and starts the experience that later produces referrals.

Examples

Examples are invented for illustration.

Call with a manufacturer's CEO (B2B analytics)

Seller (Clarify): What made you get in touch this month in particular?

Client: At the last board meeting I couldn't say which customers actually make us money. It was embarrassing.

Seller (Label): So the issue isn't a lack of data. You don't get it in time, in a form you trust. Is that right?

Client: Exactly. We have numbers, but every department has its own.

Seller (Overview): What have you tried so far?

Client: Last year we bought a reporting tool. We pay for licences, one person used it, and she's now on maternity leave.

Seller (Sell): Picture the next board meeting. You open one page, see margin by customer for last month, and answer every question in seconds without calling accounting.

Client (concern): Sounds good, but we'll pay again for something nobody uses.

Seller (Explain): That's exactly what happened last year, so it's a fair worry. Is it the only thing holding you back? In the first 30 days we build the report and review it with you twice before the meeting. Nobody on your side has to learn the tool.

Seller (Reinforce, after signing): Good decision. Our analyst calls you tomorrow at nine, and you'll have the first version of the report by Friday.

The concern was self-doubt ("it won't work for us"). The seller didn't argue. He used the client's own story from the Overview step and tied it to a specific part of the offer that removes the effort that caused the last failure.

Adult braces consultation (dental clinic)

Before. Reception explains bracket types, materials, treatment length in months and the price. The patient says she'll think about it and never comes back.

After. First the question why now: her sister's wedding in a year. Label: "You don't like smiling in photos, is that right?" Then what she has tried. Then the destination: smiling in the wedding photos. The money concern is handled with instalments, and the clinic calls that same week to confirm the first appointment.

Same product, same price list. The first version sold the plane (materials and months). The second sold the vacation, and the follow-up call was the R step.

Concerns in quote automation (AI consulting)

Group Client says Possible response
Circumstances (time) "We're in the middle of the season." "The season is when quotes cost you the most hours. How many will you write this spring?"
Circumstances (money) "The budget is spent." Compare the fee with the monthly cost of manual work they named in Overview.
Other people "I need to ask the director." "What will the director ask? Let's prepare the answers, or invite them to a short call."
Self-doubt "Our processes are too specific." "Step one is a working prototype on three of your real enquiries."

Common mistakes

  • Jumping straight to the pitch. Without C, L and O there is nothing to build on. The buyer hears a solution to a problem they haven't voiced, and everything sounds like cost.
  • Stating the problem yourself. If the buyer doesn't confirm it in their own words, they don't own it. Every label ends with a question.
  • Selling the plane. Technical sellers love explaining architecture and methodology. Each detail is a new place for a concern to appear.
  • Arguing with the concern. It hardens the buyer's position. Isolate it and go back to their own words.
  • Going silent after the signature. A few days without contact is enough for doubt to grow. Plan the first 48 hours before the call.

How Alex would apply it

  • He treats the call as the buyer's conversation. The seller asks most of the questions and the buyer does most of the talking, especially in the first half.
  • He makes the buyer say the problem. A problem the seller states is an opinion. A problem the buyer confirms is a reason to buy.
  • He uses past pain as leverage. What they tried and why it failed becomes the case for doing something different now.
  • He keeps the pitch short and outcome-first. If the offer needs a long explanation, he fixes the offer before he fixes the pitch.
  • He sees most concerns as avoidance of a decision, and helps the buyer make one, yes or no, instead of leaving it open. As a rule of thumb, a clear no is better than a vague maybe.
  • He reviews recorded calls against the six steps to find where deals are lost, then trains that step.
  • Questions he would ask: Why did they reach out now? Did they confirm the problem in their own words? What have they already tried, and why did it fail? Did you describe the destination or the process? Which concern did they raise, and did you isolate it? What happened in the first 48 hours after they paid?

Worksheet

Take your last two sales conversations, or one coming up, and answer in writing.

  1. Clarify: What is your opening question that reveals why the buyer got in touch right now?
  2. Label: Write the last buyer's problem as one sentence that ends with "is that right?".
  3. Overview: Which three solutions do your buyers usually try before you, and why don't they work?
  4. Sell: Describe the vacation of your offer in two sentences without a single technical word.
  5. Explain: List the concerns you hear most. Which group does each belong to, and what is your response?
  6. Reinforce: What happens in the first 48 hours after purchase, and what should the buyer feel by the end of them?

Bistvo

Dober prodajni klic je diagnoza. Kupec pride s problemom, z razlogom, zakaj je ta pomemben prav zdaj, in z zgodovino neuspelih poskusov, da bi ga rešil. Tvoja naloga je, da mu vse troje pokažeš, nato pokažeš, kje bi lahko bil, in mu na koncu pomagaš opraviti s tem, kar ga še zadržuje.

Clarify (razjasni, zakaj je tu) · Label (poimenuj problem) · Overview (preglej preteklo bolečino) · Sell the vacation (prodaj počitnice) · Explain away concerns (odpravi pomisleke) · Reinforce (utrdi odločitev)

Vrstni red je pomembnejši od katerega koli posameznega stavka. Prve tri črke so v celoti posvečene kupcu. Šele ko problem potrdi z lastnimi besedami in prizna, da dosedanji poskusi niso delovali, ima smisel govoriti o tvoji ponudbi. Prodajalec, ki skoči naravnost na S, predstavlja ponudbo nekomu, ki problema še ni »kupil«.

Kako deluje

C: Razjasni, zakaj je tukaj (Clarify)

Ljudje ne rezervirajo klica brez razloga. Nekaj se je zgodilo: izgubljen kupec, nova zahteva uprave, še en zamujen rok. Ta sprožilec poganja celoten pogovor. Začni z vprašanjem o njem (»Kaj vas je spodbudilo, da ste se oglasili prav zdaj?«) in sprašuj naprej, dokler ne slišiš pravega razloga, ki je redko prvi vljudni odgovor. Če kupec ne ve, zakaj je prišel, tudi ne bo vedel, zakaj bi kupil.

L: Poimenuj problem, ki ga kupec potrdi (Label)

Ko slišiš dovolj, problem povzameš v en stavek in končaš z vprašanjem: »Torej je težava v tem, da podatke imate, a jim pred sejo uprave ne morete zaupati. Je tako?« Kupec mora potrditi. Ko reče »ja, točno to«, je problem njegov. Če te popravi, je tudi to koristno: zdaj imaš natančnejšo sliko. Poimenovanje je ogledalo, ki ga nastaviš. Nikoli ni diagnoza, ki jo vsiliš.

O: Preglej preteklo bolečino (Overview past pain)

Vprašaj, kaj je že poskusil, koliko časa je trajalo, koliko je stalo in zakaj ni delovalo. S tem dosežeš tri stvari:

  1. Kupec sam sebi prizna, da ga dosedanji pristopi ne bodo rešili.
  2. Izveš, česa ne smeš ponuditi, ker je že propadlo.
  3. Problem ločiš od krivde: prejšnja rešitev ni delovala, ker ni bila prava, in kupčev ponos ostane nedotaknjen.

S: Prodaj počitnice, ne leta z letalom (Sell the vacation, not the plane flight)

Nihče ne rezervira počitnic zaradi leta. Ljudje hočejo plažo. Opiši, kako je videti kupčev teden ali mesec, ko je problem rešen, z njegovimi besedami iz korakov C in L. Proces omeni le toliko, da je obljuba verodostojna. Vsaka dodatna podrobnost o letalu (metodologija, sestanki, arhitektura) je še ena stvar, ki se lahko zdi naporna ali tvegana, kar v enačbi vrednosti (Value Equation) dvigne trud in zniža zaznano verjetnost.

E: Razloži in odpravi pomisleke (Explain away concerns)

Pomisleki so normalni in navadno pomenijo »še nisem prepričan«. V Alexovem učenju se večinoma zvedejo na nekaj skupin:

Skupina Kako zveni
Okoliščine »Zdaj nimam časa«, »Predrago je«
Drugi ljudje »Moram vprašati partnerja / direktorja«
Dvom vase »Pri nas to ne bo delovalo«, »Ne vem, ali bom zmogel«

Praktično zaporedje: pomislek ponovi, vprašaj, ali je to edino, kar ga zadržuje, nato se vrni k temu, kar ti je povedal pri C, L in O. Njegov razlog, njegov problem in njegovi neuspeli poskusi so tvoji najboljši argumenti, ker so njegovi. Ne prepiraj se. Ko se prepiraš, ljudje branijo svoje stališče.

R: Utrdi odločitev (Reinforce the decision)

Takoj po nakupu mnoge prevzame kesanje kupca (buyer's remorse). Prodaja se ne konča s plačilom. Čestitaj, natančno povej, kaj se zgodi naslednje in kdaj, in kupca čim prej pripelji do prvega stika ali prve majhne zmage. Utrjevanje zmanjša odpovedi in vračila ter začne izkušnjo, ki kasneje prinese priporočila.

Primeri

Primeri so izmišljeni in služijo ponazoritvi.

Klic z direktorjem proizvodnega podjetja (B2B analitika)

Prodajalec (Clarify): Kaj vas je spodbudilo, da ste se oglasili ravno ta mesec?

Stranka: Na zadnji seji uprave nisem znal povedati, kateri kupci nam dejansko prinašajo denar. Bilo je neprijetno.

Prodajalec (Label): Torej težava ni pomanjkanje podatkov. Ne dobite jih pravočasno in v obliki, ki ji zaupate. Je tako?

Stranka: Točno. Številke imamo, a vsak oddelek ima svoje.

Prodajalec (Overview): Kaj ste že poskusili?

Stranka: Lani smo kupili orodje za poročanje. Licence plačujemo, uporabljala ga je ena oseba, ki je zdaj na porodniški.

Prodajalec (Sell): Predstavljajte si naslednjo sejo. Odprete eno stran, vidite maržo po kupcih za zadnji mesec in na vsako vprašanje odgovorite v nekaj sekundah, brez klica v računovodstvo.

Stranka (pomislek): Zveni dobro, ampak spet bomo plačali nekaj, česar nihče ne bo uporabljal.

Prodajalec (Explain): Prav to se je zgodilo lani, zato je skrb upravičena. Je to edino, kar vas zadržuje? V prvih 30 dneh poročilo pripravimo mi in ga pred sejo dvakrat skupaj pregledamo. Nihče pri vas se ne rabi učiti orodja.

Prodajalec (Reinforce, po podpisu): Dobra odločitev. Naša analitičarka vas pokliče jutri ob devetih, prvo različico poročila pa imate do petka.

Pomislek je spadal v skupino dvoma vase (»pri nas ne bo delovalo«). Prodajalec se ni prepiral. Uporabil je strankino lastno zgodbo iz koraka Overview in jo povezal s konkretnim delom ponudbe, ki odstrani trud, zaradi katerega je prejšnji poskus spodletel.

Posvet za ortodontski aparat za odrasle (zobna ordinacija)

Pred. Recepcija razloži vrste aparatov, materiale, trajanje terapije v mesecih in ceno. Pacientka reče, da bo premislila, in se ne vrne.

Po. Najprej vprašanje, zakaj zdaj: poroka sestre čez leto dni. Poimenovanje: »Ne marate se smejati na fotografijah, je tako?« Nato, kaj je že poskusila. Nato cilj: nasmeh na poročnih fotografijah. Pomislek o denarju rešijo z obročnim plačilom, ordinacija pa jo isti teden pokliče in potrdi prvi termin.

Isti izdelek, isti cenik. Prva različica je prodajala letalo (materiale in mesece). Druga je prodajala počitnice, klic po posvetu pa je bil korak R.

Pomisleki pri avtomatizaciji ponudb (AI svetovanje)

Skupina Stranka reče Možen odziv
Okoliščine (čas) »Sredi sezone smo.« »V sezoni vas ponudbe stanejo največ ur. Koliko jih boste napisali to pomlad?«
Okoliščine (denar) »Proračun je porabljen.« Primerjaj ceno z mesečnim stroškom ročnega dela, ki ga je stranka omenila pri Overview.
Drugi ljudje »Moram vprašati direktorja.« »Kaj bo direktor vprašal? Pripraviva odgovore ali ga povabite na kratek klic.«
Dvom vase »Naši procesi so preveč specifični.« »Prvi korak je delujoč prototip na treh vaših resničnih povpraševanjih.«

Pogoste napake

  • Skok naravnost na predstavitev. Brez C, L in O ni na čem graditi. Kupec sliši rešitev za problem, ki ga ni izrekel, in vse zveni kot strošek.
  • Problem izrečeš ti. Če ga kupec ne potrdi z lastnimi besedami, ni njegov. Vsako poimenovanje se konča z vprašanjem.
  • Prodaja letala. Tehnični prodajalci radi razlagajo arhitekturo in metodologijo. Vsaka podrobnost je novo mesto, kjer se lahko pojavi pomislek.
  • Prepiranje s pomislekom. Kupčevo stališče se le utrdi. Pomislek izoliraj in se vrni k njegovim besedam.
  • Tišina po podpisu. Nekaj dni brez stika je dovolj, da dvom zraste. Prvih 48 ur načrtuj že pred klicem.

Kako bi to uporabil Alex

  • Klic obravnava kot kupčev pogovor. Prodajalec postavlja večino vprašanj, kupec pa večino govori, posebej v prvi polovici.
  • Poskrbi, da problem izreče kupec. Problem, ki ga izreče prodajalec, je mnenje. Problem, ki ga potrdi kupec, je razlog za nakup.
  • Preteklo bolečino uporabi kot vzvod. Kar so poskusili in zakaj ni delovalo, postane argument, da zdaj naredijo nekaj drugače.
  • Predstavitev je kratka in začne z izidom. Če ponudba potrebuje dolgo razlago, najprej popravi ponudbo, šele nato predstavitev.
  • Večino pomislekov vidi kot izogibanje odločitvi in kupcu pomaga, da se odloči, za ali proti, namesto da ostane odprto. Kot pravilo palca: jasen ne je boljši od nejasnega morda.
  • Posnete klice pregleduje po šestih korakih, da najde, kje se posli izgubljajo, in nato trenira prav ta korak.
  • Vprašanja, ki bi jih postavil: Zakaj so se oglasili prav zdaj? Ali so problem potrdili z lastnimi besedami? Kaj so že poskusili in zakaj ni delovalo? Si opisal cilj ali proces? Kateri pomislek so izrazili in si ga izoliral? Kaj se je zgodilo v prvih 48 urah po plačilu?

Delovni list

Vzemi zadnja dva prodajna pogovora ali enega, ki te čaka, in odgovori pisno.

  1. Clarify: Katero je tvoje uvodno vprašanje, ki razkrije, zakaj se je kupec oglasil prav zdaj?
  2. Label: Problem zadnjega kupca zapiši v en stavek, ki se konča z »je tako?«.
  3. Overview: Katere tri rešitve tvoji kupci najpogosteje poskusijo pred tabo in zakaj ne delujejo?
  4. Sell: Opiši »počitnice« svoje ponudbe v dveh stavkih brez ene same tehnične besede.
  5. Explain: Naštej pomisleke, ki jih slišiš najpogosteje. V katero skupino spada vsak in kakšen je tvoj odziv?
  6. Reinforce: Kaj se zgodi v prvih 48 urah po nakupu in kaj naj kupec ob koncu teh ur čuti?
