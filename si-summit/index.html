I

The day

01 · What happened on September 29?

One lunch. Three announcements.

On Tuesday, September 29, 2026, President Donald Trump hosted a luncheon on "Super Intelligence" in the East Room of the White House, together with House Speaker Mike Johnson. The guests ran the companies that design SI models, make the chips they run on, and operate the data centers that host them.

Fact

The same day, six of those companies signed the White House Accord on Super Intelligence, the President signed Executive Order 14434 renaming AI as SI across the executive branch, and the administration launched America.gov.

White House gallery, EO 14434 and fact sheets · CBS News · Nextgov

Analysis

Competitors this fierce rarely put their names on one page. Whatever the accord achieves in practice, the day showed the industry and the government trying to coordinate in public.

02 · Who was in the room?

The whole supply chain at one table.

Axios published the RSVP list: 31 guests. It reads like a map of the industry, from the labs that build frontier models to the companies that make chips and memory, rent out cloud computing, and secure it all. OpenAI's chief executive, Sam Altman, was at his company's annual conference in San Francisco; its president, Greg Brockman, came instead.

Fact

Six leaders signed the accord alongside the President: Sundar Pichai (Google), Dario Amodei (Anthropic), Mark Zuckerberg (Meta), Greg Brockman (OpenAI), Elon Musk (xAI) and Jensen Huang (Nvidia). Microsoft, Amazon, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Palantir, Palo Alto Networks and ServiceNow attended but are not among the signers. No source has reported why.

    II

    The agreement

    04 · Why competing companies agreed to work together

    Rivals, one page.

    These companies compete every day for customers, researchers and chips. Supporters see the accord as a shared floor for safety: if one company gets something badly wrong, trust in the whole industry suffers. It also gives the industry and Washington a common starting point while Congress is still debating national rules.

    "This is a start and an accord that the whole industry could come to."

    Mark Zuckerberg, Meta · Semafor
    Critics say

    A voluntary pledge can head off tougher, binding regulation. House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries called the agreement "entirely unenforceable."

    Washington Examiner · Benzinga

    05 · What is the Super Intelligence Accord?

    About 300 words. Four layers of checks.

    The White House Accord on Super Intelligence: Joint Commitment on Frontier Responsibilities says every company is responsible for developing its own technology safely. For companies training frontier models, the most advanced SI systems, it lists four layers of controls, and the signers agree to meet to develop standards and best practices together.

    Fact

    The text adds that "over time, it may make sense to codify these steps into laws or regulations." It sets no penalties and does not require audit results to be published.

    Accord text, American Presidency Project (UCSB) · CBS News · CNBC-TV18

    1. Internal controls

      Monitor a model's capabilities and alignment during training, covering cyber, biological and chemical threats.

    2. An empowered internal team

      Checks that controls, monitoring and detection work as intended, and fixes issues it finds.

    3. An independent auditor or evaluator

      An outside party assesses whether the safeguards function. The company chooses it.

    4. A board committee

      An independent committee of the board receives the reports and makes sure problems are remediated.

    They said

    "I think it's morally binding."

    President Trump, asked whether the accord is binding · official stakeout video

    Fact

    "Commitments that are voluntary"

    Speaker Mike Johnson: "commitments that are voluntary on behalf of the industry" · ABC News

    Supporters

    "A solid basis for moving forward."

    Sundar Pichai, Google · Al Jazeera

    "These are voluntary principles, but they are a constructive first step."

    John Miller, Information Technology Industry Council · Bloomberg Government
    Analysis

    The Council on Foreign Relations, while calling the pact toothless, credited its outside-auditor and board-oversight layers as real structure to build on.

    Critics

    "Self-regulation efforts by big tech have been a proven failure."

    Lina Khan, former FTC chair · ABC "This Week", Oct 4

    "Nothing in the document legally compels any of the signers to change their behavior."

    Council on Foreign Relations, Oct 1
    Analysis

    Lawyers quoted by Bloomberg Government note one way a voluntary pledge can still bite: the Federal Trade Commission can act against companies whose public promises turn out to be deceptive.

    III

    The new name

    06 · Why AI became SI

    AI→SI

    Executive Order 14434, "Inaugurating the Era of Super Intelligence," tells federal agencies to write "Super Intelligence" and "SI" instead of "Artificial Intelligence" and "AI" in their correspondence, websites, reports and policy documents. Earlier regulations and historical documents are not changed.

    They said

    The White House says the new term "conveys the true capabilities of the technologies being developed today."

    White House fact sheet, Sept 29

    Critics say

    Senator Mark Warner: the companies are warning that the technology "is advancing faster than our safeguards. The president's response? To rename it and tell the companies developing it to regulate themselves." A YouGov poll that week found 53% preferred "artificial intelligence" and 9% "super intelligence."

    CBS News · CNBC

    Section 2 · What changes

    Agencies use "Super Intelligence" and "SI" going forward. Old rules and records stay as written.

    Section 3 · What SI means

    For now, exactly what "artificial intelligence" means in federal law (15 U.S.C. 9401(3)).

    Within 60 days

    The Assistant to the President for Science and Technology proposes legislative language for a federal definition.

    07 · Does SI mean machines are already smarter than humans?

    No. The name changed. The machines didn't.

    Researchers use several older terms, and they mean very different things. The government's new "SI" is a name for today's technology, not a claim that it has become superhuman. Legally, SI means what AI meant the day before.

    They said

    "Superintelligence is already a word, and it means something different than AI; specifically, AI that is vastly superhuman."

    David Krueger, SI researcher · SF Standard, Oct 1

    Four terms, one ladder

    Analysis · definitions as commonly used by researchers

    ASI

    Artificial superintelligence. A system that greatly exceeds the best humans in virtually every domain. Hypothetical: no one has built one.

    Does not exist
    AGI

    Artificial general intelligence. Roughly human-level ability across most kinds of thinking. No settled definition; experts disagree on when, or whether, it arrives.

    Debated
    AI

    Artificial intelligence. The broad, traditional term for software that writes, translates, recognizes images and finds patterns. Improving quickly.

    Today
    SI

    Super Intelligence, the federal term since Sept 29, 2026. A new official name for the same technology as the rung above. A formal definition is due within 60 days.

    Same as AI, by law
    IV

    The front door

    08 · What is America.gov?

    Ask the government, in plain language.

    America.gov launched on September 29 at a separate "Hello, America" event at the Mellon Auditorium in Washington. It is a chat-style search across roughly 29,000 government websites. Its team says it runs on Google's Gemini and xAI's Grok.

    Fact

    The White House says Americans spend about 10 billion hours a year on federal paperwork. Signing in through Login.gov and completing tasks such as passport renewal are planned for later, with transactions targeted for 2027. IRS tax filing and national-security services are excluded.

    White House fact sheet · Fedscoop · Bloomberg

    Critics say

    Early testers found it mostly an information service so far; one expert called answering questions "the easiest 5% of the problem." Within hours it began declining political questions.

    Fedscoop · The Next Web, citing AP

    10 · America and China: why SI became strategic

    Competition and conversation, at the same time.

    US policy increasingly treats advanced SI as strategic: something that shapes a country's economy, science and security, and depends on what it can physically build. Five days before the lunch, China's President Xi Jinping was at the White House for a state dinner.

    Fact

    During Xi's state visit (September 23–25), the two countries agreed to set up a channel for SI-related incidents, with a dedicated dialogue planned for November.

    PBS NewsHour · Washington Post · CBS News

    They said

    On Sunday, September 27, President Trump estimated the US is "about a year, maybe a year and a half up on China." Earlier in 2026, David Sacks put China "three to six months behind" and Google DeepMind's Demis Hassabis said about six months. Estimates like these are hard to verify.

    CBS News · PolitiFact/Poynter, Feb 2026

    What comes next

    13 · What could happen next?

    Promises are easy to sign. Watch what follows.

    • By late November 2026

      A legal definition of SI

      The President's science adviser has 60 days from September 29 to propose one.

    • November 2026

      US–China SI dialogue

      The first talks under the new incident channel are planned for November.

    • Early 2027

      The Super Intelligence Force report

      Due within 120 days of October 4, on the technology's risks and opportunities.

    • Ongoing

      Follow-through on the accord

      Do signers name outside auditors and board committees, and publish anything they find?

    • Ongoing

      Congress

      The accord says its steps may one day belong in law. Several lawmakers want binding rules now.

    • 2027

      America.gov transactions

      Forms and applications are targeted for 2027, as agencies connect.

    14 · Why September 29 may matter historically

    The people building the most powerful SI systems are beginning to coordinate with government about what comes next.

    Rivals signed the same page. The technology got a new official name, and the government a new front door. The accord is voluntary, and critics say voluntary isn't enough. Supporters call it a start that can grow into something stronger.

    Analysis

    Whether September 29 is remembered as a turning point depends on what happens next: audits that actually run, a definition that clarifies more than it confuses, and cooperation that survives the competition.
